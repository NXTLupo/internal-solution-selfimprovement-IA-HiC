# How to Get More Followers on Instagram in 2026 (13 Tried & Tested Tactics)

Source: [buffer_resources](https://buffer.com/resources/grow-on-instagram/)

I have over 15,000 followers on Instagram, and I've helped grow Buffer's Instagram account to over 100K.
One thing I've learned on my journey is that there are no definitive "growth hacks" for increasing your Instagram followers. But the good news is, there's still plenty you can do.
I've tested a lot of these over the years, and I know which ones lead to burnout and which ones actually move the needle.
A larger, engaged following opens up some pretty incredible things: brand partnerships, traffic to the products and pages you want people to find you, and the social proof that pulls in even more followers. And you know what they say about anything worth having: it's worth doing right.
So let's do that, shall we?
In this article, I'll unpack the exact tactics that more than doubled Buffer's Instagram reach, and they've helped my own account grow too. (That 2× is our own account data, separate from the industry report I cite throughout.) They really are tried and tested!
Whether you're looking to grow beyond a plateau or you're starting to grow your Instagram following from scratch, there's something for you here.
These tactics focus on sustainable, organic growth: the kind that compounds over time. Before we get into them, here's the quick answer (though I recommend you read this full article for some important nuance).
Short answer: To get more followers on Instagram, post 3–5 times a week, lead with reels to reach new people and carousels to keep them around, put searchable keywords in your bio, reply to comments, and collaborate with creators in your niche. In Buffer's analysis of 52 million+ posts, reels earned 36% more reach, carousels drove 12% more engagement, and replying to comments lifted engagement by 21%. Growth compounds over months, not days.
Key takeaways
- Posting 3–5 times per week grows followers 2× faster than posting 1–2 times, based on Buffer's State of Social Engagement report, which analyzed 52 million+ posts across 200K+ accounts.
- Instagram Reels generate 36% more reach than other post types: best for attracting new followers.
- Carousels drive 12% more engagement than other formats: best for retaining existing followers.
- Replying to comments boosts engagement by 21%.
- Optimize your bio with keywords in your name field to appear in Instagram search results.
- Cross-promote your Instagram handle on other platforms to convert existing audiences into followers.
- Increasing your Instagram followers comes down to consistency: the accounts that compound are the ones that show up every week, not the ones chasing one viral moment.
- Don't buy Instagram followers: Fake Instagram accounts lower engagement rate and may trigger Instagram penalties.

1. Build an Instagram growth strategy before chasing followers
A growth strategy is what turns random posting into follower growth: it lines up who you're talking to, what you post, and why, so every post pulls its weight instead of disappearing into the void.
Having a clear idea of where Instagram fits into your overall social media marketing strategy will not only help you gain more followers, but also help you get more relevant content ideas for Instagram. When I mapped this out for Buffer, there was none of the usual "what should we post?" guesswork because I'd already worked it out in the strategy.
But how do you create an Instagram growth strategy?
Step 1. Get clear on your goals
Instead of learning how to get more followers on Instagram, step back and reflect on your 'why.' What do you want to gather all those followers for?
Common goals include:
- Building a personal brand
- Raising brand awareness for your company
- Driving website traffic
- Collecting user-generated content
Step 2. Get a 360-view of your target audience
Basic demographics are a great starting point, but try to go a little deeper. What does your audience care about? What are they struggling with? And how can your content actually help them with that?
Next, get a 360-degree view of your ideal follower. Start by clarifying these three things:
- Identity: Who am I talking to?
- Value: What am I helping them with?
- Emotion: What do I want them to feel when they land on my profile?
Natasha Pierre, creator and host of the Shine Online Podcast, says losing sight of your ideal follower in exchange for virality is the single biggest mistake creators make.
"People often focus so much on going viral and reaching as many people as possible that they lose sight of the ideal follower they're trying to reach," she says.
"You could go viral today, and if you're reaching mostly the wrong people, chances are it won't result in them following you. [Even if they do], it would lead to Instagram followers who aren't engaged."
"Taking the time to reflect on who your ideal follower is will help you create specific-to-them content, which will not only result in better growth but quality new followers."
Step 3. Define your brand voice and aesthetic
Even if you're a creator and not a company, it's worth crafting a social media marketing voice that's uniquely you, so Instagram users can identify your posts without seeing the username.
Brand voice is hard to track or quantify, but it can make your content easier to recognize and remember. On Instagram, you can also define your aesthetic along with your brand voice. Use brand colors, stick to a consistent content theme, and have a personality.
Step 4. Create content pillar themes and stick to them
Decide on a niche for your Instagram account. You don't have to box yourself in — but do have a few overarching topics you'll post about, and don't deviate from them too much.
Pick three to five content pillars that sit at the intersection of what you know, what your audience cares about, and what you genuinely enjoy creating. Then stick to them long enough for the algorithm to learn who to show your posts to.
2. Optimize your Instagram bio
Your Instagram bio is a search field: putting keywords in your name field, not just your handle, makes your profile discoverable in Instagram search. It's also the first thing someone sees when they land on your profile, and it's often what decides whether they hit Follow or keep scrolling. Think of it as your visual business card — it needs to tell someone who you are, what you do, and why they should stick around, all in a few seconds.

Use pinned posts in your profile grid
Treat your grid like a storefront by making sure your most compelling content is right up front. Use pinned posts in your profile grid that showcase your best work or a reel that answers your most commonly asked questions.
Want to show up in more searches?
The Instagram algorithm prioritizes search results containing keywords in the name and username. Add one or two keywords that describe what you do right in your name field or handle, like "vegan recipes" or "wedding photographer."
For example, creator Hannah Hargrove has the keywords "Baby + Toddler Nutrition" on her Instagram account, right next to her name. Her username is also "tinyeatersnutrition" — these specifics instantly tell you what to expect from her Instagram account.
Instagram users searching for "toddler nutrition" will also find her account pop up in search (that's how I found her), helping her grow her Instagram followers.
Just don't go overboard with keywords in your Instagram name field, as it'll come across as spammy and water down their purpose. Use just a few phrases that tell people what your Instagram account is about.
3. Cross-promote your Instagram handle on other channels
Cross-promoting your handle is the fastest way to make the most of an audience you already have. Point people from your other channels to your Instagram, and you're not starting from zero.
For example, at Buffer, we have our Instagram link on our website footer, and I add mine anywhere it makes sense.
Add your Instagram page's link to:
- Your product packaging
- Your blogs (when relevant)
- Marketing and transactional emails
- Your website's footer and/or sidebar
- Social media posts from team members
- Your and your employees' email signature
- Bios on platforms like TikTok and YouTube
4. Post 3–5 times per week for consistent growth
Posting 3–5 times per week grows followers roughly twice as fast as posting once or twice, according to Buffer's State of Social Engagement report. Accounts at this frequency also see 12% more reach per post.
