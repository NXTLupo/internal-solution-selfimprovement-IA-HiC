# 13 Trending Songs on TikTok in August 2026 (+ How to Use Them)

Source: [buffer_resources](https://buffer.com/resources/trending-songs-tiktok/)

I don’t know about you, but my earworms are pretty much exclusively trending songs from TikTok at this point. (Even the non-music sounds from TikTok tend to get stuck in my head, but I digress…)
The catchiness of these tunes is incredibly powerful for creators and brands on the social media platform to tap into, because not only do users remember the song; chances are, videos that featured the track will probably stick in their minds, too.
Beyond memorability, of course, is the algorithmic effect of being right on the cusp of a trending TikTok song. And who would say no to a little boost for the content they worked so hard on?
In this post, I’ll share the most viral songs and sounds on TikTok in August 2026 — and how to use them to boost your content.

Why trending TikTok sounds matter
Since you’re here, I probably don’t need to tell you that using a hot sound on TikTok can increase your chances of landing on the For You Page, especially if you’re early to the trend.
TikTok’s algorithm often prioritizes familiarity and repeatability, so when a song is on the rise, jumping on it can give your post more visibility.
For example, if I’m scrolling through my FYP and watch a video with a particular audio, the TikTok algorithm will likely test the waters with another video that uses the same sound a couple of videos later. The algorithm wants to understand exactly why I lingered on the first video. Was it the music?
If yes, boy, does the FYP have more where that came from.
The right TikTok sounds or songs can instantly set the tone, spark emotion, or cue a punchline for your audience, particularly if the sound, like many of the ones below, has been used in a specific way.
The songs or audio that rise to that level of virality that they become a moment in time for internet culture are the really special ones.
All that said: you don't need to spend hours scrolling to find the right audio for your next post. Here are the most popular TikTok songs and audio right now, updated in 2026. We’ll keep this list regularly updated, so do be sure to bookmark this page and come back if you need more inspiration!
How to find trending songs on TikTok
- Browse TikTok’s Creative Center: Visit the Creative Center to see top trending songs by region and time period (above). It’s TikTok’s built-in trend tracker for sounds, creators, and hashtags. Note: TikTok Creative Center trends lists are currently undergoing some changes, and no trending audio is available for now. Watch this space for updates.
- Use TikTok’s search bar: Search phrases like “viral sound” in the TikTok app, tap the Sounds tab, and scroll through the most-used tracks labeled popular.
- Get personalized recommendations from TikTok: When you upload a video, TikTok will suggest music that matches your content, a great shortcut for finding relevant sounds.
- Discover playlists curated by TikTok: Tap Add Music > Discover more sounds to browse playlists by genre, category, or what’s hot in your region.
- Check out Tokchart: Tokchart shows the fastest-rising TikTok sounds from the past 24 hours, updated in real time. You'll need a paid account to get all the details.
- Look for trending audio on Instagram: Instagram Reels often feature songs that cross over to TikTok. Tap the music icon in the Reels editor and check the Trending tab for current hits.
💡 Bonus tip: Always preview how a sound is being used. Tap the sound name on TikTok to watch example videos — this gives you the context you need to ride (or remix) the trend.

1. FREAKED OUT
This track by fatpapimusic&Shushy is being used for the “Reasons to Get a Bob” trend, which often features people showing off their cropped locks. My personal favorite, though, is the spin-off trend of parents jokingly showing why they'll never cut their children's hair, thanks to the (generally less than flattering) bobs the parents sported when they were young.
2. original audio by Daryl Hall & John Oates
This original audio from Daryl Hall & John Oates is behind the "How different our lives are" trend. Creators compare two wildly different routines side by side, making it a funny way to show contrasting lifestyles. Tip for brands: show the difference between doing something manually versus using a product that makes life easier.
3. original audio by Ken Eurich
This sound is for anyone who's ever broken their own rules at the first sign of temptation. Creators use text overlays to reveal the very specific thing that makes them abandon their plans, whether it's sticking to a budget, being healthy, or resisting an impulse purchase. Relatable.
⚠️ Contains an expletive.
4. Saxophones getting louder - Sped Up
This sound uses the sax from the 1991 film, Boyz n the Hood, to signpost something going wrong. "The saxophones getting louder" is now generally used to refer to the moment folks start to spot the red flags. The ominous build-up is the perfect cue for calamity.
5. On a Mission
On a Mission by Duomo is the soundtrack to the "Preparing for my Netflix documentary" trend. Creators film mock confessionals about hilariously niche or everyday situations as if they're the subject of a dramatic true-crime documentary. See you on the small screen.
6. original sound by Irina
This original audio from Irina is back for another summer of travel content. Creators lip sync "I'm in pain... oh no, I'm in Spain" over clips from... You guessed it: Spain. Soak up the last of the summer travels.
7. original audio by Sonicallygifted
This original audio uses a clip of Beyoncé explaining how she freestyled the intro to Partition. Creators are lip-syncing the sound while sharing the stories behind their biggest viral moments.
8. original sound by maciejlubomski
This clip uses the iconic Rush Hour line, "Do you understand the words coming out of my mouth?" Like the movie, creators are lip-syncing the line to tell stories about initial misunderstandings and unlikely friendships or relationships that got off to a rocky start before everything finally clicked.
9. u + me = <3
u + me = <3 by Olivia Rodrigo is becoming the soundtrack to some of TikTok's sweetest photo carousels. Creators are pairing the lyric "I know everybody changes, but I hope that we don't" with friendship, family, and relationship content. Even Lara Raj and Sophia Laforteza of KATSEYE went in on the trend with this cute video.
10. petal
petal by Ariana Grande may have only just been released, but it's already making the rounds on TikTok. Creators are using the track for everything from fan edits to fashion, food, and felines.
11. original audio by babychiefdoit2612
This original audio from BabyChiefDoIt is built around the infectious hook, "Baby, I just wanna dance." And how can you not? I'm loving all the cutesy pet and animal jams.
12. Mind Blank
Mind Blank by OTE feat. Zorro is one of those tracks that works with almost anything. Creators are using it for travel edits, outfit videos, aesthetic vlogs, and everyday moments. It's an easy go-to when you want a feel-good soundtrack without following a specific trend.
13. original sound by Hope Along The Way
This original audio from Hope Along the Way puts a fresh spin on the viral Suspect Challenge. Creators reveal that the "suspect" was hiding in plain sight all along...
⚡Pro tip: For business use on TikTok, only use songs from TikTok’s Commercial Music Library or tracks you have a royalty-free/commercial license for. Using personal-use music in ads or branded content can get your videos muted or removed, so always double-check the license before posting.
