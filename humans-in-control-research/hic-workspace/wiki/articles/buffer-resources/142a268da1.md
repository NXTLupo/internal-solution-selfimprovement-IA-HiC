# Buffer vs. Agorapulse (2026): How Do They Compare?

Source: [buffer_resources](https://buffer.com/resources/buffer-vs-agorapulse/)

What do you need out of a social media management tool? The answer is (of course!) it depends on how you work.
Buffer is built for creators, small businesses, and agencies that want an affordable, flexible way to manage scheduling and content. It’s me, hi! I fall into this category today.
Agorapulse is built for teams that need to manage high volumes of inbound messages, monitor brand mentions, and produce reports for clients or leadership. As a marketer of 11 years, this is a category I know well.
So when I sat down to test both tools, I had a pretty good sense of what each type of user actually needs — and what to watch out for. Also, I’m a bit of a geek, and I love going deep on tools. So when I say I left no stone unturned feature untested here, I’m really not kidding.  
Here’s how Buffer and Agorapulse compare.

Key takeaways
- Buffer has a permanent free plan that includes three channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel, an AI Assistant, API and MCP access, and analytics. Agorapulse doesn’t have a free plan. You get a 30-day trial, then you need to choose a paid plan starting at $99 per user per month for 10 channels, with X/Twitter as a paid add-on.
- Buffer’s paid plans scale with the number of channels you connect, while Agorapulse’s scale with the number of users. Buffer bills per connected channel, starting at $6/channel per month. Agorapulse bills per user, starting at $99/user per month. A single person managing five channels pays $30/month on Buffer’s Essentials plan; the same person on Agorapulse’s Standard plan pays $99/month regardless of channel count, with X being extra.
- Buffer’s Team plan ($12/channel per month) includes unlimited users. Agorapulse charges per user on every plan, so a two-person team on Standard runs close to $198 per month before adding anything else.
- Agorapulse’s inbox lets you manage different types of audience engagement. It consolidates comments, mentions, direct messages, reviews, and paid ad comments into one stream. Buffer's Community inbox handles comment replies and mentions across connected channels, but doesn’t pull in DMs or reviews.
- Agorapulse offers social listening and competitive benchmarking. Both are available as paid add-ons on any plan, so if they’re important to your strategy, Agorapulse is the one that offers them.
- Both platforms have AI features at different price points. All of Buffer's AI features are included on every plan, including Free. Agorapulse's AI reply suggestions for the inbox are available on Custom plans only.
- Buffer supports 12 platform types, including X/Twitter and Mastodon. Agorapulse supports 11 platforms, including X that’s offered as a paid add-on.
Buffer vs. Agorapulse at a glance
Here’s a closer look at each of these.
Plans and pricing
Buffer charges by channel, Agorapulse charges by seat. Here’s what each one costs per month in 2026.
Buffer pricing (billed monthly)
- Free: 3 channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel (refill anytime), 1 user, AI Assistant, API access, analytics, community inbox
- Essentials: $6/channel per month — unlimited posts, 1 user, analytics exports, hashtag manager, first comment scheduling
- Team: $12/channel per month — unlimited posts, unlimited users, approval workflows, custom permissions
Both paid plans use tiered per-channel pricing that drops as you connect more channels. The more channels you manage, the lower your average cost per channel gets.

Agorapulse pricing (billed monthly, per user)
- Standard: $99/user per month — 10 social profiles, publishing, unified inbox, basic reporting
- Professional: $149/user per month — everything in Standard, plus ad comments moderation, Instagram product tagging, simple approval workflows
- Advanced: $199/user per month — everything in Professional, plus advanced ROI reporting, sentiment detection, bulk scheduling, post queues, ad reporting
- Custom: Custom pricing — unlimited profiles, multi-step approval workflows, AI reply suggestions, Reports Open API
- Add-ons across paid plans: X/Twitter, at two tiers:	- $29 per connected X profile per month for publishing only
- $69 per connected X profile per month for publishing, inbox, and reporting
 
If X/Twitter is part of your social media strategy — and especially if you use it as a customer support channel — this is a cost to pay attention to when comparing both tools on pricing.
Every plan includes 10 social profiles regardless of how many users you have (X/Twitter doesn't count towards this). Each profile beyond 10 costs an additional $10/month, on top of the per-user cost. So both users and channels push the bill up independently, with no volume discount on either.
Agorapulse also offers social listening, advocacy, and competitive benchmarking paid add-ons for all plans.

What this means in practice
Let’s look at a couple of scenarios: a three-person team managing eight channels (two Instagram accounts, two Facebook pages, two LinkedIn pages, one TikTok, one X/Twitter), and a five-person agency managing 20 channels across a few clients without X/Twitter. Here’s what each plan costs at both sizes.
For the three-person team, that’s $420 savings in a month, or $5,040 in a year, with Buffer if you compare it to the Professional plan. This is the lowest plan to offer features teams need, like approval workflows. Some of Agorapulse’s best features are only available on Advanced and Custom, so it’s worth comparing to Advanced too — and the gap widens to $570 a month, or $6,840 a year, in this scenario.
For the five-person agency managing 20 channels, the gap on Professional is $685 a month, or $8,200 a year. For Advanced, it’s $935 a month, or $11,220 a year.
Is that worth it? It comes down to whether your team needs these features, or if Buffer’s approval workflows and unlimited-user Team plan already cover what you actually need.
Scheduling and publishing
Buffer and Agorapulse cover the same core scheduling ground: a visual calendar with monthly and weekly views, scheduled first comments, thread scheduling on X, and per-channel content customization. Where they differ is how quickly and flexibly you can schedule posts.
Buffer gives you more room to work with here. You can set up a queue with posting time slots for each channel — either times you manually select or using the latest data from Buffer’s data science team on the best times to post for each channel. When you hit the schedule button, the post gets added to the queue to go out in the next available slot. It removes the guesswork of “when should I post this?”
And if you're scheduling a lot of content in one batch, you can upload up to 100 posts in a CSV file to add to the queue (users on Buffer’s Free plan can bulk schedule 10 posts at a time per channel).
Agorapulse places these features behind higher pricing tiers: post queues and bulk scheduling are available on the Advanced and Custom plans. Without them, you’re scheduling one post at a time and manually selecting the time for each one. Buffer includes both features at every tier.
When Agorapulse’s bulk scheduling is available, it goes further: up to 200 posts at once, compared to Buffer’s 100 for paid plans.
If you’re on a plan with a queue on Agorapulse, you commit to either a queue slot or a custom time before you draft a post. Switch from one to the other after you start customizing and you lose the edits, so you’re starting over. No fun. Buffer lets you move a post between a queue and a specific date and time without losing what you’ve written.
Agorapulse has an edge on the extras: you can add tags to your Instagram products directly in the composer and boost a Facebook post without leaving the tool. There’s also a content library where your team can store and share media assets you need to reuse across campaigns or platforms.
Analytics and reporting
Buffer’s analytics live in Insights, available on every plan and covering account-level, post-level, and follower growth data across all your platforms. It’s designed to give you both the raw numbers and the key takeaways to help you figure out what to do next.
Start with the all-channels view and you get a big-picture look at your entire social media presence: how you’re performing overall, which channels are pulling their weight and which need a little help, plus followers and posts across everything you manage in one place. It’s a useful way to see the whole picture at a glance before drilling into any individual channel to zoom in on how it’s going.
At the channel level, weekly takeaways are super handy — a short read on what’s working and what to do next. One thing I love about these takeaways is that they don’t just hand you advice and call it a day: each one comes with a button to get you there. Filling a gap in your posting schedule or replying to a wave of comments is one click away. It’s a small thing, but I’ve found it to be the difference between “I’ll do this now” and “I’ll get to this later.” It helps me keep momentum going where things are going well.
If you’re on a paid plan, you can export your data: PDF works great if you need something to present, and CSV or Markdown are perfect if you’re pulling the numbers into an LLM or custom app to dig in deeper.
Buffer also lets you track your campaigns in Google Analytics without needing to connect or authorize it — simply enable campaign tracking in your channel settings. Every link you share in a post will go out with UTM parameters attached. You can see how much traffic you receive from your posts directly in Google Analytics.
Agorapulse’s analytics are more focused on raw numbers, and they go deep on every metric. You can build a custom report from scratch or start from a template, then drag in whichever metrics you need across all your data.
