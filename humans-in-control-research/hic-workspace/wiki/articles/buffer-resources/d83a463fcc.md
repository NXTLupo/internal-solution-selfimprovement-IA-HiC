# Buffer vs. Hootsuite (2026): Which Social Media Management Tool Is Better For You?

Source: [buffer_resources](https://buffer.com/resources/buffer-vs-hootsuite/)

If you're on the hunt for a social media management tool, Buffer and Hootsuite are probably two of the first names you'll come across — and for good reason. Both offer solid scheduling, analytics, and engagement features to help you stay consistent on social.
So which one is best? There's no single answer — the better question is: which social media management tool is right for you?
Buffer is the better fit for creators, solopreneurs, and small businesses that want an affordable, easy-to-use tool, while Hootsuite is built for large marketing teams that need social listening, ad management, and competitive benchmarking. Buffer starts free and costs $5 per channel/month on paid plans; Hootsuite has no free plan and starts at $99 per user/month.
While both Buffer and Hootsuite offer similar core features (social media management, scheduling, and analytics), they were built with pretty different users in mind.
Buffer serves small to medium businesses, solopreneurs, and creators. Hootsuite is built primarily for enterprise social media marketing teams.
You'll see these target users reflected in different ways across the tools, from how features work to the pricing structure.
Key takeaways
- Buffer is built for creators and small-to-mid-sized businesses; Hootsuite is built for large enterprise marketing teams.
- Buffer has a forever-free plan for up to 3 channels; Hootsuite offers no free plan, only a 30-day trial.
- Buffer charges per social channel ($0–$12/mo); Hootsuite charges per user, starting at $99/mo billed annually.
- Both include AI assistants, but Buffer's AI Assistant is unlimited on every plan while Hootsuite's OwlyWriter has token limits.
- Buffer supports niche channels like Mastodon and Google Business Profiles that Hootsuite does not.

Buffer vs. Hootsuite pricing compared (2026)
The clearest difference between the two tools is how they charge: Buffer bills per social channel, while Hootsuite bills per user. Cost matters (a lot), and this is particularly important when some of your essential features might only be available on specific plans or cost extra.
Buffer pricing
- Free: $0 — up to 3 channels, 10 queued posts per channel
- Essentials: $5/channel/mo (billed annually; $6/mo monthly)
- Team: $10/channel/mo (billed annually; $12/mo monthly) — unlimited team members
You can see the full breakdown on Buffer's pricing page.
Hootsuite pricing
- Standard: $99/user/mo (billed annually; $149/mo monthly)
- Advanced: $249/user/mo (billed annually; $399/mo monthly)
- Enterprise: Custom pricing (contact sales)
Free plan: Buffer vs. Hootsuite compared
Buffer has a forever-free plan; Hootsuite does not. Buffer's free plan covers up to 3 channels with 10 queued posts per channel, analytics, a universal comment inbox, and a Start Page link-in-bio.. Hootsuite offers no free plan, only a 30-day trial of its paid tiers, after which you pay from $99 per user/month.

If you're a creator or a small team, Buffer's per-channel model keeps costs low — you can add teammates without paying more. If you're a large team where lots of people need access, Hootsuite's per-user pricing is built for that scale, but it adds up fast for a small group.
Free trials
Buffer offers a 14-day trial for its Essentials and Team plans, on top of the forever-free plan for up to 3 channels. Hootsuite offers a 30-day trial of its Standard and Advanced plans, after which pricing starts at $99/user/mo (Standard) or $249/user/mo (Advanced).

Ease of use
How simple a tool is plays a massive role in whether you can get started quickly or spend time wrestling with the interface. The faster you feel comfortable, the faster you can focus on your content strategy.
Buffer
Getting started on Buffer is quick. You can connect your social media accounts, set up a posting queue, and start publishing within minutes of signing up. The onboarding flow walks you through how to draft and publish content, so you're ready to go within minutes of signing up.
Publishing content is far from the only thing Buffer offers. There's the powerful new Insights tab for measuring the performance of all your channels in a single, easy-to-understand hub. There's even a 'Takeaways' feature that interprets the data for you (a huge win if you're not a natural number cruncher).
The Community tab, a single 'inbox' for all your comments, is similarly easy to use and get going with. A lot of work went into making the space calm and comfortable (as opposed to the chaos that comes with responding to messages on multiple platforms!).
Buffer also integrates with tools like Canva, Unsplash, Dropbox, and Google Drive, so you can pull in images and designs without leaving the platform. It keeps the creative process smooth and all in one place.
Hootsuite
Hootsuite includes some useful extras (like paid post management), and it's built to handle a wide range of social media needs under one roof. That also means it takes a bit longer to find your footing and get real value out of it.
You can expect to spend some time figuring out where to find different features and how to use them. Navigating between streams, inbox settings, and analytics dashboards takes some getting used to. I definitely had to make a few trips to their knowledge base to find my way around.
Social media planning and scheduling
Both tools cover the fundamentals of social media scheduling, but they differ in the details.
Available channels
Both tools connect to the major networks. Beyond those, Buffer supports niche channels like Mastodon and Google Business Profiles; Hootsuite supports WhatsApp and paid ads on Facebook, LinkedIn, and X. Which matters more depends on the social media platforms you actually publish to.
Creating and scheduling posts
Posting across platforms
Both tools let you draft a post once and customize it per channel before it goes out.
Bulk scheduling posts
Buffer lets you bulk-upload up to 100 posts via CSV on paid plans, with no queue limit. Hootsuite supports bulk scheduling of up to 350 posts on its Advanced and Enterprise plans.
AI assistance
Buffer's AI Assistant is available on all plans, including free, with no usage limits, and Buffer's new API lets you build ChatGPT and Claude workflows on top of it.
Hootsuite's OwlyWriterAI is included on paid plans and offers a wider range of AI-powered tools, including campaign idea generation and content repurposing based on trending topics. That said, it comes with token limits, so you'll need to be more selective about when you use it.
Hootsuite's API is available for custom integrations and enterprise workflows, though access and implementation are generally geared toward larger organizations.
Cover images for short-form video
Hootsuite can't select covers or thumbnails for Instagram Reels (on the Creator plan) or TikTok. Buffer lets you set them.
Crossposting to Pinterest
Hootsuite can't crosspost to Pinterest. Buffer can.
Calendar views
Buffer offers channel-specific week and month views. Hootsuite's week view shows detail, but its month view shows post status rather than the platform, which makes it harder to build a clear social media content calendar at a glance.
Hashtag support
Buffer has a free Instagram hashtag generator plus an in-app hashtag manager. Hootsuite offers hashtag recommendations inside its composer.
Capturing ideas and content planning
Planning ahead looks different depending on the tool you use. Buffer provides a dedicated space to gather and organize social media content ideas, while Hootsuite leans on AI to help spark and shape them.
Buffer
On the planning front, Buffer offers Create Space, an ideas hub where you can store any post content ideas, text, and media to access when you're ready to publish them.
Tags and a Kanban board view help you keep this library structured, and built-in templates plus the AI Assistant help you quickly turn a saved idea into a polished draft.
Hootsuite
Hootsuite takes a different approach. Its planning features instead center on AI, with OwlyGPT acting as a chatbot that gives personalized suggestions based on your connected brands and accounts. You can use it to generate campaign ideas, write posts or video scripts, or even build a drafting schedule for one channel or for your social presence as a whole.
There’s no dedicated space for storing unfinished post ideas as you work on them, and you could save your post as a draft as a workaround.
Social media engagement
Both Hootsuite and Buffer offer social media engagement features that will help you stay on top of messages and comments across multiple social media platforms.
Buffer’s engagement features
Buffer's Community feature is designed to help you build genuine connections with your audience. You can jump into conversations right from the Community tab in Buffer, and when you're stuck on what to say, the AI Assistant offers suggestions to get the conversation flowing.
If you find yourself answering the same questions over and over, you can save those responses and pull them up whenever you need them instead of typing the same thing repeatedly.
You'll also see a comment score for each channel based on how quickly and consistently you respond, so you can understand where engagement feels strong and where it might need attention.
The Community tool also gives you a window into how your audience is responding, whether that's comments on individual posts or replies and mentions across an entire social media account.
