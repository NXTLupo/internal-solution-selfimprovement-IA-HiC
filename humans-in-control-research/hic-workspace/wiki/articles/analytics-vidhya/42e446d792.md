# 7 Best AI Agent Collaboration Platforms in 2026

Source: [analytics_vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2026/11/best-ai-agent-collaboration-platforms/)

Building an AI agent is one thing. Making it available to an entire team is another. Shared access, permissions, integrations, and reusable workflows determine whether an agent becomes a reliable team resource or another setup everyone must recreate.
Platforms approach this differently. Some share a single assistant, others coordinate multiple agents, while some distribute independent workflow copies. In this article, we share seven Agentic AI collab platforms highlighting the workflows each handles best.
AI agent collaboration platforms let teams build, share, manage, and reuse AI agents without configuring each one from scratch. They provide a shared environment where team members can access agents, connect tools, assign tasks, and collaborate on workflows.
These platforms differ in how they handle collaboration. Some let multiple users interact with the same agent and its shared context, while others support multi-agent coordination or allow teams to clone and distribute agent templates. Depending on the platform, teams can also manage permissions, monitor agent activity, and maintain consistent workflows across projects.
Copilot Studio lets you configure an agent’s instructions, knowledge and tools, then publish it to supported channels such as Microsoft Teams. Integration with Teams, allows the tool to be accessed and used by a large group of people.
Child and connected agents support more complex designs. Before building, check licensing and tenant approval requirements: a working preview doesn’t guarantee colleagues can access the published agent.
Best for: Organizations building agents within Microsoft 365 and Teams.
Try it: Microsoft Copilot Studio
Workspace Studio builds flows across Gmail, Chat, Drive and other apps from templates or natural-language instructions. A scheduled flow could summarize unread email and deliver the digest to Google Chat.
Sharing creates independent copies. Colleagues don’t inherit the original flow’s run history or access to its files, so treat it as distributing a recipe. Availability depends on account eligibility and administrator settings; personal accounts cannot share flows.
Best for: Teams automating recurring work across Google Workspace apps.
Try it: Google Workspace Studio
Taskade combines projects, custom agents and automation. AI Teams lets you route a request to one agent, selected agents, everyone or an orchestrated team. The Ask Agent Team automation step brings that collaboration into recurring workflows.
AI Teams require Pro or higher, and each responding agent consumes credits. Calling every agent for a narrow question can add unnecessary usage.
Best for: Project teams combining tasks, agent teams and recurring automation.
Try it: Taskade
Oasis puts people and agents in shared rooms, where colleagues can discuss a task, direct agents and follow handoffs. Prebuilt and custom agents, app integrations and local-agent connections support different jobs. You can keep the discussion, context and agent work together.
For a research team, that could mean coordinating source gathering and review in one room. The listed free plan has three combined human and agent seats; check room size, models and integrations before committing.
Best for: Teams that want people and agents collaborating in shared rooms.
Try it: Oasis
Dust connects reusable team agents to company knowledge and tools. A support agent grounded in approved product documentation gives colleagues a common starting point for answers, with administrators controlling access to sources and capabilities.
Check connector coverage and source permissions, and assign someone to maintain the documentation. Connecting stale material won’t make it current.
Best for: Teams using shared agents to find and apply internal knowledge.
Try it: Dust
Lindy packages its product as an AI employee working across everyday business tools. It’s a straightforward option for recurring coordination work, such as following up across messages and calendars, without designing a specialist agent for every step.
Evaluate the exact task: confirm supported integrations, actions, approval behavior and credit usage. A shared assistant may be enough when the workflow doesn’t need explicit coordination between multiple agents.
Best for: Business teams automating follow-ups and coordination across everyday apps.
Try it: Lindy
Relevance AI’s Workforce builder connects specialized agents into a defined process. You can make a handoff mandatory or let AI select the next step. For sales operations, that could connect company research with a separate agent preparing a review brief.
Connections are directional, so plan how information moves and how incomplete results are handled. Check the required tools, action limits and credit costs.
Best for: Teams configuring specialist agents with explicit roles and handoffs.
Try it: Relevance AI
If your team already works in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, start there. Consider Oasis or Dust when people and agents need to steer work in a shared room. Test one workflow from trigger to output, including what a colleague inherits and how much a complete run costs.
