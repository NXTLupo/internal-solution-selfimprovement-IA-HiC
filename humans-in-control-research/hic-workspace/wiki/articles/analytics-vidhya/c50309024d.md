# FDE vs AI Engineer: Roles, Skills, and Career Paths Explained

Source: [analytics_vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2026/10/fde-vs-ai-engineer-roles-skills/)

Python, APIs, cloud deployment. You’ll find these skills in both forward deployed engineer and AI engineer job descriptions. Yet the two jobs can lead to very different working days.
An FDE might spend the morning tracing a customer’s approval process before writing an integration. A product AI engineer might spend it investigating why the same application became slower after an update. Both need to ship working software. What changes is how their responsibilities are organized, which problems reach them and how success is judged.
Treat this graphic as a broad orientation. FDEs may retain production ownership; AWS’s description includes maintenance and production incidents. Travel varies by role. The “far more roles” claim isn’t supported by a vacancy count, so the job-market row shouldn’t determine your choice.
A forward deployed engineer builds and deploys software alongside customers. In AI companies, that often means taking a model-based workflow from an initial idea into production. OpenAI’s FDE role spans discovery, technical scoping, system design, implementation and rollout. The customer’s operating environment shapes the engineering: restricted data, old systems and undocumented approval rules all become part of the problem.
FDE work draws on several disciplines to deliver a usable customer system.

That last step matters. Anthropic’s FDE description explicitly includes sharing deployment patterns with Product and Engineering. Customer work can inform reusable capabilities, rather than becoming an endless collection of custom fixes.
An AI engineer builds and operates software that uses AI. Depending on the role, that could mean an assistant, recommendation service, vision application or tool-using agent. On a product or internal team, the work often continues across releases. Criteo’s Senior AI Engineer role, for example, includes agents, debugging, deployment, monitoring and maintenance.
AI engineering combines application development with evaluation and production delivery.
A model upgrade is an engineering decision
Suppose a cheaper model answers routine questions well but misses unusual policy exceptions. Switching models reduces the cost per request while introducing a quality problem. The engineer needs representative evaluation cases, latency and cost measurements, and a way to roll back the release.
Following the application over time builds knowledge of which inputs fail, which dependencies are fragile and which changes help users. Maintaining that system involves data, integrations and software operations alongside the model. Training a model from scratch isn’t a requirement of every AI engineering position.
A release decision should explain the trade-off: which cases changed, what happened to operating costs and what evidence justifies letting users depend on the new version. That explanation belongs alongside the implementation.
Imagine a retailer introducing an AI assistant for support staff. It must answer policy questions, look up orders and leave refund approvals to authorized employees. Both engineers could help build it. The difference becomes clearer when you examine the decisions they need to make.

The FDE might discover that an outdated policy repository is the immediate obstacle. The product AI engineer might discover that a model upgrade introduced a regression. Skills both roles need
FDE work adds a strong customer-discovery requirement: ask useful questions, negotiate scope and explain trade-offs. Product AI work rewards sustained knowledge of a system and its behaviour. Both need communication and engineering depth. To explore one shared skill, start with RAG evaluation frameworks for assessing retrieval and answer quality.
Depth depends on the application. A vision role may need image pipelines; a document assistant may need retrieval and permissions. Treat the job description as a set of problems to investigate, rather than a shopping list of frameworks. Ask what the team ships and which parts you would own.
Build a document assistant that someone else can use without your help. Treat the project as evidence of your decisions, not just a working chat interface. The same application can help you practise both kinds of responsibility.
1. Define the workflow
Interview a prospective user. Identify the questions they need answered, which documents they can access and when a person must approve an action. Write down the first release’s scope and success criteria. This develops the discovery and scoping skills central to FDE work.
2. Build beyond the demo
Add permission-aware retrieval, an evaluation set, monitoring and deployment instructions. Include cases involving missing documents, conflicting policies and unavailable services. Record failures instead of hiding them behind the assistant’s best responses.
3. Ship a second release
Change the retrieval settings or model, then compare the versions. Document what improved, what regressed and how you decided whether to ship. This demonstrates the continuing evaluation and maintenance expected in product AI work.
4. Present the engineering decisions
Include a workflow map, permission rules, evaluation results, a failure log and release notes. For FDE applications, explain how user feedback changed the scope. For product AI applications, show how you diagnosed and resolved a regression.
For a structured introduction, explore The Forward Deployed Engineer: Taking AI From Pilot to Production. It offers a certificate of completion. Use it to organize your learning, then demonstrate what you can deliver through the project.
Demonstrate how customer-facing engineering and product AI translate user needs into measurable outcomes.
Consider FDE work if you enjoy learning how unfamiliar organizations operate, clarifying incomplete requirements and working directly with customers. Consider a product or internal AI engineering role if you want to follow a system across releases, develop architectural depth and investigate recurring failures.
Look at the working day
FDE work can involve frequent changes of context: a customer workshop, an integration problem, then a discussion about scope. Product AI engineering often offers greater continuity with one system, although incidents and changing priorities can still interrupt planned work. Neither role is a promise of uninterrupted coding.
