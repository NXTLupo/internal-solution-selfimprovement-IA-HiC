# Penn State Extension Offers a Hotline For The Latest Crop Production Issues

Source: [agr_morning_ag_clips](https://www.morningagclips.com/penn-state-extension-offers-a-hotline-for-the-latest-crop-production-issues/)

UNIVERSITY PARK, Pa. — A Penn State Extension toll-free hotline that provides quick updates on the latest crop production, weather outlook, and disease and pest management issues for Pennsylvania horticultural crop producers has expanded to include agronomic content for the 2026 growing season.
While the hotline is designed primarily for growers who do not use computer technology to receive timely crop management decisions and to be informed of upcoming extension events and programs, anyone can call 800-PENN-IPM (800-736-6476) to access this information.
As experts in agriculture and horticulture, teams of Penn State Extension faculty specialists and county-based educators meet regularly to discuss the latest production issues and release their recommendations through a variety of media, including electronic news bulletins, podcasts and educational kiosks. Messages released on the 800 PENN IPM system are condensed versions of these — averaging about three minutes in duration — and focus on the most critical concerns seen on farms and in greenhouses across the state.
The hotline also features:
— Multiple lines for vegetable crops, such as onions and other Allium species, tomatoes and potatoes, sweet corn, and vine crops.
— Multiple new lines for field and forage (primarily agronomic) crops, covering weed, disease, and insect control and a complete directory of team contacts.
— Dedicated lines for the cut-flower industry, small fruit production and Christmas tree production.
— A dedicated line for the weekly weather outlook.
— Multiple lines for tree-fruit production issues, general industry concerns, announcements for upcoming events and a directory of tree-fruit team contacts.
— Dedicated Spanish-language translation lines for vegetable, small fruit and tree-fruit updates.
Instructions for navigating the hotline are available on the Penn State Extension website. Callers can reach out to their local extension educators to suggest topics that should be addressed or crops that should receive a dedicated message box on the hotline.
— Penn State Extension
