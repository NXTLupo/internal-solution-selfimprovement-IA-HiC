# Bryce White Named Executive Director of Texas Corn Producers

Source: [agr_morning_ag_clips](https://www.morningagclips.com/bryce-white-named-executive-director-of-texas-corn-producers/)

LUBBOCK, Texas – Texas Corn Producers (TCP) announced that Bryce White has been selected to serve as the organization’s next executive director, effective Sept. 1, 2026. White will succeed David Gibson, who will retire in September.
White has been a part of the TCP team for nearly a decade. His experience across the corn industry has given him a deep understanding of the issues facing Texas farmers and the insight needed to lead the organization alongside its board of directors.
The Lubbock, Texas, native has a rich agricultural background and remains active in his family’s operation, White Land & Cattle Company. At TCP, he has led advocacy endeavors in Washington, overseen the organization’s research efforts, and helped expand aflatoxin mitigation efforts into a broader mycotoxin initiative. Prior to joining TCP, White worked for the U.S. House Committee on Agriculture. He is a Texas Tech graduate, with a master’s degree in agricultural communications and a bachelor’s degree in animal science.
“I’m honored by this opportunity,” White said. “This is a challenging time for many in agriculture, and Texas corn farmers deserve an organization that meets that moment. I’m ready to lead TCP as a steady resource and a strong advocate for our farmers on the issues that will shape the next generation.”
The TCP selection committee, composed of board officers and directors for both the checkoff and association boards, selected White to continue the organization’s work and lead it into the future.
“We’re confident Bryce will build on the foundation David spent nearly three decades building,” Aaron Martinka, chairman of the checkoff board, said. “He has the background and drive to see TCP grow in its work on behalf of the state’s corn farmers.”
Gibson’s retirement closes 27 years of leadership at TCP. Under his direction, the organization grew its relevance – both to the farmers it serves and to the decision makers at the state and national level – establishing TCP as a respected voice on the issues that matter most to corn farmers.
“Whatever this organization has accomplished, it was never one person: it was the board, the staff, and the farmers who showed up,” Gibson said. “I’m leaving knowing it’s in good hands. Bryce understands these farmers and the issues they face, and TCP will be stronger for his leadership.”
— Texas Corn Producers
