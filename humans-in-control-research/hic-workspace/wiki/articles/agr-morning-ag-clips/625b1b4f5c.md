# American Farm Workers Burn Thousands of Calories Daily: Are They Eating Enough?

Source: [agr_morning_ag_clips](https://www.morningagclips.com/american-farm-workers-burn-thousands-of-calories-daily-are-they-eating-enough/)

BOISE, Idaho — Agriculture is one of the most strenuous occupations in the United States. It is typically around 4 am or 4 pm in the morning and evening. Heavy equipment, lifting the feed bags, hauling hay bales, repairing fences and walking miles across the fields all use a lot of energy. But agricultural health is generally concerned with crop and livestock health. Few voices are heard of the one who is doing all the work.
Depending on the season and the work load, the normal farm worker will burn from 2,500 to 4,500 calories each day. This is even higher in the harvest season. This is in comparison to an office worker who will burn approximately 1,800-2,200 calories per day, and it’s obvious which one burns more calories. Farmers are similar to athletes. But they are not being treated like one.
But most farm workers aren’t athletes, either. In general, it is common to consume fast foods, lack breakfast, grab a snack and dinner at a gas station throughout the rural belt. This type of dieting functions for some time. But with time it leads to fatigue, delayed recovery, loss of muscle mass, and chronic health problems, all of which take a toll on productivity, without anyone realizing.
Muscle loss after the age of 40 is one of the concerns of farm workers. This is when the body starts to lose muscle and lose the muscle more quickly than it builds it up (this is called sarcopenia). This is not something that will end upon the onset of one’s work. Protein is important for the body to have throughout the day to maintain and repair muscle tissue. Many of the farm workers do not receive adequate protein in the day because it is not available or convenient to prepare protein in the 12 hours duration of work.
This is where nutrition products for physically active individuals can come in handy. For more and more American farm workers and ranch hands, a convenient way to obtain their daily dose of calories and protein without adding another meal to their busy lives is mass gainer protein powder. When mixed with milk or water, it provides a serving of 600-1,200 calories and 40-60 grams of protein in less than 5 minutes. To someone who has just finished 3 hours of pre-dawn chores, and really wants to get moving, that’s a lot of work.
There are numerous protein supplement varieties available. The needs of farm workers are different from those of the gym athletes. They need sustained energy throughout rather than a maximum energy during the exercise. Combinations of fast and slow digesting proteins (such as whey and casein protein) work best for this group. The formula is a complex carbohydrate, which helps to maintain a steady blood sugar level during prolonged exercise. Farm work has been known to be tough on the joints and healthy fats keep them healthy.
Hydration adds to the problem. In the heat of the day, especially during the summer when planting and harvesting takes place, Midwest and South farmers are diligent. Dehydration leads to decreased appetite, foggy thinking and more tiredness. Many farm workers tend to mistake thirst for hunger and vice versa. There is no compromise on the need to drink sufficient water all day long and there is a need to match electrolyte loss with water loss due to sweat.
Adults that are involved in strenuous physical work should aim for approximately 0.6 to 0.8 grams of protein per pound of body weight each day. This equals about 108 – 144 grams of protein a day for a 180 lb. farm worker. It’s difficult to consume that much in one day, especially when working on a farm. For most workers, it’s just not a possibility.
Wellness programs for farm workers are gaining momentum in farm states like Iowa, Nebraska, Texas and California. A few large farmers have started to incorporate nutrition into their worker health programmes. It’s an astute buy. A well fed farm worker will recover from calving quickly, have less chance of being a victim of illness and will have increased production throughout the entire growing season.
The American farmer is the supplier of food to the world. Strength, endurance and resilience are needed in that work, every day. Now it was time that those who worked looked after themselves with the same care and thoughtfulness with which they tended to their land.
With good people, comes good farm work. Those of strong character, eating well, are good health.
