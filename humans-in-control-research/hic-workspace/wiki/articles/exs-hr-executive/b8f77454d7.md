# Oracle plans more layoffs before its next fiscal quarter: Report

Source: [exs_hr_executive](https://hrexecutive.com/oracle-plans-more-layoffs-before-its-next-fiscal-quarter-report/)

Oracle is preparing another round of layoffs this month, with managers asked to identify affected employees ahead of the company’s second fiscal quarter, which starts Sept. 1, according to a new Business Insider report. Some teams could see workforce reductions in the double digits, the report said. Oracle has not publicly confirmed the plans.
Oracle reported approximately 141,000 full-time employees as of May 31, 2026, compared with approximately 162,000 a year earlier. Its fiscal 2026 Form 10-K reported $1.84 billion in restructuring and other expenses, consisting primarily of employee severance costs associated with its fiscal 2026 restructuring plan.
According to the filing, Oracle has tied its workforce reductions to funding a buildout of AI data centers, spending $55.7 billion on AI infrastructure in fiscal 2026 alone and borrowing $43 billion to help cover it. The company is reportedly looking to raise another $40 billion in debt and equity this year. Cloud infrastructure revenue grew 77% to $18.1 billion over the same period.

What HR leaders are watching
HR Executive has covered how Oracle handled its earlier cuts when employees learned of the news through a 6 a.m. email signed by “Oracle Leadership.” Within hours, the news was circulating widely on LinkedIn, largely through employees’ own posts. Communications specialists who spoke with HR Executive at the time pointed to the speed and emotional weight of that self-reported narrative as the key challenge for HR, more than any statement the company itself put out.
In April, the company’s severance terms drew comparisons to Block’s in HR Executive coverage. Oracle offered four weeks of base pay plus one week per year of tenure, capped at 26 weeks. Block offered 20 weeks of base pay, one week per year of tenure, six months of healthcare coverage and a $5,000 transition stipend.
If this next round proceeds as reported, HR leaders will be interested in how the cuts are delivered and what the severance offering looks like. Neither has been confirmed yet, and Oracle has stayed quiet publicly on both the earlier round and this one.
