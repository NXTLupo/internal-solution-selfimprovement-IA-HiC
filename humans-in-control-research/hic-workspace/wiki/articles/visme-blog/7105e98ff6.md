# How to Speed Up Sales Content Production: Insights From 164 B2B Revenue Teams

Source: [visme_blog](https://visme.co/blog/sales-content-production/)

How to Speed Up Sales Content Production: Insights From 164 B2B Revenue Teams
As your organization grows, so does the amount of customer-facing content your sales team ships, sometimes with the same headcount.
Pitch decks, quarterly business reviews, proposals, and renewal decks all need to be accurate, on-brand, and tailored to the account receiving them
But scaling the production of this content often comes with a trade-off: teams either spend more time creating it or move faster at the expense of quality and personalization. Our research with 164 B2B revenue teams shows where that process starts to break down and what more efficient teams do differently.
In this post, we'll unpack those findings to show where sales content production loses time and the practical changes teams can make to speed things up without cutting corners on quality.

What Is Sales Content?
Sales content is any material revenue teams use to educate, engage and guide active prospects through the decision stages of the buyer journey.
Most of it is external, built for a buyer to read: pitch decks, proposals, case studies, one-pagers and pricing summaries. Our guide to sales collateral covers the full range.
But also, some of it never leaves the building. Battlecards, objection-handling guides, call scripts and rep onboarding material are also considered sales content, more specifically, sales enablement content.
On the external side, what a buyer needs also changes as the deal moves. This leads to constant personalization that passes through several contributors before the content reaches the buyer or client.
On top of that, existing client accounts need their own content, such as onboarding decks, QBRs and renewal cases.
These high-volume, internal and customer-facing deliverables are typically rebuilt repeatedly by sales teams because they are used so often.
For optimal performance, sales representatives and account executives rely on the smooth combination of sales content production and sales content management.
Sales production is creating new content that sales reps and account execs need. Sales content management is responsible for keeping all created content findable in storage, setting permissions and tracking version history.
Every sales team follows a similar content production workflow, with many people involved at different stages.
Why Sales Content Production Breaks Down at Scale
When the sales content process runs like a well- oiled machine, there’s hardly any reason for production to break down.
But as an organization grows, production and personalization volumes increase rapidly, especially when there are many sales reps and several accounts to manage.
When things do go wrong, it tends to be one of these issues. Which together compound into a lot of lost time.
- Content is hard to find. If the library is disorganized, reps and execs struggle to locate the file they need. So they build a new one or send the request to the content team, and work that already existed gets done again.
- Not enough checks on what goes out. A file can be easy to find and still be wrong. Without guidelines or regular reviews, a deck reaches a client carrying last quarter's pricing, retired messaging or an old logo.
- The data is moved by hand. Reps are expected to personalize what they send, and every value entered manually can be typed incorrectly. This happens when there is no permanent connector to the CRM, when data cannot be imported from its source, or when numbers get copied and pasted between tools.
Let’s look at how this would affect a QBR deck:
- The layout is identical every quarter, but the account data, usage numbers and renewal risk are not.
- Someone opens last quarter's file and replaces those figures while others update different parts.
- The deck goes back for another round of reviews, but some of the data errors are missed.
- Next quarter, the same sequence runs again.
What’s the real cost of this type of scenario? Too many hours spent on rework, fixing outdated information, double-checking and triple-checking data visualizations, ensuring no words are used from the “don’t use list” and much more.
Across the 164 teams surveyed for the Visme B2B Sales Content Benchmark 2026, 85% of respondents said they lose between 10% and 50% of their working week redoing content that was supposedly already finished.
That is up to 2.5 days of rework per week. And the fact that no one is really tracking that lost time simply becomes regular work hours.
The compounding effect is visible throughout the industry. Salesforce found that reps spend 70 % of their time on non-selling tasks, including administrative work and meeting preparation, based on responses from 5,500 sales professionals across four regions. Some of that “administrative work and meeting preparation” is likely time spent reworking content repeatedly.
How to Use Content for Sales More Effectively
Getting more out of the content you already have is what separates an effective sales content strategy from one that runs on rebuilds. It comes down to how content is accessed, chosen, transferred, timed and measured.
Let's discuss six habits that will improve your team’s efficiency in producing and updating sales content.
Match Content to The Buyer's Stage and Current Conversation
A deal that's moving forward needs the next piece of content to match the right stage of the sales funnel. Matching well means the content answers the questions the buyer is currently considering, which in turn earns you the move towards the next step. A misalignment might cause the deal to stall.
For instance, a pitch deck sent at the negotiation stage reads like a step backward, since the buyer has already moved past that stage of the conversation.
To help your team match content to the buyer's conversation and align with the pipeline, map each asset to a deal stage or sales cycle.
For example:
- Awareness: Overview one-pager or short deck
- Evaluation: Comparison sheet or case study matched to the prospect's industry
- Negotiation: Pricing summary or ROI breakdown
- Onboarding: Implementation guide or kickoff deck
- Renewal: Usage recap or QBR

Personalize For The Conversation, Not Just The Company Name
Personalization often gets handled as a merge-field task. And yes, dynamic fields are a great resource for this part of the process. But you can’t leave it at that; you have to add deeper personalization.
It helps to think of personalization as having levels, and to know which level a piece of content needs.
- Field level. This includes a logo, company name, contact name or job title. Dynamic fields handle these automatically once they're set up, so the deck arrives addressed to the right account without much effort.
- Stage level. At this level, the personalization fits the deal as it currently stands. Someone comparing vendors gets a comparison sheet, someone waiting on legal gets a one-page pricing summary, and a client three weeks from renewal gets a usage recap rather than a pitch.
- Content level. Here, the assets themselves change to fit the account, not only the details on them. A logistics prospect gets a logistics case study, a 200-person company gets benchmarks from companies its size, and the QBR opens with their own usage numbers.
- Conversation level. At this level, the content responds to what the buyer actually raised with you. A buyer negotiating terms gets a pricing summary that highlights the features they mentioned needing, not a general overview of everything you offer.
