15 Best Employee Training Software: An In-Depth Guide
Choosing the right employee training software for your L&D programs is vital for success.
But there are so many options, each with different capabilities.
To help you pick the one that best aligns with your organization's training goals and actual situation, I set out to find the absolute best platforms available.
I tested and reviewed 15 top employee training platforms to provide the information you need to make an informed decision.
When I first published this guide, I separated the tools into three categories: LMS (Learning Management System), LCMS (Learning Content Management System) and LXP (Learning Experience Platform).
But over time, as AI moves deeper into the training and development industry, the differences between these categories have blurred. That's why I've added a fourth category in this update: comprehensive training software. Here you'll find platforms that genuinely blend all three into one product.
A few tools that look like they'd fit here don't. That’s because some LMSs sell authoring or personalization as separate paid add-ons rather than building them in. And some LCMSs belong to larger companies that also sell an LMS, but as their own separate product. In both cases, I kept the tool in its original category, since you're still buying one thing, not a combination.
So, let's dive in.

What is Employee Training Software?
Employee training software is a digital tool or platform that helps businesses create, manage and deliver training programs to their employees. They’re designed to upskill teams, onboard new hires and track learning progress. Some offer everything in one place, while others need complementary tools to achieve high-standard training objectives.
Not all software and platforms are built the same. But most employee training tools fall into one of four categories: LMS (Learning Management Systems), LCMS (Learning Content Management Systems), LXP (Learning Experience Platforms) and Comprehensive Learning Platforms.

How to Choose Employee Training Software
With so many employee training platforms out there, it can be difficult to choose the right one. So, as you research and assess, have a list on hand of characteristics your ideal choice must have.
Since the majority of these types of software don’t offer free trials, ask for demos so you can see under the hood.
Here's what to look out for before you commit.
1. Does it align with your training plan and goals?
Have clarity about what exactly the software needs to do and compare it to what the software actually does. So, if:
- You need to assign courses and track progress, completion and assessment scores, you need an LMS.
- Your team needs to build the course content itself, you need an LCMS.
- You’d like employees to explore training on their own terms according to their interests and skill gaps, you need an LXP.
If you need more than one of these, check whether the platform bundles them or sells them as separate add-ons. Some platforms now cover all three. Others do one well and expect you to pair it with something else.
2. Fit with your job scenario
A tool built for desk-based employees won’t cover the needs of other kinds of teams. Check these characteristics. If:
- Your workforce is frontline, warehouse, or field-based, check for offline access and a real mobile app, not just a responsive website.
- Your organization operates across multiple locations or franchises, check that the tool can manage separate teams without duplicating setup work for each site.
- The team is remote; check that setup and support don't depend on someone being on-site.
- Your team works in-office, check that the tool integrates well with the desktop tools they already use every day.
- You run a hybrid workplace, check that training works equally well whether someone's at home or at a desk that day.
- The workforce is global or multi-language, check for built-in translation or multi-language support.
3. Ease of use and adoption
For this assessment, you’ll need both a demo and a trial. If the software only offers a demo but not a trial after the conversation, it can be tough to really see if the tool will work for you. Here are a couple of suggestions.
- Don't sit through the sales demo alone. Bring the colleagues who'll be building and running the training program. If you can't get everyone in the room, ask them for specific questions you should ask and record the call.
- Get hands-on access and have your team test both sides of the platform: the creator side and the learner side. Assess how easy the interface is to navigate, how fast things can be completed and what it takes for things to be considered “done.”
4. Integrations with your existing stack
A platform that sits outside your existing tools adds friction for admins and learners alike. So, check for:
- Single sign-on, so employees aren't managing different logins.
- HRIS connections, so user data doesn't need manual updates.
- Slack or Teams integration, so training integrates with their work.
- Video conferencing (Zoom, Google Meet), for live or virtual instructor-led sessions
- Content libraries (LinkedIn Learning, OpenSesame, Coursera), if you want to supplement in-house content with off-the-shelf courses.
- Calendar tools (Google Calendar, Outlook), for scheduling instructor-led sessions.
- CRM (Salesforce), if training ties into sales enablement or customer-facing certification.
- API or Zapier access, for connecting to tools not natively supported.
5. Security, compliance and data privacy
Training software touches employee data, so this is worth checking regardless of your sector, and non-negotiable if you're in a regulated industry. Assess for the following things:
- Data storage location, especially if you need to meet regional requirements like GDPR.
- Compliance certifications such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001.
- Role-based access controls, so only the right people can see sensitive data.
- Encryption, both in transit and at rest.
- Industry-specific compliance if you're regulated, like HIPAA for healthcare.
- Data retention and deletion policies, in case you ever need to leave the platform.
