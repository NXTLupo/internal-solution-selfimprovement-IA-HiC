What is a Company Profile
A company profile is a marketing tool that’s used to introduce and tell the story of a business.
It typically includes information such as the company's history, mission, values, products or services rendered, team members, testimonials, awards, pricing, financial information and more.
Using a company profile template makes it easy for you to create a professional overview of a business or showcase your company's essence and offerings.
A well-designed company profile creates a positive first impression on potential clients, partners, investors and other stakeholders. It is suitable for use in different contexts, such as creating presentations, proposals or marketing materials.
But why create a company profile rather than a pitch deck?
A pitch deck and a company profile are both good ways to tell people about your business.
However, a pitch deck is brief and tailored for selling ideas or seeking funding.
In contrast, a company profile is a marketing tool that tells the bigger story. It gives customers, partners, investors and other stakeholders a clear picture of who you are, what you do, who you serve and why they should trust your business.
And that last part matters. People aren’t only evaluating what you sell. They’re also evaluating the company they’ll be doing business with. According to Edelman's 2025 Brand Trust special report, 80% of consumers across 15 countries say trusting the company behind a brand is either a deal breaker or important to them.
A well-crafted company profile gives them more reasons to trust what they see.
What's the Difference Between a Company Profile, a Business Plan and an About Us Page?
A company profile introduces your business to the outside world, a business plan maps how the business will operate and grow and an About Us page is the web version of your profile built for search engines and online viewing.
All three describe the same company, and each one exists for a different audience.

What to Include in a Company Profile
A company profile includes your brand story, mission statement, team, values, history, products or services, pricing, testimonials, awards, financial performance and contact details. Not every profile needs all eleven. Your final combination will depend on your objectives and the final output.
To guide your outline and final content, consider that buyers judge what you can do by how well you explain your business, not by how hard you sell it.
This means that you must write every section below to explain, not to pitch.
Brand Story
Your brand story explains why the company exists and what problem you are trying to fix. It gives your offering meaning and puts recognizable humans behind the brand.
Share the specifics: the passion, the struggle, the setbacks and the values that came out of them. For example, RXBAR tells the story of two friends, Peter Rahal and Jared Smith, who knew each other since first grade, who put $5,000 into a protein bar business in 2012 because every bar on the shelf was full of ingredients nobody could pronounce.
The story keeps all the unglamorous parts, making it more real and relatable: A makeshift kitchen in a basement. A first label designed in PowerPoint, industry advisers calling it a mistake to print "3 egg whites, 2 dates, 6 almonds" on the front of the pack instead of using clear branding.
These real stories are what make a brand story memorable. Their voice is conversational, the details are checkable, and the ingredient decision the experts warned against became an indisputable part of the final product packaging.
Some company stories fail because they are assembled from internal opinion, not real storytelling. Andy Raskin, a strategic narrative consultant who has built narratives for more than 100 companies, pinpoints this mistake in What I Learned Crafting Strategic Narratives for 100+ Companies: "One of the biggest mistakes I made early on was making customer interviews optional."
Following Raskin’s suggestion, answer these questions first, and then talk to your customers!
- What inspired you to start this business, and what problem were you solving?
- What impact do you want to have on your industry or community?
- Who is your ideal customer, and what are their pain points?
- What challenges did you face, and what did you learn?
- What sets you apart from competitors?

Mission Statement
A mission statement says why your company exists in one or two lines. Keep it clear, short and specific enough so that it can only apply to your business and no one else's.
For instance, Tesla's mission statement reads: "Accelerating the world's transition to sustainable energy." Clear, direct and with no extra words.
In a mission statement, vague superlatives won’t do you any favors. Hoa Loranger, former Vice President at Nielsen Norman Group, put it this way in Great Summaries on "About Us" Pages Engage Users and Build Trust: "Steer clear of hollow phrases like 'revolutionary' — at best, they tend to confuse users; at worst, they make them mistrust the site."
Your Team
Customers want to see who they will be working with. Feature photos of founders and key people, and ideally some varied shots of the workspace or team events.
Kinsta’s About Page combines a single photo of its entire team with informal employee shots that show what it's like to work at the company.
