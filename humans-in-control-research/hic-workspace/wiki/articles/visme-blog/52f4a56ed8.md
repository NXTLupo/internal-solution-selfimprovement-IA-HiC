# How to Create Effective Employee Training Content +Templates

Source: [visme_blog](https://visme.co/blog/training-materials-templates/)

How to Create Effective Employee Training Content +Templates
Search for how to create employee training content, and you'll find format advice: which template to use, which software to build it in, how many slides are too many.
But none of that matters if the content underneath is thin. A well-designed deck that teaches the wrong things, or nothing specific at all, still fails the trainee.
Content is the substance underneath the format: the objective, the explanation, the example, the question that checks whether it landed.
It's the piece every guide assumes you already have, and few ever explain how to write.
This guide covers exactly that: what training content is, what needs to be included in any piece of training content, and how to write it, step by step.

What Is Employee Training Content?
Employee training content is the substance of what a training program needs to teach. It’s the written material, the diagrams, the step-by-step instructions and the assessments that check if the trainee has absorbed the information.
Employee training content vs. training materials
When you know what you need to teach, you create the content. Then you use the content to design the materials.
- Training content: What does the employee need to learn
- Training materials: The containers that deliver the content to the trainee
Here’s an example: You need to teach new customer service reps how to process a refund.
The content explains what triggers a refund, how to verify it, how to process it and how to confirm it with the customer. It describes the flow that the rep needs to follow.
With this content, you can create different materials to deliver it. For instance:
- A visual job aid they can reference easily.
- A 10-minute video they watch on day one.
- A live demo during onboarding.
- A section in the handbook.
- A checklist in their email.
High-Quality Training Content Improves Learning Outcomes
High-quality training content can make your training programs more successful and effective. By applying these best practices, you’ll achieve the best learning outcomes. Try to build them into every part of your program.
Characteristics Of Effective Training Content
Plain Language Makes The Content Understandable By Everyone
Plain language avoids confusion and improves retention by setting four governing principles: content should be relevant, findable, understandable and usable. To achieve that, you need to organize, sequence and label the information.
For example, this sequence expects the trainee to memorize it before acting.
"Before you begin the shutdown sequence, make sure all secondary systems have been notified and the area has been cleared. Then proceed to close valve B after confirming valve A is fully sealed."
In plain language standards, it’s a numbered list. After the list, go into the details and include visuals.
- Notify secondary systems
- Clear the area
- Confirm valve A is fully sealed
- Close valve B
Short Content Holds Attention Better
Working memory can only hold a few new items at a time, so covering too much in one session is ineffective. Also, TalentLMS found that 58% of employees multitask during training, so you need to ensure that their full attention span is maintained.
Shorter content is more effective because it's common for a trainee not to finish a 45-minute module in one sitting. They’ll get pulled into an urgent task, pause it, come back and have to find where they were before leaving.
Short content also aligns well with microlearning, fast and punctual course modules, easy-to-follow job aids and refresher emails.
Role-Specific Content Is Essential For Specific Job Type Training
Generic content can cover a lot of ground, but for specific jobs, you’ll need role-specific content. In recent research, Docebo found that 57% of learners feel their training isn't relevant to their specific role. That’s a significant gap you don’t want your training programs to fall into.
Role-specific doesn't mean a separate program for every job title. Create a general foundation and then build role-specific versions from it.
Scenario-Based Content Builds Skills Better Than Only Text
Scenario-based content builds skills better than simple content recall after reading text. That’s because reciting a rule and applying it under pressure are different skills that need to work together. Instructional designer Cathy Moore, creator of Action Mapping, suggests you “start by asking what people need to do rather than what they need to know.”
In an in-person session, the trainer could first ask, "What is the refund policy?" and continue with, "A customer wants a refund on an item bought 45 days ago with no receipt. What do you do?"
Accessible Content Reaches All Trainees
Accessible content is not only for people with disabilities, such as those with diminished vision or limb differences. It also supports trainees with situations like subtitles on videos watched in loud environments or language options for non-English speakers.
In fact, research from Babbel for Business, found that 25% of US workers have faced a safety risk because of a language barrier.
Notably, checking off all the accessibility factors isn’t easy. So stick to the basics and then analyze the future trainees’ actual reality. Then, ensure that the training content and the program as a whole are accessible to all of them.
Regular Refresher Content Prevents Knowledge Decay
According to Axonify, a quarter of frontline retail, hospitality and food service workers say their training happens at onboarding and never gets reinforced. Refresher content fills that gap
It works by spacing recall over time and is delivered in several ways. For example, microlearning modules triggered by specific actions in an employee’s portal, short emails with simple polls to gauge knowledge retention over time and in-person reviews that check performance and provide tips for improvement. In some cases, you’ll need a full refresher course after a set period of time or when changes have occurred in the workflow.
Types of Employee Training Content
Training content covers a wide range of topics and contains a lot of information. Let’s take a look at the main content types that most employee training programs are based on.
What are the main types of employee training content?
Most employee training programs are built from seven content types: onboarding, compliance, product, sales enablement, soft skills, hard skills and leadership. Each one covers different information.
Onboarding Content
Onboarding content is information for new employees that explains the organization’s policies, processes and expectations, as well as what they need to know to do their job.
Some of that content includes:
- What the organization’s values are and how the culture is maintained
- What the role is responsible for and how performance gets measured
- Who reports to whom, usually visualized as an org chart
- Instructions for how to get into the systems and tools the role uses
- What each workplace policy requires of the employee
- How benefits and payroll work

Compliance Training Content
Compliance content covers requirements set by law, regulations, industry standards or organizational policies.
Common content in compliance training includes:
- Specifics about each rule
- The regulation or standard the rule comes from
- The specific behavior required
- The consequence of getting it wrong, for the employee and the organization
- Worked examples of edge cases where people got things wrong
- How often does the requirement have to be revisited
Common topics include workplace safety, harassment prevention, data protection under GDPR or HIPAA, anti-bribery, and sector standards like PCI DSS and OSHA.
In this case, exact wording matters more than anywhere else, so write the rule in the regulator's language and put your explanation alongside it rather than in place of it.

Product Training Content
Product training content explains what the product does, how it behaves and how to use it most effectively.

- Descriptions of each feature and which customer problem it solves
- Instructions for how the product gets configured, step by step
- Details about pricing and subscription packages
- Annotated screenshots showing the interface at each step
