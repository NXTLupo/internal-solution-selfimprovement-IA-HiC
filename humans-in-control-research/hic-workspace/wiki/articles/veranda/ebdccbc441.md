# Designers Reveal the Rooms Where Dark Paint Always Works Best

Source: [veranda](https://www.veranda.com/decorating-ideas/color-ideas/a73488141/where-to-use-dark-paint-in-home/)

Light and bright spaces have been the order of the day for many years. Now that people are craving cozy, character-filled homes more than ever, it only seems natural that homeowners are increasingly turning to dark paint colors to cocoon their rooms.
And although it seems as if these dark hues are trending, designers would argue that they’ve always been in style. For instance, interior designer Miles Redd has always loved rich, dark colors, with chocolate brown as a favorite.
“I love to use them in darkish rooms, but often, when a room gets no natural light and you try and paint them a light color—they just seem drab and gray, but a dark color becomes cool and luminous, catching reflections and giving sparkle, particularly if you use a glossy paint.”
Interior designer Katie Ridder believes that dark paint makes for an ideal art backdrop, since it “highlights art in a way you can't with white paint.”
Also, contrary to the old-school way of thinking, Ridder thinks that small rooms actually seem larger when painted a dark color—and also offer a welcome juxtaposition against other rooms in the home. “A dark room can be a reaction to another room which is light-filled,” Redd says. “It is the contrast in the house that makes it exciting.”
Ahead, consider these specific spaces for dark paint colors.
Libraries
A library seems to naturally lend itself to dark paint colors, since it’s a space where you want to be enveloped and cozy as you settle in with a good book. Redd says that you can typically find rich colors in libraries.
And while you may be tempted to embrace the dark feel in a library, if you want to illuminate your reading, you’ll need to carefully think through your lighting choices.
Redd says that dark rooms, in general, need to be “cleverly lit.” This can include things like picture lights, uplights, and soft-shaded lamps, which Redd says give any dark room “glow and sparkle.”
Living Rooms
Though many people gravitate toward neutral-colored living rooms, Ridder says, “a dark-painted matte living room with high ceilings and light-colored furniture would be so good looking.” She adds, “I am imagining a deep, deep gray, with a touch of deep blue and black.”
Bars and Butler’s Pantries
Since dark colors can work beautifully within small spaces, it makes perfect sense to embrace such hues within bars and butler’s pantries, places where dark colors can act as elements of surprise and set them apart. Redd says he particularly enjoys using dark colors in these cozy entertaining spaces.
Kitchens
As you prepare meals and wind down after a busy day, a moody kitchen can actually feel quite comforting. Redd’s own kitchen is outfitted in black lacquer paint, and the designer says he also has a penchant for dark brown lacquer. “There are no rules,” Redd adds.
Entryways
A dark color in an entryway is a way to greet guests using an unexpected, and pacifying, color palette. “I've used very dark cerulean blue verre eglomise for a windowless front entry to great effect,” Ridder shares. “I am inclined to use a very dark blue with a touch of green for high-gloss walls.”
Go for a deep, bold shade for a design surprise that greets guests when they walk into your home.
