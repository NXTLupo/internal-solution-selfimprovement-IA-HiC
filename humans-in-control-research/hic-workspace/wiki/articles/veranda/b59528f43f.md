# How to Style a Bookshelf the Right Way, According to Interior Designers

Source: [veranda](https://www.veranda.com/decorating-ideas/advice-from-designers/a73394853/how-to-style-bookshelf-designer-tips/)

There’s a reason we haul heavy boxes of books from house to house over the years: They become a part of us. Our bookshelves act as deeply personal spaces in the home that highlight the books and objects we’ve collected over time. Styling a bookshelf may feel overwhelming, especially if you’re working with a wall-to-wall library, but it can be one of the most rewarding (not to mention quick and inexpensive) projects you undergo in your home.
However, if you take the advice from our panel of design experts and archival home tours, you’ll be equipped with the confidence you need to build beautiful, meaningful vignettes filled with novels, design books, academic tomes, and more that your household will gravitate to for years to come.
Start from scratch.
Even if you’re reorganizing a bookshelf or library you’ve had for years, interior designer Ashley Gallion advises clearing everything out and starting with a blank canvas—and not rushing the process. Styling your bookshelves should feel like an art project that evolves and becomes more curated as you go.
Gallion says, “Give yourself room to move things around, adding or taking away as needed, and just let yourself be creatively free.”
Incorporate your favorite artworks.
A bookshelf is already a deeply personal space filled with collections that highlight your household’s interests, education, travels, and more. Why not layer it with a beautiful piece of art you adore to further enrich this space?
We adore this library in a Connecticut home designed by David Netto (pictured above) that features a 1960s photograph by Malick Sidibé. The artwork elevates the room and adds visual interest without dominating the room. Plus, it allows the bookshelves to function like a one-of-a-kind frame.
Only showcase items you love.
Designer Lauren Conner is big on using pieces that do more than just take up space; they should bring joy to everyone in the room. She sticks to this philosophy when it comes to styling a bookshelf as well. Conner advises starting with an edited variety of books layered with objects you actually love and will want to see every day. Her favorites include original artworks, family photographs, objects with organic textures and natural finishes, and beloved items you’ve collected through the decades.
“It’s a waste of real estate to have a wall dedicated to displaying objects that have no purpose,” says Conner. “It is always more interesting for shelves to reflect the personality and meaningful experiences of the family who lives there. As books give way to screens, a collection of antique books, favorite coffee table books or treasures collected over time is much more engaging than a row of unread titles.”
Embrace negative space.
It’s tempting to want to cram as much as possible in your bookshelves, but this tranquil library space in a century-old Atlanta home (pictured above) shows that less really can be more. However, this makes it that much more important for every featured element to be carefully thought through in terms of location, how it is displayed, and what will surround or frame it.
We love the well-curated mix of literary classics, design books, and British artworks that all nod to the home’s Cotswolds-inspired charm. Designer Amy Morris’s choice to let the art have breathing room makes it feel all the more important and worth admiring.
Balance your pursuit of symmetry and desire to create visual interest.
While it may seem intuitive to pair every book based on height and color, designer Kelly Neely says that the goal for styling bookshelves is “to find symmetry in size of objects without the shelves feeling ‘matchy.’” Creating multiple heights throughout the shelf will help keep things looking fresh and interesting.
Neely says a few ways to bring both symmetry and style to your bookshelf are by using artwork propped in the back of the shelf to fill vertical space, layering coffee table books with an object to create medium height, and adding vases or pots to achieve multiple heights. (Bonus points if you place fresh greenery in those vessels.) Neely is also a fan of utilizing cordless lamps to illuminate favorite vignettes and pieces on your shelf.
This inviting library in a historic Hudson Valley home (pictured above) designed by Louise Copeland is a prime example of Neely’s advice. Copeland keeps things interesting with a host of colors, textures, and collected pieces, though the bookshelf still feels like an organized, thoughtfully curated place.
Think beyond the shelves.
Think beyond simply placing books and objects on shelves; paint the trim (go ahead and lacquer those shelves if you’re up for it!) or decorate it with borders for a wow factor. Case in point: This stunning New York City apartment library (pictured above) that belonged to historian Maureen Footer. The shelving makes a statement while letting other beautiful elements of this room shine.
Additionally, selecting elegant gallery lighting to surround your shelving, as shown here, brings elegance and provenance to this space you’ve just invested in decorating. No matter the weather or time of day, your beloved collections can always shine.
