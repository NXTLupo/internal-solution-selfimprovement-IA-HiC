# 6 Curtain Mistakes That Make Your Home Look Cheap, According to Designers

Source: [veranda](https://www.veranda.com/decorating-ideas/advice-from-designers/a73447398/curtain-mistakes-make-home-look-cheap-explained/)

If windows are the eyes of a home, your curtains are like a pair of sunglasses. “[They] have such an impact on how a room feels,” says Rosemary Hallgarten, owner of her eponymous textile company. “Beyond privacy or controlling light, they add softness, texture, and movement and can really change the atmosphere of a space.” Some window treatments can act as the perfect finishing touch, making your home feel more elevated, while others can make your space feel cheap.
There are plenty of ways your window treatments can undermine the rest of your home décor, but Melissa Oholendt of Oho Interiors says most of them stem from treating your curtains like an afterthought. “People sometimes forget that drapes and window treatments are architectural,” explains the designer, who splits her time between Minnesota and Colorado. “Often, we are planning for window treatments during the construction phase to ensure they aren't forgotten.”
To level up your home and create an air of luxury, keep an eye out for the common curtain mistakes—and what to do instead.
Opting for Puny Panels
When it comes to your curtains, more really is more—just ask designer Maggie Vollrath of Provision Studios. While full drapes lend lavishness, a limp panel will instantly make a room fall flat.
“When panels are too narrow or skimpy for the window, they tend to read as an afterthought rather than an intentional part of the room,” says the Georgia-based designer. “I would much rather see a simple fabric used generously than an expensive fabric used sparingly.”
Amy Sklar, an interior designer in Los Angeles, suggests window treatments that are 150 to 200 percent of the width of the window’s opening. “Most standard fabrics come in a 54-inch-wide roll and the length is fully adjustable depending on your needs,” she adds. “A double width fabric is what the name implies and up to 120 inches wide, which gives you the opportunity to have fewer seams and will lower your labor and material costs.”
Overlooking the Lining
If you ask Nashville designer Robin Rains, custom drapery is always worth the investment. That said, if you’re working with a tight budget, make sure they’re lined. “Fabric needs a lining to look more custom made,” she says. “Rarely does a fabric look nice without being lined.”
Though a backing is typically associated with room-darkening or blackout curtains, Rains says a lightweight sheer lining bridges the gap between refined and relaxed.
Hanging Them Incorrectly
Kevie Murphy, a designer in New York, believes hanging them too low or too tight is “the death by a thousand cuts of drapery.” She explains, “Curtains need room to breathe ... When a panel stops at the windowsill or barely skims the frame, the whole room feels like it’s wearing pants that are too short.” Murphy likes to install her curtain rod near the ceiling to create the illusion of tall ceilings—and add some grandeur to a space. Bonus points: Murphy says raising your rod is a low-cost tweak that can create a huge impact. “It costs you nothing but a few extra holes in the wall.”
Hallgarten is also a fan of hanging your curtain rod closer to the ceiling, and wants you to also pay attention to your setup’s width. “Drapery looks best if you can take it 10 to 12 inches out on either side of the window,” she says. “This makes the window look larger and lets plenty of light in.”
Choosing Too-Short Curtains
Regardless of where you choose to hang your curtains, designers are begging you to recheck their length. “Curtains that are too short are probably the first thing I notice,” Hallgarten says. While she opts for longer curtains that “puddle” about an inch onto the floor, Sklar says curtains made with a looser weave or heavier material may stretch out over time and need “room to grow.”
According to Oholendt, a happy medium is to have curtains that either kiss the ground or are a quarter-inch off the floor. “There are exceptions, of course, but curtains that awkwardly stop six inches above the floor almost never do anyone any favors,” she says. “Conversely, the most expensive curtains in the world will still look wrong if they're dragging three inches on the floor.”
Nixing Natural Materials
With the right material, cost-efficient curtains can feel fancy. “Fabric is where luxury actually lives,” Murphy says. “A lightweight, flimsy fabric will never hang with the kind of weight and movement that reads well, no matter how good the color or pattern is.” The designer encourages you to look for natural materials with “body and durability,” such as cotton, linen, or silk.
Selecting Hardware Hastily
It’s easy to zero in on a panel’s proportions, lining, and materials, but don’t discount how your curtains are hung. If curtains are a window’s sunglasses, Sklar says rods and hardware are the jewelry. Since rings can get dirty and stick, she prefers a traverse rod. “It can be ceiling- or wall-mounted that make closing larger drapes much easier,” she says. “It’s also much more elegant.” Valances, trim, or a tieback can make your curtains look way more expensive than they actually are.
Meanwhile, Oholendt admits she’s “particularly picky about hardware finishes.” “A great unlacquered brass or medium-toned wood rod will stand the test of time,” she says. “In the last couple of years, it has become easier to find at the consumer level.”
