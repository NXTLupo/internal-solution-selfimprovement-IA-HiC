# 12 Designer-Approved Colors That Make Orange Feel Surprisingly Chic

Source: [veranda](https://www.veranda.com/decorating-ideas/color-ideas/a62630330/colors-that-go-with-orange/)

Not many colors inject a sense of optimism and creativity like orange. It combines the confidence of red and the playfulness of yellow, resulting in a color that is attention-grabbing no matter the exact shade. Earthy burnt orange upholstery or a juicy melon can transform a dark sitting area into a rejuvenating retreat—and that’s just the start.
While some may find orange to be too bright or overwhelming for their home, its diverse shades make it a surprisingly versatile color. New York-based designer Garrow Kedigian points out that orange can brighten and anchor a room's decor, whether it's used to cover the entire space or simply as an accent in artwork or upholstery.
He explains, "Orange is one of those colors that exudes great energy. For me, using bright and vibrant colors such as orange, used in the right way, can vary the perfect spots of accents and set the stage for design drama."
New York-based decorator Katie Ridder adds that people may not realize that orange works wonderfully as a secondary color when paired with complementary or even contrasting hues. She says, "I think orange is, in terms of a secondary color, is really effective because it goes with brown, green, red, and it's even a nice when paired with colors opposite on the color wheel."
Whether you lean toward more maximalist and daring styles or find comfort in more relaxing and serene rooms, there's a shade of orange that can energize the interiors of your house. Here, the best colors to pair with orange when decorating a room, according to designers.
The Best Colors That Go with Orange
Orange and Cobalt
Sitting directly opposite from orange on the color wheel, blue offers an unexpected yet high-energy pairing. Kedigian explains, "Blue is always a great contrast to orange, as blue helps to anchor it but lets the orange still sing its tune."
Saturated shades of blue, such as Cosmic Cobalt and marine blue, carry enough depth and complexity to complement cantaloupe and tangelo shades without being overwhelmed by their brightness. For example, the deep blue hues in the triangular-cut window valance and the sofa fabric harmonize beautifully with the lacquered salmon walls in this New York library designed by Chiqui Woolworth.
Orange and Yellow
When working with muted tones, using peachy oranges and buttery yellows can create a welcoming atmosphere that encourages guests to mingle and relax. However, Ridder cautions against going "too graphic" with colors or patterns, as this can make a room feel dated and reminiscent of the ’70s. Instead, opt for sunset hues that are present in fabrics or patterns; this allows them to stand out subtly throughout the room instead of overwhelming it.
At her Bel Air estate, an antique Spanish rug inspired Renvy Graves Pittman to adopt a warmer palette in the living room (above), which is complemented by Lee Jofa’s Hollyhock chintz upholstery and apricot silk draperies.
Orange and Black
Kedigian admits that pairing orange and black can feel “too ‘Halloween’ if you’re not careful about which tone of orange you select.” However, pairing deep ebony with tangerine or burnt oranges can result in a color scheme that feels quite contemporary and striking. Incorporating a third shade such as cream or muted gold, like in the hall and bar above from historian Maureen Footer, grounds the two more bold shades, keeping the color story from feeling too kitsch.
Orange and Chartreuse
Turn up the decorating drama within your home by drowning it in vivacious shades of chartreuse with fiery orange details. There's nothing subtle about this unexpected pairing, so our editors recommend using it in a smaller room, such as a hallway or powder room, that needs a boost of personality or character.
Above, gallerist and historian Emily Eerdmans dressed the doorways of her upper salon with orange-red drapery, adding a lively pop against the lime lacquered walls.
Orange and Apricot
If you've already committed to drenching your walls in a ripe melon hue, why not go full out with the addition of furnishings in a similar orange shade? For a client who was begging for color in her home, Katie Ridder covered the walls of this Birmingham library in a lush apricot shade that is elevated by burnt orange mohair sofas and floral upholstery fabric. The slight difference in the orange tones within the room keeps the monochromatic palette from feeling boring.
Orange and White
Crisp white acts as a brilliant frame for vivid orange, making the energy in a room feel electric and invigorating. The color pairing makes a statement when deployed in wallcovering like a custom Gracie covering the foyer by Shelby Wagner (above). Touches of natural brown keep the color story from overpowering the furnishings within the room.
Orange and Gold
It’s almost impossible to find a color that doesn’t shine when paired with gold. However, combining a rich, vibrant color with a radiant orange-red creates an opulent atmosphere in a dining room or bar area. Kedigian implemented this bold color scheme in his Manhattan apartment, painting the study walls a seductive fiery hue and complementing it with a glamorous golden ceiling.
Orange and Oatmeal
More muted than pure white, beigey oatmeal creates a more balanced (yet never boring!) look when paired with a softer melon or peach shade in a powder room or wellness space. Designer Lindley Arthur made this powder room at the Kips Bay Decorator Show House into a destination-worthy idyll by covering the walls in a tangerine and taupe-colored wallcovering that pays homage to tropical locales.
Orange and Lilac
Pale lilac makes an unexpected match to orange, resulting in a more whimsical turn from the usual warmer neutrals or soft pinks. The duo is surprisingly impactful when generously sprinkled throughout a living room with fabrics and artwork. A persimmon-colored breakfast banquette mingles flawlessly with a plush lilac sofa in Caroline Gidiere's Birmingham den, thanks to the pillow and armchair fabric that tie the two hues together.
Orange and Pastel Blues
Orange may often be associated with a more vibrant and energetic disposition, but it can feel surprisingly sweet and feminine when used strategically with more pastel and light blues. In this romantic dining room by designer Kim Scodro in the Kips Bay Decorator Show House Dallas, a show-stopping tangelo-colored settee sings against the sea of blues in the Gracie wallcovering and on the room's ceiling and trim.
Orange and Pink
If you're aiming for a playful atmosphere, there's no better combination than the timeless pairing of orange and pink. This vibrant color scheme truly comes alive in a family playroom, much like the one designed by Chauncey Boothby, and it can also add energy to a cozy breakfast nook. On the other hand, when using more muted tones like marigold and dusty pink in a study or bedroom, the combination feels more sophisticated and timeless.
Orange and Chocolate Brown
Chocolate brown tends to feel a bit more formal, thanks to its richness in tone, making it a wonderful shade to temper youthful and bright shades like tangerine. Since they are both strong in their own right, the duo makes an electrifying statement when used in small ways, like a chocolate brown sofa with orange piping trim. In designer Caroline Gidiere's home bar, fiery orange trim enlivens the built-in bar cabinetry, antique glass, and Chippendale fretwork.
