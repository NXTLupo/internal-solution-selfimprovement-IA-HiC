# 6 Stunning Home Trends Designers Want You to Try This Fall

Source: [veranda](https://www.veranda.com/home-decorators/design-trends/a73496850/fall-2026-home-decor-trends/)

Although the new year always calls us to turn over a new leaf, there’s something about autumn that equally inspires change. As interior designer Mark D. Sikes puts it, fall has always had that back-to-school feeling with a sense of getting organized and starting fresh after summer.
“I think that renewed energy naturally makes us look at our homes with fresh eyes and feel ready for change,” Sikes says. “It’s also naturally a season of wanting comfort and for all things to feel inviting and welcoming.”
And for designer Phillip Thomas, fall is actually more so a time of renewal than entering a new year. “For me, the start of the fall is almost of greater importance than the start of a new year. Since a young age, fall has always been a time of transition and growth to me,” Thomas remarks.
To harness this feeling of newness as the leaves begin to turn, consider these fresh home decor trends for the season.
Rich Colors
“As the seasons shift, so does the color palette,” Sikes observes. “We become inclined toward rich, warm colors that we see in the changing foliage instead of the light and bright colors we associate with summer.”
Colors like chocolate brown, chestnut, camel, gold, and warm ivory will be everywhere come fall, but you don’t have to change your entire design scheme to incorporate these shades. “A great way to change up the color palette of your home with the seasons is slipcovers,” Sikes recommends. “Slipcovers allow you to completely transform the feeling of a room without changing the furniture itself, making it easy to transition from lighter summer fabrics to richer colors and textures for fall.”
Additionally, Thomas says that you can incorporate these colors through pillows, rugs, and other accessories.
Heavy Textures
Thomas says that he’s noticed heavy textures being used more often in home decor for their ability to “give flesh to a piece and to bring it to life.” He notes, “These textures also help to reflect light in different ways to flatter fabrics. As we go into the winter months and darker days, finding ways to create the illusion of more light in our interiors is always important, as this impacts the way we feel on a daily basis.”
Sikes is specifically drawn to velvets, wools, mohairs, and silks for fall. He says, “Switching out your sofa pillows seasonally is an excellent way to incorporate these textures in those colder months.”
Paint Instead of Architectural Features
Good news for those designing on a budget: Thomas says that he is seeing the use of paint as a substitute for architectural details in interiors. “It is amazing what paint can do to transform a space,” he says. “For example, when paneling does not exist, one can always create the illusion of panels through the use of stripes of varying width. It is quite remarkable what a little paint and some well-prepared tape can do to change an interior.”
Warm Wood Finishes
“We’ve seen the return of ‘brown furniture’ in recent years; dark and medium-toned woods—walnut, mahogany, oak, burlwood—are back after years of very pale woods and painted furniture,” Sikes says. “My favorite pieces of brown furniture are usually antiques. Wood tones naturally echo the season’s colors and bring warmth without requiring overtly seasonal decorating.”
You don’t have to swap out every piece of furniture in your home, either. Sikes says you can weave this trend into a space by simply introducing one beautiful wood piece, such as an antique chest, drinks table, writing desk, or bookcase.
Rounded Edges
Things are getting softer in the world of interiors. Thomas has noted a shift away from “rigid geometry” to softer lines, specifically in the use of rounded, soft corners and through soft edges at archways.
“These softer edges and architectural features help to deceive the eye in determining where one plain ends and the other begins, often giving the illusion of greater space,” Thomas says. “If you’re not ready to take the leap into soft edges in your interiors, try changing out one of your more rectangular club chairs for a barrel or round-back chair. You will see just how much larger the space feels without any compromise in the comfort of the chairs.”
Perfection in Imperfection
Thomas has long been about embracing imperfection, and it’s something that’s trending this season. “When a piece is too perfect, it almost lacks soul and character,” Thomas says. “Living in a world of digital renderings, we have come to believe that that is how our homes should be. Quite the contrary to me. Homes should feel lived-in and loved.”
Thomas says that the “perfectly imperfect” look can be achieved by breaking the symmetry “so often sought-after in easily digestible interior sound bites,” and instead creating interiors with “unique, individual pieces that tell their own stories but come together to tell an overall story of who the homeowner is.”
