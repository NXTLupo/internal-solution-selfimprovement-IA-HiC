# Six AI-Readiness Questions for Marketing Leaders to Ask

Source: [marketing_ai_inst](https://www.marketingaiinstitute.com/blog/ai-readiness-maicon-2026)

Organizations are eager to dive into AI, but many initiatives still stall before they deliver meaningful results… or never move beyond experimentation.Why?
It’s no longer about resistance to change. We’re seeing employees integrating AI into their day-to-day roles. The missing piece now is organizational readiness.
In our 2026 State of AI report, more than half of professionals have moved beyond experimentation, but only 29% of respondents say their organization has an AI roadmap.
In other words, organizations are falling behind their employees when it comes to putting AI into practice.
Before building an effective AI adoption strategy, leaders need to understand where their organization stands today. Asking the right questions can help identify gaps in strategy, people, technology, data, governance, and workflows, and reveal where additional preparation may be needed.
Bonus: Each section includes a relevant session at the upcoming Marketing AI Conference (MAICON), so you can dig deeper into the areas where your organization needs the most work.
Is Your AI Strategy Aligned with Business Goals?
AI readiness starts with knowing what you’re trying to accomplish. Your organization should be able to identify specific business problems that AI could help solve, connect potential use cases to broader business objectives, and prioritize opportunities based on their impact. Just as important, leaders should have a shared definition of success and understand where AI should and should not be used.
Once you know where AI can create value, the challenge becomes turning those opportunities into strategic priorities. At MAICON 2026, The AGI Chronicles: The Final Frontier, explores how leaders can separate AI hype from reality when making investment decisions, while Marketing Forward examines how AI is reshaping marketing strategy and offers a framework for making better decisions as technology and customer expectations evolve.

Does Your Team Have Skills to Adopt AI?
Technology alone won’t create meaningful AI adoption. Teams need a baseline understanding of AI, clarity around how it can augment their expertise, and confidence about how they are expected to use it. Employees should know which tools are approved, have access to the resources they need, and understand how their roles and ways of working may evolve. Leadership also needs people who can champion AI adoption across key functions.
MAICON approaches this challenge from several angles. AI Adaptation: The People Side of Scale focuses on what leaders need to drive meaningful behavior change across teams. The AI-Native Content Team shows the workflows and systems enabling teams to dramatically increase their capacity for experimentation and innovation. Is Your Data Ready to Support AI?
AI initiatives need reliable data and a clear way to measure whether they’re actually creating value. Organizations should understand where their data lives, whether it is accurate and usable, and which information is sensitive, confidential, or proprietary. Teams also need baseline performance metrics, defined KPIs for AI initiatives, and systems for measuring business impact and ROI.

Do You Have Governance and Guardrails to Use AI?
Governance shouldn’t prevent teams from using AI; it should give them the confidence to use it responsibly. A well-organized organization has clear policies governing AI use, confidential and personal information, and applicable legal and compliance requirements. It also knows what organizational knowledge AI systems may need access to, where that information will live, and who is accountable for monitoring AI risks and policy compliance.

Can Current Technology Support Your AI Ambitions?
AI readiness doesn’t necessarily mean adding more tools. It means understanding what capabilities your organization actually needs and whether your existing technology can support them. Teams should know which AI capabilities already exist within their stack, where gaps remain, how potential platforms will be evaluated, and what process will be used to safely onboard new technologies.

Are Your Workflows Ready to be Reinvented with AI?
Some of AI’s biggest opportunities emerge when organizations stop asking how to automate existing tasks and start reconsidering how the work itself should be done. AI-ready teams have identified where they can save time, improve quality, or eliminate unnecessary manual work. They’re evaluating both existing and entirely new workflows based on business impact, preparing employees for changes to their daily processes, and establishing ways to test new workflows before deploying them broadly.
