# How to Painlessly Move Apartments in New York

Source: [curbed_vox](http://www.curbed.com/article/moving-apartments-in-new-york.html?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=social_acct&utm_campaign=feed-part)

You incessantly scrolled through StreetEasy, dodged ghosting brokers, and finally found a place to live. Congrats! Now it’s time to move your stuff. Writer Cassie Hurwitz tracked down the best moving companies, professional packers, and tips for loading everything into your new home.
  
  
    .
    Book your move early — and move off-peak and mid-month.
  Many movers take reservations at least two weeks out during popular times, says Rick Carruthers, the owner of Sven Moving, and deposits are often refundable. Moving on the first or last of the month will be expensive and busy; scheduling for a random day is cheaper and calmer. “The 26th to the 2nd is what we call ‘hell week,’” says Lou DeFabrizio of Lou Moves You.
  
  
    .
    Before you hire a well-known moving company, try negotiating.
  Use quotes from multiple companies to attempt to secure a cheaper price from your top pick. Just note: The quality of service by big-name companies can be inconsistent because their businesses rely on seasonal subcontractors.
  
  
    .
    If you have to move yourself, a U-Haul and Taskrabbit combo is the cheapest option.
  You can rent a nine-foot U-Haul cargo van (from $20 per day) or a 20-foot truck (from $40), which is better for moving a two- or three-bedroom. Adding someone from Taskrabbit will cost anywhere from $40 to $80 an hour.
  
  
    .
    Deal with a gap between moving out and in by renting a storage space — Manhattan Mini Storage is the most affordable.
  A medium-size, climate-controlled unit in its Chelsea location starts at $99 a month. If you need to store your stuff for only one night, look for a mover like Liberty Moving & Storage or Sven Moving that charges by the weight and length of time stored and won’t commingle your stuff with other people’s.
  
  
    .
    There isn’t really a budget option for packing and unpacking services, but it can make a big difference for larger moves.
  Joanna Muenz, a photo director in Queens, paid Oz Moving $3,000 to pack up her family of four. “What would’ve taken me weeks took them a few hours,” she says. If you’re packing for yourself or live in a studio or one-bedroom, again, Taskers are your best bet.
  
  
    .
    Keep your clothes on hangers by using wardrobe boxes.
  This saves you a ton of energy during the packing process, says Olivia Hyde, an account director who has moved almost every year of her life, including four times within New York City.
  
  
    .
    If your couch doesn’t fit, call a “couch doctor.”
  While the original New York Couch Doctor (516-779-1720) is famed for disassembling bulky furniture in emergency situations 24/7, the charge is anywhere from a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars. Similar services like Couch Doctor NYC (from $450; 347-509-5003), Dr. Sofa (from $250; 888-837-7632), and Jose Nunez (from $250; 347-446-8879) can take apart sectionals and large pieces such as kitchen tables.
  
  
    .
    Tip your movers 20 percent.
  This is especially appropriate for a $1,000-plus move, but don’t be a miser if it’s a smaller job. It’s customary to hand over cash to the foreman; they’ll distribute it evenly to each mover, according to Matt Graber, the co-owner of Cool Hand Movers. But Venmo or Zelle is often ideal (“The last thing you want is that tip falling out of your pocket when moving up and down stairs,” says DeFabrizio).
  
  
    .
    Wizard of Homes will do a specialized “move-out” deep clean of your empty apartment from $200.
  In addition to vacuuming and mopping, Wizard will clean the insides of windows, closets, and cabinets. Si Se Puede Women’s Cooperative (from $145) is another favorite, and Butch4Hire, a do-it-all company marketed to queer folks, does apartment cleaning starting at $100.
