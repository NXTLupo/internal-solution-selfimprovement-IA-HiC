## Which tool works from your own course?

### AskSia: Best for course-grounded study

We built AskSia for the part of studying that general chatbots skip: the course itself. The Chrome extension syncs a Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle or Brightspace course in one click, read-only, in roughly 15–45 seconds, so lectures, slides and readings are in the workspace before the first question.

Multi-source Q&A then answers across up to 80 PDFs, textbooks and lecture notes, and every answer cites the page, slide or timestamp it came from. Lecture transcription runs in 40+ languages with auto-translation into 20+. A recording becomes a Sia Note, then a flashcard deck scheduled with FSRS, then a Mock Exam in the course's own format.

Nothing is uploaded twice. A synced lecture is already a Sia Note when class ends.

AskSia's[2026 Student Learning Outcomes Report](https://www.asksia.ai/research) analysed 200,000+ conversations from 800 students across six markets: 95% were about understanding, reviewing or connecting concepts, and 5% asked for a direct answer. Bilingual students, 61% of the sample, averaged 72 minutes a day against 42 minutes overall.

Pricing, checked September 2026: a Free plan with no card required; Plus at $9/month with 600 audio minutes and 1,500 pages a month; Super at $18/month with live transcription, 2,400 minutes and 3,000 pages; A+ at $25/month, unlimited, carrying the GPA Guarantee.

The public [AskSia Library](https://www.asksia.ai/explore) holds course guides for universities in Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and Hong Kong.

## Which general assistant should you pick?

Three general assistants dominate student use, and universities have started buying them. In the HEPI 2026 survey, 38% of students said their institution provides an AI tool, up from 9% in 2024, most often ChatGPT, then Microsoft Copilot. The differences below are about caps, context and study modes, not raw intelligence.

Check the campus licence first. A seat that is already paid for changes every other decision.

### ChatGPT: Best for everyday questions and drafting

ChatGPT is the tool most students already have. In the Digital Education Council's 2024 Global AI Student Survey of 3,839 students in 16 countries, 66% used it, more than double the next tool, and OpenAI's own student page pitches it as study partner, career coach and daily assistant.

The Free plan now allows unlimited text chats, subject to abuse guardrails, with limited uploads, limited deep research and roughly 12 pages of input text per prompt, according to the ChatGPT pricing page (checked Sep 2026). Study mode, which works through a problem step by step instead of handing over the answer, is included on every plan, Free included.

Go costs $8/month and may show ads. Plus, at $20/month, adds Projects, scheduled tasks, custom GPTs, larger uploads and the advanced reasoning models. OpenAI lists no student discount for Plus; ChatGPT Edu exists, but the university buys it, not the student.

What it does not do is read a course. Files are uploaded per chat or Project, and there is no LMS sync.

### Claude: Best for long readings and writing feedback

Claude's context window runs up to 1 million tokens depending on the model, which suits a semester of readings or a 60-page thesis draft pasted in one go.

The claude.com pricing page (checked Sep 2026) lists a Free plan with web search, file creation, memory and up to 5 Projects; Pro at $20/month, or $17/month billed annually, with unlimited Projects, Research and more models; and Max from $100/month.

The feature students ask about is Learning mode. Anthropic introduced it with Claude for Education in April 2025: instead of answering, Claude asks how the student would approach the problem and what evidence supports the conclusion. The mode opened to all users as a style option in August 2025.

There is no student discount. Claude for Education is an institutional plan, so access at Northeastern, LSE or Champlain comes through the university's licence, not a .edu checkbox.

For essays, the practical use is feedback on an argument's structure. With Learning mode on, that is also the default behaviour.

### Google Gemini: Best for the free student AI Pro year

The offer decides this one. On 19 August 2026, Google gave eligible US college students 12 months of Google AI Pro free, a plan that otherwise costs $19.99/month, with students in 140+ other markets getting a year of Google AI Plus instead. Redemption runs until 31 December 2026, requires SheerID verification and a payment method, and the plan auto-renews at $19.99/month after the trial.

The bundle matters as much as the model: higher Gemini usage limits, Gemini inside Gmail and Docs, 5 TB of Drive storage, and NotebookLM at Pro limits.

Two study features sit on the free tier. Guided Learning, launched August 2025, breaks problems into steps and quizzes instead of answering outright. The Student Hub at gemini.google.com/students, launched with the 2026 offer, builds Study Notebooks from an uploaded syllabus, runs a diagnostic quiz to find gaps, and can push exam dates into Google Calendar.

Set a renewal reminder on day one. A free year claimed in September starts billing in September 2027.
