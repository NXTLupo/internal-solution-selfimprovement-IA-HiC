## What Do Students Actually Use AI For?

Adoption is now close to universal, and the interesting question has moved on from whether students use AI to what they use it for. In the UK, the **[Higher Education Policy Institute’s Student Generative AI Survey 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)** found **95%** of undergraduates using AI in at least one way, and **94%** using it to help with assessed work. That survey was run by Savanta with **1,054 full-time UK undergraduates** and published in **March 2026**.

The pattern underneath those numbers is consistent. Students use AI most for explaining material they did not follow in a lecture, summarising long readings, generating practice questions, drafting and tidying written work, and debugging code. The heavy use sits in comprehension and revision rather than in producing final submissions.

The gap is institutional rather than technical. As **[reported from the same survey](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/q6zw9wco2mttajdbd1jyeonjnpiovu)**, **77%** of faculty now use AI in their teaching, up **16 percentage points** on 2025. Yet **57%** of students said their assessments came with inadequate AI guidance, and only **29%** believed their instructors were equipped to advise them on it. In practice you are likely to be ahead of your course on this, which makes reading your own institution’s policy carefully more important, not less.
