This guide brings together **50 AI tools for students in 2026–27**, covering studying, research, writing, mathematics, coding, presentations, productivity, language learning and career preparation.

## Category 1: Best general-purpose AI tools for students

### 1. ChatGPT — Best all round study assistant

**Best for:**Homework support, concept explanations, revision, writing feedback, coding, and brainstorming.

ChatGPT can act as a study partner across subjects. Instead of asking it to write an entire assignment, ask it to explain the topic, test your understanding, or identify gaps in your reasoning.

For example, a Class 12 student studying physics could ask it to explain electromagnetic induction using everyday examples, followed by five progressively harder numerical problems.

**Try this prompt:**“Act as my physics tutor. Explain electromagnetic induction in simple language, give me three real-life examples, and ask me five questions one at a time. Don’t reveal the answer until I try.”

Free or paid? A free plan is available, with [paid plans offering](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/) additional capabilities and higher usage limits.

### 2. Google Gemini — Best for students using Google services

**Best for:** Concept explanations, brainstorming, multimodal learning, and working with Google tools.

Gemini can help students explore topics, understand images, solve problems, and develop study plans. Its integration with Google’s ecosystem can be useful for students who regularly work with Docs, Gmail, and other Google services.

Free or paid? Free access and paid [Google AI plans](https://gemini.google.com/) are available, subject to regional availability and usage limits.

### 3. Claude — Best for long readings and detailed writing feedback

**Best for:**Understanding lengthy documents, analyzing arguments, improving essays, and reviewing code.

Claude is useful when you need to work through substantial amounts of text. Upload permitted course material, ask it to identify central arguments, and request questions that test your understanding.

Free or paid? Free and [paid plans](https://claude.ai/) are available, with different usage allowances.

### 4. Microsoft Copilot — Best for Microsoft users

**Best for:** General questions, brainstorming, research assistance, and Microsoft-oriented workflows.

Students who work extensively with Word, PowerPoint, and Excel may find Copilot useful for organizing information and improving productivity. Access to advanced features within Microsoft 365 applications depends on the relevant account and subscription.

Free or paid? [Free Copilot access and paid Microsoft](https://copilot.microsoft.com/) offerings are available.

### 5. DeepSeek — Best for exploring complex reasoning

**Best for:**Logical explanations, programming questions, mathematics practice, and comparing problem-solving approaches.

DeepSeek can be useful when students want to explore a problem step by step or compare alternative approaches to a technical question. Verify calculations, technical claims, and references independently.

Free or paid? [Free access](https://www.deepseek.com/) has been available through its consumer service, but available models, API pricing, and usage conditions differ.

## Category 2: AI tools for research, assignments, and academic papers

### 6. NotebookLM — Best for studying your own notes and PDFs

**Best for:**Exam preparation, lecture notes, textbook summaries, and source-based revision.

[NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/) lets you add supported source documents and ask questions grounded in those sources. Depending on current availability, features can include summaries, study guides, flashcards, quizzes, and audio overviews.

Try asking: “Create a chapter-wise revision guide. Identify the five most important concepts, explain each in simple language, and generate 20 multiple-choice questions using only the uploaded material.”

Free or paid? A free offering is available, with higher limits or additional capabilities through eligible paid plans.

## 7. Perplexity — Best for web research with citations

**Best for:** Finding sources, exploring current topics, and beginning research for assignments.

[Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/) searches for information and provides answers with source links. Open the linked sources and verify that they actually support the claims. Free or paid? Free and paid plans are available.

## 8. Elicit — Best for academic literature reviews

**Best for:** Finding research papers, comparing studies, and organising evidence.

[Elicit](https://elicit.com/) can help students discover relevant papers and extract information such as research questions, methods, and findings. Use it to organise reading, not to replace reading the papers themselves.

Free or paid? Free access with limits and paid plans are available.

## 9. Consensus — Best for research-backed questions

**Best for:**Exploring scientific questions and locating relevant academic studies.

[Consensus](https://consensus.app/) focuses on scientific and academic literature. It can help identify papers worth reading in full, but study quality, sample size, and contradictory findings still matter.

Free or paid? Free access and paid options are available, subject to current limits.

## 10. Scite — Best for examining research citations

**Best for:** Literature reviews, dissertation research, and evaluating how studies are cited.

[Scite](https://scite.ai/) helps users examine citation context and investigate whether later publications support, contrast with, or mention a scientific claim.

Free or paid? Access to features and analysis depends on the current plan.

## Category 3: AI writing and language improvement tools

## 11. Grammarly — Best for grammar and clarity

**Best for:** Essays, reports, emails, applications, and proofreading.

[Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/) identifies grammar mistakes, spelling errors, and opportunities to improve clarity and tone.

Free or paid? A free tier is available. Paid plans provide additional writing assistance and features.

## 12. QuillBot — Best for paraphrasing and summarizing

**Best for:**Reworking your own drafts, shortening text, and improving sentence clarity.

Use [QuillBot](https://quillbot.com/) after you understand the original meaning. Changing the words of a source does not eliminate the need to cite that source.

Free or paid? Free tools have limits, while paid plans unlock additional capabilities.

## 13. LanguageTool — Best for multilingual proofreading

**Best for:**Grammar, spelling, punctuation, and writing in multiple languages.

[LanguageTool](https://languagetool.org/) helps identify mistakes and improve sentence construction in assignments, reports, and professional correspondence.

Free or paid? Free and premium options are available.

## 14. DeepL Write — Best for improving sentence structure

**Best for:** Clearer academic and professional writing.

[DeepL](https://www.deepl.com/write) Write offers suggestions for wording, grammar, tone, and style. Compare suggestions with your own writing rather than accepting every change automatically.

Free or paid? Availability and usage limits depend on the current offering and region.

## 15. Wordtune — Best for exploring alternative wording

**Best for:**Improving clarity, adjusting tone, and rewriting awkward sentences.

[Wordtune](https://www.wordtune.com/) suggests alternative ways to express an idea, which can help refine an internship application or make a project summary more concise.

Free or paid? Free access with limits and paid plans are available.

## Category 4: AI tools for mathematics, science, and exam preparation

### 16. Wolfram Alpha — Best for mathematical computation

**Best for:** Algebra, calculus, equations, scientific calculations, and data analysis.

[Wolfram](https://www.wolframalpha.com/) use it to evaluate an integral, explore a function, or check a scientific calculation. Attempt the problem first, then use the tool to check the result or investigate the steps.

Free or paid? Basic access and paid Pro features are available.

### 17. Photomath — Best for understanding maths problems from images

**Best for:** School level mathematics and step-by-step problem practice.

[Photomath](https://photomath.com/) lets students scan supported maths problems and examine solution steps.
