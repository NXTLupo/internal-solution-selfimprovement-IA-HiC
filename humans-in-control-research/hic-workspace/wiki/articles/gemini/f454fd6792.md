Markdown Content:
95% of full-time UK undergraduates now use AI in some form, and 94% use generative AI to help with assessed work, according to the [HEPI and Kortext Student Generative AI Survey 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/). US surveys land lower but in the same direction: 85% of college students and 54% of teens report using AI for schoolwork. What students do with it is mostly explaining and summarizing, not pasting in finished essays.

Each number below lists its survey year, sample and country. A UK undergraduate panel and a US teen panel measure different things, so treat each figure as its own measurement.

## Key statistics

*   95% of full-time UK undergraduates used AI in at least one way in the 2025/26 academic year, up from 66% in 2024 (HEPI and Kortext, 1,054 students, December 2025).
*   94% of UK undergraduates used generative AI to help prepare assessed work, but only 12% pasted AI-generated text directly into it (HEPI and Kortext, 2026).
*   85% of US college students used generative AI for coursework in the past year (Inside Higher Ed and Generation Lab, 1,047 students, July 2025).
*   54% of US teens aged 13 to 17 have used AI chatbots for help with schoolwork, and 10% do all or most of their schoolwork with chatbot help (Pew Research Center, 1,458 teens, fall 2025).
*   The share of US students from middle school to college using AI for homework rose from 48% in May 2025 to 62% in December 2025 (RAND American Youth Panel, 1,214 students aged 12 to 29).
*   84% of US high school students reported using generative AI for schoolwork in May 2025, up from 79% in January 2025 (College Board).
*   61% of UK undergraduates have used AI to explain concepts for assessed work, the most common use in HEPI's 2026 survey.
*   67% of US students agreed that the more students use AI for schoolwork, the more it will harm their critical thinking, up from 54% earlier in 2025 (RAND, December 2025).
*   Proven AI cheating cases at UK universities ran at 5.1 per 1,000 students in 2023-24, up from 1.6 per 1,000 the year before (Guardian Freedom of Information data from 131 universities).
*   In a blind test at the University of Reading, 94% of fully AI-written exam submissions went undetected by markers (Scarfe et al., PLOS ONE, 2024).
*   Only about one-third of US students said their school had a schoolwide AI policy (RAND, December 2025).

## How many students use AI?

### UK university students

*   The share of UK undergraduates using AI in at least one way rose from **66%** in 2024 to **92%** in 2025 and **95%** in 2026 ([HEPI and Kortext, 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/), polled by Savanta, margin of error about 3%).
*   Use of AI for assessed work went from just over half in 2024 to **89%** in 2025 and **94%** in 2026 (HEPI and Kortext, 2026).
*   Using text generators such as ChatGPT actually fell, from **64%** of students in 2025 to **56%** in 2026. HEPI says the reason is unclear and suggests students may be moving to more specialized tools.
*   In contrast, only **34%** of UK higher education students said they used AI tools "as part of their learning" in Jisc's 2024/25 survey of 15,398 students ([Jisc, 2025](https://digitalinsights.jisc.ac.uk/reports-and-briefings/our-reports/2024-25-uk-higher-education-students-digital-experience-insights-survey-findings/)). The gap with HEPI's 95% is a question wording and sampling difference, not a contradiction; HEPI counts any AI use for any purpose.

### US college students

*   **85%** of US two- and four-year college students used generative AI for coursework in the past year ([Inside Higher Ed and Generation Lab](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/academics/2025/08/29/survey-college-students-views-ai), 1,047 students at 166 institutions, July 2025).
*   **61%** of US undergraduates use AI at least weekly, up from 42% a year earlier ([Tyton Partners Listening to Learners 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/15/3361822/0/en/tyton-partners-releases-listening-to-learners-2026-a-national-portrait-of-students-balancing-aspirations-academics-and-ai-revealing-important-gaps-between-student-and-institutional.html), 1,800+ undergraduates, May 2026).
*   **32%** of US college students use generative AI daily, less than administrators (43%) but more than faculty (25%) ([Tyton Partners Time for Class 2026](https://tytonpartners.com/time-for-class-2026-the-ai-tipping-point-from-monitoring-students-to-engaging-them/), spring 2026).
*   Only **7%** of US college students describe themselves as "all in" on AI. "Worried about dependence" was the most common stance, and 40% said they worry about depending on AI tools ([Inside Higher Ed and Generation Lab](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/academic-life/2026/06/11/why-students-arent-all-ai-and-what-they-want-colleges), 1,038 students, May 2026).

### US teens and K-12 students

*   **54%** of US teens aged 13 to 17 have used chatbots for help with schoolwork: 10% for all or most of it, 21% for some and 23% for a little. 45% have not ([Pew Research Center, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/), 1,458 teens, September to October 2025).
*   Teens report more chatbot use than their parents realize: **64%** of teens say they use chatbots, while about half of parents say their teen does (Pew Research Center, 2026).
*   Use of ChatGPT specifically for schoolwork doubled among US teens in a year, from **13%** in 2023 to **26%** in 2024. 11th and 12th graders (31%) were more likely to use it than 7th and 8th graders (20%) ([Pew Research Center, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/01/15/about-a-quarter-of-us-teens-have-used-chatgpt-for-schoolwork-double-the-share-in-2023/), 1,391 teens, fall 2024).
*   **84%** of US high school students used generative AI for schoolwork in May 2025, up from 79% in January, and 69% used ChatGPT for assignments and homework ([College Board](https://research.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ai-research-brief-1_vf.pdf), about 600 students per wave).
*   AI use for homework among US students rose from **48%** to **62%** between May and December 2025, driven by middle and high schoolers; use among college students stayed relatively steady ([RAND, 2026](https://www.rand.org/news/press/2026/03/student-use-of-ai-for-homework-rises-as-concerns-grow.html)).
