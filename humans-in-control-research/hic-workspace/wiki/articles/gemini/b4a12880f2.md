## 1. A new era of AI-driven decision-making

In 2026, the banking industry – as anticipated in the [November 2025 finance roundup](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/11/summits-push-blended-finance-and-other-finance-news-to-know/) – is moving from AI "assistance" to "transactional authority". These systems are no longer just summarizing reports; they are being integrated as semi-autonomous "digital co-workers" designed to settle routine trades and manage compliance checks under human oversight.

Goldman Sachs, for instance, is[developing autonomous agents powered by Anthropic's Claude model](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/06/anthropic-goldman-sachs-ai-model-accounting.html) to handle core trade accounting and client onboarding tasks. The bank says that these agents will act as digital co-workers, reducing the amount of time such essential but process-intensive functions take.

Meanwhile, Lloyds Banking Group has said 2026 will see "[enterprise-wide deployment](https://www.lloydsbankinggroup.com/insights/2026-the-year-of-agentic-ai-and-a-new-era-for-finance.html)" across financial services of agentic AI. The bank expects these systems to add £100 million in value this year by automating fraud investigations and complex complaints – [diverting routine cases to AI](https://www.lloydsbankinggroup.com/media/press-releases/2026/lloyds-banking-group/ai-driven-benefits-2026.html) while reserving human staff for the most nuanced client escalations.

## 2. Private credit's $41 trillion expansion

As traditional bank lending remains constrained by tighter capital standards, corporate funding is continuing to migrate towards private credit.

The market for trading private deal stakes (secondaries) reached a record $226 billion in total volume, [Evercore’s 2025/2026 market data shows](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-16/private-secondaries-deals-hit-226-billion-amid-thirst-for-cash). This surge is driven by a critical need for liquidity, as limited partners (LPs) increasingly use secondaries to manage portfolios in the absence of a robust Initial Public Offering (IPO) market.

Regulators are focusing on the growing "[interconnections" between banks and private funds](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d607.htm). The Basel Committee recently stated that the boom in "significant risk transfers" (SRTs) – where banks pay private funds to take on the risk of their loan books – requires continued supervision. Excessive reliance on SRT could reduce the resilience of the banking system if the risk-bearing capacity of transactions were to fail, it said.
