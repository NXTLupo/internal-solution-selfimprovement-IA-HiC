# How to pitch AI more effectively to workers

How to pitch AI more effectively to workers
Debbie Lovich thinks most leaders have it all wrong when it comes to their messaging around AI. Telling people “we’re doing AI [to improve] productivity” as if that's the only benefit is “dead on arrival,” she said recently. “Who’s going to want to engage with that?”
Lovich, a longtime management consultant with BCG and author of the forthcoming Make Work Work, has spent the last two decades focused on the human side of transformation, whether for digital initiatives, hybrid work, or now, generative AI. The focus, according to Lovich, can’t just be about productivity, which implies “use the tool, and you’ll fire me.” Instead, the messages leaders send—and more fundamentally, the goals they set for AI transformation—have to be broader. “[It] has to be what’s in it for employees, as well as shareholders and customers. And 90% of organizations are getting it wrong.”
Too many leaders, she said in a recent Charter webinar and Forum session, are focused on the technology changes they’re making, rather than the very messy work of changing how people, teams, and technology work together. A former peer, Lovich recalled, recently posted on LinkedIn about seven things that need to be done to get value from AI spending, and all of them were technical. “Number eight is being employee-centric,” she said. “It's how you talk about it.”
