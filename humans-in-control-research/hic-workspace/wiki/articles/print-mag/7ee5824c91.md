# Type Tuesday: Rosebriar Brings Storybook Charm Without Feeling Stuck in the Past

Source: [print_mag](https://www.printmag.com/type-tuesday/type-tuesday-rosebriar-brings-storybook-charm-without-feeling-stuck-in-the-past/)

Decorative display faces often ask you to commit to a very specific aesthetic. Rosebriar takes a different approach. It embraces ornament, history, and personality while leaving enough room for designers to decide exactly how far to push the mood.
Designed by Laura Worthington of Laura Worthington Design, Rosebriar reflects the foundry’s signature approach to type: hand-drawn letterforms that balance artistic expression with meticulous craftsmanship. Worthington’s work is rooted in hand lettering and calligraphy, resulting in typefaces that feel both personal and refined. Whether playful and casual or elegant and sophisticated, her fonts are designed to bring character to a wide range of creative projects.
At first glance, Rosebriar feels familiar. Its Roman letterforms are punctuated with selective gothic flourishes—wedge-shaped crossbars, splayed terminals, and diamond-shaped tittles—that give the typeface a handcrafted quality. The result reads medieval manuscript, storybook illustration, and the Arts and Crafts movement without becoming a historical mish mash. It’s nostalgic, but not relying on nostalgia for meaning.
Designed primarily as a display face, Rosebriar suits headlines, titles, and other moments where typography is expected to do more than simply communicate words. Its restrained decorative details allow it to fit comfortably into subheads, pull quotes, and other short passages where many ornamental typefaces would quickly become overwhelming.
The family also includes thoughtful OpenType features—including fractions, stylistic alternates, and several additional typographic refinements—that make it more versatile than its decorative appearance might initially suggest. These features give designers greater control while preserving the typeface’s distinctive personality.
In a design landscape where many display fonts rely on extremes to capture attention, Rosebriar succeeds through nuance. It offers enough ornament to establish a memorable voice while remaining adaptable for editorial, publishing, branding, packaging, and cultural work that calls for a touch of historical character without feeling tied to a single era.
