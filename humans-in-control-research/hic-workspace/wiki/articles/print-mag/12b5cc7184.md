# What Matters to Justin Wong

Source: [print_mag](https://www.printmag.com/what-matters/what-matters-to-justin-wong/)

Justin Wong is a design lead and art director from New York City, crafting design and advertising for cultural and commercial clients like Apple, The Wall Street Journal, and more. He is the incoming president at AIGA NY, leading its board of directors in service of the organization’s mission of championing the future of design for all. @justinwongofficial
What is the thing you like doing most in the world?
Creating. Making something new. Sometimes that’s design, but I also really enjoy cooking. There’s something so special about sharing amazing food and the experience of preparing it with and for the people that matter to you. In my dream world, I just host dinner parties every week with interesting people.
What is the first memory you have of being creative?
One thing that I do recall is a moment as a 2/3 year old mixing finger paints. I blended up this beige color and thought it was the most revolutionary discovery (as most things are to toddlers). It was so exciting and special that I created this mature color that didn’t look like any of the ones in the tubes. Quiet luxury for kids.
What is your biggest regret?
Letting other people tell me who I should be. I spent a lot of time and energy in my younger years searching for validation and punishing myself when I didn’t find it, wishing I was someone else. All of my decisions came from a place of fear and anxiety. I wish I would have learned to trust my instincts and give grace to myself sooner.
How have you gotten over heartbreak?
Taking time is really important. There really isn’t any other way to work through pain and disappointment than to realize that there is still more out there. Do things that remind you who you are, and that your identity is not tied to any one other person, experience, or opportunity. Also… dessert doesn’t hurt.
What makes you cry?
Endings— the idea that something can really be final, with no “do-over” or second chances. Jack Wolfe as Orpheus in Hadestown. Seeing my friends break up. Seeing my friends get married. Sunday scaries. General anxiety and frustration. The fig tree analogy from Sylvia Plath’s The Bell Jar.
How long does the pride and joy of accomplishing something last for you?
A lot of the time, I feel a sense of accomplishment when I know that I’ve come up with a solution— that breakthrough moment. So by the time the thing is finished and done, I’m already on the move to the next discovery. But I also don’t experience imposter syndrome often, so I definitely acknowledge my achievements on some level. I just don’t ever want to be concerned about where my “peak” is.
Do you believe in an afterlife, and if so, what does that look like to you?
I really go back and forth on this one. I grew up Catholic and though I haven’t been practicing for many years, I enjoy the idea of an afterlife. As long as there is peace, I’m happy. (Not that this is of any concern to the universe.) I imagine it’s like sleep, but better.
What do you hate most about yourself?
I tend to focus on what’s next before fully appreciating what’s now. It’s easy to get excited about the future, but constantly looking ahead can mean missing the moments right in front of you. Lately, I’ve been making a conscious effort to slow down, be present, and recognize that not everything meaningful exists on the horizon.
What do you love most about yourself?
I am really driven to make things happen. For myself, for others. I’ve always been someone who struggles to leave a good idea alone. When I see potential in a project, a person, or a community, I want to help move it forward. It’s responsible for many of the most meaningful things in my life.
What is your absolute favorite meal?
Food takes up an inordinate amount of real estate in my mindscape, so it’s impossible to select a favorite. However, I do have a meal that is very special and important to me. At the end of each year, I meet with my best friend in the city at one of our favorite restaurants. We order a spinach artichoke dip, Caesar salad, two French dip sandwiches with extra horseradish sauce, and an icy bottle of Saratoga sparkling water with lemon wedges. We sit and reflect on the year, the journeys we’ve taken, and the road ahead. There’s always something to celebrate as we meet new versions of ourselves each year.
