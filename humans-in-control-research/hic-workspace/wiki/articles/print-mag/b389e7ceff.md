# Pianist Sofiane Pamart Creates Immersive Visual Worlds for His Albums and Score Books

Source: [print_mag](https://www.printmag.com/design-news/sofiane-pamart-movie/)

Physical media always presents an opportunity for exploration. The boundaries of printed material are meant to be pushed, and I’m always eager to see artists of all kinds doing just that. French composer and pianist Sofiane Pamart falls into this category, recently expanding the traditional form of sheet music for his latest album, “Movie.” Pamart worked closely with designer and illustrator Adrien Beaujeant and creative director Guillaume Héritier for the project, reimagining his piano score as an art book.
“Movie” isn’t the first time this trio have pushed the limits of the sheet music and album form. Instead of treating each of Pamart’s releases as standalone campaigns, they’ve spent years building them out as a cohesive series. “From day one, we never wanted to create ‘just’ album covers. The idea has always been to build a collection,” says Beaujeant. “Every project has its own identity—its own color, typography, graphic language and materials. But when you put them all next to each other, you instantly feel that they belong to the same universe. A bit like a bookshelf full of novels or a collection of films; every piece stands on its own, but together they tell a much bigger story.”
The visual language they created for the “Movie” vinyl and art book takes cues from the history of cinema, including vintage screenplay covers, classic title sequences, and Hollywood iconography. “We never wanted to reference one specific film,” says Beaujeant. “Instead, we wanted to tap into our collective memory of cinema. We prefer to suggest rather than spell things out. The idea is to leave enough space for people to project their own story into the album.” The distinctive pink color at the center of the “Movie” identity was inspired by an old movie script cover, complemented by a distinctive typeface that invokes classic film titles and signage from Hollywood institutions such as the Château Marmont. A fantastical illustrative depiction of Los Angeles portrays a dreamlike view of the city from Pamart’s childhood home, suspended between reality, memory and fiction. Meanwhile, Beaujeant’s illustrations of theatre curtains, sheet music, film props, masks, textures and cinematic symbols appear throughout the album and art book, carrying a series of hidden narratives.
The visual identity is dripping in sensations of soft nostalgia, with photographs from Pamart’s childhood and textural details like doily design motifs. One of these childhood photos of Pamart, taken by his father, serves as the album cover. “You see a young Sofiane looking straight ahead, almost as if he were already watching the movie of his own life,” Beaujeant explains. “That image became the starting point for the entire narrative.” Additional childhood photographs of Pamart in Hollywood and elsewhere appear throughout the rest of the album and printed materials, like fragmented memories.
The “Movie” piano score book blurs the lines between a music edition, artist’s book. and design object. As beautiful as the score book is, no decision was made purely for aesthetics. “We spend a huge amount of time thinking about details, but nothing is there just for decoration,” says Beaujeant. “Every element has a purpose.” The book extends the universe of the project beyond the record, while making the music accessible to pianists of different levels and musical backgrounds.
