# How to Write a Targeted Resume (With Examples)

Source: [resume_genius_blog](https://resumegenius.com/blog/resume-help/targeted-resume)

Struggling to get interviews?
Generic resumes get skipped. Targeted resumes get interviews. Learn how to tailor your resume to each job posting so it speaks directly to what employers want.
In this guide, we’ll walk you through how to write a resume that targets a specific job, with clear examples to show you exactly what works.
What is a targeted resume?
A targeted resume is a customized version of your resume written specifically for a particular job. Instead of listing all your experiences and skills, it highlights only the qualifications that directly match the requirements of the job posting.
This tailored approach shows employers that you understand what they’re looking for, and that you’re the right fit for the role.
How to write a resume that targets a job
A targeted resume shows employers you’re the right person for the role before they even meet you. Follow these steps to write a resume that aligns with the job description.
Streamline the process with a master resume
To make the tailoring process more efficient, maintain a master resume, a comprehensive, multi-page document that serves as the ultimate source of truth for your career history. Unlike a targeted resume, this version is never sent to employers.
Instead, it acts as a repository for every job title, responsibility, certification, and accomplishment you have ever achieved, ensuring that no valuable data is lost as you progress through your career.
When you are ready to apply for a specific role, simply create a copy of your master resume and delete any information that does not directly support your candidacy for that position.
This approach allows you to pull from a pre-written bank of high-quality descriptions, saving time and allowing you to focus your energy on perfectly aligning your qualifications with the job description.
Read the job description carefully
Before you start editing your resume, take time to read the job description word for word. Pay close attention to the required skills, experience, and specific language the employer uses.
Highlight key responsibilities, repeated terms, and qualifications that are emphasized. These are your clues for what to highlight on your resume.

Customize your resume summary
Think of your resume summary as your personal elevator pitch tailored for the specific role. Focus on the top 3–5 skills or achievements the employer is looking for, and use language that mirrors the job posting to grab attention immediately.
SUMMARY
Marketing professional with 2+ years of experience coordinating multi-channel campaigns across email, social, and paid media. Skilled in using Google Analytics and HubSpot to track performance and optimize engagement. Recognized for excellent communication and project management skills in fast-paced agency environments.
Adjust your skills section
The skills section of your resume should closely reflect what the employer has emphasized in the job ad. Put your most relevant skills at the top, and match their exact wording when possible to communicate that you have the precise qualifications they want.
SKILLS
- Market research
- Project management
- Content creation and copywriting
- Google Analytics
- HubSpot
- Social media scheduling (Hootsuite, Buffer)
- Mailchimp
- CMS Platforms (WordPress, Squarespace)
- Basic HTML & CSS
Tailor your work experience
Make sure each bullet point in your work experience section shows why you’re perfect for this new role.
Pick out the most relevant tasks and achievements from your previous jobs, and write about them in a way that matches what the employer wants, like this:
WORK EXPERIENCE
Marketing Assistant
NewPath Digital | Boston, MA
June 20XX–Present
- Supported the execution of multi-channel marketing campaigns across email, social media, and paid platforms, resulting in a 20% increase in lead conversions over six months
- Created and scheduled content using social media scheduling platforms (Buffer, Hootsuite), and maintained brand voice across channels
- Monitored campaign performance and generated weekly reports using Google Analytics and HubSpot, identifying insights that improved open and click-through rates
- Assisted in drafting marketing materials like landing pages, email campaigns, and promotional assets, ensuring alignment with campaign goals
- Maintained detailed timelines and collaborated with design and sales teams to meet all project deadlines with consistent attention to detail
- Conducted regular market research to inform strategy and contributed ideas during campaign planning sessions
Customize other sections
Add certifications, awards, or volunteer work that relate to the job you’re applying for. If you’re a recent graduate or are switching careers, highlight relevant coursework or projects that demonstrate your skills and knowledge in your target industry.
EDUCATION
Bachelor of Arts in Marketing
Ohio State University
May 20XX
Relevant Coursework: Digital Marketing Strategies, Consumer Behavior Analysis, Content Creation and Copywriting, Data Analytics for Marketing
Senior project: Developed a comprehensive social media marketing campaign for a local nonprofit, resulting in a 30% increase in online engagement
Activities: Member of the Marketing Club, collaborated on email marketing and event promotion initiatives
Optimize for applicant tracking systems and human readers
When applying for jobs, your resume often goes through two filters: an applicant tracking system (ATS) and a human recruiter. ATS software scans your resume to look for specific keywords and qualifications, helping employers quickly sort through and narrow down large applicant pools.
At the same time, once your resume passes the ATS, it needs to be clear and easy to read for recruiters. A well-organized, concise, and relevant resume helps employers quickly understand your experience and skills.

Targeted resume examples
These targeted resume examples can help you see how to customize your own resume to fit the job you want.
Entry-level applicant
Even if you don’t have any professional experience, you can make a targeted resume by describing any job-relevant skills, training, coursework, or volunteer work you’ve done.
Here’s an ad for an entry-level graphic designer job (we used bold, underlined text to indicate the information applicants should target in their resumes):
Seeking a talented graphic designer to design various digital and printed materials as a Designer for Mama’s Cookies. Please submit a portfolio of your work along with a resume and cover letter that explain how you meet the following requirements:
- Detail-oriented, capable of working independently and as part of a team, and comfortable with receiving feedback
- Skills in Photoshop, Figma, and Microsoft Office, and ability to use Zoom, Slack, and other tools to communicate with remote coworkers
- Previous experience designing food-related content is preferred
