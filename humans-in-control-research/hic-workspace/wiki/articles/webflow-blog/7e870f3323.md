# The 6 best headless CMS platforms for marketing teams in 2026

Source: [webflow_blog](https://webflowmarketingmain.com/blog/best-headless-cms-platforms)

The right headless CMS can give your marketing team visual editing autonomy, reduce developer bottlenecks, and scale multi-channel content.
A headless content management system (CMS) stores content and serves it through an API, leaving page rendering to other tools. Many platforms fit that definition, but they serve different teams.
Teams with dedicated engineers feeding content to a website plus an app want a structured, developer-led platform. Non-technical teams who need to build and edit pages without a developer queue want a visual or hybrid CMS. And teams that must own their infrastructure want a headless CMS they can host themselves, which usually means an open-source one.
This guide compares six platforms across those approaches, including Webflow, which renders and hosts the site itself. Each one is judged from a marketing team's seat: how much page building marketers can do without a developer, and what the platform really costs once you factor in a front end and hosting.
Key takeaways:
- Webflow renders and hosts the site, so marketing teams ship pages without a front-end build, then read the same CMS through an API when a second surface needs it.
- Storyblok and Prismic offer visual editing over front-end components, giving marketing teams page-building flexibility when developers support it.
- Strapi provides an open-source, self-hosted option for cost-sensitive or compliance-focused teams that need full control over their infrastructure.
- Selecting the right platform depends on front-end engineering capacity (if any), multi-channel or localization needs, and overall budget (including hosting and overages).
How we evaluated the top headless CMS platforms for marketing teams
Webflow, Storyblok, Contentful, Sanity, Strapi, and Prismic cover the visual-and-hosted, visual-and-hybrid, developer-led, and self-hosted approaches for marketing sites. In this comparison, we check each against the vendor's live pricing page (verified September 2026) and, where a public listing exists, against G2, Gartner Peer Insights, Reddit, and Hacker News.
Here’s how the platforms line up:
We’ll cover each option in more detail below, starting with the platform that removes the front-end build entirely, then the visual, developer-led, and self-hosted options.
1. Webflow
Webflow is the only platform on this list that stores structured content and also renders and hosts pages, so a marketing team can ship a site without building or deploying a separate front end.
Designers and marketers work on the Designer canvas with full control over layout and styling, editors update structured CMS Collections, and publishing goes to managed hosting in the same product. That sets it apart from the headless-only tools, which leave page building to developers.
Headless delivery is built in. The read-only, CDN-backed Content Delivery API and the REST Data API let a second site, an app, or a script read the same Collections, and Webflow's MCP server is generally available for agents in Claude Code, Cursor, ChatGPT, and Codex, with every agent change written to the site activity log.
Keep in mind that Webflow is a website and marketing platform, not exclusively a headless-only backend. It runs on its own managed infrastructure and exposes a REST API. For a marketing and content site, that trade removes the separate build and hosting bill the other five all carry.
Webflow pros:
- Design, CMS, and hosting in one product: Marketers publish pages themselves instead of waiting for a developer to stand up and maintain the site.
- Structured CMS with read APIs: Content lives in Collections with reference and multi-reference fields, and the Content Delivery API (available to all customers) plus the REST Data API let the same items feed an app or a second surface.
- Agents and add-ons in one platform: The MCP server connects agents to the site the marketer edits. Features like Localize, Analyze, and Optimize add translation, analytics, and A/B testing without a separate stack.
Webflow cons:
- SaaS only, REST only: Webflow runs on its own hosting with no self-host option and documents no GraphQL endpoint, so a team that needs either should look elsewhere.
- Relationship limits: Collections carry up to 10 reference fields (20 on Enterprise), and nested Collection lists stop at three levels, 10 per page, and 100 items per parent, so a deeply relational model can outgrow them.
- Item ceilings and add-on costs: Published sites hit CMS-item and API rate limits, and Localized, Analyze, and Optimize are paid add-ons, so a multi-locale or high-traffic project should price them upfront.
What users say about Webflow
G2 reviewers agree the visual control and all-in-one hosting are the draw, and split on CMS limits and add-on costs:
- Design control without code: Reviewers credit visual building plus hosting in one place with helping non-developers ship and maintain sites themselves.
- One platform replaces a stack: Reviewers describe dropping a separate CMS, host, and front-end build once the site lives in Webflow.
- CMS caps and export friction: Reviewers name CMS item caps, add-on costs across many sites or locales, and the difficulty of exporting CMS records as their main frustrations.
- Localization slows at scale: Translating CMS collections item by item is slow work, so multi-market teams may need to price a dedicated translation tool alongside Webflow Localized.

2. Storyblok
A live page preview sits at the center of Storyblok, which Forrester rated a “Strong Performer” in The Forrester Wave: Content Management Systems, Q1 2025, the analyst firm's evaluation of CMS vendors. Editors click into that preview and change content inside reusable components that a developer has defined once (Storyblok calls them “blocks”).
Content goes out through the REST API on every plan to whatever front-end framework the team runs. The product targets marketing teams that want to assemble and update pages themselves without giving up a headless architecture.
However, its visual editing has a notable limitation: Editing happens in a side panel while the preview updates. Inline editing directly on the canvas is listed as "In Evaluation" on Storyblok's public roadmap and has not shipped.
Storyblok pros:
- Workflow testing with real content: Marketing teams can test the editing workflow before anyone signs a purchase order.
- Storyblok Agents on paid plans: Released in September 2026, the agents read stories, assets, releases, and translations and act on them within the user's existing permissions, with custom and scheduled agents for recurring tasks.
- Three localization models: Field-level, folder-level, and space-level translation are all built in, so regional teams pick the structure that matches how their content is organized.
Storyblok cons:
- The visual editor depends on developer-maintained code: The live preview renders only after developers install the Storyblok Bridge in the front end. As a result, editors lose in-context preview whenever a framework upgrade breaks that integration.
- Staged campaigns need a sales call: Teams that need grouped releases, staging, custom workflows, or GraphQL cannot stay on a self-serve plan.
- Short version history on self-serve plans: Content teams may be unable to restore older campaign pages without a contract.
What users say about Storyblok
The visual editor accounts for most of what reviewers like, but they also praised multilingual work and campaign autonomy. Reddit is harsher on the commercial side, with agency discussions mixing praise with support complaints and price-increase reports:
- Fastest editor for non-technical teams: An agency commenter in an r/webdev thread on Storyblok said Storyblok is the CMS its non-technical clients pick up quickest, and G2 reviewers describe launching campaigns with little developer help once the component library exists.
- Multilingual content is a strength: Reviewers running several markets say the locale features cut translation errors and maintenance work.
- Price jumps tied to usage thresholds: Posters describe being moved to a far higher tier after crossing an asset limit, and one team in an r/nextjs thread reported a 6x monthly increase after Storyblok reclassified its content category as enterprise-only.
- Components remain developer territory: Gartner Peer Insights reviewers note that creating or changing a component is not self-serve, so the developer-free experience ends where the component library does.
