# What are the best online course platforms for creators and businesses?

Source: [webflow_blog](https://webflowmarketingmain.com/blog/online-course-platforms)

The best online course platform depends on what you teach and whether you want to own your course business or tap into a marketplace audience.
The best online course platform comes down to two decisions: whether you build on a platform you control or sell through a marketplace, and which tool fits the kind of course you are running. The two models are not mutually exclusive, and plenty of creators use both, but one usually sits at the center of the business.
On a dedicated platform, such as Kajabi, Teachable, Thinkific, Podia, or LearnWorlds, you build and sell courses on your own site, set your price, and keep the customer relationship, in exchange for a monthly fee and the work of bringing your own audience.
On a marketplace like Udemy or Skillshare, you publish into an existing catalog and reach students already browsing there, in exchange for a share of revenue and less control.
For a business building courses as a core revenue line, an owned platform usually pays off. A marketplace is the faster way to test a topic or reach students on day one. Below, we compare the nine we recommend by the job each one does best.
Key takeaways:
- If you want to own your course business and keep the customer relationship, a dedicated platform beats a marketplace, and Kajabi, Teachable, and Thinkific lead the pack.
- Kajabi is the premium all-in-one platform for a serious course or coaching business; Teachable and Thinkific are simpler and cheaper to start with.
- Podia is the simplest, lowest-cost way to sell your first course, whereas LearnWorlds is the pick for advanced, interactive courses and branded academies.
- For courses built around a community, Mighty Networks and Circle place the discussion and the course under a single membership.
- Udemy and Skillshare get your course in front of an existing audience fast, in exchange for less control over pricing and the customer relationship.
What is the difference between a course platform and a course marketplace?
When you are choosing how to sell a course, the model comes before the tool:
A platform makes you the owner of the site, the price, and the customer list, and puts the job of finding students on you. A marketplace hands that discovery problem to an existing catalog and takes a cut in return.
Neither rules the other out. A common pattern is to use a marketplace to be found and an owned platform to build the business, once you know the topic sells.
The 9 best online course platforms
To choose the right platform, weigh four things:
- Who owns the audience and the customer relationship: your platform, or the marketplace
- The real cost at volume, once you add transaction fees to the monthly price
- What the platform lets you teach, from a simple video course to cohorts, assessments, and a full academy
- How much marketing it includes, from none to a complete email-and-funnel suite
With those in mind, here are the nine:
Let's look at each one in more detail to help you narrow the choice.
1. Kajabi
Kajabi is a premium all-in-one platform for businesses built on courses or coaching. It brings the course builder together with email marketing, checkout, and built-in landing pages, and it takes no platform cut on sales through Kajabi Payments (card processing still applies).
We recommend Kajabi to established course sellers, and steer price-sensitive beginners elsewhere. Kajabi charges a monthly fee you can justify once you are selling, because the built-in marketing tools would otherwise be separate subscriptions. If you are testing a first idea, that fee lands before the revenue does.
Kajabi pros:
- A whole business in one platform: The course, the checkout, and the marketing all run in one place, so you are not paying for and wiring up three tools.
- No platform cut on sales: Kajabi takes nothing on top of payment processing through Kajabi Payments, so at volume you keep more of each sale than on a platform that skims a percentage.
Kajabi cons:
- The highest starting price: At $143 a month, it costs more than any other platform on this list, which stings before you have the sales to use its marketing tools.
- Overbuilt for a simple course: If you only need a login on a few lessons, most of the suite goes unused, and a cheaper tool will do the job.
Pricing: Plans start at $143 per month billed annually on the Basic plan and rise for the Growth and higher tiers. Kajabi takes no platform cut on Kajabi Payments; card processing is 2.9% + $0.30, and using a third-party processor instead adds a 2% to 0.5% Kajabi fee.
Best for: An established course or coaching business that will use the marketing suite.
2. Teachable
Teachable is the straightforward way to launch and sell a course, and it is where many first-time course creators start. You upload your content, set a price, and Teachable handles hosting and checkout so you can publish fast.
We point people here when the goal is to ship a course this month without learning a large platform. Setup is quick, and the interface stays out of the way. The tradeoff shows up in the fees.
Teachable's entry Starter plan takes 7.5% of each sale, so creators doing real volume move up to the Builder plan, where the platform fee drops to 0%. Read the plan against your expected sales, not the monthly price alone.
Teachable pros:
- Fast to launch: Little to set up, so a course owner can go from content to a live, paid course in a day rather than a sprint.
- 0% fees at higher volumes: The platform fee disappears on the Builder plan and above, so growing volume doesn't mean a growing cut.
Teachable cons:
- A 7.5% fee to start: That is a bigger cut than most entry plans here, so real volume makes upgrading to the 0% Builder plan the cheaper path.
- Light on marketing: The lower price buys fewer built-in email and funnel tools than an all-in-one like Kajabi, so you will add a separate marketing tool as you grow.
Pricing: Starter is $29 per month billed annually (with a 7.5% fee) after a 7-day trial, and Builder is $69 with no transaction fee.
Best for: A creator who wants to launch and sell a course quickly and simply.
3. Thinkific
Thinkific is a flexible course platform built for a business that expects to grow, and it charges 0% in transaction fees on every plan. It sits close to Teachable on simplicity but leans further toward customization and scale.
We recommend Thinkific when you want room to expand without switching platforms later. The entry plan includes course building, commerce, and a community, and higher tiers add cohorts, memberships, and B2B selling. Its all-in-one marketing does less than Kajabi's, so a business that runs heavily on email funnels may still want a dedicated marketing tool next to it.
Thinkific pros:
- 0% fees on every plan: Thinkific never takes a cut of a sale, even on the entry tier, so the monthly price is the whole platform cost.
- Built to scale: Cohorts, memberships, and B2B selling arrive on higher tiers, so a growing catalog doesn't force a migration.
Thinkific cons:
- Less marketing automation: The built-in email and funnel tools are thinner than Kajabi's, so a funnel-heavy business bolts on a separate tool.
- Caps on the entry tier: The cheapest plan limits communities and advanced selling, so scaling means moving up.
Pricing: Basic is $40 per month billed annually after a 30-day trial, with Start at $82 and Grow at $164.
Best for: A course business that wants flexibility and room to scale without switching tools.
4. Podia
Podia is the simplest, lowest-cost way to sell your first course, and it doubles as a store for digital downloads. Where Kajabi is a full business suite, Podia is the tool you reach for to get a first product live without a big monthly commitment.
We point early creators and lean teams here. You start on a trial, publish a course or a download, and pay once it makes sense. The catch is the fee on the entry-level paid plan, which takes 5% of each sale until you move up to a plan that removes it.
Podia pros:
- A low-risk start: A low monthly price and a 30-day trial let you publish and see whether anyone buys before you commit real money.
- More than courses: The same tool sells courses, memberships, and digital downloads, so an early creator runs everything from one place.
Podia cons:
- A 5% fee on the Mover plan: That cut is larger than Kajabi's or Thinkific's platform fee, so growing sales are the signal to upgrade to a 0%-fee tier.
- Fewer growth tools: Podia offers less marketing and automation than Kajabi or Thinkific, so a scaling seller will eventually outgrow it.
Pricing: The Mover plan is $42 per month billed annually with a 5% fee, after a 30-day trial, and the fee drops to 0% on the higher Shaker and Earthquaker plans.
Best for: Selling your first course or digital product without a large monthly commitment.
5. LearnWorlds
LearnWorlds is the pick when the course itself has to be advanced, with interactive video, assessments, certificates, and a branded academy you fully control. It is built for training businesses and companies running a customer or partner academy.
We recommend LearnWorlds when learning outcomes matter as much as sales. You get interactive video, graded assessments, SCORM support, and a white-label site and app, which is why training teams and larger course operations choose it.
The tradeoff is the entry plan, whose Starter tier adds a $5 fee per enrollment, so LearnWorlds makes the most sense once you move to a flat-fee plan or run enough volume to justify it.

- The deepest teaching tools: Interactive video, graded assessments, and SCORM support handle structured learning that simpler platforms cannot.
