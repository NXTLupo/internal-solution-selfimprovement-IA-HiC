# IKEA Is Launching 22 Designer Art Objects for the Home

Source: [elledecor](https://www.elledecor.com/design-decorate/a73477124/ikea-launches-limited-edition-art-collection-konstrunda/)

For younger generations of art lovers, becoming a “collector” can feel like a distant fantasy, reserved for the upper echelons of the community. The notoriously high barrier to entry—including the initial time and knowledge it takes to develop artistic preferences—tends to deter newcomers. But this fall, a new collection from Swedish furniture giant IKEA offers an accessible path to having an art-filled home.
Beloved by many for its practical yet stylish approach to design, IKEA just unveiled a line of 22 art objects under the name Konstrunda, meaning “art tour” in Swedish. The pieces, which range in size and form from chrome tabletop trays to a two-by-five-foot rug, are each priced under $300.
IKEA enlisted seven international talents to bring their creative visions to life for the collection. For instance, London-based talent Andu Masebo, a breakout star from the 2023 London Design Festival, adds artistic intrigue to a simple glass side table with the addition of an exaggerated chrome frame. Meanwhile, acclaimed Swedish designer Lisa Hilland’s pine stool, made of individual cylinders of wood joined around a scalloped edge, is a celebration and reimagining of a material often used in IKEA products.
In addition, Stockholm-based Emanuele Stamuli, founder of multidisciplinary architecture studio Stamuli, evokes the playful yet modern forms of Memphis Milano for a mismatched pair of black-and-white striped vases, each of which retail for under $40, as well as a high-contrast wool rug.
“The collection gives you the freedom to buy things based on how they make you feel or how they look, without the burden of needing to serve a specific purpose or function,” Stamuli said in a statement.
While many of the items indeed serve a functional purpose, most make a strong aesthetic statement in their own right. These are objects for decoration and intrigue as much as utility—a departure for a brand with practicality at its core. With the new pieces, IKEA brings “collectible design,” a concept typically associated with fine art and ultra-luxury, into a more approachable realm.
“Art should be available for all to enjoy, not a luxury reserved for galleries,” says Karin Gustavsson, creative leader for the collection. “We wanted to create a collection that encourages people to express themselves and surround themselves with objects they love.”
“At IKEA, we don't claim to define or explain art. Instead, our role is to create space for it,” says Gustavsson.
The limited-edition Konstrunda collection will be available globally both online and in stores starting September 1.
