# The Design Fiend’s Guide to Bentonville, Arkansas

Source: [elledecor](https://www.elledecor.com/life-culture/travel/a73495697/bentonville-arkansas-design-travel-guide/)

If you are neither an art lover nor a mountain biking enthusiast, you may not be familiar with Bentonville, Arkansas. Or maybe you recognize it as the headquarters of the retail behemoth Walmart. But this city in the northwest corner of the state is thriving—its population has nearly doubled since 2010—and visitor numbers are surging.
The engine driving Bentonville’s rising profile is, of course, Walmart and the clan behind it, the Waltons. Multiple family members rank among the wealthiest people in America, and they have been active in funneling some of those riches back into their hometown.
Undoubtedly the most impactful contribution has been the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art, spearheaded by Alice Walton. Opened in 2011, the museum is the centerpiece of a 134-acre campus that also includes the Heartland Whole Health Institute, the Alice L. Walton School of Medicine, and the independent Scott Family Amazeum. Architect Moshe Safdie oversaw Crystal Bridges’s original design—a series of pavilions that border and “bridge” across a water-filled ravine—as well as the recent expansion that added 114,000 square feet of galleries, a learning hub for kids, and art-making studios.
All of which means more space for a collection that spans five centuries, with highlights ranging from Hudson River School masterworks to important examples of American craft to standout contemporary works. Outdoors, pathways lacing the wooded grounds are dotted with sculptures and installations. For anyone interested in art and design, the museum is now a major destination.
“It’s hard to overstate how catalytic Crystal Bridges has been for Bentonville,” said Olivia Walton, Alice’s daughter-in-law and the museum’s board chair, at a recent talk. “It’s all snowballing off the spark created by Crystal Bridges.”
These days Bentonville offers everything from contemporary art galleries to farm-to-table feasts, to, yes, plenty of bike trails. And infusing it all is a sense of dynamism and creativity. “I love the diversity of the design community in Bentonville,” says Elizabeth Abston, owner of the popular shop Hemma Homewares. “You have everything from incredible interior designers to builders to muralists to sculptors. This breadth of design, to me, creates an inclusive environment: one that welcomes trying out something new, taking a risk, and working towards putting your vision out in the open.”
What to See and Do
As noted, there are two main types of tourists to Bentonville: those who come for the art and those who come for the mountain biking. Occasionally, the two overlap, as with the bikeable paths around the Crystal Bridges Museum, which are a great way to see outdoor sculptures and installations, including a freestanding James Turrell Skyspace and a 30-foot-tall Louise Bourgeois spider.
To be clear, these are not challenging mountain-bike trails but mostly paved, low-grade paths that can be tackled at a leisurely pace, though there are options for more advanced riders around Bentonville. One of the most scenic areas is Coler Mountain Bike Preserve, which has trails designed by Ecological Design Group that include natural play spaces near the stream, as well as a Brutalist-inspired coffee shop, Airship, designed by Modus Studio that’s only accessible via the trails. Riding over the Singing Bridge, a sound installation designed by artist Craig Colorusso, should also not be missed. The area is a favorite of Austen Barron Bailly, deputy director of curatorial affairs at Crystal Bridges. “Coler Preserve is where enlightened landscape architecture, trail design, artful bridges, and even soundscapes make design an integral part of the experience of being in nature,” she says.
After a morning bike ride is a perfect time to explore the town and its shops, starting in the historic central square, which hosts a lively farmer’s market on Saturdays, April through October. An essential stop is the Walmart Museum, which charts the rise of the big-box retailer from its origins as a local shop, and also features a colorful collection of historical items from the brand’s history. Its gift shop, a modernized five-and-dime, is also an excellent place to pick up old-timey souvenirs and Walmart-branded merch.
Just off the square, Marrs on Main, owned by HGTV stars Dave and Jenny Marrs, offers housewares, design books, a line of bespoke candles, and a few curated antique furniture pieces. Nearby Hemma Homewares sells a tightly edited collection of linens, pantry staples, soaps and more, all inside a charming bungalow. The focus at All Things Kindred is flowers and tropical plants, but you’ll also find gadgets and gifts for the home and garden. Once Upon a Time Books carries a robust selection of vintage titles for all ages, while Block Street Records is the perfect spot to peruse additions to your vinyl collection.
Lastly, if you find yourself in Bentonville the first Thursday through Saturday of the month, make the 15-minute drive from town to the former autobody space where the design shop Field Supply does a regular pop-up. Operated by the mother-daughter duo Lizzie Duncan and Emily Petrick, the space is a wonderland of antique furniture, vintage jeans, and art framed by Duncan herself.
As for Crystal Bridges, give yourself ample time to explore the permanent collection of over 500 works (look out for everything from Mary Cassatt to Dale Chihuly to Keith Haring), as well as the rotating exhibitions, and the outdoor areas. “One of the most surreal Bentonville experiences is walking the Crystal Bridges art path,” says Petrick. “One moment you’re ambling through the woods on a paved walking trail and the next, you’re stumbling upon a Yayoi Kusama sculpture visible through the trees.”
It’s also worth checking out the impressive architecture elsewhere on the campus. “Our new medical school building grows up out of the landscape and has a green roof you can walk straight onto from the forest trail outside,” says Olivia Walton. “We call the style Ozark rustic modern—buildings that feel carved out of this place rather than dropped into it.”
Another destination worth making time for is The Momentary, a mixed-use cultural space affiliated with Crystal Bridges. The decommissioned Kraft cheese factory hosts concerts, rotating exhibits in its art gallery spaces, and performances in its black box theater. “You can still feel that history in the bones of the building—the pipes in the ceiling, the boiler tanks still exposed,” says Walton. “You can come for a concert, stay for a craft cocktail, bring a date and walk through an art exhibition.” And don’t miss the complex’s top-floor Tower Bar, a modern drinks spot offering some of the best views of downtown Bentonville.
Where to Eat and Drink
Like so many southern cities, Bentonville does elevated farm-to-table very well and nowhere more so than at Ryn, which is located on a small working farm a short drive outside the city center. Helmed by chef Matthew Cooper, it offers a seasonal tasting menu that might include potato pancakes or flash-fried carrots prepared with ingredients grown in the restaurant’s own gardens. “Chef Cooper and his team have created a space that feels so much a part of the land and the community that it's hard to believe it hasn’t always been there,” says Petrick. “You can feel the thought, time, and passion that goes into the food with every bite.”
Near the town square, The Preacher’s Son occupies a converted 1890s church, where the soaring sanctuary-turned-dining room is arrayed with art curated by OZ Art NWA, a local organization that places art in unexpected locations. As for the menu, expect refined takes on American cuisine, like a pork porterhouse with blue cheese butter. It also has two bars, one on the rooftop that’s perfect for capturing pictures of the iconic Bentonville water tower and one in the basement that offers a speakeasy vibe and hosts live jazz.
Another speakeasy-like spot is the tropical-themed Callisto, a favorite of Jasmine Hudson, a co-founder of the Bentonville gift accessories company Black Paper Party: “It’s my favorite hidden gem, tucked inside the Midnight Gallery. You walk through the art gallery and a secret door opens into a completely transporting little world, which is such a perfect Bentonville idea—art and hospitality folded into one.”
For a low-key drop-in bite, Crepes Paulette is a go-to for All Things Kindred owner Samantha Daniels: “It’s one of my favorite casual dining experiences,” she says. Aside from classic combinations like ham and cheddar, there are also more unique variations like a Thai chicken with peanut sauce.
And any roundup of Bentonville food and drink would not be complete without mentioning Onyx Coffee Lab, whose location just off the square, featuring a 360-degree bar, is the closest thing this city has to a clubhouse. Coffee snobs, take note: Onyx’s flagship in nearby Rogers was voted best coffee shop in the world.
Where to Stay
For a city of just over 60,000, Bentonville punches above its weight when it comes to hotels. One of the first properties to signal the city’s rising profile as an arts-focused destination was the 21c Museum Hotel Bentonville, just a few minutes’ walk from the square. Like its sister properties, the Bentonville outpost contains a free contemporary museum complete with rotating exhibitions, as well as curated art on each floor, and prints from multi-disciplinary artist Tiffany Calvert in the guest rooms. The hotel’s restaurant, The Hive, is a popular spot for breakfast, not least for its flaky, buttery biscuits paired with sorghum butter and seasonal jams.
The latest addition, just across the square, is The Compton, whose spaces are defined by a natural palette of warm woods and stone. Already the hotel seems woven into the fabric of the city, thanks to its sunny, casual all-day café, Field Notes, which hums with visitors and locals alike, while its elegant Tuscan steakhouse Sestina is a lively spot for dinner.
