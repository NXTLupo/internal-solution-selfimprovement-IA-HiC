# How to Make a Small Bathroom Feel Bigger, According to Interior Designers

Source: [elledecor](https://www.elledecor.com/design-decorate/interior-designers/a73474923/how-to-make-a-small-bathroom-feel-bigger/)

A small bathroom can be challenging, but it doesn’t have to feel like an afterthought. A few strategic choices—from swapping in a glass shower door to choosing a properly proportioned vanity—can make even the tightest space feel more open. Below, designers share their best advice for making a small bathroom look bigger.
Don’t be afraid of drama
The old prescription for a small bathroom (white walls, little contrast, no risk taking) can make a fall space feel more forgettable than expansive. Michelle Squire, founder and principal of Studio SQUIRE, encourages the opposite approach: “Don’t be afraid of drama.”
The walls are an especially effective place to start. “Opt for a beautiful patterned wallpaper that creates a totally immersive experience, or go with a rich paint color in a high-gloss finish that feels dramatic while naturally reflecting light,” Squire says. Her one caveat: “Keep the ceiling painted in a light, airy color that contrasts with the walls; this will naturally draw the eye upward and make the room feel taller.”
Ditch the shower curtain
A shower curtain may be useful, but in a tight bathroom, it can visually bisect the room. “A tip for making a bathroom feel larger is to add a frameless glass shower enclosure instead of a curtain or framed door,” Squire says. “This allows your eye to travel the whole width of the room instead of stopping at the barrier.”
Kathy Kuo, designer and founder of Kathy Kuo Home, agrees: “Anything opaque visually cuts off a portion of the room, while clear glass allows for seamless sight lines throughout the entire space.”
Run the same tile into the shower
In a small bath, too many tile patterns or material changes can make the eye stop—and make the room feel chopped up. Instead, think in extended planes.
“Continuing the same floor tile into the shower pan does the same thing,” Squire says of the frameless-glass strategy. “It removes the visual ‘stop’ that makes a room read as small.”
Emily Spanos, creative director of Emily June Designs, also recommends letting tile carry the eye through a narrow space: “Using the same floor tile in the shower can allow for an unbroken visual line in a tiny footprint, without feeling overwhelming.” Keep the idea tailored to the space, she adds: “Tile can also be used to draw the eye to a focal point or highlight the most ‘open’ area in a narrow bath.”
Install an oversized mirror and vanity sconces
A generously scaled mirror can reflect light, expand a vanity wall, and lend even the smallest bath a more gracious sense of proportion. “In a bathroom with a tiny footprint, the larger the mirror, the better!” Kuo says. “Choose a mirror that runs the full length of your vanity or even the full width of the room if you really want to pack a punch.”
Then, sidestep the common one-light-fits-all approach. “Most small bathrooms rely on one flush-mount overhead fixture, which flattens the room and casts shadows that make it feel tighter,” Squire says. “Adding sconces at eye level, flanking the mirror, changes the whole feel of the space almost instantly.”
Choose a narrow or floating vanity
A bulky vanity can undo the work of an otherwise thoughtful small-bath scheme. “A vanity that is too deep or too wide for the room eats the floor clearance you need to actually feel the room open up,” Squire says. “I’d rather see a narrower vanity with a smart internal storage than a bulky one that closes in the walkway.”
Anabella Mainetti, founder of luxury tiny home builders Mainefactured, favors a floating vanity where possible: “Exposing the floor beneath tricks the brain into registering more square footage than exists.” Her larger point is worth remembering before finalizing any renovation: “In a small space, every fixture should be scaled down and pushed towards the walls to maximize open floor space in the center.”
