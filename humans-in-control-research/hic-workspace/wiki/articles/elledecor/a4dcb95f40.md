# How to Make a Galley Kitchen Feel Bigger, According to Designers

Source: [elledecor](https://www.elledecor.com/design-decorate/interior-designers/a73455654/how-to-make-a-galley-kitchen-feel-wider/)

If you’ve ever prepped for a dinner party, attempted an ambitious recipe, or rushed through your morning coffee ritual in a galley kitchen, you know that overcrowding the space is a recipe for frustration. But while common experiences—like running out of counter space or colliding with your cooking partner—may give compact kitchen blueprints a bad rap, there are also plenty of reasons to love a space-saving layout. With thoughtful design and intentional organization, even the tightest galley kitchen can feel both functional and breathable.
Despite their constrictive reputation in homes, the galley kitchen format was designed to maximize efficiency in a limited cooking space—like those found on boats, planes, and trains. Traditionally, a narrow run of open floor space is flanked by two walls of appliances, storage, and counters. “The biggest challenge is avoiding a corridor-like feeling while still providing generous prep space, efficient appliance placement, and enough room for multiple people to comfortably use the kitchen,” says Jess Nahon, designer and co-principal of Sugarhouse Design + Architecture.
The good news? Square footage isn’t the sole determinant of how spacious a galley kitchen feels. In addition to a well-planned layout and smart storage, Nahon says that design elements like lighting, palette, and material choices can “help create a calmer, more cohesive environment that feels larger than its actual dimensions.”
Ahead, we asked designers to share their best tips for how to make a galley kitchen feel bigger.
Separate Sides by Function
When installing your appliances and organizing kitchenware, consider the choreography of your own cooking habits: the space will feel less cramped if it follows your flow. “Custom millwork allows us to tailor storage around the client’s daily routines so frequently used items remain accessible, while less essential items are concealed,” Nahon says.
Not sure where to start? Break your organization down by sides of the kitchen. “I would keep one wall heavier on storage and appliances, and have the sink and dishwasher on the other, with less upper cabinetry and more open space to stand and prep,” says Berkshires-based designer Jess Cooney.
If remodeling is an option, Cooney recommends adding a cutout space on the less storage-heavy wall. A sightline into a dining area or connected space can help the kitchen feel less siloed.
Keep Countertops Spare
When things start feeling cramped in a galley kitchen, cutting down on visual clutter is a quick fix. Be selective and intentional about which items live on the countertops, and invest in hidden storage that makes for an easy sweep. “Using coordinated storage solutions inside cabinets and drawers, and replacing bulky accessories with integrated alternatives can dramatically reduce visual noise,” Nahon says.
Integrate Surrounding Design Elements
A galley kitchen typically seems tighter if it feels like a starkly different space than the surrounding living and dining areas. “We like to think of a galley kitchen as an extension of the surrounding architecture rather than a standalone room,” Nahon says. As a first line of defense, she recommends choosing stained or colored cabinetry over utilitarian white Shaker cabinets, which automatically signal ‘kitchen.’
For this Upper West Side galley kitchen, Nahon selected a minimal palette, continuous marble surfaces, and cabinetry that mimicked the existing architecture. This intentional cohesion “allows the eye to travel uninterrupted” through the kitchen into the rest of the home, and vice versa.
Layer Your Lighting
Multiple thoughtful light sources can add depth and dimension to any tight space. In a galley kitchen, Nahon recommends opting for decorative sconces, concealed architectural lighting, or subtle ceiling fixtures over more predictable under-cabinet lighting. Soft, integrated, and intentional-looking fixtures help the kitchen feel better integrated with the rest of the home.
Choose a Light, Airy Palette
Color scheme and surface material can help give a galley kitchen more breathing room. “A restrained palette with natural materials tends to create the most timeless result,” Nahon says, referencing warm neutral cabinetry, light marble, and unlacquered brass accents. While neutral-leaning, these airier choices still leave space for expression: “We also like incorporating subtle moments of character, whether through handcrafted details, vintage pieces, or decorative glass, so the kitchen feels layered and personal while remaining light and breathable.”
According to Cooney, palette uniformity can also create the illusion of more space: “Keep the colors of the cabinets and walls and counters all the same, or subtle variations of each other.” Choosing a paint with a high-gloss finish that allows light to “bounce around” can add even more openness, she says.
