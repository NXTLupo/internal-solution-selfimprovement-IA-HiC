# An A-List Designer's Guide to Creating a Luxury Bathroom

Source: [elledecor](https://www.elledecor.com/design-decorate/interior-designers/a71941916/an-a-list-designers-guide-to-creating-a-luxury-bathroom/)

Large slabs of marble. Deep soaking tubs. Walk-in showers. These are the types of features that usually come to mind when you think about a luxurious bathroom. But according to A-List designer Delia Kenza, creating a luxe bath starts with the atmosphere and tactile experience: “It’s about the feeling you want to create,” she says.
Once you establish your design vision—whether it's a serene spa escape or a sophisticated space for daily rituals—the fixtures and finishes become the foundation for everything that follows. Ultimately, the materials you choose aren't simply about the colors—they determine how light will move around the room.
That philosophy is especially evident in GROHE SPA Collections, where finish isn’t simply decorative, but rather a design tool. “I think about finishes in regards to how I want the light to bounce off of them,” explains Kenza. “How reflective should they be? Do I want them to stand out in the space, or do I want them to recede into the background?”
A polished surface can amplify light and energy while a more muted material lends a subdued feel. “If it’s more of a spa experience, I might not use something that’s so shiny,” says Kenza. “I’ll choose something that’s a little bit more toned down so that it blends with the environment.”
Here, Kenza shares how to build a bathroom around GROHE SPA fixtures in four popular finishes, each with a distinct mood.
Polished Chrome: Modern Radiance
Polished chrome is a classic for a reason: It adds an element of brightness and works well in contemporary interiors. “I use polished chrome when I want a little more sparkle,” says Kenza. “The light really bounces off of it nicely.”
She likes to pair it with expressive materials like dramatic marble, terrazzo, exposed concrete, and natural stone, where the reflective finish contrasts with the organic surfaces. The effect works well with the elegant curves of the GROHE SPA Atrio Private Collection, which features knobs that can be elevated with Caesarstone inserts.
“The high arc is such a classic faucet shape,” she notes, “but you don’t always see it in the bathroom,” which is precisely what makes it luxurious. “If you use something that’s a little unexpected, the shape is a luxury.”
Brushed Nickel: Quiet Luxury
Brushed nickel is an understated choice. It quietly supports the overall aesthetic without demanding attention. “Brushed nickel is kind of silent,” says Kenza. “It recesses into the background.”
That subtle quality makes it a natural fit for serene, spa-inspired bathrooms layered with Venetian plaster walls, seamless surfaces, and softly veined marble. To reinforce the calming mood, she gravitates toward rich, saturated hues like deep forest green or navy blue. “I love to see a brushed nickel more in a space where the colors have a mood to them.”
The GROHE SPA Allure Collection, with its slender architectural silhouettes, works particularly well with this less-is-more approach. The wall-mounted design keeps the vanity clean and uncluttered, while lending a sense of intention to the room. “It makes the bathroom look more thought out,” Kenza says.
Matte Black: Depth & Drama
When done right, matte black feels less like a trend and more like timeless sophistication. Rather than juxtaposing it against light colors, Kenza suggests leaning into the drama and pairing black fixtures with richly veined black marble to create a layered monochrome space. “There are a lot of nuances to black,” she says. “It’s not flat. It creates a really moody, sexy ambiance.” For those who want a similarly bold look with added depth, darker metallic finishes like GROHE’s Hard Graphite and Brushed Hard Graphite offer a softer, smoky alternative that complements the same rich, tonal palette.
But not every fixture needs to match. Kenza encourages mixing metals to create a more bespoke look. She'll often specify one finish for the faucets, bath, and shower fixtures, and another for towel hooks, cabinet hardware, or accessories. “If you mix metals in a very thoughtful way, it looks more intentional, like something that's been collected over time.” The result is a bathroom that feels curated rather than perfectly coordinated.
Brushed Brass: Soft Luminosity
Rather than making a statement, brushed brass creates softness. Kenza gravitates toward the finish when designing bathrooms with an intimate, inviting atmosphere, pairing it with earthy neutrals and peachy undertones. “When I’m creating spaces where I want a softness, I like gold tones very much.”
GROHE SPA offers two interpretations of brushed brass—the yellow-gold Brushed Cool Sunrise and the rose-tinted bronze Brushed Warm Sunset—both of which work beautifully with warm, natural lighting. It’s an important consideration, Kenza says, because everyone wants a bathroom that makes them look and feel their best.
“We want to spend time in places where we look good,” says Kenza. “Your bathroom should be beautiful, and you should want to spend time in it.” You can reinforce that feeling by carefully considering all of the things you touch in your bathroom—the towels, bath mats, soap, even your toothbrush—and opting for elevated versions of these everyday essentials.
Ultimately, luxury isn’t about creating a room that's too precious to use; it’s about designing one that effortlessly supports daily rituals. “It’s not luxurious if it’s not practical,” says Kenza. “Anytime you can use materials that create ease, that’s luxury.”
