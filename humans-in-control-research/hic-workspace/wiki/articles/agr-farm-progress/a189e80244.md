# 10 farm inventions that built a nation over 250 years

Source: [agr_farm_progress](https://www.farmprogress.com/farming-equipment/10-farm-inventions-that-built-a-nation-over-250-years)

The year was 1794 — about 18 years since Thomas Jefferson scrawled his name on the Declaration of Independence and 11 since the Treaty of Paris ended the Revolutionary War. Having recently resigned as President George Washington’s secretary of state, Jefferson, a botanist and innovator, sought to create a tool for tilling fallow land at his farm in Monticello, Va.
Jefferson had conceptualized a wooden plow, or moldboard, that could easily lift and flip heavy dirt. The idea came to him while serving as the new nation’s ambassador to France. Now, in his free time, he dug wood into soil to test his invention. His plow proved successful, but he did not patent the design. Instead, he detailed its exact measurements and angles in a 1799 publication of the American Philosophical Society.
Eventually, Jefferson’s step-by-step directions would be perfected with polished steel by a little-known blacksmith named John Deere, and the rest is history.
Tech alters society
Over America’s 250-year history, ag tech innovations like the plow have not only changed the way farmers work but also created profound social consequences.
For example, after Eli Whitney invented the cotton gin in 1793, it added fuel to a dark era of slavery by making cotton so profitable that demand for enslaved labor exploded.
By contrast, the mechanical cotton harvester, introduced in the 1940s, displaced thousands of farm laborers and sparked a massive migration to urban centers. As machines increased productivity, fewer workers were needed. In 1900, 40% of Americans lived on farms. Today, less than 2% do, a shift driven by decades of agricultural mechanization.
Through increased agricultural production, machinery helped fuel the economic growth that made America a global superpower. As the nation marks its semiquincentennial on July 4, ag machinery manufacturers continue to push the boundaries of farm innovation.
“For nearly 200 years, John Deere has been a leader in innovation and manufacturing, helping our fellow Americans grow and build this country,” said Mara Downing, vice president of corporate communications and brand management at John Deere in a statement about America’s 250th anniversary.
“Being part of this celebration of agriculture is a proud moment for our team and for the farmers we serve,” said Scott Harris, president of CNH Industrial for North America, about the brand’s special-edition patriotic Magnum tractor. “Built in Racine since 1988, the Magnum tractor reflects the strength of American manufacturing and the performance farmers depend on every day. We are committed to supporting U.S. agriculture with world-class equipment.”
