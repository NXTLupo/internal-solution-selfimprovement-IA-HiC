# Young jobseekers squeezed out by older, overqualified candidates

Source: [exs_personnel_today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/young-jobseekers-squeezed-out-by-older-overqualified-candidates-cmi-research/)

Early-career jobseekers are increasingly being squeezed out of entry-level roles by older, often overqualified applicants competing for the same limited pool of jobs, research has suggested.
The polling for the Chartered Management Institute (CMI) found almost a third of UK managers (32%) report an increase in the number of more experienced workers applying for junior or entry-level positions. The biggest surge has come from 41 to 50-year-olds (48%), followed by 31 to 40-year-olds (44%).
The finding adds a further layer of complexity to the challenge the country, government and employers all face to support increasing numbers of young people not in education, employment and training (Neets) into the workplace.

It also runs counter to the call in June by former health secretary and author of the review into young people and work, Alan Milburn, that employers need to be prioritising new starters more than existing employees for opportunities.
The CMI research, of 1,050 managers, found that this influx of more experienced talent is making it harder for young people to gain elusive first roles.
Where early-career hiring has dropped, 23% of managers admitted to having an explicit preference for experienced candidates.
One in five (20%) pointed directly to the automation of entry-level roles and tasks by AI tools as a reason for needing fewer new recruits.
Managers also identified a lack of workplace readiness in younger workers, with 18% citing shortcomings in communication skills and resilience as factors working against hiring young people.
Petra Wilton, CMI director of policy and external affairs, said: “These findings tell us that young people are not leaving education and starting out in their working lives with the self-management skills they need to stand out in their job search, including communication and a clear understanding of the workplace.
“Employers also need to think long and hard about hiring over-qualified people for entry-level jobs as they are unlikely to stick around, and they’ll find themselves in a recruitment loop that is both time-consuming and expensive. Skilled managers know that hiring and training the next generation of leaders is not just the right thing to do, it is also an investment in succession planning for any business,” she added.
