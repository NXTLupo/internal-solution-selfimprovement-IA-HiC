# Mental strain increasing for employees, suggests study

Source: [exs_personnel_today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/headspace-mental-health-research-strain-increasing-for-employees/)

One in three UK employees at large organisations feel emotionally ‘checked out’ at work, distrust their leaders and believe their mental health needs are not being met.
According to research by mental health app firm Headspace, 41% of employees at organisations with more than 1,000 people have said their mental or cognitive strain increased over the past year.
The biggest reported driver of strain, cited by 39% of employees, was unclear or shifting priorities, followed by job insecurity at 36%. The pace of organisational change was cited by 27%, while 24% pointed to new technologies such as AI.
Among employees affected by organisational change or new technologies, found the company’s 2026 Workforce State of Mind report, 60% had experienced restructuring or layoffs, 55% reported budget cuts and 53% had used new technologies such as AI and automation.
Lisa Mulrooney Gross, chief people officer at Headspace, said: “Many organisations are struggling to navigate an unpredictable business market, while employees are absorbing constant change without a clear roadmap and weakening trust in leadership.”
She added: “UK employees aren’t asking for less change, but rather more clarity and support while they navigate it.”
The effects of workplace strain were also being felt outside work. Nearly 65% of UK employees said it had affected their ability to be present with family and friends at least once in the past year.
Sleep was particularly affected, with 76% saying strain had negatively affected their sleep. Some 74% said mental or cognitive strain had negatively affected their ability to focus and their productivity, while 47% said it had compromised their judgment when using AI and new tools.
The report also found a gap in workplace resilience support. Some 64% of UK employees had not received a single hour of resilience, stress management or change management training in the past year.
Only 8% said their organisation fully met their mental health needs.
AI adoption was widespread, with 53% of UK employees reporting they had adopted AI and other new technologies during the past year. However, 32% did not trust the output of the technology and 28% feared it could ultimately displace them.
Nearly a quarter (23%) said they did not understand how to use the new tools appropriately, while 21% pointed to poor performance or inconsistent availability. A further 17% feared new tools would increase their workload rather than reduce it.
Headspace chief clinical officer Jenna Glover said resilience should be viewed as an organisational capacity rather than an individual sacrifice.
“In the era of chronic strain, resilience is about staying steady when the pressure never fully lifts,” she said. “The organisations that invest in resilience are both supporting their people and protecting their ability to perform over time.”
The report recommends organisations provide more accessible mental health support, use managers to help employees navigate change, communicate transparently where there is uncertainty, and track participation in wellbeing and skills training.
The research was based on a YouGov online survey of 513 UK full-time employees aged 18 and over working for companies with more than 1,000 employees. Data was collected between 4 and 8 June 2026.
