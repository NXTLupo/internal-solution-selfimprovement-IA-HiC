# GUEST POST Why retailers misunderstanding Gen Z are becoming culturally disposable

Source: [internet_retailing_uk](https://internetretailing.net/guest-post-why-retailers-misunderstanding-gen-z-are-becoming-culturally-disposable/)

Richard Jackson, head of content at Pion, looks at why retailers are failing to build meaningful connections with the key Gen Z demographic.
There is a dangerous myth dominating retail and marketing: that Gen Z wants less engagement with brands. They don’t. What they want is less friction between inspiration and action. In the race to make commerce instant, brands have started confusing speed with relevance. The result is a generation of highly visible but emotionally forgettable brands.
Platforms like TikTok Shop have trained consumers to expect discovery, validation and purchase to happen almost simultaneously. A creator mentions a product, comments validate it, social proof builds and checkout happens instantly. From a conversion perspective, it is a dream.
From a brand-building perspective, however, it creates a dilemma: the easier commerce becomes, the easier brands become to replace. Modern shopping is optimised for transactions, not attachment. But Gen Z doesn’t build loyalty transactionally. They build it emotionally, socially and culturally.
Platforms own the relationship
For years, retailers invested in owning the customer journey. Today, platforms increasingly own it. TikTok determines what gets discovered, algorithms decide what gets amplified and social commerce platforms influence what converts. Brands are increasingly renting attention inside someone else’s ecosystem.
The old retail model rewarded consistency. Brands built awareness over time, created aspiration through campaigns and nurtured consideration through repetition and trust. The new model rewards immediacy: Can the content stop the scroll? Can the product sell in seconds?
The problem is that many businesses have become so focused on conversion efficiency that they are weakening the emotional depth that makes consumers care. Retail has entered an era where products are increasingly discoverable, purchasable and forgettable. Brands mistaking visibility for loyalty are sleepwalking into irrelevance.
Gen Z didn’t abandon the marketing funnel. They rebuilt it
The industry loves declaring the marketing funnel dead. It isn’t. It has simply collapsed into itself. Discovery, consideration, validation and purchase can now happen almost simultaneously.
That doesn’t mean Gen Z has stopped wanting trust, belonging or emotional connection. It means the sources of that validation have changed. Today, they come from creators, peer recommendations, comment sections, user-generated content, cultural participation and real-world experiences.
This is where many brands misunderstand Gen Z. They assume speed has replaced storytelling. It hasn’t. Gen Z still wants stories, but they want stories that feel socially verified. The most effective brands don’t simply advertise products. They create environments people want to participate in. Culture has become the new conversion funnel.
Performance alone isn’t enough
Every product is now one swipe away from comparison. Consumers can instantly assess price, aesthetics, social proof and cultural relevance. That makes brand meaning more important, not less. Yet many companies are responding by becoming even more transactional: louder, faster, trend-driven and permanently reactive. The result is a wave of brands chasing visibility while eroding distinctiveness.
Gen Z is particularly good at spotting performative marketing. They know when a brand genuinely understands its audience and when it is simply trying to hijack relevance. That is why some supposedly “old-fashioned” brands continue to outperform digitally native competitors. They behave like participants in culture rather than brands observing it from the outside.
Community, humour, physical presence and emotional familiarity all create something performance marketing cannot: attachment. Consumers remember how brands make them feel long after they forget the transaction.
The future belongs to retailers that create relevance
The challenge facing brands is no longer simply creating awareness. It is creating immediate emotional clarity. Can consumers quickly understand who you are, what you stand for, why you matter and whether you belong in their world?
In frictionless commerce, brands have less time to establish that relevance. That doesn’t make branding less important. It makes it more important. The retailers that win over the next decade won’t necessarily have the biggest media budgets or fastest checkouts. They will be the ones capable of compressing storytelling into culturally resonant moments, using cultural intelligence, creator fluency, experiential thinking and community-building across physical and digital environments.
This is also why physical experiences are becoming more valuable. Despite living increasingly digital lives, Gen Z still craves real-world connection: live activations, university events, immersive retail and shared experiences.
Physical isn’t replacing digital. It is becoming more valuable because emotional connection is harder to create in purely algorithmic environments.
The real risk isn’t friction. It’s forgettability
Marketing leaders are asking the wrong question. The challenge is no longer how to reduce friction. Platforms have already solved that. The real challenge is how brands remain meaningful in an environment designed to make everything interchangeable.
Gen Z expects instant purchasing, validation and gratification. But speed doesn’t create loyalty. Meaning does. The brands that win won’t be those optimising hardest for conversion. They’ll be those that understand how Gen Z forms genuine connection: through culture, community, identity and participation. Because when every product is instantly accessible, the scarcest thing a brand can create is emotional relevance.
