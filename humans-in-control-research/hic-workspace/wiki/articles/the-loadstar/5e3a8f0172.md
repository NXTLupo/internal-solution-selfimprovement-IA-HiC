Spot rates in the US trucking sector rose sharply in the second quarter and are on course to climb further , with the looming peak season auguring more upward pressure, according to RXO.
The transportation provider is advising shippers to secure capacity, which continues to shrink.
The Curve, RXO’s proprietary truckload spot rate index, climbed to its highest level in more than four years, propelled by a 32.4% surge in pricing over Q2 25, a blistering acceleration from the 16.5% annual increase seen in the first quarter.
It was the ninth consecutive quarter of spot rate increases and the highest annual growth rate since the second quarter of 2021.
Figures through 21 August indicate that the trend is continuing in the third quarter, which could show a year-on-year increase of over 40%, said RXO.
The acceleration in truckload pricing is driven by shrinkage in capacity, the result of Washington’s campaign to weed out non-domiciled holders of commercial driver licences and drivers with insufficient command of English, noted Jared Weisfeld, RXO’s chief strategy officer.
Enforcement has been high in recent months, but there is still considerable shrinkage ahead. The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Association estimates that the campaign will push nearly 200,000 drivers out of the market – about 5% of all active interstate licence-holders.
According to RXO, this capacity reduction “represents the biggest structural change to the US carrier market since industry deregulation in 1980”.
Mr Weisfeld pointed to the Logistics Manager’s Index, which shows a 2.4% reduction in capacity, to a reading of 28.4 for July, the second-fastest rated of contraction in the history of the index, behind September 2020.
He stressed that pricing, on the other hand, had risen on the index, to a reading of 86.9, adding: “That spread is an all-time wide. It speaks to the fragility of the market.”
Demand, on the other hand, remains muted. In year-on-year comparisons the Cass Index continued its downward trend, with a 3% drop year on year in the second quarter, worsening to a 5% decline in July.
Equally concerning for shippers is the gap between contract and spot rates, the latter outpacing contract rates. Whereas spot pricing jumped 32.4% year on year in Q2, contract rates were 6% higher, up from a 2.4% year-on-year increase in Q1.
This widening gap is putting increased strain on shippers’ routing guides, said Corey Klujsza, RXO’s VP of pricing and procurement. “That trend is not only continuing, but picking up steam as we head into peak season,” he added.
The prospect of increased volumes pouring into the system as peak season gets under way points to increased upward pricing momentum.
“We’re already in a fragile shipping environment, and any increase in demand will drive rates and volatility even higher,” Mr Weisfeld warned.
He noted that recent figures from the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach indicated rising traffic, adding: “The ingredients are there for a healthy peak, but it’s too early to make predictions.”
Orders for new trucks are on the rise after a long spell of stagnation, but Mr Weisfeld cautioned against reading this as a sign of expanding capacity. A lot of these orders are to replace equipment that was kept going longer than intended as the downturn lasted, he said.
“Companies are still reluctant to expand their fleet as margins are still low,” he added.
Despite the steep ascent of rates, truckers are still facing headwinds on their margins as operating costs have risen across the board, from insurance to tyres – before the increased fuel costs are factored in, he noted. Compared with the previous peak, the costs of operating a truck are nearly 30% higher.
“Profitability is still challenged,” he said. “Many carriers are still under considerable pressure.”
Mr Klujsza noted that truckers were engaging in ‘mini bids’ to secure volume, but were keeping back capacity for the spot market. Recently at 13%, tender rejections are at their highest level since 2022 and are expected to rise further.
“It’s going to be important to secure capacity,” Mr Weisfeld advised.
This may be a challenging undertaking in the cross-border trucking market at Laredo, which vies with the port of Los Angeles for the crown of leading US gateway for imports.
According to Uber Freight, a shortage of drivers that hold valid B-1 temporary visitor visas to the US is exacerbating a capacity squeeze in the northbound market that has been fuelled by rising volumes heading into the US. It is further aggravated by an imbalance in trade flows, which sees northbound traffic outstripping shipments in the opposite direction, making it challenging for carriers to manage.
With the driver pool stretched to handle current volumes, amid reports of more difficulties obtaining B-1 visas, improvement of the situation appears unlikely in the near term. Uber Freight advises customers that it may be better to transload freight in Laredo, but most customers are not enthusiastic, citing elevated costs as well as risk of damage or theft in the process.
