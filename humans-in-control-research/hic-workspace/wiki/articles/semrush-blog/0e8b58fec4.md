# 234 ChatGPT prompts (and how to write your own)

Source: [semrush_blog](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-prompts/)

The best ChatGPT prompts give you a starting point for almost any task. Fill in the brackets, run the prompt, adjust the output. For most everyday work, that's enough.
What a library can't give you is a way to build prompts from scratch when your task doesn't match a template, or to strengthen one when the output isn't quite right.
Those decisions come down to what you include: relevant context, what the output should look like, and which of your materials the response should draw on.
Below, you'll find a short set of versatile prompts to try first, a framework for writing your own, and the full library of 234 prompts organized by role, each ready to copy and adapt.
What are ChatGPT prompts?
A ChatGPT prompt is the input you give ChatGPT to communicate what you want it to do. It can include a question, instruction, or goal, along with text, files, images, data, source material, or examples of the result you want.
A prompt can be as simple as:
Summarize this client’s Q3 performance.
Or it can be more detailed:
Summarize this client’s Q3 performance for their executive team. Compare the results against these Q3 targets: [paste targets]. Use their organic traffic, lead volume, and conversion rate data: [paste data]. Match the structure and length of last quarter’s approved report: [paste example].
The second prompt supplies details specific to this task: the audience, performance targets, source data, and an approved reference format.
Think of ChatGPT as a new coworker who has broad, industry-level knowledge but lacks the context specific to your company. The context and materials you provide shape that knowledge into a response you can use.
The PROMPT framework below shows how to create your own prompts, which you can then adapt to fit your task requirements.

Best ChatGPT prompts to try first
The prompts below cover common ways people use ChatGPT: summarizing information, organizing work, evaluating ideas, learning new topics, and communicating clearly.
Writing and communication
- Extract the takeaway from a long document
Read the document below and extract its most important takeaway for [audience].
Keep the response under [word count] words and include:
- Bottom line: The main conclusion or implication in one sentence. State if the document reaches no clear conclusion.
- Key findings: The three to five points essential to understanding it.
- Implications: The decisions, risks, or opportunities the reader should consider.
- Essential evidence: Important numbers, examples, or supporting facts.
- Uncertainties: Assumptions, limitations, or unresolved questions.
- Next step: The clearest action, if supported by the document.
Prioritize implications over background. Preserve important names and numbers, use only the document, and label interpretations or inferences.
Document: [add document]
- Turn a transcript or meeting notes into a scannable brief
Turn the transcript below into a concise brief for [audience], under [word count] words.
Use these sections:
- Bottom line: What the meeting established, decided, or advanced and why it matters.
- Decisions made: Confirmed decisions or changes in direction.
- Key discussion points: Only the reasoning needed to understand them.
- Open questions: Unresolved issues or dependencies.
- Next steps: Action, owner, and deadline where stated.
- Risks or blockers: Stated issues that could affect progress.
Prioritize outcomes over chronology. Preserve important names, dates, numbers, and commitments. Distinguish decisions from proposals and open discussion. Do not infer owners, deadlines, or conclusions; write ‘not specified’ where needed.
Transcript or notes: [paste transcript]
- Rewrite for a specific audience
Rewrite the message below for [specific audience].
Audience context:
- Already knows: [relevant context]
- Cares most about: [priorities or questions]
- Needs to do: [decision, action, reply, or understanding]
Lead with what matters most, use the audience’s vocabulary and knowledge level, and make the takeaway or action clear. Preserve all facts, names, dates, numbers, commitments, qualifications, and meaning.
Use a [tone] tone, stay under [word count] words, and format it for [email / Slack / presentation / report].
Return only the rewritten message.
Original message: [paste message]
- Draft a difficult email
Draft a [type of email] to [recipient and relationship].
Context: [what happened and what the recipient needs to know]
Desired outcome: [what they should understand, decide, or do]
Use a [tone] tone and stay under [word count]. Preserve the relationship while making the purpose and next step clear. Do not invent facts, promises, commitments, or concessions.
Return only a subject line and the email.
Productivity and planning
- Break a big project into an actionable sequence
I need to [goal] by [deadline].
Starting point: [what is complete, available, or decided]
Constraints: [time, budget, team, tools, approvals, or other limits]
Success looks like: [specific outcome or deliverable]
Create a week-by-week plan working backward from the deadline. For each week, include the milestone, two or three priority actions, dependencies or approvals, and evidence of completion.
Sequence dependencies first, identify the critical path, flag deadline risks, and show where contingency time is most needed. State assumptions and flag if the deadline appears unrealistic.
End with the first three actions.
- Prioritize a week's worth of tasks
Prioritize the tasks below for this week.
Tasks: [paste tasks, deadlines, and time estimates]
Top priority: [priority]
Available time: [hours or working days, if known]
Weigh impact on the priority, urgency, dependencies, cost of delay, effort, and available time. Rank and label each task:
- Do first
- Schedule this week
- Delegate
- Defer
Give a one-sentence rationale for each. Then identify the first two tasks to start and the lowest-downside task to delegate or defer. State any assumptions or missing information that could change the ranking.
- Prepare for a meeting you're leading
I’m leading a [meeting type] with [attendees and roles] about [topic].
Length: [duration]
Required outcome: [decision, approval, plan, alignment, or next steps]
Context: [prior discussions, disagreements, knowledge, and constraints]
Create a timed agenda that fits the meeting length. Include:
- The meeting objective
- Agenda items in sequence, with time and intended outcome
- Decisions required
- Critical points of alignment, if applicable
- Three likely difficult questions based on the context
- Required pre-reading or preparation
Label assumptions where context is limited. End with a closing script that prompts me to recap the decisions made and confirm owners, deadlines, and next steps before the meeting ends.
Business and strategy
- Brainstorm ideas grounded in customer or performance data
Generate [number] [idea type] from the data below.
For each, include:
- The idea
- The specific data point or pattern it draws from
- The connection between the evidence and the idea
- Evidence level: directly supported or hypothesis
- The main assumption to validate
Rank the ideas by evidence strength. Base stated customer needs and trends on the supplied data, label inferences, and flag dataset limitations that could affect the recommendations.
Data: [insert data]
- Pressure-test a strategy
Evaluate the plan below from the perspective of a [role], focusing on:
- [Criterion 1]
- [Criterion 2]
- [Criterion 3]
Identify the three highest-risk assumptions. Label each as explicit or inferred and explain:
- Why it is uncertain
- What must be true
- What evidence would confirm or refute it
- The consequence if it is wrong
- The fastest credible test
Rank by uncertainty and potential impact. Also identify one strong element to preserve.

- Compare business options against defined criteria
Compare [option A] and [option B] for [use case and outcome].
Criteria and weights:
- [Criterion 1]: [weight from 1, low importance, to 5, high importance]
- [Criterion 2]: [weight from 1, low importance, to 5, high importance]
- [Criterion 3]: [weight from 1, low importance, to 5, high importance]
Constraints and non-negotiables: [insert details]
Apply non-negotiables as pass/fail gates first. Then score each option from 1–5 for every criterion, where 1 = poor fit, 3 = adequate, and 5 = strong fit.
For each score, reference the supplied evidence and flag assumptions or unknowns. Do not score missing evidence as poor performance.
Return:
- A comparison table with scores, reasoning, and trade-offs
- A weighted score for each option: multiply each score by its criterion weight, add the results, and divide by the total of the weights. Show the calculation.
- A recommendation and its deciding criterion
- The condition under which the other option would be better
Recommend an option only when the evidence supports it; otherwise, state what information is needed.
