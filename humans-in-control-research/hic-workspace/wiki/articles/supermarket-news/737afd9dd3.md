# Kroger’s declining traffic not a surprise to some: social media reacts

Source: [supermarket_news](https://www.supermarketnews.com/grocery-trends-data/kroger-s-declining-traffic-not-a-surprise-to-some-social-media-reacts)

Kroger’s declining traffic not a surprise to some: social media reacts
Those responding to SN story agree the grocer is focusing more on digital than the in-store experience
Kroger’s traffic has declined for weeks, but the trend doesn’t appear to be surprising many people.
Patrick McDonough shared our story, “Kroger’s traffic has been declining,” on his LinkedIn page, and his comment section has blown up. Most believe the grocer has chosen this path.
“Kroger’s declining traffic doesn’t surprise me,” Roy W. said. “The bigger issue may be what’s happening inside the stores.
“Kroger has leaned heavily into ecommerce, one of the most labor-intensive operations it has undertaken. When pickup needs help, that labor often comes from other departments. Managers pull employees to fulfill online orders while large picking carts clog aisles never designed for them. Meanwhile, the in-store experience deteriorates.
“Meat, deli and bakery remain important to customers, yet they often operate with minimal staffing despite the demands of production, sanitation and food safety.
“Retention compounds the problem. Stores already struggling with labor cannot afford to continually lose experienced employees through inconsistent policy enforcement.
“Kroger also appears to bet heavily on scale. But every new location inherits the same operating model. If labor, ecommerce economics and the in-store experience aren’t solved, adding locations simply reproduces the problems.
“The irony is Kroger invests heavily in helping customers avoid entering its stores while taking resources away from what makes customers want to shop inside them.
“Declining traffic may not be the problem. It may be the symptom.”
“Between scanning QR codes on the shelf to get certain coupons and long self-checkout lines caused by shutting down all human checkout lanes, I find myself ordering more for pickup and avoiding it all together,” Megan said.
“I stopped going because the shelves always seemed empty of items that I needed, online carts were everywhere and I couldn’t move around with my cart because abandoned pallets or carts were in main walkways,” Sheri said.
One poster, however, said the industry is moving through a slow time.
“Most vendors I spoke to claim their sales are down also,” Tony said. “The beverage company delivery guys said they are down in most of their stores but in varying degrees. Some say between 5% to 10%. There’s a difference between actual sales and forced sales that sit for weeks.”
The latest data from Placer.ai shows traditional grocers saw a 0.9% increase in traffic in July.
