# Price and value add to the seafood marketing riddle

Source: [supermarket_news](https://www.supermarketnews.com/seafood/price-and-value-add-to-the-seafood-marketing-riddle)

Price and value add to the seafood marketing riddle
Higher costs and diverse shopper priorities create sales obstacles
August 20, 2026
Flexibility is becoming a more crucial seafood merchandising component.
Because shopper segments have a mix of interests and respond differently to merchandising strategies, it is important for retailers to closely monitor the market’s reaction to specific seafood products and presentations and make adjustments when necessary, analysts said.
Such a focus can help revive a sluggish and increasingly pricey seafood sector. Fresh seafood volume sales were down 0.4% for the 52 weeks ending July 12, reports Circana, a Chicago-based market research firm. Average category prices were up 2.6% to $9.94 a pound.
Volume sales rose 5% for fresh salmon, the best-selling seafood selection, which had a comparatively modest 1.2% price increase to $11.61 a pound. In contrast, sales fell 6.9% and 17.8%, respectively, for fresh shrimp and fresh crab, the second and third most popular items, which had 7.7% and 13.1% price increases.
“Price is a top challenge to growing seafood sales,” said Chuck Anderson, partner and vice president of sales and operations at Certified Quality Foods, a Dallas-based analyzer of seafood quality, and the former category manager for seafood at San Antonio-based H-E-B. The cost of wild-caught seafood, he said, is prohibitive for many consumers. “This has been happening for some time, as most wild fisheries are maxed out, but global demand for healthy wild seafood keeps growing,” Anderson said.
While emphasizing the value of seafood beyond price may help defray shopper cost concerns, merchandising seafood still can be tricky as the motivations behind consumption differ significantly among the various age groups, said Iris Yim, principal and chief strategist at Sparkle Insights Inc., a Chapel Hill, N.C.-based research and strategies firm. “Balancing the diverse expectations of multiple generations within a single seafood department is one of the greatest challenges facing retailers,” she said.
Merchandisers can address the issue by analyzing and responding to the key interests of each group, Yim said. Boomers (persons born between 1946 and 1964), for instance, are among the most consistent seafood consumers with activity often driven by long-standing habits and concerns about heart health, cholesterol and overall wellness, she said.
Generation X shoppers (persons born between 1965 and 1980) similarly value health benefits but often balance the considerations with family needs and budget constraints, “making them practical shoppers focused on versatility and value,” she said.
Millennials (persons born between 1981 and 1996) have a broader curiosity toward seafood due to travels, multicultural dining experiences and interest in food media, Yim said. The segment is more likely to experiment with species, flavors and preparation methods, she said.
Younger shoppers, meanwhile, will typically be more receptive to experiential environments, Yim said. “The consumers often respond positively to discovery, storytelling, sampling and interactive experiences instead of static displays and product-focused merchandising that may feel transactional,” she said. “It helps them connect with products and builds confidence in their purchasing decisions.”
