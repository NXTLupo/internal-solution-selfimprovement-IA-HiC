# Walmart is bringing in younger and higher income shoppers

Source: [supermarket_news](https://www.supermarketnews.com/grocery-trends-data/walmart-is-bringing-in-younger-and-higher-income-shoppers)

Walmart is bringing in younger and higher income shoppers
But the retailer is losing to Amazon when it comes to other demographics
August 20, 2026
Walmart’s strategy for competing with Amazon and winning online is creating two distinct consumer stories, according to the latest data from Numerator released Thursday. Gen Z and higher-income households earning over $100K are fueling new growth, while boomers and lower-income households—central pillars to its business today—are pulling back. The tension between attracting its new growth cohorts while retaining its longtime core shopper base is creating opportunities and potential risk for the world’s largest retailer.
Gen Z and higher-income households are driving opportunities for Walmart:
- Traffic gains among growth cohorts are translating into billions in new spending for the retailer. In the past year, Gen Z added 77 million trips worth $3.4B total across in-store and online. Higher-income households added $8.9B primarily online, supported by 115 million more trips 
- Everyday food categories drive gains among growth cohorts. Higher-income households grew spending by $3.6B across beverages, snacks, candy and frozen food, while Gen Z added nearly $1.2B across the same categories 
- Private label is gaining with Gen Z. Gen Z households have made over 390M trips with Walmart’s private label CPG products in the basket in the past year which is more than Costco, Target, Aldi, Trader Joe’s and Whole Foods private label combined 
Boomers and lower-income households, however, represent potential risk:
- Walmart.com is only partially recapturing boomer spending. Boomers made 160 million fewer CPG in-store trips to Walmart, contributing to a $6.2B decline. Walmart.com captured $3.5B as boomers migrated online, while Amazon gained 1.5 share points and $5B in CPG spending among boomers 
- Walmart.com is retaining most lower-income spending, but Amazon is gaining ground. Lower-income households shifted $7.8B away from Walmart stores while adding $7.3B at Walmart.com. Meanwhile, Amazon gained 1.3 share points with $6.8B CPG spending among the same shoppers 
- Private label declines reveal a larger gap with Walmart’s boomer base. Walmart lost $2.4B in boomer private label spending in-store and recovered just $780M online, while nearly $1B more shifted to private label brands at Amazon, Aldi, Trader Joe’s, and Whole Foods
