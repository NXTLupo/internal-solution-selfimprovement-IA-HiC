# Walmart’s U.S. grocery strength helps drive Q2 sales growth

Source: [supermarket_news](https://www.supermarketnews.com/finance/walmart-s-u-s-grocery-strength-helps-drive-q2-sales-growth)

Walmart’s U.S. grocery strength helps drive Q2 sales growth
The retailer raises its fiscal year outlook
August 20, 2026
Walmart continued to gain U.S. market share in the second quarter as grocery sales and growing use of its ecommerce services helped the retailer increase sales and transactions.
Walmart U.S. reported net sales of $125.2 billion for the fiscal second quarter ended July 31, up 3.5% from $120.9 billion a year earlier. Comparable sales excluding fuel increased 2.6%, compared with 4.6% growth during the same quarter last year. Transactions rose 1.5%, while the average ticket increased 1.1%.
The Bentonville, Ark.-based retailer said U.S. results included broad-based market share gains and continued strength in ecommerce. Comparable sales were negatively affected by about 1.25 percentage points from pharmacy deflation related to new maximum fair price regulations.
For the grocery industry, one of the more notable results came from Sam’s Club, where comparable sales excluding fuel increased 4.4%. The company said the increase was driven by higher transaction counts and unit volumes, with strength in grocery as well as general merchandise.
Transactions at Sam’s Club jumped 7%, although the average ticket fell 2.5%. Net sales increased 8.8% to $25.7 billion, while sales excluding fuel rose 4.5% to $22.1 billion.
The results underscore Walmart’s continued push to use its large store network not only as a traditional grocery destination but also as a base for pickup and delivery.
Walmart U.S. ecommerce sales increased 24% during the quarter, led by store-fulfilled delivery, advertising and marketplace sales. Ecommerce contributed approximately 5.1 percentage points to Walmart U.S. comparable sales, up from about 4.2 percentage points a year earlier.
Sam’s Club reported a similar trend. Ecommerce sales at the warehouse club increased 26%, with growth in club-fulfilled pickup and delivery. Digital sales contributed approximately 4.5 percentage points to comparable sales, compared with about 3.5 percentage points a year ago.
The retailer's U.S. grocery business remains particularly important because food can drive frequent store visits and give Walmart opportunities to sell customers merchandise from other departments.
Walmart U.S. operating income increased 20.6% to $8.1 billion. The company said the improvement reflected higher gross profit and better ecommerce economics. Inventory in the U.S. business increased 6.3%, which Walmart attributed primarily to strategic initiatives and inflation.
Tariffs also played a role in the quarter. Walmart said its U.S. gross profit benefited from tariff refunds, which were partially offset by price investments and higher fuel costs. The company said it plans to prioritize remaining tariff refunds for additional price investments during the second half of the fiscal year.
“Our team delivered another good quarter, and we continue to make steady progress on the long-term value drivers of our business,” Walmart President and CEO John Furner said. Furner pointed to the retailer’s multiyear ecommerce growth as evidence that customers are increasingly using Walmart across stores and digital channels.
Walmart raised its outlook for fiscal 2027 following the quarter. The company now expects net sales on a constant-currency basis to increase 4% to 5%, up from its previous forecast of 3.5% to 4.5%. Adjusted operating income is expected to increase 7% to 8.5%, compared with the previous forecast of 6% to 8%.
Adjusted earnings per share are now projected at $2.80 to $2.87, up from the company’s previous range of $2.75 to $2.85.
For the third quarter, Walmart expects net sales growth of 3% to 3.75% and adjusted operating income growth of 2% to 4%. Adjusted earnings per share are expected to range from 62 cents to 64 cents.
