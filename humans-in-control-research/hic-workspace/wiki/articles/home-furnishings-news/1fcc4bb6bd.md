# Target boosts outlook: 3 take-aways from Q2

Source: [home_furnishings_news](https://www.hfndigital.com/financial/target-raises-outlook-on-strong-q2-sales-tariff-refunds/)

Jennifer Marks// Editor in Chief//August 24, 2026
Jennifer Marks// Editor in Chief//August 24, 2026
Minneapolis – Traffic drove top-line gains and tariff refunds fattened the bottom line at Target during the second quarter.
Net sales rose 5.3% to $26.5 billion in the quarter ended Aug. 1, with merchandise sales up 5.0% and non-merchandise sales (Roundel ad revenue, Target Circle 360 membership revenue, and the Target+ marketplace) up 20.1%. Traffic increased 3.6% while average ticket was flat.
Sales gains were broad-based, although the lift in home and apparel was “flattish,” CEO Michael Fiddelke told investors. The Back-to-College season is off to an “encouraging” start. Top performers so far include $5 gadgets, $10 headphones and dorm essentials.
Comp sales grew 3.8%, with same-store sales up 2.7% and comp digital sales up 8.7%.
“While there’s still meaningful work ahead, we’re encouraged by the progress we’re making and remain focused on executing with discipline, staying agile in a dynamic operating environment, and investing in our team and capabilities to drive sustainable, profitable growth over the long term,” said Fiddelke.
Quick take-ways from last week’s investor call include:
Home reset is just beginning. During the quarter, Target replaced nearly 75% of its decorative accessories assortment. Work is now underway on a comparable overhaul of the kid’s home and bedding areas. Because of long lead times for home products, the work will continue into 2027 and beyond, execs said.
The store fleet is key to reviving the Tar-Zhay magic. Target is remodeling 130 stores this year, with 100 currently underway. Expanded emphasis on beauty, baby & kids, and health & wellness departments appears to be delivering a durable lift in sales. The company has also opened 24 new full-sized stores as far this year – 17 in Q2 – several in new areas.
Price cuts versus tariff pressures. Since early 2025, Target has cut prices on more than 10,000 items across apparel, home, baby essentials, food and beverages. More are on the way,” said Cara Sylvester, EVP and chief merchandising officer. Simultaneously, Target is working to mitigate ongoing tariffs with changes to country of origin and vendor collaborations to offset higher costs.
During the quarter, Target recouped most of the IEEPA tariff refunds it has applied for. Operating income included a $994 million benefit from the refunds, rising to $2.6 billion from $1.3 billion in the year-ago quarter. Target’s operating income margin rate of 9.6% included 3.7 percentage points of benefit from the tariff refunds and gross margin rate was 33.7 percent reflecting 3.7 percentage points of benefit from tariff refunds.
Excluding tariff refunds, second quarter gross margin rate expanded approximately 100 basis points over Target’s prior-year margin rate of 29.0 percent, reflecting the comparison over last year’s elevated markdowns and IEEPA-related purchase order cancellation costs. It also reflected continued growth in advertising and non-merchandise sales.
Net earnings boomed to $1.88 billion, or $4.11 per diluted share, compared to $935, or $2.05 per diluted share, in last year’s second quarter.
