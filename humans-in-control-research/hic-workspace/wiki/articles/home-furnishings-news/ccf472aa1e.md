# Widgeteer’s change in strategy leads to new brands and new customers

Source: [home_furnishings_news](https://www.hfndigital.com/housewares/widgeteers-change-in-strategy-leads-to-new-brands-and-new-customers/)

Allison Zisko// Editor in Chief//August 25, 2026
Allison Zisko// Editor in Chief//August 25, 2026
Since its founding 18 years ago, tabletop and housewares company Widgeteer has done a “complete 180,” in the words of founder Bill McHenry, and has taken the business in new directions with new customers.
Although it started out as an importer of home brands such as Sagaform and Tognana, the company decided in 2022 to create and build its own brands so it could be more creative with product design and packaging, offer customers the types of products they were looking for, and control pricing and inventory levels, according to Chief Operating Officer, and McHenry’s wife, Nancy McHenry.
It created three brands centered around home entertaining: Jeanne Fitz, Tootles and Gezellig.
Jeanne Fitz, named after Bill McHenry’s mother, encompasses tabletop and serveware pieces like glassware, serveware, porcelain and woodenware that it sells to retailers like Dillard’s and Williams-Sonoma.
Tootle is its whimsical brand with seasonal themes for holidays throughout the year.
And Gezellig is a Dutch word that loosely translated means “pleasant” and is generally used to refer to convivial social gatherings and a sense of well-being that arises from spending happy times with friends. As a brand, it is all about experiential, design-oriented products for home entertaining.
Widgeteer has manufacturing partnerships with family-owned factories in India, China, Taiwan and other points in Asia. Headquartered in the Chicago suburbs of Crystal Lake, Ill., the company opened a new warehouse in southwestern Chicagoland, said Bill McHenry, and can also ship directly from its factory partners.
Widgeteer generates more than $4 million in annual sales and is looking for continued growth in 2027, said Nancy McHenry.
Its retail customer base is diverse. It has a longstanding relationship with Cooper’s Hawk, a Chicago-area establishment whose 69 locations include a full-service restaurant, wine tasting room and retail space. Widgeteer first sold Sagaform decanters to Cooper’s Hawk, but the most recent collection it developed for them is Slant glassware in four colors whose notable design is its slanted rim. In two and a half years, it has sold more than half a million units, according to Nancy McHenry.
Its ombre flower coupes are sold at the MoMA Design Store and at Uncommon Goods, a partnership that paved the way to a deal with Disney (Widgeteer supplies glassware to the entertainment giant.) It created Mr. Bingle snow globe glassware for Dillard’s and a collection of utensils, linens and glassware for the National Gallery of Art.
It also designed glass giftware with a city skyline theme for GQ magazine’s subscription box program, and the Jeanne Fitz Olive Oil and Vinegar Dipping Tray to fellow box subscription service, FabFitFun. These innovative wooden boards have carved leaf designs that allow balsamic vinegar to float on top of olive oil. It creates a visual that went viral on social media, garnering more than one million views and more than 55,000 likes in a short amount of time, according to a story in HomePage News in January 2024. The dipping tray also received a 2026 Registry Award from The Knot.
Widgeteer is working on its spring 2028 lineup now, which includes glassware with a playful duck icon inside, rolling stemware wine glasses and wood serveware made from purple heart wood from Cambodia.
Bill McHenry sits on the board of the International Housewares Association. Nancy was recently elected board vice president of the Cookware & Bakeware Alliance. Nancy McHenry had a long career with Marriott International as a regional vice president for sales and marketing, responsible for roughly $2 billion in sales across the U.S. and Canada. In 2020, Bill McHenry convinced her to come out of retirement and work with Widgeteer, where he said her background in hospitality and her focus on service and operations make her an asset to Widgeteer. Their son, James McHenry, who also worked for Marriott as well as Fairmont Hotels & Resorts, is director of sales.
Widgeteer exhibits at the Inspired Home Show in Chicago, and in the Best of Show rep showroom in Atlanta. It is leasing a showroom at World Market Center for Las Vegas Market in 2028. But the McHenrys spend most of their days on the road, traveling to visit customers and show their wares. “That’s where you can really make a difference. There’s more trust and a greater bond,” said Nancy McHenry. “I think that’s the big part of our success.”
Bill McHenry’s objective is to offer “customer service on steroids.”
“My goal is to make us as good as 3M … our follow-through is second-to-none,” he said. (McHenry once worked at 3M.) When Widgeteer makes a mistake, McHenry said, they “make [things] right, right away.”
“Our customers really see that,” he said.
