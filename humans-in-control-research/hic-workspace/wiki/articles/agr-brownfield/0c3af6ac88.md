# Mixed finish to a short week for corn, soybeans, and wheat

Source: [agr_brownfield](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/market-news/mixed-finish-to-a-short-week-for-corn-soybeans-and-wheat/)

Market News
Mixed finish to a short week for corn, soybeans, and wheat
Soybeans were mixed, closing out the week mixed as well. High temperatures along with heavy rains in some areas are expected to impact crop conditions. July and August are usually the key months for determining soybean yields in the Northern Hemisphere. The USDA’s weekly crop progress and condition numbers are out Monday afternoon. Old crop sales were a marketing year low at 1.5 million bushels, with China and Japan topping the list, but mostly offset by a cancelation from unknown destinations. New crop sales were down from the prior week with no new sales to China. Mexico bought the bulk of the week’s 2026/27 total, followed distantly by Vietnam. Soybean meal futures were mixed on bull spreading, while soybean oil was mixed, mostly firm, generally consolidating ahead of the holiday weekend. The Buenos Aires Grain Exchange says 99% of Argentina’s soybean crop is harvested.
Corn was mixed Thursday and mixed overall for the week. Corn is monitoring weather as the crop moves through the pollination phase. Recent high temperatures especially in parts of the Easten Corn Belt might have had at least some impact at this phase, but the generally drier weather in that region was welcome. There’s the possibility of a heat dome in the Western Corn Belt as well. The trade is also watching harvest activity in Argentina and Brazil. The Buenos Aires Grain Exchange says 53% of Argentina’s crops is harvested. Old crop U.S. export sales were below the previous week at 28.8 million bushels. The leading old crop buyers were Mexico and Colombia, but there was a significant cancelation by unknown destinations. New crop sales were up from the week before, primarily to Mexico and unknown destinations. The USDA will issue new supply and demand numbers on the 10th, while CONAB’s updated outlook for Brazil is set for the 14th.
The wheat complex was mixed, while ending the week with solid gains. Wheat consolidated after the higher move sparked by the USDA’s lower-than-expected acreage and stocks numbers on June 30th. The trade is also watching U.S. and world harvest activity and development weather, including in Argentina, Australia, Canada, Europe, Russia, and Ukraine. The Buenos Aires Grain Exchange says 81% of Argentina’s wheat crop is planted. Weekly U.S. wheat sales were lower than the week before at 11 million bushels. The big buyers were Mexico and unknown destinations and there were only a couple of minor cancelations. While some classes of U.S. wheat are more competitive on the export market, that might not really become apparent for a couple of more weeks, and there is still plenty of competition right now.
