# Wheat, soybeans, corn kick off July with more gains

Source: [agr_brownfield](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/market-news/wheat-soybeans-corn-kick-off-july-with-more-gains/)

Market News
Wheat, soybeans, corn kick off July with more gains
Soybeans were higher on short covering and technical buying. There was renewed talk about China buying U.S. beans ahead of Thursday’s export sales report. No new sales to China have been announced yet this week, but unknown destinations did buy new crop U.S. beans Monday. ANEC estimates Brazil’s June soybean exports at 14.05 million tons, well below the previous guess of 15.21 million tons. Weather in many areas is expected to moderate late this weekend, and the extreme heat in parts of the region was expected to have a larger potential impact on corn than beans. Soybean meal futures were up, following beans, while soybean oil was mixed on bear spreading. After the session, the USDA reported May’s soybean crush at 213 million bushels, down 5 million on the month, but up 9 million on the year, with soybean product stocks above a year ago.
Corn was higher on short covering and technical buying. Corn is monitoring the heat dome impact on the pollination phase, along with rain chances in parts of the Midwest and Plains. This pattern has led to a generally welcome drier stretch in parts of the Eastern Corn Belt. Ethanol production hit an 11-week high, helped out by solid margins. The U.S. Energy Information Administration says production averaged 1.117 million barrels per day, rising 27,000 on the week and 41,000 on the year, with stocks of 24.69 million barrels, an increase of 105,000 from the previous week and 573,000 from a year ago, while exports averaged 126,000 barrels per day, 5,000 more than the week before, but 5,000 less than last year. The USDA says 471.784 million bushels of corn were used for ethanol production in May 2026, up 10% from April and 6% from May 2025. Production of distillers dried grains with solubles was 1,774,035 tons, 11% higher than the prior month, but 1% lower than a year ago. Corn is also watching harvest activity in Argentina and Brazil. CONAB’s next look at Brazil’s crop is out July 14th.
The wheat complex was higher on short covering and technical buying. There was more support from the USDA’s lower than expected acreage and quarterly stocks numbers Tuesday. That record lower acreage number and the better than expected fourth quarter demand, possibly from an increase in livestock feed usage, have lowered the margin of error for U.S. production. Wheat looks oversold and could rally further into the holiday weekend, but sustained upside might be limited by ample near-term global supplies, with the USDA’s next round of supply and demand numbers out July 10th. The complex is also looking at crops in Argentina, Australia, Canada, Europe, Russia, and Ukraine.
