# Beef-on-dairy breeding continues to limit dairy replacement heifer supply

Beef-on-dairy breeding continues to limit dairy replacement heifer supply
A livestock economist says a shift is underway in how farmers are balancing replacement heifers with breeding more beef-on-dairy animals.
Abbi Groves with CoBank tells Brownfield nearly 800,000 fewer heifers are expected to enter the milking herd through next year before the herd rebuilds.
“By 285,000 head more heifers year over year, and then for 2028, we’re forecasting about 75,000 head more coming online,” she says.
Groves says the projected increase in heifers won’t fully cover the current deficit, but milk components have a key role to play.
“Producing more butterfat, producing more protein per pound of milk rather than just total fluid volume,” she says, is more important. “The milk check is largely priced on both of those components. If we can make cows more efficient that way, we won’t have to worry about really growing the dairy herd to be extremely large.”
Groves says herd expansions are also following regional processing investments in states like South Dakota, Kansas, Texas, and Wisconsin.
