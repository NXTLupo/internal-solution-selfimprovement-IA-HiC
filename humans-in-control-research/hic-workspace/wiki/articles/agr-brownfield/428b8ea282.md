# Hog futures lower on profit-taking

Source: [agr_brownfield](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/market-news/hog-futures-lower-on-profit-taking-18/)

Market News
Hog futures lower on profit-taking
At the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, live cattle were mostly higher, feeder cattle were mixed watching direct business develop. Feeders had additional pressure from the higher move in corn. August live cattle closed $.60 lower at $241.82 and October live cattle closed $.07 higher at $236.72. August feeder cattle closed $.45 lower at $364.15 and September feeder cattle closed $.35 lower at $362.17.
There was a light round of direct cash cattle trade that developed on Wednesday. Dressed deals in parts of Nebraska ranged from $403 to $405, that is $3 to $5 lower than the previous week’s weighted averages. Bids surfaced in other parts of cattle country at $252 to $255 live. Look for more business to develop over the balance of the week.
At the Unionville Livestock Market in Missouri, steers and heifers sold $10 to $20 higher with spots of sharply higher. The USDA says there was a top notch offering of calves and yearlings that sold on a very active market. Quality and condition were very attractive. Supply was heavy and demand was very good. Receipts were up significantly for the Annual Customer Appreciation BBQ Sale. Feeder supply included 74 percent steers with 73 percent of the offering weighing more than 600 pounds. Medium and Large 1 feeder steers 603 to 644 pounds brought $492 to $509.50 and feeder steers 871 to 892 pounds brought $365.25 to $369. Medium and Large 1 feeder heifers 637 to 647 pounds brought $410 to $424 and feeder heifers 706 to 743 pounds brought $365 to $381.
Boxed beef was lower with light demand for moderate offerings. Choice was $1.90 lower at $391.26 and Select was $1.99 lower at $369.69. The Choice/Select spread was $21.57. Estimated cattle slaughter was 110,000 head, up 2,000 on the week and down more than 11,000 on the year.
Lean hog futures finished the day lower on profit-taking. August lean hogs closed $1.15 lower at $97.05 and October lean hogs closed $.50 lower at $81.50.
Cash hogs closed lower with a solid negotiated run. Processors were able to move needed numbers without having to get too aggressive in their procurement efforts. The hog market has been largely focused on demand. Summer grilling season is in full swing and with pork’s competitive price in the retail space, domestic demand is likely seeing a boost. Global demand for pork has had some bright spots, but there are concerns about long-term strength. The industry continues to monitor the availability of market-ready hogs and hog weights, which were up less than a pound on the week and are 2.8 pounds heavier than year-ago levels. Barrows and gilts at the National Daily Direct closed $.66 lower with a base range of $92 to $98 with a weighted average of $96.92; the Iowa/Minnesota closed $.67 lower with a weighted average of $97.06; the Western Corn Belt closed $.65 lower with a weighted average of $97.08. Prices at the Eastern Corn Belt were not reported due to confidentiality. Butcher hog prices at the Midwest cash markets are steady at $63.
Pork values closed firm, up $.22 at $95.71. Loins, ribs, and bellies were up. Hams, picnics, and butts were weak to lower. Estimated hog slaughter was 474,000 head, up 8,000 on the week and up more than 2,000 on the year.
