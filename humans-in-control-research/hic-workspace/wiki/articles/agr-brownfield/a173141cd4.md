# Cattle futures finish holiday-shortened week lower

Source: [agr_brownfield](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/market-news/cattle-futures-finish-holiday-shortened-week-lower/)

Market News
Cattle futures finish holiday-shortened week lower
At the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, live and feeder cattle were lower on cash and wholesale pressure. August live cattle closed $2.60 lower at $239.22 and October live cattle closed $2.45 lower at $234.30. August feeder cattle closed $3.52 lower at $360.62 and September feeder cattle closed $3.70 lower at $358.47.
There was another light round of direct cash cattle trade on Thursday. For the week, live deals were at $255 to $256, mostly $255, about $3 lower than the previous week’s business. Dressed deals were at $403, $5 lower than the prior week’s weighted average basis in Nebraska.
At the Green City Livestock Auction in Missouri, the offering sold sharply higher. The USDA says most of the cattle were in attractive conditions. Receipts were down on the week, but up on the year. Feeder supply included 59 percent steers with 46 percent of the offering weighing more than 600 pounds. Medium and Large 1 feeder steers 511 to 546 pounds brought $462.50 to $500 and feeder steers 601 to 648 pounds brought $432.50 to $475. Medium and Large 1 feeder heifers 511 to 546 pounds brought $438 to $475 and feeder heifers 610 to 643 pounds brought $392.50 to $425.
Boxed beef was sharply lower with light demand for solid offerings. Choice was $4.19 lower at $387.07 and Select was $2.26 lower at $367.43. The Choice/Select spread is $19.64. Estimated cattle slaughter was 109,000 head, even on the week and down nearly 5,000 on the year.
Lean hog futures were mixed, adjusting spreads. August lean hogs closed $1.70 higher at $98.75 and October lean hogs closed $.52 higher at $82.02.
Cash hogs closed lower with a very light negotiated run. The cash hog market has struggled to find significant support in recent weeks. The market is largely focused on demand. And while there have been some bright spots when it comes to global demand, there are long-term concerns about strength that linger. It’s summer grilling season which could provide a boost to domestic demand, especially as pork remains a competitively priced protein in the retail space. Barrows and gilts at the National Daily Direct closed $1.45 lower with a base range of $92 to $98 and a weighted average of $95.47; the Iowa/Minnesota closed $1.13 lower with a weighted average of $95.93; the Western Corn Belt closed $1.31 lower with a weighted average of $95.77. Prices at the Eastern Corn Belt were not reported due to confidentiality.
Butcher hog prices at the Midwest cash markets are steady at $63. At Illinois, slaughter sows were steady with light demand for moderate offerings at $43 to $55. Barrows and gilts were steady with moderate demand for moderate offerings at $57 to $67. Boars ranged from $25 to $30 and $15 to $20.
Pork values closed higher, up $.35 at $96.06. Picnics, bellies, and hams were sharply higher. Butts were up. Ribs and loins were sharply lower. Estimated hog slaughter was 455,000 head, down 11,000 on the week and up about 51,000 on the year.
