# MO ag groups: SCOTUS pesticide ruling restores regulatory certainty

MO ag groups: SCOTUS pesticide ruling restores regulatory certainty
The policy director at the Missouri Soybean Association says a recent U.S. Supreme Court ruling reinforces federal pesticide law over state labeling requirements, restoring regulatory certainty for farmers.
“The concern was due to the litigation and costs, had the Supreme Court not clarified things, we were actually on the precipice of losing access to critical technologies like glyphosate. We were concerned about the snowball effect that could have occurred thereafter of what other products would be then on the chopping block,” says Ben Travlos.
State ag groups in Missouri, including MSA, the Missouri Cattlemen’s Association, Missouri Farm Bureau and Missouri Corn Growers Association, issued a joint statement following the SCOTUS announcement saying the decision was a major victory and they’ll continue standing up for farmers’ access to safe and effective tools.
Travlos says the ruling likely settles a core legal question, according to attorneys advising the association, but work is still needed to maintain crop protection tools for farmers.
At the end of June, the court ruled 7-2 the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act overrides failure-to-warn lawsuits bringing “safety” claims.
Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway recently told Brownfield “all of these lawsuits that get filed based on a state common law standard for failure to warn won’t be able to be filed. They’ll be held unconstitutional, which is the right thing, that the federal law preempts it. And so it’s a good thing for ag.”
A statement from Bayer said the company expects the ruling to lead to the dismissal of current warning-based claims and block future state failure-to-warn lawsuits.
The Center for Food Safety, which filed a brief in the case, says the court eliminated one way the public held pesticide corporations accountable for their products’ harms, and the fight is far from over.
State lawmakers in Missouri also proposed legislation addressing failure to warn this year, but it was stalled awaiting the SCOTUS decision. Travlos says it’s unclear if the topic will come back up in the state legislature next year.
