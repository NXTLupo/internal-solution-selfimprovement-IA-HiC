# Are shoppers still buying Canadian?

Source: [canadian_grocer](https://canadiangrocer.com/are-shoppers-still-buying-canadian)

Are shoppers still buying Canadian?
Younger millennials stand out as the exception. Their prioritization of Canadian products and brands remains steady versus last year, with no noticeable decline. They are also more likely to connect buying Canadian with environmental reasons, and they are using social media, loyalty programs and retailer newsletters to recognize Canadian brands. For this cohort, buying Canadian can signal sustainability, community alignment and values-based shopping.
Support for Canadian goods and brands remains the biggest driver. However, support for local businesses has lost some momentum, especially among older millennials and boomers. This matters because the movement’s first wave was powered by an emotional story: support Canadian workers, protect local businesses, respond to tariffs and show national pride. The intensity has cooled, particularly among shoppers facing high living costs and macroeconomic uncertainty.

This divide is clearest by income. Lower-income shoppers are significantly less likely than last year to say they will keep preferring Canadian products if tariffs are called off, that Canadian products are higher quality or that they are willing to pay a premium. By contrast, affluent Canadians continue to over-index on these attitudes, and their views have not noticeably changed.
Implications for Canadian grocers and suppliers:
• “Buy Canadian” still has equity, but it needs to be easier, clearer and better connected to value. Shoppers are paying less attention to packaging labels to determine Canadian origin, especially generation Z and younger millennials. Retailers cannot assume a maple leaf or origin statement will do all the work. Shelf tags, digital filters, loyalty content, social media and clearer brand storytelling all need to reinforce the same message.
• Canadian brands still have permission to win. But the next phase of the movement will be less about symbolism and more about affordability. Shoppers need to see Canadian origin, understand why it matters and feel that choosing it still fits their budget.
