# Psychology of Unboxing: Why Customers Remember the Delivery More than the Product

Source: [retail_minded](https://retailminded.com/psychology-of-unboxing-why-customers-remember-the-delivery-more-than-the-product/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=psychology-of-unboxing-why-customers-remember-the-delivery-more-than-the-product)

Psychology of Unboxing: Why Customers Remember the Delivery More than the Product
There’s a moment that takes place before a customer ever sees your product. It begins when the delivery notification lands on their phone, then gradually escalates as the box sits on their doorstep, ultimately reaching a peak when they lift it up and feel its weight shift in their hands. Before the customer peels back a single flap of the box, they’ll start forming an opinion about your product—and by extension, your brand.
This is the unboxing experience, and it’s one of the most effective yet underutilized tools for customer retention. First impressions dictate whether they choose to order from you again, so here’s what you need to know about the power of unboxing.
The Brain Makes Snap Decisions About Your Brand
Long before they receive the product, a customer’s brain is already working. The anticipation of tearing open that box on their doorstep activates a dopamine release—the brain’s reward chemical—creating excitement and setting an emotional tone.
Now the customer starts to envision what comes next: The crisp resistance of a sturdy box. The soft give of tissue paper. The faint sound of material shuffling inside. Each sensory cue either reinforces or undermines the quality signal of your brand.
Research backs this up consistently. Studies show that first impressions can be formed in about one fifth of a second. What’s more, 94 percent of initial judgments about a project are based on the external package, and 60 percent of consumers will not purchase again from a retailer if their order arrives in an inferior package.
The inverse is equally powerful. When a package meets—or even surpasses—expectations, customers associate these positive feelings with your brand, incentivizing them to continue supporting and referring you. Think of the box as a physical handshake between you and the person who is about to open it and see what’s inside.
The Sensory Details Influence Perception
When you think about a package, it’s easy to focus on design aesthetics. But the emotional experience of receiving an order is about so much more than what’s printed on the box. It’s a full sensory tableau that includes the distribution of weight, texture of materials, how the box opens, density of the fill, and how the contents are organized.
Each of these details instantly communicates something to the customer about your brand. A box that arrives crushed says, “We never took the time to protect this.” Excessive plastic fill in an otherwise sustainable package says, “We just pulled whatever material was on the shelf.” Meanwhile, crinkle-cut tissue trimmed neatly, then nestled intentionally around the product says, “Someone here was paying attention.” This is the emotional architecture of a delivery—and it leaves the customer with a sense that you care.
Why Unboxing Matters More than Ever
For many e-commerce brands, a package is the only tactile interaction they’ll ever have with customers. Since there’s no storefront, sales associate, or physical transaction to establish a connection, the unboxing moment is invaluable for online businesses.
Research shows that almost 70 percent of surveyed consumers make purchasing decisions based on unboxing videos they encounter on social media. A package that’s worth showing off will pay for itself in both new and repeat customers.
How to Make Better Packaging Decisions
Intentional package selection comes down these core principles:
- Match materials to brand identity: The unboxing experience should feel cohesive with your brand as a whole. A mismatch between these two elements can erode customer trust before they even see the product itself.
- Let protection and presentation work together: The best packages seamlessly combine both durable function and visual aesthetics.
- Be serious and authentic about sustainability: 80 percent of U.S. consumers are more willing to buy products that come in eco-friendly packages.
- Work with the right supply partner: Reliable product packaging suppliers offer the best materials and bring cross-industry expertise for a smooth delivery.
Make the Unboxing Experience Count
In a market where products can be replicated efficiently and prices can be matched easily, the feeling a customer has when they open your box is one of the few experiences that’s genuinely hard to commoditize. Every packaging choice is a decision about the experience you aim to deliver and the customers want to retain—make it count.
