# These are the best AI laptops for motion design and VFX

Source: [creative_bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/tech/laptops/best-ai-laptops-for-motion-design-and-vfx)

These are the best AI laptops for motion design and VFX
Speed up your workflow with these AI laptops, all of which are powerful enough to run motion and VFX software smoothly.
The question of whether you need an AI laptop becomes much easier to answer if you work in motion design and/or VFX. The software you need to do this kind of work typically pushes laptops harder than almost anything else.
An AI laptop lets you take advantage of today's AI-assisted creative tools which run locally rather than in the cloud (denoising, upscaling, auto-rotoscoping, motion tracking), without draining the energy of the CPU and GPU. If you already have our list of laptops for graphic design bookmarked, this is the specialised, more demanding tier above it.
Our overall pick for the best AI laptop for motion design and VFX is the Apple MacBook Pro 16-inch (M5 Max, 2026), thanks to its combo of raw multi-core power, a hugely capable GPU and Apple's most advanced Neural Engine yet. We've also included alternatives if you're specifically looking for a Windows laptop or a workstation, while there's also a budget option if you're funds are more limited.

If you're running demanding motion graphics or VFX software, the more powerful the machine, the faster and smoother everything will go. And the MacBook Pro with M5 Max is built for exactly this kind of work.
Its 18-core CPU pairs Performance and Efficiency cores with a beefed-up GPU, and each GPU core now carries its own Neural Accelerator, so AI-assisted tasks in Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve and Cinema 4D can run locally without waiting on the cloud. As a result, VFX previews render quickly, with graphics performance up around 50% on the previous M4 Pro and M4 Max generation, and DaVinci Resolve Studio rendering as much as five times faster.
With up to 128GB of unified memory and 4TB of storage, this super-powerful laptop is able to deal with enormous project files without complaint, and there's also excellent battery life alongside all that power. It is, however, very expensive: a fully-specced M5 Max configuration runs into five figures; something you might find that galling, given that Apple still hasn't brought OLED or a touchscreen to the Pro line. For anyone serious about motion graphics or VFX who can stomach the cost, though, this may well be an investment in time and efficiency that pays off in the long term.

If you want a Windows laptop, the ASUS ProArt P16 is the closest thing to a MacBook Pro rival built specifically for motion work. Its RTX 5070 GPU marks a huge step up from the previous model, offering a 25-28% jump in graphics performance, and Premiere Pro, Photoshop and generative AI tools all run smoothly even with feature-heavy files. Its AMD XDNA NPU, rated up to 50 TOPS, also handles a lot of the on-device AI legwork in ASUS' own Creator Hub tools, keeping tasks like sketch enhancement and image generation off the GPU so it's free for rendering.
The 3K OLED touchscreen, meanwhile, measured close to full DCI-P3 coverage with excellent colour accuracy, making it a strong option for artists who want hands-on, stylus-driven control over their work. (Apple, let's not forget, still hasn't given us a touchscreen.)
The trade-offs are noise and heat under sustained rendering loads, plus a proprietary charging port that locks you into ASUS' own cables. Plus, as you might expect, it's pretty darned expensive. With all that in mind, though, we still rate this as the best all-round Windows AI latop for creatives working in motion graphics and VFX today.

For motion design and VFX professionals on a tighter budget, the ASUS TUF Gaming A16 proves that even an entry-level discrete GPU makes a big difference over relying on a CPU's integrated graphics. Its RTX 5050 handles Photoshop and everyday 2D creative work comfortably, and it isn't bad at 3D and AI-assisted apps either, even if it drops behind pricier machines once you move into heavier video editing in Premiere Pro. Its NPU is modest next to the other machines here though, rated at just 16 TOPS, so it's the GPU rather than on-device AI that's doing most of the heavy lifting.
The screen is bright and covers sRGB in full but falls short of the wider DCI-P3 and Adobe RGB gamuts that serious colour work benefits from. For that reason, this laptop is better suited for layout, compositing and general motion graphics than colour-critical grading. Battery life, meanwhile, is a surprise for a gaming laptop, delivering nearly eight hours in everyday use. It's not the best AI laptop for motion graphics and VFX work, but it is an affordable entry point when a full workstation-grade GPU is out of reach.

FAQs
What are the most important specs in an AI motion design laptop?
A capable NPU speeds up on-device AI tools like motion tracking, rotoscoping and frame interpolation without leaning on the cloud or slowing down your render. But let's be clear: GPU performance matters most. As a baseline, aim for at least a mid-range dedicated GPU (an RTX 5050 or Apple's integrated GPU cores are a reasonable floor), 32GB of RAM, a 1TB SSD for large project files and video assets, and an NPU rated at 40 TOPS or above if on-device AI tools are a regular part of your workflow.
What's the cheapest AI laptop for motion design and VFX?
Of the AI laptops on the market right now that are suitable for motion design and VFX, the ASUS TUF Gaming A16 is the most affordable, pairing an entry-level Nvidia RTX 5050 GPU with a capable AMD CPU for a fraction of the cost of the workstation-class options. It won't match the ASUS ProArt P16 or Lenovo Legion Pro 7 for colour accuracy or rendering speed, but its discrete graphics still comfortably outperform integrated-only laptops for 2D creative work and lighter 3D and AI tasks
