# How to Create Infographics Using ChatGPT

Source: [venngage_blog](https://venngage.com/blog/how-to-create-infographics-using-chatgpt/)

OpenAI released ChatGPT Images 2.0 in April 2026, with much better text rendering, instruction following and support for different languages and aspect ratios. These improved text and layout capabilities make it much easier to generate infographics, something AI image generators have traditionally struggled with.
You can now create infographic designs with a simple prompt in ChatGPT, but the final output still depends a lot on what you ask for and how you guide it. In this guide, I’ll show you how to generate infographics using ChatGPT, the prompts that work and where you might still need a dedicated AI infographic generator.
Why use ChatGPT for infographic creation?
ChatGPT Images 2.0 can handle both the information and the visual at the same time. You can give it your content, data or even a rough idea, then use prompts to shape how that information is presented.
Here are some of the capabilities that make it useful for creating infographics:
- Text inside images: ChatGPT can generate headings, labels, statistics and supporting text directly inside the infographic, so you don’t have to add every piece of copy separately.
- Dense text rendering: Infographics often need to fit quite a lot of information into one visual. Images 2.0 is better at rendering denser blocks of text while keeping them readable.
- Stronger instruction following: You can be specific about the structure, visual hierarchy, style and information you want included, and ChatGPT is better at following those directions.
- Images with thinking: For more complex prompts, ChatGPT can spend more time reasoning through the request before generating the image. This can help when you need a structured infographic layout.
- Flexible aspect ratios: You can create vertical, square or horizontal infographics depending on where you plan to use them, whether that’s a report, presentation or social media post.
- Conversational editing: You don’t have to get everything right in the first prompt. Ask ChatGPT to shorten a section, move elements around, change the style or revise specific parts of the infographic and keep refining the same design.
What do you need before creating an infographic with ChatGPT?
Infographics are more about thinking than design. You need to know what you want to say, how to say it clearly and why someone should care enough to read it.
Once you have clarity, designing an infographic becomes the easy part. But without a plan, even the best visuals can fall flat.
And unless you’ve a designer on payroll, it’ll take you a long time to learn the tools and design a good infographic on your own.
But here’s the good news: you don’t need to be a designer to create an infographic. Not anymore.
You just need to know two things before we get started:
- A rough idea of what is an infographic. Think of it as a visual storyboard that explains a complex topic easily.
- A basic grasp of prompt writing. Again, you don’t have to be a “prompt engineer.” But you should know how to give clear instructions to ChatGPT.
If you’ve these fundamentals in place, you’re ready to build an infographic with ChatGPT.
How to create an infographic with ChatGPT (step-by-step process)
Let’s go through the step-by-step process on how to use ChatGPT to create an infographic:
Step 1: Define the topic and audience
Before you write an infographic prompt for ChatGPT, be clear about two things:
- What’s the topic?
- Who’s it for?
This will help you design an infographic around a core point. Without this clarity, you might fall into the trap of trying to say too much and appealing to everyone.
Let’s say you work for a fast-growing marketing agency. Instead of creating another broad infographic on “The State of Digital Marketing,” narrow the idea down to something timely and specific, like “AI Design Trends Shaping 2026.”
The second one is more specific and highly relevant for your audience (i.e. content marketers in growing companies).
Step 2: Gather important information
Next, gather the information you want your infographic to communicate. This could be statistics, industry benchmarks, expert quotes, process steps or key facts.
For example, if your topic is AI Design Trends in 2026, you might collect points like:
- AI becomes part of the design workflow: 54% will prioritize AI-assisted creation.
- AI adoption grows faster than AI maturity: Nearly 70% of marketers use AI, but only 8% have a systemic setup.
- Human + AI aesthetics take over: 59% favor AI illustrated storytelling and 54% are experimenting with hybrid photography.
- Brands push back against the generic AI look: 44% say unrealistic 3D renders already feel overused.
- Consistency becomes the new AI challenge: 43% marketers say keeping AI output on-brand is their biggest headache.
The clearer your topic, audience and supporting information are, the easier it will be to give ChatGPT a prompt it can actually work with.
Step 3: Pick infographic design references
Before you prompt ChatGPT, find a few infographic designs that match the look and feel you want.
Pay attention to things like:
- Layout and information flow
- Color palette
- Illustration or icon style
- Typography
- How much text each section uses
You don’t need to copy a design exactly. The goal is to give ChatGPT a clearer visual direction instead of asking it to decide everything from scratch.
You can then upload the reference to ChatGPT along with your content and ask it to create something with a similar structure or visual style.
Step 4: Define the visual direction
Once you have a few design references, decide which infographic elements you actually want ChatGPT to follow.
Think about:
- Color palette: Brand colors, muted tones, bold contrasts or something more playful
- Visual style: Minimal, editorial, illustrated, corporate or data-heavy
- Icons and illustrations: Simple line icons, flat illustrations, realistic visuals or no icons at all
- Typography: Clean and modern, bold and playful or more formal
- Overall feel: What should someone think or feel when they first see the infographic?
If you have a brand style guide, pull the relevant colors, fonts and visual rules from it. If not, your reference designs can do most of the work.
The important part is to translate those references into instructions ChatGPT can actually follow. Instead of saying “make it look professional,” say something like:
Use a clean vertical layout, navy and cream color palette, simple line icons and bold typography for the key statistics.
That gives ChatGPT far more direction than a vague style request.
Step 5: Ask ChatGPT for infographic design ideas
You don’t have to jump straight from research to the final infographic. Before generating the image, ask ChatGPT to suggest a few visual directions based on your topic, content and references.
This helps you explore different ways to present the same information before committing to one design.
For our AI Design Trends 2026 infographic, you could prompt:
I’m creating an infographic on AI design trends for 2026. Based on the research and design references below, suggest 3 distinct visual concepts. For each concept, recommend the layout, color palette, typography style, illustration or icon approach and how the five trends could be visually separated. Keep the ideas modern, editorial and suitable for a professional audience.
ChatGPT might suggest, for example:
- a futuristic editorial layout with bold typography and modular trend cards
- a more human, tactile style using textured illustrations and softer colors
- a clean data-led design with large statistics and minimal graphics
Pick the direction that best fits your audience and the story you want the infographic to tell. You can then use that concept as the base for your final image prompt.
Step 6: Write a prompt to generate the infographic
Now you’re ready to bring everything together.
If you found an infographic design reference you like, upload it to ChatGPT. Or, if you preferred one of the visual concepts ChatGPT suggested in the previous step, tell it to use that direction.
Then give ChatGPT your content and a few clear instructions about what the final infographic should look like.
A good infographic prompt should cover:
- What you’re creating: The topic and infographic format
- What to include: Your final data points, trends or supporting information
- Visual direction: The design reference or concept you want it to follow
- Hierarchy: What should stand out first
- Style details: Colors, typography, icons or illustrations
- Format: Vertical, horizontal or square, depending on where you’ll use it
For our AI Design Trends 2026 example, here’s the prompt I used:
Prompt example 1
Create a vertical infographic titled “AI Design Trends Shaping 2026” using the research below.
Use the editorial design direction we selected earlier, with bold typography, clean modular sections and a mix of simple illustrations and prominent statistics.
Feature these five trends:
- AI-assisted creation becomes standard: 54% of teams plan to prioritize AI-assisted creation in 2026.
- AI adoption is ahead of AI maturity: Nearly 70% of marketers use AI in some form, but only 8% have a systemic setup.
- Hybrid visual styles gain momentum: 59% are leaning into AI-illustrated storytelling, while 54% are experimenting with human + AI photography.
- The polished AI aesthetic is wearing thin: 44% say unrealistic 3D renders already feel overused.
- Brand consistency becomes a bigger AI concern: 43% say keeping AI-generated output on-brand is their biggest challenge.
Make each trend visually distinct, with the statistic as the most prominent element and a short explanation underneath. Keep the copy concise and easy to scan. Use clear visual hierarchy and enough white space so the infographic doesn’t feel crowded.
