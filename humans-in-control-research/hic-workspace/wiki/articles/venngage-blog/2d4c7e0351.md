# How Much Does PDF Accessibility Cost? Complete Pricing Guide for 2026

Source: [venngage_blog](https://venngage.com/blog/pdf-accessibility-cost/)

The DOJ recently extended the ADA Title II compliance deadline by a year. Public entities serving populations of 50,000 or more now have until April 2027 to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA requirements, while smaller entities and special district governments have until April 2028.
If you’re planning for ADA compliance, one of the first questions you’ll likely ask is: How much does PDF accessibility actually cost?
A complete accessibility budget should account for remediation, accessibility software, staff time, accessibility reviews and documentation audits. Your total cost will also depend on how many PDFs you need to remediate and how complex those documents are.
In this guide, I’ll explain the factors that influence PDF accessibility costs, where organizations typically spend the most time and resources and how an accessibility-first workflow with Venngage can help reduce long-term costs.
What is the cost of PDF accessibility?
While remediation is often the first expense organizations think about, it’s usually just one part of the total cost. Staff time, disconnected tools and inefficient workflows often contribute to PDF accessibility costs.
Here are the major categories you need to consider for an accessibility budget:
1. PDF remediation
The average PDF remediation cost is $5-$25 per page. Every vendor sets its own pricing based on document complexity, volume, turnaround, and their own tooling and QA process.
Here’s what published rates look like across a few known vendors:
The cost of remediation increases with document complexity. A simple PDF with mostly text and images requires far less work than one with tables, charts, forms and complex layouts.
2. Staff time and accessibility reviews
One of the most overlooked PDF accessibility costs is staff time. Accessibility teams spend a lot of time identifying accessibility issues, requesting revisions and coordinating with designers and content creators.
According to Indeed, the average salary of a digital accessibility specialist in the U.S. is $80,210 per year. If you have a large document inventory, you need to hire more accessibility professionals, which increases the total cost.
Outsourcing PDF accessibility to external vendors is often seen as a way to reduce this burden, but it doesn’t reduce the cost in reality. You still need an internal team member to manage the vendor, QA their output and route documents through internal review. That’s a recurring cost on top of your internal staff time.
3. Software and accessibility tools
You might be designing a document in one platform, exporting it as a PDF, editing it in another application, and running an accessibility check using a separate tool. Then going back to the original platform to fix your document.
Every additional tool adds licensing costs. Here’s how it works:
- Outsourced per-page vendors (Accessible.org, Skynet, Allyant, etc.). They have no upfront license cost, but recurring per-document spend.
- In-house licensed tools (Equidox, CommonLook, PREP, Adobe Acrobat Pro). These tools have upfront/annual licensing and offer customized quotes depending on your organization’s requirements.
4. QA testing
Once the PDF is remediated, you need to do a final quality assurance test to ensure it complies with the required WCAG 2.1 AA, Section 508 and PDF/UA requirements.
Third-party vendors usually bundle PDF remediation and QA pricing. However, if you have done remediation in-house, you might have to pay extra for the audit.
There are two ways to do it:
- Self-testing using a PDF accessibility checker: If you want to check a small number of documents, you can use free PDF Accessibility Checkers like Venngage’s Accessible PDF Checker, PAC 2024, axesCheck and VeraPDF. Some of these tools run AI-assisted accessibility. However, you still need a human for reading order, alt-text meaning, and semantic tag review.
- By third-party vendors: If you’re publishing at scale and have hundreds of documents, you need a third-party vendor for QA testing. The pricing for QA testing again varies from vendor to vendor. According to Accessible.org, the cost of a digital accessibility audit is $1,250 to $2,750. DigitalA11Y charges $1,500 to $5,000 for web accessibility audits. PDF accessibility audit might cost you a bit less.
5. Workflow issues
The biggest factor driving PDF accessibility costs across organizations is the traditional review loop. The document goes through multiple rounds of revisions, testing and approvals and accessibility is treated as the final step.
Each time the document re-enters the workflow, designers, content creators and accessibility reviewers need to revisit it before it can move forward. What starts as a simple accessibility fix can quickly turn into multiple rounds of edits and reviews, especially for complex documents or organizations publishing at scale.
These repeated review cycles extend publishing timelines, increase labor costs, delay important communications and create unnecessary operational overhead.
How much does ADA non-compliance cost?
The Department of Justice (DOJ) extended the ADA Title II compliance deadline because many public entities, especially smaller organizations, flagged that they didn’t have the cost, resources, or technical capacity to meet the original timeline.
The extension gives ample time for organizations to plan for PDF accessibility. If you want to reduce PDF accessibility costs, it’s best to start sooner to avoid higher rush surcharges on remediation, emergency vendor fees, and last-minute hires closer to the deadline.
ADA Title II (which covers state and local government) doesn’t have its own civil penalty schedule, but public entities face DOJ enforcement actions and private lawsuits that carry their own financial consequences. Depending on the court’s ruling, you may face:
- Payouts to individuals who were denied access to your PDFs
- Legal fees, both yours and the plaintiff’s, which stack quickly in a drawn-out case
- Ongoing reporting and audits, often for years after settlement
- Loss of federal funding under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act (relevant for public higher ed and any grant-receiving entity)
Even for private organizations that fall under ADA Title III, the civil penalties for non-compliance with ADA are up to $118,225 for first offenses and $236,451 for subsequent violations.
How to reduce PDF accessibility costs?
You can reduce PDF accessibility costs by creating accessible documents from the start, standardizing templates, training teams on accessibility best practices and automating repetitive tasks using PDF accessibility tools.
These changes help minimize remediation, improve efficiency and reduce manual work.
Design for accessibility, not remediation
A design-first, remediate-later mindset is one of the biggest reasons PDF accessibility costs continue to rise. Teams often find themselves stuck in a cycle of designing, reviewing, revising and publishing, only to repeat the process when accessibility issues are identified.
The goal is to move away from this traditional workflow and adopt an accessibility-first approach to create documents.
While you fix existing PDFs to comply with ADA Title II requirements, any new content that you create needs to be accessible. This is where you need to focus on accessibility from the start so you don’t get in a constant loop of PDF creation and remediation
