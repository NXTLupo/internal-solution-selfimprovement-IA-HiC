# AI Isn’t a Tech Challenge—It’s a Culture Challenge

Source: [exs_hro_today](https://www.hrotoday.com/magazine/hro-today/ai-isnt-a-tech-challenge-its-a-culture-challenge/)

Successful AI adoption requires addressing employee identity, building trust, and reimagining the HR playbook from the inside out.
By Sophie Hurcombe
As AI redefines the way people work, HR leaders need to ensure that company culture remains a priority. Organizations can put culture at the forefront of AI adoption by treating readiness as a human challenge rather than a technical one. The companies leading in this space are almost always those that do the cultural work first, defining the values that will guide how these tools are used before they ever deploy them. If there are underlying gaps in trust, transparency, or psychological safety, AI will surface them fast. But if HR leaders build a strong foundation of trust first, AI will only amplify those strengths.
To start, leaders must meet employees where they’re at. People aren’t starting from the same place; some are actively experimenting, while others are still building basic awareness. By understanding where individuals sit on this spectrum, organizations can tailor AI enablement. A personalized approach produces far stronger results than a uniform, top-down roll-out.
Putting culture first also means resisting the urge to treat every roadblock as a technical glitch. When something stalls, the instinct is to look for a process fix or a product gap. More often, the answer lives in the people data. When companies connect AI usage to performance outcomes, feedback quality, and work outputs, a much clearer picture emerges. Organizations that develop the discipline to layer their data this way will spot misalignments earlier and respond more precisely.
Transparency about the “why” is a critical piece to company culture, and it’s often underinvested. When employees understand that AI is being introduced to remove administrative overhead and create space for more meaningful work that only humans can do, the conversation shifts. That transparency is what builds the trust necessary for transformation to take hold.
Rethinking Skills
As AI transforms job roles and how people work, the skills needed to succeed will shift as well. HR can manage this transition by reframing the conversation from “job security” to “identity preservation.” Some employees have spent decades building their craft and are being asked to evolve overnight, without a clear sense of where they’re heading. Beneath most of this AI anxiety is a question about professional identity: When the skills that have defined someone’s value for years become automated, what does their contribution look like going forward? HR needs to engage that question directly as it relates to business goals and company culture.
65% of respondents to a Deloitte study believe their culture needs to change significantly considering the impacts of AI.
Leadership has a responsibility to create a culture and space where employees can voice what they’re experiencing without having their feelings redirected. This AI moment has moved faster than any workplace transformation for many, and each person’s experience of it is shaped by different factors like their role, career history, or what this change means for their sense of professional identity. Sorting employees into any predefined category neglects that complexity. Employees want forums, substantive conversations, and the reassurance that leadership is asking questions with the same attention it gives to adoption timelines.
There are many capabilities that cannot be replaced or replicated by AI—things like judgment, leadership, trust-building, and creativity rooted in lived experience. HR leaders need to help employees identify those strengths and build development pathways around them. The approach in leading Lattice’s internal AI transformation is based on the principle that in order to drive business success in the AI era, the way humans work across the organization needs reinvention. That means investing in each person’s development as their role evolves, while making sure the technology elevates the work people do.
HR leaders need to be the voice in the room asking the human questions. When decisions about workflows, tools, or organizational design are being made with AI in mind, someone needs to advocate for the people impact. That role belongs to HR, and this moment calls to lead with it.
Growing Through Change
To enable employees to grow during this shift, organizations must take two critical actions.
1. Slow down and think. When AI saves an employee five hours a week, the instinct is often to assign them five more hours of transactional tasks. Instead, leaders need to redefine what productivity looks like. Build a culture that encourages employees to reinvest that time into deep focus, strategic planning, or creative problem-solving—the high-value tasks that often get pushed to the end of the day.
2. Treat learning as a core part of the job description. Growth in the AI era requires continuous upskilling. At Lattice, upskilling includes modeling curiosity from the top down and providing structured learning pathways. When organizations treat learning not as an extracurricular activity, but as a critical business deliverable, employees have the psychological safety and the actual time they need to evolve their skills alongside the technology.
The organizations that will thrive won’t simply deploy AI well—they’ll build workplaces where people and AI succeed together.
Sophie Hurcombe is chief people and operations officer at Lattice.
