# Chinese Footwear Brand Xtep Steps Up Running Tech With Aerospace Carbon Plate Technology

Source: [footwear_news](https://wwd.com/footwear-news/shoe-industry-news/xtep-running-tech-aerospace-carbon-plate-maye-musk-1239148686/)

“You are just bouncing on these shoes,” said Maye Musk, the model and bestselling author, sporting the latest Lingyun Max from Xtep, a Chinese footwear brand born in the country’s footwear capital of Fujian over 20 years ago.
Commenting on the ongoing “Chinamaxxing” trend, Musk said that she sees it as an urge to strive for efficiency. “Efficiency makes me happy,” added Musk, who was appearing with Ding Shui Po, founder and chairman of Xtep, during a livestream to promote the new footwear launch.
“’Maxxing’ is embedded in Fujian locals’ genes, we are born ‘maxxing,’ ‘Max ’til we win’ is our anthem,” said Ding. He sees China‘s increasingly competitive sportswear market as an opportunity for the brand to “face the unknown, to explore, and challenge itself,” added Ding, explaining the brand’s “Run to X” ethos.

The unexpected linkup between Xtep and Musk — bringing together a Chinese sportswear brand, a global model and the internet-fueled concept of “Chinamaxxing” — set the tone for the brand’s latest push to bring running into the cultural mainstream.
To fete the launch, Xtep hosted a splashy event in Shanghai to promote its latest releases, which range from professional-grade to casual footwear, all of which feature lightweight styles with bouncy soles.
The star of this year’s new releases, the 160X 8.0 Ultra, is embedded with a carbon plate — dubbed GT1200 — that incorporates cutting-edge technology from China’s Zhongfu Shenying, one of the country’s leading carbon fiber producers. It is the first time the proprietary aerospace material has been used in a consumer product. The carbon plate can help professional runners break through speed thresholds by around four seconds per kilometer, testing data conducted by Xtep’s sports technology lab claimed.
The series, which includes two other styles with differentiated carbon plates and a striking yellow colorway, will see a gradual global release.
“In the AI age, anyone can bootstrap a pair of customized carbon-plated running shoes; how can Xtep create real value and experience for runners and athletes?” said general manager of Xtep‘s Running Category Center Lu Yijia at the launch event. “It’s about unlocking lightweight durability and propulsion at the same time.”
Another key innovation from this year’s lineup is the Xtep Float, which was seen on C-pop idol Fan Chengcheng. Utilizing the proprietary Xtep Float Zero-Gravity Technology and dubbed “defy gravity at ease,” the style falls under the new Lingyun range.
Xtep Group, which operates its core Xtep brand, X Young, and Saucony, as well as Merrell in China, is betting on the running category to facilitate growth in the country’s competitive sportswear market. “Running is the only sport where professionals and the public can share a playing field,” said Ding.
According to Frost & Sullivan’s March 2026 report, Xtep has been China’s number-one marathon running shoe brand by wearing rate for three consecutive years.
Initially described by Chinese netizens as “a ceremonial rite for the middle class,” the sport has grown to include over 140 million runners, according to Xtep. Last year alone, around 600 marathon races were held nationwide, with over 4.5 million participants, Ding shared on stage.
“I’ve always said that running is the sport with the lowest barrier to entry — you can run alone or in a group; you can run indoors or outdoors. Chinese people are running in more diverse scenarios than before — racing, training, jogging, trail running, and everyday running. Previously, one pair of shoes fit all scenarios, but as the market gets more sophisticated, it’s about finding the right shoe, having multiple shoes for each scenario,” added Ding.
Hosted at Shanghai’s lifestyle outdoor retail complex Gate M, Xtep’s “Run to X” event featured a star-studded lineup that also included Chinese celebrities Wang Dongcheng and Enli Yu, as well as local star runners, such as He Jie and Feng Peiyou, who recently broke the Chinese record at the 2026 Tokyo Marathon as the fastest Chinese runner.
