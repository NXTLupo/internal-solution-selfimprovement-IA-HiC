# Google AI Brief: Everything You Need to Know

Source: [wordstream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ai-brief)

It’s no secret that AI has made its way into just about every portion of paid advertising strategy. In some ways, that can be a great thing, with the machines having access to many more data points that can oftentimes lead to better results than what we can do manually. They’re great at finding patterns in the user behaviors of our customers.
But one area that they’re not great at: positioning our businesses in the way we want to be. The AI algorithms can craft compelling messaging that speaks to our customers’ desires, but they’re not always great at making sure that the messaging is compatible with your brand voice and market position.
The solution: AI Brief. It’s a new tool announced by Google in May 2026 that aims to help advertisers leverage AI systems that can get better results, but with direct advertiser input to make sure the messaging Google uses passes the sniff test. Here’s the full rundown.
In the shortest terms, AI Brief is a new feature that lets advertisers provide context to Google’s AI to help give direction on the type of messaging to use, searches to match for, and audiences to engage with.
In the image below, you can see what this new input system looks like. You start by defining your ideal ads. This is a simple open text box for you to give directions in.
There are additional areas to help craft messaging. Specifically, you can add words to avoid, as well as longer messaging to include or exclude from messaging.
While the image doesn’t show it (and I haven’t seen this live in any of my accounts yet), you can also give direction on the queries your ads match to, as well as the audiences you serve for.
This feature was announced for AI Max campaigns in Google Ads, but has since been expanded to include AI Max for Shopping, and will be rolling out to Performance Max as well.
Essentially, any area of the Google Ads platform that leverages the “asset optimization” feature is likely going to get access to the new AI Brief functionality.
AI Max campaigns will now have an AI Brief option during the creation process.

While you are able to give direction on matching and audiences, AI Brief is not intended to be a replacement for keyword targeting, audience signals, Smart Bidding, or landing pages. It’s essentially just another layer of signals that can help Google get you the best results based on your account or campaign goals.
While Google hasn’t released a fixed rollout calendar, AI Brief will roll out in AI Max for Search campaigns (English-only to start) over the coming months before expanding to Performance Max and AI Max for Shopping campaigns. Any of your existing text guidelines will be migrated into AI Brief once the feature is activated in your account(s).
Once that rollout is complete (or close to it), AI Brief functionality will begin rolling out to Performance Max and AI Max for Shopping campaigns.
Ask any of us old-timers; we’ll tell you search behavior has changed quite a bit over the last 10 years. Searches are regularly longer and more conversational than they were in the past. Users are no longer trying to distill their query into the shortest amount of text they can, because the machines can understand their intent much better now. Google can now serve ads to people based on the intent signals it gathers from those queries.
Additionally, Google tends to have a decent handle on what messaging the user wants to see at that moment. The introduction of automated messaging helps businesses serve a compelling message to someone in the midst of a high-intent interaction with Google.
The problem really comes down to making sure that the compelling message Google serves in that high-intent moment is in line with your business. With AI Brief, you’re able to give direction to the AI exactly like you would with a creative agency or copywriter.
At this point, everything AI Brief has to offer probably sounds pretty good. No business is ever too disappointed when they’re able to make sure the brand messaging is followed while getting good results, right?

It’s been said for a while that advertisers in Google Ads are slowly shifting from campaign managers to machine managers to make the campaigns work. AI is a part of every system in the platform, but AI Brief is the most direct example of this shift. Advertisers simply give direction, then lean on the machine to execute.
Again, it’s not a full solution, but it’s the first step in that direction. This can be a great tool for getting better results from AI Max campaigns, but it is also likely a skill advertisers are going to need to develop to see success in the future.
