# How to Advertise on LinkedIn: The Complete Cheat Sheet

Source: [wordstream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/linkedin-advertising)

LinkedIn Ads is one of my favorite platforms. I suggest it to nearly every B2B client we work with and the non-B2B folks as well!
That said, I find that LinkedIn is the platform I hear the most failures from. Many, many people come to me and say they tested on LinkedIn, but they just couldn’t make it work. More often than not, it’s only because they tried to apply best practices and assumptions from other platforms onto LinkedIn, rather than adjusting for the unique channel that it is.
In this comprehensive LinkedIn advertising guide, I’ll review everything you need to know to get started.
LinkedIn advertising is the practice of placing paid promotions for your business across the LinkedIn platform, which is a social media site and app that’s geared towards professionals, students, and businesses of all stages and industries. Advertising on LinkedIn is an effective way for both B2B and B2C businesses to reach prospective customers. LinkedIn has nearly 900 million active users, so this is a major paid channel you’ll want to consider when planning your marketing strategy.

LinkedIn is one of the biggest social media platforms, so odds are, your ideal customers are on there and ready to see your business’s LinkedIn ads. After all, LinkedIn is the most trusted social network in the US.
Businesses trust and like this platform just as much as consumers, too. It’s been found that LinkedIn is the third most popular platform for small business marketing, with 48% of businesses currently using it.
Plus, LinkedIn Ads offers tons of different ad format options for you to reach your target audience. In fact, there are many to consider—even some that aren’t available anywhere else. With all that diversity, there’s a lot you can do to stand out.
As for lead generation, LinkedIn Ads can be beneficial in that regard as well. I’ve been in the advertising space for well over 10 years now, and for lead-generating ads, nothing holds a candle to LinkedIn. The targeting options are by far the best, and the results are the most accurate when trying to reach a certain persona.

LinkedIn has many different ad formats you can select from, and each has a different look and feel, and some have different placements they can show in.
These are likely the ads you see the most (or second most) in your personal newsfeed because they’re the most common on the platform. They leverage a single image along with some introductory text, a headline, and a call to action.
Video ads are very similar to single image ads except the creatives you can use are, you guessed it, videos instead of images.
Carousel ads allow advertisers to add multiple images (cards) to the same ad creative that the end user can swipe through. Each card can have its own headline and destination URL, so they’re highly customizable.
Text ads are a very small, throwback-style ad format that leverages only a very short headline, description, and a small thumbnail image.
Message ads operate similarly to a user-to-user message on LinkedIn. They show up in their inbox, so they can feel a bit more invasive than the other ad formats, so you need to earn that privilege.
Conversation ads are effectively Message Ads but souped up. They allow for more of a chat-like experience and give you the option for multiple call-to-action buttons that can help the user effectively “choose their own adventure”.
Document Ads are one of the newer formats in recent years and, quite frankly, I’m a huge fan of them. This format lets you upload a document, a PDF, for example, and tease a portion of the document, maybe the first 2-3 pages, as the ad creative, then require the user to fill out a form to get access to the rest.
Within Dynamic ads, there are three separate options: Follower, Spotlight, and Jobs Ads. Each is designed to do pretty much what the name says and uses the user’s information in the ad itself.
With the exception of a new beta test, all ad sets only let you use one ad format. So if you want to test Image ads and Carousel ads, you would need to create two different ad sets. While this might not seem like a big deal, that means your budget will be separated by ad set.
If you have too many different ad formats being tested (and you’re not using campaign-level budget controls), you may run into an issue with having too little budget for each ad set.
In my opinion, the targeting options on LinkedIn are really the bread and butter of the platform. The specificity we can leverage just makes this platform a no-brainer for many businesses we work with, but the targeting is only good if you know how to use it!
The first thing you need to know is the highest-level breakdown of how targeting works. In the image below, the biggest categorization is audiences versus audience attributes. As a shorthand, Audiences means a group of users you’ve put together by one method or another, and audience attributes are targetable characteristics of that user based on what LinkedIn knows about them from their profile, behaviors, etc.
Now that we have that out of the way, here’s a rundown of the majority of targeting options on LinkedIn Ads:
This feature allows you to upload a CSV file of companies or individual users to target on the LinkedIn platform. You start by downloading the templates provided, add your information, and make sure your formatting is correct; then LinkedIn will try to find those users or companies based on the info you provided.
For users, you’ll add first and last name, email, and other fields (if you know them) like job title, company, etc. LinkedIn will then match the information you have to users on the platform and compile them into a list for you to target. Super slick and very easy.
These audiences (company or contacts) can be used for upselling, winning back lost accounts, or supporting an ABM campaign.
These audiences let you leverage LinkedIn’s AI and platform data to build audiences that are similar to your existing audiences or converters.
Predictive audiences let you select your source audience, then target location(s), then find new users LinkedIn believes are similar. This could be a good option for expanding your reach beyond your known contacts while honing in on their characteristics to maintain quality.
On the whole, retargeting is advertising to a list of users who have already engaged with your company in one way, shape, or form. LinkedIn gives tons of different ways you can do that.
The most common, and the retargeting you’ve likely heard of, is website visitors. Here, you’ll make a list of everyone who has been to your site before (or a subset of them based on certain pages, timing, etc.) and then use them as a targeting list in your account.
But there are other options for retargeting as well.
On LinkedIn, you can still use website audiences, but you can also retarget on all of the different sources above. If someone engaged with your video ad, submitted a lead gen form, responded to an event ad, or any of the other options above, you can make a list of them and target them in your campaigns.
The last option I want to cover in the Audiences section is a third-party list.
These are still lists of users and can be companies or contacts, but the thing that sets them apart is their source.
Rather than downloading a CSV from your CRM and then uploading it to the LinkedIn Ads platform, you can simply integrate your CRM to automatically sync audiences. This is a huge time saver and can help keep your lists fresh so long as your CRM regularly adds new users and purges old users from those lists.
