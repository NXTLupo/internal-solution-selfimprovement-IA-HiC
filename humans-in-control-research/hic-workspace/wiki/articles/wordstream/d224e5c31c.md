# Display & Video Ads Benchmarks 2026: Exclusive Data & Insights for Every Industry

Source: [wordstream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/display-video-ads-benchmarks)

A picture (or a gif, or a video…you get the idea) is worth a thousand words, which is partly why display ads are such a valuable strategy for so many businesses. In fact, 74% of small businesses are satisfied with their display advertising results.
However, like all digital marketing strategies, it comes with challenges. Nailing down display ad campaign performance can be tricky. Since these media-based ads show passively across sites as people surf online, they pack on impression numbers quickly.
To help navigate this lucrative but complex channel, we analyzed over 60,000 LocaliQ display and video advertising campaigns from two different data sources to bring you our first-ever Display & Video Advertising Benchmarks, including performance averages for key metrics like click-through rate, cost per click, cost per lead, and viewability.
As mentioned, the data is split two ways: industry benchmarks covering display ads running across properties like Google, Microsoft, and YouTube, then overall performance averages for display ads running solely across the USA TODAY Network.
You can use these benchmarks to gauge how your display ads are performing and find out where you may need to improve—whether it be your audience targeting or display ad creative.
Let’s get into it!

This report contains our new display and video ads benchmark data to help you better understand and maximize your campaign performance and budget. A couple of things to know about this report:
The metrics we chose to look at are specific to the nature of display ads. Display ads impact conversion rates indirectly (which is a measurement called view-through conversions), but they naturally have lower conversion rates attached to them directly, which is why we decided to omit that metric.
“My biggest advice when evaluating visual ad formats is to honor that they might not be delivering conversions themselves. Even when they do, they should not be held to the same benchmarks as search formats,” said Navah Hopkins, Ad Product Liaison at Microsoft.
Meanwhile, display ads yield tons of impressions, so even though their click-through rates look quite low if you compare them to other tactics like search ads, that can still equate to a lot of impactful clicks in the grand scheme of impressions versus clicks.
“Most people aren’t going to immediately click a display ad. They’ll come back to your site directly, weeks or months later, when they need you,” said Greg Carter, Vice President of Marketing at Clutch.
Ultimately, you want your display ads to get seen (or watched) and turn into real results, which is why we included viewability, video completion rate, cost per click, and cost per lead.
For our first round of display ads benchmarks, we’ll look at click-through rate, cost per click, and cost per lead for standard display campaigns broken down by industry. These ads run on over 350 exchanges, networks, and publishers, including Google, YouTube, Meta, and Yahoo, and include multiple formats, such as banner ads, video, and more.

While click-through rate isn’t a make-or-break metric for display ads (because they’ll naturally yield way more impressions than clicks), it can be an initial indicator of how enticing or clickable your image or video creative is.
The industry with the highest click-through rate for display ads was Education and Instruction at 0.40%, followed by Arts and Entertainment at 0.27%. Arts and Entertainment is a very visual industry to begin with, so it makes sense that people would be more likely to click on those ads.
Costs per click and lead for display ads vary. Typically, clicks are cheaper for this ad type compared to search ads. Beauty and Personal Care had the highest cost per click at $5.58, which checks out, as this particular industry is extremely competitive.

Ready to take your display and video ads to the next level? Here are some display advertising tips (based on this data) to help you get started.
On average, all of the display ad types we covered in this report brought in a good number of clicks and views. For maximum reach, be sure to have display ad creative that’s flexible across major placements on the Google Display Network (including YouTube), Microsoft Audience Network, USA TODAY Network, and more.
“Display can add depth to your strategy—just simply getting in front of your target audience and reinforcing your position in their mind is deeply valuable,” said Brett McHale, Founder of Empiric Marketing.
It can be easy to let your display campaigns fall by the wayside when planning your budgets, but it’s not that search ads are better than display or vice versa; they’re complementary to one another. Your display ads are part of your pre-marketing strategy, meaning you want them to be familiar with your business before it’s even time to buy the types of products and services you offer. This drives users to search for your business later.
“What makes display advertising so useful is that it can be leveraged at the beginning of the customer journey to inspire awareness, just as easily as it can remind a customer to complete their transaction towards the end with remarketing,” Navah said.
So, while you can factor in that display ads have a lower average CPC than search ads, you’ll want to treat budget allocations similarly. With display ads, you’re often paying per view or impression, rather than per click. You’ll need a competitive budget to get seen as much as possible.
“One way to improve display performance is to have a larger budget allocated for these types of campaigns. More budget means more impressions, and more data to optimize with,” said Rhett Chassereau, Supervisor of Programmatic Buying & Optimization at USA TODAY Co.
While you’ll want to leverage multiple placement types across networks in your display strategy, you should also constantly test various creative elements. For example, see if your audience is more receptive to certain color schemes, image types, calls to action, and more.
“Cycle in new creative and make multiple copy variations for each. Launch a number of ads and find out which one performs the best,” Brett said.
“From there, you can take the top-performing and make more variations of them,” he added.
Like anything with PPC advertising, testing your display ads will be a bit of a balancing act that requires both patience and agility.
“Give the channel time, then be proactive about your display ad creative. Judge individual ads fast and rotate them out as soon as the data is clear. But judge overall display campaigns more cautiously, because they’re a brand play that won’t necessarily pay back on a short timeline,” Greg said.
AI, when used thoughtfully, can help you quickly scale your display campaigns. For instance, it can help you brainstorm test ideas or quickly duplicate and tweak current creative your team has designed. Just be sure to give any AI-generated display assets a final, manual edit before you set them live to avoid any glaring AI fails or off-brand elements.
Whatever your industry, you can make display ads work for your business. Display ads help you reach a wide but highly qualified audience. You can remarket to potential customers already interested in your business and pre-market to brand-new potential customers, too. Just be ready to flex your display strategy according to test results and placement trends, so your display ads always catch the attention you’re looking to grab.
