# A Proven Instagram Strategy for Business Growth

Source: [social_media_examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/a-proven-instagram-strategy-for-business-growth/)

Wondering why your Instagram content isn't converting the way it used to? Curious what separates the accounts that consistently drive revenue from those stuck chasing views?
In this article, you'll discover a three-part Instagram marketing strategy for 2026 that combines authentic content creation, sustainable posting systems, and a simplified approach to Meta paid ads that amplifies organic results.
Why Instagram Feels Different in 2026
Instagram's algorithm in 2026 has undergone one of the most significant shifts in the platform's history, driven largely by a flood of AI-generated content that often lacks the depth and originality audiences respond to. Audiences are questioning whether content is AI-generated, and when that suspicion takes over, the creator's actual message gets lost entirely.
Trial reels have added another layer of change. The feature has become a testing ground for creators willing to A/B test hooks, wording, and formats before committing a reel to their main feed. Ellen now tests all of her reels as trial reels, running two to three variations (occasionally four) to see which hook or phrasing resonates most. The opportunity is real, but the feature has also made it easier for some creators to mass-produce content.
The result is an algorithm overwhelmed with volume. Reach has dipped for many business owners, and the competition for attention has intensified. In fact, 2026 is the first year Ellen has had to temper her usual advice of “just focus on making better content” with a recognition that the playing field has genuinely shifted.
Still, Instagram remains her primary revenue-driving platform, and the strategy she outlines in this episode reflects how she has adapted.
#1: Create Natural-Feeling Instagram Content
The first layer of any Instagram strategy starts with the content itself. Without strong foundational content, no amount of ad spend or advanced tactics will make up for it. Trial reels, paid ads, and growth hacks are great acceleration layers, but they require something worth accelerating.
Today, audiences are rejecting the perfectly curated aesthetic that dominated Instagram for years. High-production brand shoots, carefully designed Canva graphics, and polished video setups are underperforming compared to content that feels spontaneous and real.
Ellen has tested polished against unpolished content repeatedly in her own account. Every time she posts a heavily designed carousel, engagement drops. When she posts a casual phone photo of herself with text overlaid, engagement climbs. One recent carousel featuring a selfie with a miniature pony at a weekend cafe reached 50,000 views, more than double her follower count.
JessiJeanHome’s account is a prime example of this shift. The entrepreneur and creator launched her Yap Challenge, which encourages participants to show up on camera and tell their stories in an unscripted, casual format.
Over two months and two launches, the challenge generated over $7 million in US sales. Ellen points to that number as evidence of both the revenue potential of authenticity-first content and the market demand for it. People want to hear from real humans, and marketers are willing to pay for guidance on how to show up that way.
Pro Tip: It’s important to note that natural-feeling doesn’t exclude the use of AI in Ellen’s process. She uses AI, specifically Claude, as a collaborative content partner. She has trained her AI with five years of her own content, opinions, and frameworks, so when she brings a half-formed idea or a rant about something she noticed on the platform, the AI helps shape it into a structured post. She brings the idea, the opinion, and the human experience grounded in real interactions with clients, students, and her own observations. The AI refines and formats.
How to Create Natural-Feeling Instagram Reels
For reels, Ellen recommends placing the camera off to the side during a natural activity rather than staring directly into a lens because many people adopt an unnatural tone and cadence when the camera goes live.
A florist might set the phone to the side while arranging flowers and talk about a particular bloom mid-arrangement. A podcaster might record during a conversation, then pull 30-second clips that capture a natural flow.

How to Create Natural-Feeling Instagram Carousels
Carousels are currently Ellen's highest-performing content format. Her approach mirrors the same authenticity principle seen in her reels: casual phone photos with text overlaid, rather than designed graphics.
She makes the carousels in Canva for easy duplication and repurposing, but the visual style is deliberately unpolished. She uses a 4:5 aspect ratio to maximize screen real estate. The photos come from daily life: selfies, snapshots from her workspace, images from weekends, or travel. The text is a simple black font on a white background, prioritizing readability over brand aesthetics.
Ellen points to the Instagram account FriendsThatInvest as another strong example of this approach. The creator, who has 400,000 followers, posts carousels using candid photos from holidays, cooking at home, and everyday moments, with simple text overlaid. Despite her large following, the content feels personal and intimate because of its casual visual style.
For those who struggle with knowing which images to pair with which content, Ellen suggests using AI as a brainstorming partner: feed it a batch of photos and a topic, and ask for pairing suggestions. She also says the images themselves should stay real and unmanipulated; using AI to generate or alter photos risks triggering the same audience skepticism that undermines polished content.
How to Find Your Format Mix and Posting Cadence With Instagram Metrics
Ellen recommends alternating between carousels and reels, posting one of each in rotation. Your exact ratio should follow the data: if analytics show carousels consistently outperform reels for a given audience, shift to a 3:1 carousel-to-reel ratio for a month and measure the results.

I'M READY FOR REAL AI RESULTSWhen evaluating content performance, Ellen doesn't look at likes. The shift away from likes as a primary metric aligns with broader audience behavior. People aren't liking posts the way they used to, but they will comment on content that offers something they want and save posts they plan to return to. The metrics that indicate genuine resonance are comments, saves, and shares.
Ellen tested four trial reel variations for a recent freebie launch. The version she selected for her main feed wasn't the one with the most views or likes. It was the one with the most comments and saves, because those signals indicated the content had genuinely connected with her audience.
Another of her posts received modest likes but generated 200 comments from people who triggered her ManyChat automation to access a digital product link.
Pro Tip: Ellen recommends approaching analytics with emotional distance, treating metrics as data rather than personal validation. A post with low views isn't evidence of failure; it's information about what didn't resonate. That analytical mindset keeps frustration from leaking into the content itself, where low energy and visible discouragement directly translate into lower-performing reels.
#2: Build Systems That Support Consistent Posting
Consistency is the second layer of the strategy, but Ellen defines it as showing up on a sustainable schedule with a system that supports that schedule, not matching the output of full-time influencers whose entire business model revolves around posting volume.
Ellen's own system involves a bi-weekly filming day. She blocks the calendar, takes no meetings, and spends the day creating video content for Instagram, YouTube, and her podcast, while also capturing photos for future carousels. A second day is dedicated to editing and assembling carousel posts. This batching approach concentrates creative energy into focused blocks rather than scattering it across the week.
For business owners building their own systems, Ellen recommends two tactics.
The first is to attach your content creation to an existing routine. She cites a skincare creator who films a video every day during her skincare routine, and notes Michael Stelzner's unscripted three-minute videos recorded on his front porch at 5:50 each morning. The content itself is unedited and authentic, and the routine removes the friction of deciding when or how to create.
The second is to set a phone alarm every 60 minutes as a reminder to snap a photo of whatever is happening during your work or content-creating days, whether it's packing orders, sitting at a desk, or grabbing coffee. Those images can be reused with different text for different topics, across weeks of carousels.
