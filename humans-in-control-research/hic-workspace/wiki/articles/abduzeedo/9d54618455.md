# Best of the Week: Monograms, Serifs, and Tactile Systems

Source: [abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/best-week-monograms-serifs-and-tactile-systems/)

Best of the Week: Monograms, Serifs, and Tactile Systems
Our curated roundup of the best of the week highlights architectural monograms, editorial serif fonts, and tactile apothecary packaging from ISO week 2026-W40.
This week’s editorial throughline explores the dynamic balance between structural typographic discipline and tactile materiality. Across stark monochrome legal monograms, apothecary aluminum cans, and optical dot-matrix posters, designers prioritized rigorous modular frameworks, bespoke glyph anatomy, and tangible print finishes over superficial digital ornament.
Featured Design and Branding Picks
PCK Intellectual Property by Brand Brothers
 Parisian studio Brand Brothers disrupted the traditionally conservative visual tropes of North American IP law by deploying a high-contrast typographic monogram with an interlocking C-K ligature. The identity establishes a stark monochrome visual territory where wide-stance geometry and razor-sharp baseline cuts project technical precision and structural stability. The system scales effortlessly from heavy blind-embossed stationery to monumental architectural signage.
View Feature
Gestura Typeface by Sociotype Foundry
 Sociotype Foundry introduced Gestura, an editorial serif family that navigates the delicate historical tension between 18th-century rationalist proportions and organic calligraphic brush dynamics. Engineered alongside the critical essays of Sociotype Journal, the typeface transitions from hairline display cuts with expressive terminals down to robust, highly legible text grades. Its sharp bracketed serifs and asymmetric counterforms bring intellectual poise to high-density publishing.
View Feature
Himkok RTD by OlssønBarbieri
 Oslo-based OlssønBarbieri translated the world-renowned speakeasy cocktail bar into a ready-to-drink format utilizing tactile matte aluminum cans. The design rejects colorful FMCG tropes in favor of an apothecary-inspired information hierarchy, complete with technical distillation metadata, bespoke botanical icons, and micro-typographic formulation tables. Raised tactile varnishes and raw metallic finishes the humble beverage can into a collectible Nordic design artifact.
View Feature
ATELIER CERVANTES by SAVVY STUDIO
 SAVVY STUDIO created a masterclass in editorial book design for curatorial arts laboratory Atelier Cervantes. The monograph pairs rational geometric margin proportions with expansive poetic whitespace, allowing the contemplative photography of Clément Pascal to breathe. Printed on tactile uncoated fine-art paper stocks with thread-sewn lay-flat binding and subtle blind-debossed cover typography, the publication turns arts consultation into an intimate physical experience.
View Feature
Kelvin Zero by Cuberto
 Cuberto engineered a nocturnal digital experience for cybersecurity and cryptography firm Kelvin Zero, defined by obsidian fields and precision optical telemetry grids. Real-time WebGL distortion shaders respond fluidly to cursor movement, transforming complex cryptographic hash streams and telemetry data into sophisticated interactive ornament. The result is a tactile, dark-mode web environment that communicates computational security through restraint rather than gratuitous 3D excess.
View Feature
FOLLOW THE DOT by Molnár Péter
 Hungarian graphic artist Molnár Péter explored optical raster mechanics in FOLLOW THE DOT, an uncompromising series of silkscreened typographic studies. By varying halftone dot frequencies and mathematical raster diameters, the compositions generate hypnotic letterforms that shift dramatically between micro-inspection and monumental distance. The tactile porous bleed of heavy black screenprint ink onto newsprint grounds the digital raster logic in physical print craftsmanship.
View Feature
Voluta by Tiago Araujo
 Brazilian designer Tiago Araujo translated national modernist architectural heritage into a modular visual identity for furniture restoration studio Voluta. Letterforms draw structural inspiration from Oscar Niemeyer's expressive concrete curves, unfolding into modular graphic patterns reminiscent of architectural cobogó breeze blocks. Paired with warm terracotta stationery, blind-debossed authenticity certificates, and ceramic artifact tagging, the system merges physical materiality with historic modernism.
View Feature
Cinéma Cinéma by Deux Huit Huit
 Montreal studio Deux Huit Huit crafted a chameleon editorial identity system for cultural platform Cinéma Cinéma. Centered around high-impact condensed typography inspired by mid-century French New Wave movie marquees, the framing device expands and contracts to frame diverse programming stills without diluting institutional identity. Stark monochrome editorial broadsheets, newsprint festival schedules, and dynamic street posters how typographic hierarchy commands cultural space.
View Feature
LÔCÔ Art Market by M — N Associates
 Saigon-based M — N Associates brought industrial energy to the LÔCÔ Art Market, combining vernacular factory signage aesthetics with bilingual typographic wit. The identity utilizes custom high-contrast stencils, industrial rubber-stamp motifs, and silkscreened fluorescent exhibition posters that celebrate manual labor and craft. Rather than relying on generic art market tropes, the studio built an interactive collateral suite that bridges local street culture with contemporary brand architecture.
View Project
Flynny’s by Studio South
 Auckland practice Studio South designed an authentic visual identity and packaging suite for artisan delicatessen Flynny’s. Striking a delicate balance between nostalgic European corner-store warmth and contemporary hospitality branding, the identity incorporates custom illustrative typography and tactile greaseproof butcher wraps. The unpretentious hand-lettered lockups and unbleached Kraft paper bags give the brand an honest, tactile presence that stands out against over-sanitized gourmet retail styling.
View Project
Melius by View Source
 Design studio View Source engineered a serene, evidence-led visual identity for precision health and longevity platform Melius. Departing from typical biotech tech-gradients and clinical clutter, the system deploys an understated editorial grid hierarchy, delicate grotesque typography, and architectural packaging vessels. The identity achieves an authoritative bedside presence that frames advanced biometric telemetry with humanistic calmness and material clarity.
