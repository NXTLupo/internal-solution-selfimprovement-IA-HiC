More than 14 million people in the U.S. deal with rosacea, according to Johns Hopkins Medicine, which is a skin condition marked by long-term redness, a flushed-looking face, broken blood vessels, a burning sensation, and the occasional pus-filled bumps. If you have fair skin or are between the ages of 30 or 60, you’re more likely to experience rosacea, and though the cause of it is not known, rosacea is more likely to flare up with extreme temperatures, stress, the ingestion of spicy foods, and other factors. What to Look for in the Best Sunscreens for Rosacea
Mineral vs. chemical composition: Sunscreens can be sorted into two main buckets based on how they sit on skin — mineral or chemical. Mineral sunscreens sit on top of skin to create a physical barrier of sorts while reflecting back UV rays and scattering them away from the face; chemical sunscreens, on the other hand, absorb into skin just as they absorb said rays, converting them into heat that can be then released by your skin back into the atmosphere.
Mineral and not chemical sunscreens are most beneficial for sensitive and rosacea-prone skin, as they don’t contain chemicals, don’t clog pores, don’t trigger those telltale rosacea “flushes,” and don’t permeate the barrier. That being said, they’re not completely flawless, either — oftentimes being less lightweight than the alternative and harder to spread. Mineral sunscreens typically include the UV filters zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, so look for these ingredients when you’re shopping for the best sunscreens for rosacea.
Fragrances: By nature, rosacea is an inflammatory skin condition that can get worse with even minor exposure to irritators like fragrances. Translation: For best results, see to it that the sunscreen you end up buying doesn’t have a strong scent (not to mention coming with labels like “noncomedogenic,” “oil-free,” “alcohol-free,” and other signifiers of a clean, safe, gentle formulation that will leave flare-ups in the rearview mirror).
Color correction: It’s completely your choice whether to go with a color-correcting sunscreen for rosacea or stick to a basic white one, but we think green-tinted options really go the extra mile to address the top priority in question — your rosacea. They do so by neutralizing red undertones, and the most worthy ones manage to do so without that dreaded white cast. Tinted sunscreens across common skin shades also act as a cover-up layer for the redness while evening out skin tone.
Rosacea-friendly ingredients: Most sunscreens these days are backed by ingredients that aren’t necessarily crucial to sun protection but work to improve skin in a more holistic sense.
