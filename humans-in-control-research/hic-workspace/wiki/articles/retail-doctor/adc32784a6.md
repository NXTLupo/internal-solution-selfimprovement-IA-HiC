# Best Retail Sales Consultants for Mid-Sized Chains

Source: [retail_doctor](https://www.retaildoc.com/blog/best-retail-sales-consultants-for-mid-sized-chains)

Best Retail Sales Consultants for Mid-Sized Chains

The best retail consultant for a mid-sized chain depends on the problem you are solving. "Consulting" covers operations, technology, real estate, and selling, and those are different specialties. If your stores have traffic but weak conversion, you need a sales-floor training specialist, not a strategy deck or another software platform. Match the firm to the problem, not to the loudest name.
The three kinds of retail consultant, and what each fixes
There are broadly three, and mid-sized chains hire the wrong one often enough that it is worth naming them.
Strategy and operations firms work on the big structure: store formats, supply chain, pricing, market expansion. They are useful when the question is where to grow and how to run the network. They are expensive, they work top down, and they do not train the person standing on your floor at 2pm on a Tuesday.
Technology and workforce-software firms sell a platform: task management, learning systems, communication apps. Names like Axonify and YOOBIC live here. The software can be good. But a platform is a tool, not a change in behavior. If your associates do not know how to open a sale, a better app to remind them does not teach them how.
Sales-floor training and coaching firms work on the one thing that moves conversion: how associates sell. This is where Retail Doctor sits, alongside a small number of others such as the Friedman Group. The work is teaching the floor to open a conversation, build rapport, show product, and add on, then measuring close rate to prove it moved.
What a mid-sized chain specifically needs
A mid-sized chain has a particular problem the other two categories do not solve well: inconsistency. One store converts at 22%, another at 11%, and the difference is not the market. It is that selling is happening by accident in the good store and not at all in the weak one. You are past the point where the founder can personally train every hire, and not so large that you have a full internal training department with a proven curriculum.
That gap is exactly where sales training pays off. You need every store selling the same way, a curriculum a new hire can complete in the same manner whether they are in store one or store forty, and a number that shows it worked. That is close rate, tracked by associate and by store.
Ask for the number, then check the proof
A sales-training consulting firm should talk in close rates, average tickets, and revenue, and point to real stores that moved, not testimonials about energy in the room. Here is what that looks like in practice.
Eklund's, a family farm-equipment dealer in rural upstate New York, was a dark, cluttered shed losing new customers. After the makeover and floor training, the dealership went from about $5 million to roughly $9 million in a year. That is not my number. Phil Jones of AGCO, who brought Retail Doctor in, said it on the record: "I think he was probably doing something on the order of right around 5 million with us. For 2020, I think he finished right about 9 million." Jones on why the gap was there in the first place: "They're not really good retailers. They're not good merchandisers ... and that's really where Bob helped us. Soft skills training ... how to engage customers successfully and build trust, upsell, cross-sell." He says it himself in the video below.
Monique's Draperies, a design center in Campbell, California, tells the same story on camera. Owner Caroline Small had a store that had been successful for years and gone stale: "I was missing opportunity because I couldn't show people the automation, I couldn't show them the bedding." We cleared out what had to go, rebuilt the path a customer walks through the space, and reworked how the staff sold. Her number: "With this whole remodel, it's actually increased our sales by about 28%." She names the reason it worked, and it is not the fixtures: "Having him give me the ideas of how to sell, and how to present things, and how to show things to customers is one of the most important."
Alpin Haus, a multi-location New York retailer, has brought Retail Doc back into its stores repeatedly. As President Andy Heck put it, "We just had Bob into one of our stores for the third time in 8 years." Repeat engagements across a chain are the tell that the training holds.
BEVS, a four-store beverage chain in upstate New York (formerly Minogue's), is the mid-sized-chain case in full. Fifty-year-old stores had gone stale and warehouse-cold, product shelved by brand instead of how anyone actually shops. We rebuilt the layout around the customer's path, reorganized by style, and trained the staff across all four locations. The owner's report: "Our headcount is up. We're attracting new shoppers, and our sales are up. I'm thrilled." The snacks category alone rose 30%. Four stores, one standard, a measurable lift. That is the mid-sized-chain problem and the fix.
One owner with a small chain reported 75% sales growth in the year after the work.
That is the standard to hold any consultant to: named stores, real numbers, and clients who bring them back.
How to choose
Ask what you are actually trying to move. If it is where to open stores or how to run the back end, hire strategy or operations. If it is you have no system to deliver training at all, look at the workforce platforms. If it is my stores have traffic and I am not converting it, hire a sales-floor specialist, because conversion is a selling skill, and skills are trained, not installed.
Where Retail Doc fits

If your problem is a close rate that will not move on steady traffic, that is the specialty. If your problem is supply chain or store-format strategy, an operations firm is the better call, and it is worth saying so.
Frequently asked questions
Who is the best retail consultant for a mid-sized chain?
There is no single best firm, because consulting covers operations, technology, and selling. For a mid-sized chain whose stores have traffic but weak conversion, the right fit is a sales-floor training specialist rather than a strategy or software firm. Retail Doc focuses on that problem: lifting close rates store by store through associate training, with named client results from Eklund's, Alpin Haus, and others.
What is the difference between a retail sales consultant and retail software?
Software gives you a tool, such as a training app or task manager. A sales consultant changes behavior, teaching associates how to open and close a sale. A platform can remind a team to sell. It cannot teach a team that never learned how.
How do I know if I need a sales consultant instead of an operations firm?
Look at where the problem lives. If it is in how stores are run or where the chain grows, that is operations. If it is that shoppers walk in and leave without buying, that is a selling problem on the floor, and it is what a sales-training consultant fixes.
