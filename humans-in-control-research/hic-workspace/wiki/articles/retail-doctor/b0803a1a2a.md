# 7 Cringe-Inducing Behaviors That Push Retail Customers Away

Source: [retail_doctor](https://www.retaildoc.com/blog/7-cringe-inducing-behaviors-that-push-retail-customers-away)

Customers leave and never come back because of small, everyday habits on your floor, not because of Amazon or your prices. Traffic is down, shoppers are waiting for the deal, and it is easy to blame the economy. Walk your own floor and watch what your people actually do. Here are seven behaviors that quietly push shoppers out the door, and the fix for each.
1. "How are you today?" called from behind the counter
The intent is right. The execution kills it. A greeting yelled across the store as someone walks in, before they have had a second to get their bearings, lands as noise. They cannot see who it came from or who it was aimed at, so they ignore it, and now the next person who approaches them starts in a hole.
The fix: greet with the right words, and only when you can make eye contact with no counter between you.
2. Reaching for a handshake with "Hi, I'm ___, and you're?"
Few things are creepier to watch. It is a 1950s car-lot move. A handshake is earned, not forced, and grabbing for one from a stranger reads as anything but personal.
The fix: keep your hand to yourself. Do the job and they will reach for your hand at the end of the sale.
3. "Do you want to see it?"
Your whole presentation should get the customer to touch it, try it, hold it. Ask "do you want to see it?" and the answer is usually no, and you just talked yourself out of the sale.
The fix: take it off the rack, open the case, put it in their hands, and say, "Try it."
4. "No, it shows we don't have it"
Telling a shopper no from a tablet is faster than a trip to the stockroom and just as wrong. It ends the sale with no alternative. A great shoe salesperson who knows the stock comes back with something: the same shoe in another color, a size up just to see if they like it, anything but no.
The fix: if an associate checks a tablet, they have to come back with an alternative, never just "we don't have it."
5. "Here's my card, ask for me when you come back or I won't get credit"
This is a loser's limp. You did not close the sale but you want the credit as if you did. Handing over your card looks desperate and leaves the customer feeling guilty, and a customer who feels that way does not return.
The fix: once someone leaves, the sale belongs to whoever closes it. Close it now.
6. "These were on sale"
A shopper reaches the register ready to pay full price and the cashier tells them it is on sale. It feels friendly. It robs you. They should have seen it on the tag or a sign and had the chance to buy two. When the price is reduced you need to move more units, and sending them out with one costs you the margin.
The fix: sign every promotion so shoppers see it on the floor, and use the sale to move more before they reach the register.
7. "That was my sale"
Commission is a good thing. It rewards the person who closes. But an inexperienced manager lets commissioned people fight over credit on the floor, and shoppers hear it. Two associates snapping over a scrap of a sale, like I once heard at a national department store, and those shoppers want nothing to do with either of them or your store again.
The fix: any conversation about credit happens in private, never within earshot of a customer.
Why these push customers away
Every one of them stalls the sale and drags your conversion rate down with it. None of them is rude. Nobody would get written up for any of it, which is exactly what makes it hard to see and easy to let slide. It is not a customer service problem, it is a selling problem, and selling is trained.
People have not changed. They still buy from someone they trust. Fix these seven and you convert more of the traffic you already paid to get in the door. That is what SalesRX trains your floor to do.
Frequently asked questions
Why do customers leave a store without buying?
Usually because of a small behavior on the floor, not price and not the website. A greeting yelled from the counter, a forced handshake, a flat "we don't have it," a business card at the door. Each one stalls the sale, and the shopper leaves feeling unhelped.
What is the worst thing a retail salesperson can say?
"Here's my card, ask for me when you come back." It admits the sale was not closed and asks the customer to do the closing later, which they never do. Close the sale while the customer is in front of you.
How should you greet a retail customer?
With real words, eye contact, and no barrier between you, and only once they have had a moment to settle. Skip "how are you today" shouted from the register. The greeting exists to open a conversation, not to check a box.
