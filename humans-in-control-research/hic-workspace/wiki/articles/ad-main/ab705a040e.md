# 19 Best Ceramic Brands and Online Pottery Shops (2026)

Source: [ad_main](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/where-to-buy-pottery)

The best ceramic brands and online pottery shops offer wares that come in many forms: A beautiful vase filled with fresh flowers, a statement fruit bowl, and oversized planters to name a few. But our favorite innovative integrations lean more toward the unexpected. Think: Gwyneth Paltrow’s plate wall or the Sean Gerstley cocktail table from this California Canyon home. In our search for the best pottery and ceramics, we discovered textured terra-cotta pots, food-safe stoneware, and several colorfully glazed creations that will incorporate an artisanal feel to your decor. Our picks below include one-of-a-kind vintage pieces and Etsy gems that will fool guests into thinking you’re a professional designer.
Our Top Picks for the Best Ceramic and Pottery Brands
Arhaus isn’t just the place to splurge on a really nice sectional sofa. The brand’s selection of decor and accents is also a great place to source elegant tabletop ceramics. Much like the furniture, the color palettes here are diffused and feel like something you could have come across in a flea market—the Eva and Chulucanas vases are just a sampling of what else you can find from the retailer.
Abask houses a vast roster of talented potters and ceramicists, from Liz Rowland’s cheeky clay creations to delectable-looking pieces from Este Ceramiche. While there’s a good mix of more muted offerings, colorful and creative designs—like Anut Cairo’s vases with hand-painted floral buds or Silvia K’s leather-strapped terracotta platter—serve as the main attraction. Note that many of the items—especially one-of-a-kind and special edition pieces—come at a higher pricepoint, making them a lovely surprise for someone with exceptional taste in ceramics or a nice way to treat yourself.
East Fork, the brand known for turning pasta bowls into works of art, uses its pottery to uphold a high standard of craftsmanship and technique—every piece of pottery feels thoughtfully made. “I use my East Fork Small Mug every morning and it brings me so much joy,” says contributor Stewart Savin. “The gorgeous prune-colored ceramic has a wonderful feel to it while being extremely durable. As a North Carolinian with a yard full of red clay, it’s exciting to see our natural resources brought to life by Western NC artisans.” (Plus, the social-media-beloved items make perfect gifts.)
DWR is an elite choice for furniture and home decor, so it’s no surprise that the retailer also stocks some of the best designs for pottery (just be prepared to pay up). Both handmade and easy-to-style, the Poltrona Frau and Sin Ceramics pieces we highlighted feel like heirlooms you’ll pass down to future generations.
Nickey Kehoe’s pottery and ceramic wares are unsurprisingly well-curated, with vintage-presenting pieces making up much of the selection. French studio Astier de Villatte’s wares—one of the only makers in Paris still honoring traditional ceramic production—can layer in sophisticated touches to your space. More playful pieces—like a ceramic winestopper in the likeness of David Bowie—make shopping online feel like an adventure. The brand’s own line of pottery, handmade in Los Angeles, rounds out the assortment with understated pieces that can easily settle into any type of space.
Lulu and Georgia—a favorite of Mandy Moore—stocks pottery and ceramic art from both their in-house label as well as trending works from Meso, Sarah Sherman Samuel originals, SIN by Virginia Sin, and more. The selection leans textural, color-filled, and playful—you won’t find any boring fruit bowls or plain-looking vases in the assortment. In addition to nonfunctional objets d’art, you’ll find ceramic and pottery-based lighting fixtures, including a particularly good collaboration with Lolly Lolly ceramics that’s worth a closer look.
For rare vintage pieces, 1stDibs is the way to go. We especially like the idea of using the retailer as a shortcut to get the look of your favorite celebrity’s Open Door. There’s a curated array of Majolica wares (to emulate Dakota Johnson’s collection), as well as lots of interesting plating (perhaps to make your own decorative wall à la Gwyneth).
Etsy is a treasure trove of one-of-a-kind vintage and handmade pottery from ceramic artists around the world. So much so that it’s easy to get overwhelmed by all the options. We recommend narrowing down your search with specific keywords of what you’re looking for—like “Italian vintage,” “Scandi,” or “midcentury modern”—which is how we were able to dig up these finds.
Villeroy and Boch, a legacy porcelain brand you’re definitely familiar with if you’ve spent time perusing wedding china, dates back to 1748 and have had their wares featured on some of history’s most distinguished table settings. While this may give you the impression that the brand’s offerings are dated, the newly released Memphis collections proves otherwise. “Bold, vibrant and dripping in 80’s attitude,” the bold pieces bring together sculptural outlines with geometric prints that are designed to be mixed and matched to manifest your own visual feast.
New York-based artist Shane Gabier’s pottery evokes modernist forms and brutalist influences. Experimenting with angular silhouettes, layered textures, and diffused color palettes, his vessels are a refreshing departure from traditional-leaning crafts. “I own some of Shane’s dessert plates in a woozy blue checkerboard pattern and they are my favorites—I love how he plays with color,” says commerce director Lori Keong. “They have a distinctly psychedelic ’70s vibe to them that feels more fun than anything else in my cupboards.”
Ceramic tableware studio Mud Australia is a favorite of senior design editor Hannah Martin. The brand specializes in minimalist aesthetics and timeless designs for everyday use. From plates and serving dishes to cups and pitchers, each piece is handmade with French Limoges clay and finished with a glazing technique that creates a glossy interior contrasted with a matte exterior texture. As an added bonus, the wares themselves are designed to be microwave-, oven-, and dishwasher-safe, comfortably suiting unfussy lifestyles.
Most recognizable for their trompe l’oeil-inspired creations, pieces from Este Ceramiche have appeared in the pages of AD before. Besides decorative plates topped with sculpted asparagus and chocolate truffle-adorned keepsake boxes, the brand’s lines of vibrant tableware are just as noteworthy, drawing upon heritage techniques that have been utilized since the brand’s 18th century beginnings.
HAY taps emerging talent like Portuguese designer Rui Pereira and artist Emma Kohlmann to mastermind their ceramic creations, which often feel like expressions of joy. Colorful and cartoon-ish signatures are a hallmark for the designs, including everyday tableware to bring more character to mundane meals as well as vases and trays that create bold color moments on your decorative bookshelves and tabletops.
One of our favorite sources for elevated design staples, Crate & Barrel’s designer collaborations make the retailer a particular standout among their peers. It’s no secret that we’re fans of Athena Calderone’s work , and this ceramic vase from her collection with the brand is an easy way to bring a small slice of the designer’s style into your own home.
Anthropologie is a paradise for anyone looking to fill their shelves with pottery and ceramic accents. The lighting and kitchenware categories are particularly robust, though there are also plenty of trinket boxes, catchall trays, and more to fuel your desire for out-of-the-box wares.
Unexpected ceramic vases and plant vessels are where CB2 shines—in addition to lots of sculptural glassware to spruce up tabletops and bare bookshelves. The options noticeably vary—from glossy pitchers with exaggerated necks to intentionally misshapen terracotta vases—but the selections feel well-suited to contemporary homes and modern design aesthetics. The hard part comes after checkout: finding a bouquet worthy of your new favorite vessel.
Like Amazon, sorting through all of Wayfair’s options can feel like an endless scroll. However, we like that the retailer’s ceramic and pottery selection features a lot of vase sets, like this peacock-esque patterned trio from Wade Logan. You can also find more expensive, one-of-one pieces in the mix, like this lunar-inspired vase with interesting texture.
No matter what your decor archetype may be, there’s a good chance you’ll be able to find what you’re looking for at West Elm, which caters to just about every design persona. In addition to their line of ceramics designed in-house, West Elm also features a selection of artisanal pottery from small artists and businesses through their West Elm Local program—like the Keraclay coil vase by Brooklyn-based artist Virginia Felix.
Amazon has no shortage of ceramics in their stacked decor section, but the tricky part is sifting through everything to find hidden gems. Luckily for you, we spotted this fish-shaped vase from Ottolenghi for Serax that’s worth a splurge, and a very budget-friendly striped bud vase—a nice demonstration of the different price points you can find from the retailer.
