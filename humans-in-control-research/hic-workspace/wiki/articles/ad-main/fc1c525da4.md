# Where Did Dolly Parton Live? A Look at the Late Music Legend’s Properties

Source: [ad_main](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/where-does-dolly-parton-live)

Beloved music icon Dolly Parton has died at the age of 80. The singer-songwriter was born on January 19, 1946, and was the fourth of 12 children. Her family lived in a small cabin in rural Tennessee—a far cry from the glamorous, rhinestone-studded persona Parton maintained throughout her career. But the musician’s modest upbringing was, in part, a catalyst for her success. “All I had ever known was housework and kids and workin’ in the fields,” she told Rolling Stone in 1977. “But I didn’t want to be domestic; I wanted to be free. I had my songs to sing; I had an ambition, and it burned inside me. It was something I knew would take me out of the mountains. I knew I could see worlds beyond the Smoky Mountains.”
Though she went on to do just that, Parton frequently tapped her origins for songwriting inspiration, perhaps most famously in one of her early solo hits, “Coat of Many Colors.” The 10-time Grammy winner stayed true to her home state, settling down outside of Nashville with her husband, Carl Dean, who died in March 2025 at age 82.
Throughout her career, which bridged gaps between many generations and demographics, Dolly wrote over 3,000 songs (including smash hits “Jolene” and “I Will Always Love You”). She was inducted into a handful of Halls of Fame (Country Music, Songwriters, and Rock & Roll), starred in several acclaimed films (including Steel Magnolias and 9 to 5), and received a lifetime achievement award from the Recording Academy. But she never forgot her Smoky Mountain roots. “I always thought that if I made it big or got successful at what I had started out to do, that I wanted to come back to my part of the country and do something great, something that would bring a lot of jobs into this area,” Parton said in 2010, referencing Dollywood, the amusement park that she opened near her birthplace in 1986. To this day, Dollywood is the county’s largest employer.
“I hope I’ll be remembered as somebody that tried to do some good in the world and left, you know, a few good things,” she said in a 2024 interview.
Read on to learn more about the places that Dolly Parton called home.
Childhood home
The “I Will Always Love You” singer grew up with 11 siblings in a small, two-room log cabin in Sevierville, Tennessee. The log cabin, which was nestled amongst the Smoky Mountains, had no electricity or running water. Despite growing up in these conditions, Parton never thought of herself as poor. The cheerful, always optimistic singer said they were rich in things that money couldn’t buy, like love, kindness, and understanding. “We had a roof over our heads, even if it did leak. We had something to eat on our table, even if it wasn’t exactly what we wanted, and we had a bed to sleep in, even if there was a bunch of us in it,” Parton said in a 60 Minutes interview with the late Morley Safer.
Those curious can visit a replica of Parton’s childhood home at her amusement park, Dollywood. Upon entering, tourists can find a sign that reads, “These mountains and my childhood home have a special place in my heart. They inspire my music and my life. I hope being here does the same for you!” The replica was built by Parton’s brother Bobby, and the interior was designed by Parton’s mother. Throughout the cabin, you’ll see original family treasures. The kitchen walls are covered in floral wallpaper, the table is set with plates and aluminum coffee cups, baskets are filled with firewood, and old photographs of her parents hang above the bed.
Red Top
In 1984, Parton’s parents, Robert Lee and Avie Lee Parton, purchased a red-roofed home in Sevierville, Tennessee. The 1,548-square-foot farmhouse was built in 1917 and featured two chimneys, three bedrooms, and two-and-a-half bathrooms—a big change from the two-room log cabin they once called home. Nestled amongst 62 acres of rolling green hills, the property offered privacy, land, and expansive views of the surrounding landscape. After the death of her parents, Parton sold the home in 2003 to a family friend. Fast-forward almost 20 years, and the owners of Red Top continue to carry on the philanthropic legacy of the Parton family. In 2022, they temporarily transformed the quaint home into a wedding venue after a nearby venue was lost in the Sevier County wildfires.
Rustic mountain escape
For many years, the “Jolene” singer and her husband, Carl Dean, owned a secluded Craftsman-style cabin in Idyllwild, California. Nestled amongst pine trees and snowbanks, the 1,800-square-foot home featured a stone fireplace, cathedral ceilings, original wood paneling, and charming moldings reminiscent of a Bavarian lodge. The couple purchased the property in June of 2000 for $235,000, according to The Press-Enterprise. They briefly listed it in May of 2014 for $599,000, then removed the listing in October. Nine years later, in November of 2023, Parton and Dean said goodbye for good by transferring ownership of their rustic mountain escape to the Ronald McDonald House through a gift deed.
Primary residence in Brentwood, Tennessee
After a time living in a two-story, red-brick house in Antioch, Tennessee, the couple was ready to invest in their forever home. While many outlets have reported that Parton and Dean bought their property in Brentwood, Tennessee, for $400,000 in 1999, it appears that that transaction was for an adjacent property. In fact, Parton and Dean’s history at their longtime residence outside of Nashville goes back much further. Parton wrote in her 1994 memoir, Dolly: My Life and Other Unfinished Business, that she and Dean built the home from scratch before Parton’s career had really taken off.
“I had not yet achieved anything that could be considered stardom,” she wrote. “Still, I said to Carl, ‘We’d better buy a big-enough lot so that we can have privacy when the tour buses come by trying to look in.’ He accepted that notion without question, showing that he had faith in my dream too.” The couple bought 70 acres on which Dean, Parton’s uncle, and Parton’s brothers all helped construct a 23-room house. According to records, it was completed in 1972. “True to my dreams, the tour buses do come by now, and fans can see the house. But it is set back far enough from the road that they don’t actually see us,” the singer penned.
In 2014, the Steel Magnolias star reportedly got approval to construct a 2,400-square-foot barn to match three other existing barns on the property. Aerial footage shows that the compound also featured a tennis court and a swimming pool.
Parton and Dean lived together at their custom dream home until Dean’s 2025 death. Parton maintained the residence up until her death in August 2026.
