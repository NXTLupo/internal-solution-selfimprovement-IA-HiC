# Dolly Parton Has Passed Away at 80—Here Are the Incredible Places the Music Legend Called Home

Source: [housebeautiful](https://www.housebeautiful.com/lifestyle/entertainment/a73523693/dolly-parton-houses-real-estate/)

Dolly Parton, the legendary country music singer, has died at age 80. Over the course of her decades-long career, the beloved artist made her mark across music, film, beauty, and fashion. Her upbeat spirit, larger-than-life style, and inspiring musical talent endeared her to generations of fans from all walks of life.
Though Parton has been a well-known artist and celebrity since the early 1970s, she maintained a surprisingly private home life. While her most famous commercial property is, of course, the namesake theme park Dollywood, the homes she shared with her late husband, Carl Thomas Dean, are less well known. The singer-songwriter owned houses in Tennessee and California and went to great lengths to keep them out of the public eye. After all, for her, family always came first.
Ahead, we’re sharing what we do know about the five fabulous homes Parton made her own throughout her extraordinary life.
A Peaceful Suburban Abode in Brentwood, Tennessee
In 1999, Dolly Parton and her husband Carl Dean bought their longtime home just outside Nashville, Tennessee. The couple paid a humble $400,000 for the 3,324-square-foot property at the time, and truly made it their own over the years.
With so much land to play with, the duo built various structures and amenities around the mansion, including gazebos (yes, multiple), barns (again, yes, multiple), a swimming pool, a tennis court, and their very own private chapel.
An Estate Neighboring the Smoky Mountains in Sevierville, Tennessee
Parton was born and raised in Locust Ridge, near Sevierville, Tennessee, a rural area nestled in the Smoky Mountains (an ancient range running along the Tennessee-North Carolina border).
We guess the saying “you can take the girl out of the Smoky Mountains, but can’t take the Smoky Mountains out of the girl” rings true, since as of January 2026, the “Jolene” singer reportedly still owned property in the area.
A Humble Bungalow in Nashville, Tennessee
From 1980 to 1996, Parton and Dean resided at 3146 Glencliff Road in Nashville, Tennessee, which was originally built in 1941. As of 2019, the two-story home sat on a 2.4-acre gated property featuring four bedrooms, three bathrooms, and 4,795 square feet of living space divided between the primary residence and guest house. The shed Parton added onto the property as a play space for her nieces and nephews also remained intact, with the original wallpaper Parton had decorated it with back in the ‘80s.
The home’s Zillow listing shows a stucco exterior (in true ‘40s fashion); it’s surrounded by wooded areas, and it includes a large primary bedroom, fireplace, covered patio, multiple heating systems, and a detached studio. According to Realtor.com, James and Oveta Pyburn bought the home from Parton in 1996 for $140,000, plus $5,000 for Parton's furnishings. The Pyburns told the real estate publication that Parton “liked wicker” and that they found a rolled-up poster of the star under the stairwell, inscribed “For Clyde,” that Oveta framed and hung over a small table.
A Secret Cabin in Idyllwild, Southern California
Parton and Dean also owned a cozy mountain cabin for decades (the sale is estimated to have been around 1996), though later, the Steel Magnolias actress donated the three-bedroom home to the Ronald McDonald House Charities of Southern California.
Though described by Realtor.com as a “petite property,” the 1,800-square-foot house is three stories high—though it only set Parton back a mere $126,000. Everything in the space is reportedly how Parton left it, with personal items and collectibles around the home, including a tea set at the kitchen table, as well as framed art pieces and fine china. Interior details worth highlighting include wood-beam ceilings, expansive windows, and multiple fireplaces, the most notable being a dramatic stone structure built around a granite boulder in the wall.
An Adobe-Style Home in West Hollywood
Parton never made Hollywood her official home, but the performer did purchase a small property in California, likely for when she had to be on location for work. The two-bedroom, two-bathroom space only spanned 1,091 square feet, though it still cost $1.2 million in April 2007.
Parton made sure the place channeled her eclectic style, as the listing featured photos of checkered seat coverings, an upholstered piano seat cover, distressed furniture, and a main closet clad with laundry baskets, an ironing board, and Yankee candles. It also features an outdoor entertaining space, with a deck and dining area, plus a patio made private by surrounding trees and planters.
