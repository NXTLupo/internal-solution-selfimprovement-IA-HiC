# He Gutted a Dated 600-Square-Foot NYC Apartment,  Exposing the Original Beams

Source: [apartment_therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/small-hells-kitchen-apartment-renovation-tour-37686709?utm_source=RSS&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=Category%2FChannel%3A+main)

When senior video editor Sean Matthew Robinson bought this 600-square-foot apartment in Hell’s Kitchen five years ago, he says it was a “stuffy two-bedroom with a dated, traditional style.”
“Located on the fourth floor of a prewar walk-up, it had tiny rooms and low ceilings, but it was otherwise in fairly good condition,” Sean continues. What made him choose this unit to purchase was the corner exposure and natural light. And although the previous renovations weren’t terrible, Sean still wanted to put his own stamp on the space.
He had the small apartment gutted from “floor to ceiling,” had all the interior walls removed, and had the ceiling opened up to expose the original beams.
“Given the age of the building, my goal was to blend its industrial character with a modern finish — similar to something you might find in Tribeca. With limited square footage, every inch had to be intentional and efficient — integrated storage quickly became a priority!” he writes of the 100-year-old apartment.
Sean worked with Matt Giddens to design the space, and contractor Francisco Sosa of General Contractor New York City brought the designs to life. “I’ve always had a strong appreciation for interior design and have very specific taste. After five years of living here, I finally feel like the apartment is complete — a long, sometimes arduous process of adding, removing, and rearranging furniture until everything felt exactly right,” he writes of the space he shares with 5-year-old cream-colored mini poodle, Carson, “who not only runs the apartment but somehow also perfectly matches the design aesthetic.”
“As a gay man who grew up in the Bible Belt to a family who didn’t always accept me, feeling safe and comfortable in my home is crucial for me and my mental health,” Sean writes. “I am proud to have built this and to now feel like my house is also my home.”
“After 25 years in New York, this apartment — my first home purchase — has become both a personal sanctuary for Carson and me and a gathering place for the many people I’ve met over the years,” Sean writes. “Because I live in the heart of Hell’s Kitchen, friends are always nearby, and people stop in often.”
