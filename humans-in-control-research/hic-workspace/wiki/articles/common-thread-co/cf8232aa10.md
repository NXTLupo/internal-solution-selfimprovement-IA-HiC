# Google Ads August 2026: The New AI Insights and Analytics Tools Every Ecommerce Brand Should Start Using Now

Source: [common_thread_co](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/google-ads-august-2026-the-new-ai-insights-and-analytics-tools-every-ecommerce-brand-should-start-using-now)

On August 10, 2026, Google announced 4 new AI and agentic capabilities across Google Ads and Google Analytics. For ecommerce brands managing paid search, shopping, or Demand Gen campaigns, this means your account now surfaces performance shifts, competitive context, and custom visualizations automatically, without waiting for your analyst team to pull a report.
What Do Google’s New AI Tools Mean for Your Campaigns?
These updates shift the burden of daily reporting onto Google’s AI, directly inside the platforms your team already uses. Your Google Ads homepage now shows AI-generated insights cards personalized to your business, your Google Analytics homepage surfaces what changed since you last logged in, and a new benchmarking tool compares your campaign performance against anonymized averages from similar businesses. For 7-9 figure ecommerce brands running complex multi-campaign structures, this level of in-platform intelligence was previously only accessible through third-party analytics tools or dedicated analyst work.
How Do Google Analytics’ New AI Overviews Work?
Google Analytics now displays AI-generated summaries at the top of the homepage, covering every significant performance shift since your last session. Whether it’s a seasonal spike in product page traffic, an unexpected dip in conversion rate, or a change in channel attribution, the AI Overview tells you what happened and what to consider doing about it.
Clicking any data card in the summary carries that context directly into Ask Advisor, Google’s in-product AI agent, where you can dig deeper with follow-up questions. You can also opt into notifications via phone or email on a cadence that fits your workflow, so your team stays informed without logging in every day.
For ecommerce brands running seasonal promotions or scaling campaigns into Q4, this kind of real-time alerting reduces the lag between a performance shift occurring and your team knowing about it. Track all the changes happening across Google this year in our full Google Ads 2026 update tracker.
What Changed on the Google Ads Homepage and How Does It Affect Your Account?
Google has revamped the Google Ads homepage to surface AI-powered insights cards personalized to your specific account and business category. These cards proactively flag things like competitor impression share changes, emerging keyword trends, and budget pacing anomalies before they compound into larger problems.
A new prompt box above the insights cards lets you generate custom insights on demand. Type a question about your campaign’s competitive dynamics, performance gaps, or opportunity areas, and get an AI-generated answer grounded in your actual account data. This replaces the traditional Ads homepage, which displayed static performance numbers without context or interpretation.
How Does the New Ask Advisor Benchmarking Feature Work in Google Analytics?
The new benchmarking capability inside Ask Advisor compares your campaign performance against anonymized averages from similar businesses in your category. You can see where your conversion rates, click-through rates, and ROAS are tracking relative to comparable ecommerce accounts, giving your team clear signals about where the biggest optimization gaps are.
This is especially valuable for brands that have historically relied on internal quarter-over-quarter comparisons without external context. If your ROAS is holding steady but industry benchmarks have improved significantly, that context changes how you prioritize optimization work. For brands working with a dedicated Google Ads agency, benchmarking adds a concrete data point for strategy reviews and quarterly performance conversations.
What Are the New Google Ads Dashboards and How Do They Change Reporting?
Google Ads now includes a Dashboards feature that lets you transform raw account data into visual reports using plain-text prompts. Type what you want to see, and Google generates the visualization, whether that’s a channel mix breakdown, a creative performance comparison, or a time-series view of key metrics. Every dashboard automatically generates a written summary explaining the patterns it surfaces.
Google Analytics will get the same Dashboards capability soon. The combination of prompt-driven visualization and AI-written interpretation is positioned to reduce the manual work of building Looker Studio or spreadsheet reports for internal stakeholders. For ecommerce teams that spend significant time assembling weekly or monthly performance decks, this is worth testing immediately.
What Should You Do Now with Google’s New AI Tools?
- Log into Google Analytics and check the new AI Overviews on your homepage. Review what’s surfaced and compare it to what your team already knows. If the summaries catch something you missed, opt into email or phone notifications to get alerts automatically going forward.
- Open Google Ads and explore the new personalized insights cards on the homepage. Use the prompt box to ask a specific question about competitive impression share or a campaign that has been underperforming. Note how accurate and actionable the AI-generated answer is before deciding how much weight to give it in your workflow.
- Run at least one Ask Advisor benchmarking query in Google Analytics. Compare your top campaign or channel performance against category averages. Identify the biggest gaps and bring that context into your next strategy session.
- Test the new Dashboards feature with one key internal report. Build your current most-used performance view using a text prompt, then compare the output to your existing report to assess whether it is accurate enough to replace the manual version.
- Brief your analytics and media team on these changes now. These tools are live in Google Ads today, with Google Analytics Dashboards coming soon. Familiarity compounds: the earlier your team builds habits around these features, the more efficiently they can act on the insights they surface heading into Q4.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is Ask Advisor in Google Ads and Analytics?
Ask Advisor is Google’s in-product AI agent available across Google Ads and Google Analytics. It lets you ask plain-language questions about your campaign performance, surface insights, run benchmarking comparisons, and take action directly from the interface. It is available in both platforms without any additional setup.
Is the new Google Ads Dashboards feature available now?
Yes, the new Dashboards feature is live in Google Ads now. You can access it from your Google Ads account and use text prompts to generate visual performance reports. The same capability is coming to Google Analytics, though Google has not announced a specific launch date.
How does Google’s benchmarking feature protect account data privacy?
Google’s benchmarking uses anonymized, aggregated data from businesses in similar categories. Your individual account data is not shared with other advertisers. The comparison gives you directional context about where you stand relative to peers without exposing any proprietary performance information.
Do these AI tools replace the need for a dedicated Google Ads analyst?
No. These tools accelerate the time it takes to identify what changed and surface initial hypotheses, but they do not replace the strategic judgment needed to interpret data in the context of your business, structure campaigns, manage bidding, or evaluate creative performance. Think of them as a faster starting point for analysis, not a substitute for expertise.
