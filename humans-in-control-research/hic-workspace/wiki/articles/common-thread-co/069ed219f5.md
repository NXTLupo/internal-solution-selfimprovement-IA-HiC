# Google Shopping Image Requirement: Every Product Needs 500x500 Pixels by January 31, 2027

Source: [common_thread_co](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/google-shopping-image-requirement-every-product-needs-500x500-pixels-by-january-31-2027)

On August 21, 2026, Google began widely alerting Merchant Center users that all product images must be at least 500 x 500 pixels starting January 31, 2027. For ecommerce brands running Google Shopping ads and free listings, this means any product with an image smaller than 500 x 500 pixels will be disapproved and will stop serving after that date, with no grace period.
What Does Google's New 500x500 Pixel Image Requirement Mean for Your Shopping Campaigns?
Products with images below 500 x 500 pixels will be disapproved in Merchant Center and removed from Shopping ads and free listings after January 31, 2027. If you have a large catalog with older product images or images sourced from suppliers, you may already be seeing "Image too small" or "Image too small due to upcoming policy changes" warnings in your Merchant Center account right now.
Why Is Google Raising the Minimum Product Image Size?
Google updated its product data specification on April 14, 2026, introducing this requirement alongside other feed changes. The new minimum reflects how Shopping ads now render across AI-powered listing formats, high-density mobile screens, and expanded surface areas like Google Lens and generative search results. Images that looked acceptable at the old minimums (100 x 100 pixels for non-apparel, 250 x 250 for apparel) are too small to display clearly in these contexts.
The 500 x 500 pixel floor is the bare minimum. Google's actual recommendation is 1500 x 1500 pixels or larger, and for AI-powered ad formats and high-density displays, even that can fall short. The gap between the minimum and the recommended size is where most of the real performance work lives. Products with 500 x 500 images will be approved, but products with 1500 x 1500 or larger images will render sharper in every format Google serves, including Performance Max creative assets and Demand Gen product overlays.
The January 31, 2027, deadline may feel distant, but the audit and remediation process for large catalogs takes time. Brands with thousands of SKUs sourced from suppliers or manufactured with legacy product photography often discover image quality issues at scale only when they run a systematic Merchant Center audit. Discovering that 20% of your catalog is non-compliant in January 2027 leaves no time to reshoot or upscale before disapprovals begin.
This requirement also interacts with Google's AI Max for Shopping expansion. Google has been expanding AI Max to Shopping campaigns throughout 2026, and the AI system selects and renders product images across placements automatically. Lower-quality images limit the system's ability to generate compelling ad variants. Higher-resolution images give AI Max more creative latitude. For more context on how Google's Shopping infrastructure is evolving in 2026, see our full Google Ads 2026 update tracker.
AI upscaling tools have improved significantly and are now a practical remediation path for brands with large catalogs of low-resolution images. Tools like Topaz Gigapixel, Adobe Firefly Generative Fill, and Midjourney's upscale function can bring most product images to 1500 x 1500 pixels while preserving product accuracy. However, if using AI-generated or AI-enhanced images in Shopping feeds, Google requires IPTC DigitalSourceType metadata indicating AI involvement, a separate requirement you must satisfy alongside the size requirement.
The brands most at risk are those selling in categories that historically relied on manufacturer-supplied images: consumer electronics, industrial supplies, automotive parts, and wholesale goods. Direct-to-consumer brands with in-house photography pipelines are generally better positioned but should still audit their full catalog rather than assuming compliance.
What Should You Do Now to Prepare for the Google Shopping Image Requirement?
- Run a Merchant Center image audit today. In Google Merchant Center, navigate to Products and filter by issue type. Look for "Image too small" or "Image too small due to upcoming policy changes" warnings. Export the affected product list. If you have a large catalog, do this now rather than waiting, because the remediation queue grows faster than it shrinks.
- Set your target at 1500 x 1500 pixels, not 500 x 500. The 500 x 500 minimum gets you compliant, but 1500 x 1500 is Google's recommendation and the floor for sharp rendering in AI-powered formats. If you are going to reshoot or upscale, do it right the first time rather than revisiting in six months when a new format requires higher resolution.
- Use AI upscaling for existing catalog images at scale. Tools like Topaz Gigapixel AI, Adobe Firefly, and similar upscalers can process large batches of product images. Test on a sample set first and manually review for artifacts, particularly on products with fine details, transparent packaging, or complex textures before running the full catalog.
- Add IPTC metadata if using AI-enhanced images. Google requires IPTC DigitalSourceType metadata on images generated or enhanced by AI. If your upscaling process uses generative AI, ensure your image processing pipeline embeds the correct metadata tag before uploading to Merchant Center or it will trigger a separate policy violation.
- Update your supplier image intake process. If you source product images from manufacturers or distributors, add a minimum size check (500 x 500 pixels, target 1500 x 1500) to your onboarding checklist for new SKUs. Catching non-compliant images at intake is faster and cheaper than remediating them retroactively across thousands of existing products.
How Do You Check If Your Google Shopping Images Are Compliant Right Now?
Open Google Merchant Center, go to Products, and select the Diagnostics tab. Filter for image-related issues. The system will show you a count of affected products and links to individual listings. You can also download a bulk report via Content API or Google Sheets integration if your catalog exceeds a few hundred products. Google has been surfacing "Image too small due to upcoming policy changes" warnings since April 2026, so if you have not checked Merchant Center diagnostics recently, the warnings may already be waiting for you.
Does This Requirement Affect Free Listings as Well as Paid Shopping Ads?
Yes. The 500 x 500 pixel minimum applies to both Google Shopping ads and free product listings across Google Search, Google Images, and Google Shopping surfaces. Products with non-compliant images will be disapproved from all Google surfaces, not just paid placements. This means the traffic and visibility impact extends beyond your ad spend to your organic Shopping presence as well. Brands that rely on free Shopping listings for incremental traffic should treat this as a high-priority fix regardless of whether they run paid campaigns. Frequently Asked Questions
What happens to products with images under 500x500 pixels after January 31, 2027?
Products with images below 500 x 500 pixels will be disapproved in Google Merchant Center and will stop serving in Google Shopping ads and free listings. They will not appear in paid placements or organic Shopping surfaces until the image is updated to meet the minimum size requirement.
Does the 500x500 minimum apply to all product categories?
Yes. The new 500 x 500 pixel minimum applies to all product categories. Previously, non-apparel products had a minimum of 100 x 100 pixels and apparel had a minimum of 250 x 250 pixels. Both minimums are being replaced with the unified 500 x 500 pixel requirement effective January 31, 2027.
Can I use AI to upscale my product images for Google Shopping?
Yes, AI upscaling is a valid and practical approach for bringing catalog images up to the 500 x 500 or 1500 x 1500 pixel target. If you use generative AI to enhance or create product images, you must embed IPTC DigitalSourceType metadata indicating AI involvement before uploading to Merchant Center. Upscaling without generative components may not require this metadata, but check your tool's documentation to confirm what type of AI processing it uses.
Where can I see which products are affected in Google Merchant Center?
Go to Products in Google Merchant Center and open the Diagnostics tab. Filter by image-related issues to find products flagged with "Image too small" or "Image too small due to upcoming policy changes." You can also use the Content API or Google Sheets export to pull a full list for large catalogs.
