# Google Is Removing Language Targeting from Search Campaigns in September 2026: What Ecommerce Brands Need to Know

Source: [common_thread_co](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/google-is-removing-language-targeting-from-search-campaigns-in-september-2026-what-ecommerce-brands-need-to-know)

On August 14, 2026, Google announced it will remove the manual language targeting setting from all Search campaigns and the Search Network component of Performance Max campaigns, starting September 2026. For ecommerce brands running ads across 2 or more languages, this means Google's AI will now determine which language version of your ad to serve based on your creative and landing page content, replacing a campaign-level setting that has existed in Google Ads for over 20 years.
Starting September 2026, Google's enhanced AI-driven ad and asset group prioritization will automatically select the most relevant language version of your ad based on each user's search context and language preferences at the moment of auction. Your ad copy and landing page language become the primary signals for matching. If you run separate English and Spanish campaigns, those structures continue to work exactly as they do now.
The change applies to two campaign types: standard Search campaigns (where the campaign-level language targeting setting will be fully removed) and the Search Network portion of Performance Max campaigns (where your language setting will no longer apply to Search placements). Performance Max campaigns serving on YouTube, Display, Discover, and Gmail will continue to use your existing campaign-level language settings. Shopping ads within Performance Max are not affected by this change at all.
For 7-9 figure ecommerce brands with large international catalogs, the practical implication is straightforward. If your ad creative and landing page are clearly in one language, Google's AI will match them to users searching in that language. If you have ad groups with creatives in multiple languages pointing to the same landing page, the AI prioritization system will determine which version to serve based on user context at the time of the search.
Google confirmed to Search Engine Roundtable on August 14 that no account restructuring is required. Single-language campaign structures, such as one campaign for English and a separate campaign for Spanish, will continue to function exactly as before. The change removes the manual setting; it does not alter how cleanly structured campaigns perform.
It is worth noting that language exclusions have never been supported in Google Ads, and that does not change with this update. If your goal was to exclude a language, that was never available and still is not. The removal affects only the ability to manually specify which language to target at the campaign level.
Google has updated its official language targeting help document with the full transition details. Developers and agencies using the Google Ads API should also review the API-specific guidance on stopping language criteria from being set on new Search campaigns and removing existing criteria for cleanliness.
