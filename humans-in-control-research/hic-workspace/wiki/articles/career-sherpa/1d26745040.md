# How To Find Target Companies For Your Job Search: 9 Proven Ways To Build Your List

Source: [career_sherpa](https://careersherpa.net/how-to-find-target-companies-for-your-job-search/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=how-to-find-target-companies-for-your-job-search)

To find target companies for a job search, use best-company rankings, industry databases, alumni networks, AI research tools, and job boards to build a list of at least 50 potential employers, ideally before they post a job opening.
A proactive job search means identifying companies you want to work for first, then researching who to contact inside them, rather than only applying to postings that go live.
Below are 9 sources to help you build (and keep growing) that list.
How to start if you don’t have any ideas yet
You probably already have a few companies in mind — one you read about in the news, one a friend raves about, or one you’re already a customer of. Add those to your list now. That’s your starting point; trust the exploration process from there.
1. Best Company Lists
What it is: Annual rankings of top employers, updated every year.
- Glassdoor’s Best Places to Work
- Fortune’s 100 Best Companies to Work For
- American Opportunity Index Top 100
- Search “Top 100 employers [your city]” or “Best Employer [your city]”
How to use it:
- Skim the list and pick 3–5 companies that sound interesting — no deep research needed yet.
- Note what they have in common: industry, employee count, geographic location.
- Use that pattern to find similar companies (via the databases below) until you have at least 50 companies on your list.
Example: If you want to work at Google because it’s innovative and hires technical writers, your next question is: what other companies are similar to Google, or do what Google does?
2. Databases
Once you know your target industry (often tied to a NAICS code), use a database to find other companies in that space.
- CareerOneStop Business Finder: Free. Search by business name, industry, occupation, or zip code.
- Your local library: Ask a librarian for help; many libraries provide free access to premium databases like Reference Solutions (formerly ReferenceUSA), sometimes even from home with a library card.
- Crunchbase: Free tier lets you search by industry, headcount, and headquarters; best for researching a specific company in depth rather than bulk list-building.
3. Lists & Directories
- Your local Chamber of Commerce directory
- Professional and industry association member lists
- Companies sponsoring or presenting at industry events
4. Alumni Databases
Your college or university likely has an alumni database or a dedicated LinkedIn alumni page showing where graduates work. This is one of the fastest ways to find companies with a track record of hiring people with your background.
5. Find Small Companies
Smaller employers post a large share of jobs but are harder to discover — they don’t have big marketing budgets or large recruiting teams. To find them, check:
- Recent recipients of venture capital funding
- Chamber of Commerce membership lists
- Professional association rosters
- Local economic development sites
- Your local newspaper’s business section, checked regularly
6. Use Google Strategically
Search with specific phrasing, e.g., “Best Marketing Companies in Detroit Michigan.” Then set up Google Alerts for terms like “[industry] + [city] + hiring” so new companies surface automatically.
7. Use AI Research Tools
AI tools like Claude.ai, Perplexity.ai, Gemini, and Copilot can research and compile company lists using real-time web data — often faster and more current than manual searching. The key is asking a specific, well-structured prompt.
Try this prompt as a starting point:
Create a list of 40-50 companies that would be a good fit for my job
search as a [insert job title]. Please include companies that:
– Operate in [insert desired industry/industries, e.g., SaaS,
renewable energy, marketing/advertising]
– Are located in [insert preferred location(s), or “remote-friendly”]
– Have fewer than [e.g., 500 or 1,000] employees
– Show growth potential or are recognized as innovative in their space
For each company, include:
– Company name and website
– A brief description of what they do
– Approximate company size
– Key competitors or similar companies I should also research
At the end, compile a list of competitors grouped by industry/
similarity so I can expand my target list further.
Tips for better results:
- Treat the AI like an assistant you’re giving instructions to, and refine your ask with follow-up questions in the same thread (“Which of these are actively hiring?” / “Which offer remote roles?”).
- Double-check company details the AI provides (size, funding, leadership) against the company’s own site. AI sometimes gets specifics wrong.
8. Ask People You Know
- Friends & family: Ask if they like where they work and why, not whether they’re hiring.
- Service providers (hairdresser, accountant, financial planner, mechanic) Try: “Have you heard of any [industry] companies in the area?”
- Past coworkers: Ask what companies they’ve moved to and whether they’re doing well.
- Clients or customers: They often know which companies in your space are growing.
9. Job Boards
The easiest source, saved for last. Job boards show which companies are actively hiring — not just for the role posted, but as a signal of future openings too. Add these companies to your target list so you can start networking with insiders before the next role is posted.
Don’t eliminate companies too early
It’s better to have too many companies on your list than too few. For your top 10:
- Set up job alerts on each company’s careers page.
- Follow each company on LinkedIn, and click “I’m Interested” where available.
- Keep researching the rest of your list. You don’t want to miss opportunities elsewhere.
Don’t remove a company from your list without a solid reason. One negative review or one person’s bad experience isn’t enough data. Keep pursuing a company until you have real information or an interview.
Put it in writing
Track every company on a spreadsheet (or your job search marketing plan), including:
- Contacts you know at the company
- People you want to meet there
- Career page link
- Jobs applied for and their status
What’s next
Building the list isn’t the finish line. Once you have your target companies, the next step is identifying people you know a lot or a little inside each to ask for an informational conversation.
PS: There does not need to be an open job in order to have this conversation. You never know what will happen with just a single conversation.
FAQ
How many companies should be on a target list for a job search?
Aim for at least 50. A longer list gives you more opportunities to network and apply before roles are publicly posted.
What’s the best free tool for finding target companies?
CareerOneStop’s Business Finder is a free database searchable by business name, industry, occupation, or zip code. Crunchbase’s free tier is also useful for researching individual companies.
How do I find small companies that aren’t actively advertising jobs?
Check recent venture capital funding recipients, Chamber of Commerce membership lists, professional association rosters, and local economic development sites, and monitor your local newspaper’s business section.
Can AI tools help build a job search target list?
Yes. Tools like Claude.ai, Perplexity.ai, Gemini, and Copilot can generate and refine company lists using real-time web data when given a specific prompt (industry, location, company size, growth stage). Verify key details like company size or funding independently, since AI-generated details can occasionally be inaccurate.
Should I remove a company from my list after one bad review?
No. One negative data point isn’t enough to disqualify a company. Keep researching until you have a fuller picture or a direct interview.
