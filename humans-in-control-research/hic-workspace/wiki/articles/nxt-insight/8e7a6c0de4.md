Ireland's slow land-use adaptation leaves farmers exposed to climate shocks and volatile markets

The government's phase-two land use review, A Living Land, warns Ireland is moving too slowly to adapt its land use, leaving farmers, communities and the environment exposed to climate shocks and volatile markets, against an agri-food industry worth €21 billion [articles/agr-agriland-ie/4c4cf66d8a.md#c1]. With roughly 80% of Irish land privately owned and decisions left to custodians, the report calls for a national land-use governance framework. Policy-adaptation lag translates directly into unmanaged climate exposure on farm balance sheets.
