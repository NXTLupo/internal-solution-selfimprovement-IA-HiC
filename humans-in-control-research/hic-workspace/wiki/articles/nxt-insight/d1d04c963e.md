El Nino drought concentrates 2026 crop and pasture loss in already-vulnerable regions, raising correlated underwriting e

FAO analysis of 41 years of ASIS satellite imagery maps a stronger-than-usual ENSO cycle with over 50 percent drought probability across the Sahel, Southern Africa, South and Southeast Asia, and Central America's Dry Corridor. More than 80 percent of drought impacts on agriculture are projected to hit low- and middle-income countries, and the 2015-16 event alone affected over 60 million people and drove $5 billion in humanitarian appeals. For underwriters, geographically correlated failed harvests, livestock losses, and rising household debt concentrate loss in the same belts year over year.

El Nino cycles expose the same vulnerabilities and tend to trigger failed harvests, livestock losses, rising household debt, and migration in search of food and water.
