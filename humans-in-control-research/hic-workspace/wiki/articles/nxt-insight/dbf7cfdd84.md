Perceived risk exceeds actual risk, so lenders default to worst-case pricing that starves viable farms of credit

ISF Advisors research cited by UNDP shows perceived risk in agriculture often exceeds actual risk, particularly among formal financial institutions and offtakers, now the largest lenders to smallholder farmers. These actors have incentives to lend to secure supply but operate with thin balance sheets unable to absorb a single climate shock, so they apply undifferentiated worst-case pricing. This overlooks differences across farmer profiles, value chains and climate-smart adoption, blocking viable agricultural credit.
