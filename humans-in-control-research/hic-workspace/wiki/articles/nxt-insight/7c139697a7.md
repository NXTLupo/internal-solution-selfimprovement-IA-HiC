Elevated interest rates put a structural floor under catastrophe reinsurance costs and farm-facing premiums

Florida insurance analysis states that when government bonds offer attractive returns, investors have less incentive to deploy capital into catastrophe reinsurance, and 'historically, the lowest insurance costs have coincided with periods of low interest rates.' If rates remain higher for longer, they keep a floor under the cost of capital and consumer rates. Because agricultural property and crop reinsurance draw on the same global capital pool, elevated 2026 rates transmit directly into higher farm premiums and tighter capacity, compounding the macroeconomic pressure on farm solvency.

When government bonds and other relatively safe investments offer attractive returns, investors have less incentive to deploy capital into catastrophe reinsurance.
