Consumer minimalism is the wrong toolkit for dense operational interfaces

Design Week argues the dominant consumer aesthetic of whitespace, large type, and one primary action per screen is a good toolkit for consumer software but wrong for operational work, where a trader needs eleven data points at once and operators quietly route around admin screens that force whitespace on them. This tempers the AI-design-variation enthusiasm: an email QA or ops console for Deciem's team should favor information density over the minimalist defaults AI generators reach for.

For these users, whitespace is not breathing room. It is hostile, the design equivalent of being asked to do your job through a peephole. [articles/design-week/d4c6d013e0.md#c3]
