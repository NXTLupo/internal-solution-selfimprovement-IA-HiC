A single major hurricane can consume decades of underwriting surplus in hours, making farm-belt cat capacity inherently

Florida insurance analysis warns that a 100-year storm can generate losses equal to three or four times the premium collected in a year, and because regulated profit margins are modest, decades of accumulated surplus can be consumed in hours. After a major event the industry must replenish billions, driving reinsurance costs up and capacity down. This tail-risk structure explains why June 1 renewals delivered 15-25 percent reductions in Florida yet remain one hurricane away from reversal, a volatility that agricultural underwriters relying on the same reinsurance towers must price against.
