High input costs, not weather, top the list of what farmers say is holding them back

In Purdue's May 2026 Ag Economy Barometer, farmers ranked high input costs (45.5%) as the top constraint, well ahead of weather risk (19%), low output prices (13.5%) and labor/equipment concerns (9%) [articles/agr-precision-farming-dealer/b1b98e0735.md#c1]. Only 22% expect their farm to be better off financially a year out [articles/agr-precision-farming-dealer/b1b98e0735.md#c1]. A Reddit macro thread links a potential oil shock to higher inflation, elevated interest rates and fertiliser shortages that disrupt food systems [articles/hic-theme-synthesis/0649f90dec.md#c12]. This ordering matters for underwriting: farmers self-identify macroeconomic input pressure as the dominant solvency threat.

Most said high input costs (45.5%), followed by weather risk (19%), low output prices (13.5%) and labor/equipment concerns (9%).
