Concentrated single-region production makes food supply chains brittle to drought, driving geographic diversification

A June 2026 study in Agribusiness by Cornell's Bingyan Dai and Miguel Gomez models expanding East Coast broccoli production to reduce reliance on drought-prone California, the top producer, showing it can lower costs and stabilize supply chains vulnerable to water shortages. The East Coast Broccoli Project has already built a $120 million industry across 10 Eastern states. The authors state the model 'can be generalized to other crops... facing increasing challenges from drought and water scarcity when production is concentrated in one state,' evidencing a structural climate-adaptation shift with underwriting implications for regional risk concentration.

expanding production outside of California, the country's top broccoli producer, can help stabilize fresh produce supply chains that are vulnerable to water shortages.
