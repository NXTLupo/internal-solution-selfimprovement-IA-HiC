Hurricane Helene flooding physically destroyed farm soil, a loss class most crop and property policies never anticipated

In September 2024 Hurricane Helene dropped up to 30 inches of rain on southern Appalachia, and on Will Runion's 736-acre Tennessee cattle and hay farm the Nolichucky River swelled to roughly 1,200 feet wide, gouging football-field-sized holes 12 feet deep and burying sections under up to 8 feet of sand or silt. Soil loss is a near-permanent asset destruction with no established remediation pathway, turning farms into 'living laboratories.' This creates an underwriting blind spot: the insured asset (productive soil) can be erased rather than merely damaged.

The flood had gouged two holes the size of football fields in his hay pastures, down to a depth of 12 feet. Other sections of the farm were buried in up to 8 feet of sand or silt.
