Prior-authorization criteria are opaque to the people automation is supposed to serve

Practitioners and patients cannot determine at point of service whether a procedure requires authorization, the exact information-asymmetry that clinical-decision-support AI is pitched to resolve. Members report that even portals and Summary of Benefits documents only say 'some imaging may require PA,' which one commenter calls 'useless' [social-intelligence/reddit-deep/healthinsurance/1rymum8.md#c0]. An infusion-center patient notes an approved prior auth is only a medical-necessity check, not a coverage guarantee [social-intelligence/reddit-deep/healthinsurance/1qdtjux.md#c4]. This opacity defines the operational gap any 2026 healthcare-AI authorization tool must close.

The SBC sometimes will say generic things like "some imaging may require PA" which is useless.
