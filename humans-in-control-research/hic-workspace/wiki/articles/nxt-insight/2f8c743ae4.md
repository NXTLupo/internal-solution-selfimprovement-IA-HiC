Students Require Human Validation for High-Stakes Outcomes While Automating Prep

Students and early-career workers readily delegate routine transaction execution, draft synthesis, and drill preparation to AI tools, but insist on human expert validation for high-stakes decisions. Medical students build complex Notion summaries and question banks via Claude Opus, but express anxiety over unverified hallucinated errors affecting study performance. Similarly, users seeking mental health, career, or financial guidance rely on AI to lower initial accessibility barriers and clarify thinking, but demand licensed human advisors for final decisions and major life choices.

AI tools are virtually universally accessible and eliminate the largest barriers to therapy. [social-intelligence/reddit-deep/academicpsychology/1uk8jq4.md#c2]
