Low financial literacy, mistrust and delayed payouts suppress farmer insurance demand behaviorally, not just economicall

The CGIAR behavioral brief identifies a general lack of risk perception and awareness, trust issues about the reliability and timeliness of compensation, and cultural preferences for traditional risk-sharing as core suppressors of uptake. In Ethiopia farmers are not fully aware of crop insurance benefits; in Ghana limited financial literacy and access hinder uptake; women farmers face unique barriers to financial resources and decision-making power. Delayed payouts damage trust across the system, informing the educational-needs dimension of the objective.
