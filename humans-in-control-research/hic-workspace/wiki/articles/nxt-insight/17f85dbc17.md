Asset managers circumvent $300 direct-to-consumer acquisition barriers by embedding white-label APIs

Standalone robo-advisors struggle with direct-to-consumer client acquisition costs frequently exceeding $300 per account. To bypass this friction, tier-1 institutions are opting to rent modular infrastructure. This allows firms like JPMorgan to embed complex rebalancing and fractional-share engines directly into their existing mobile banking footprints without paying direct marketing premiums.

Major players are opting to "rent" rather than "build". For example, JPMorgan uses modular platforms like WealthOS
