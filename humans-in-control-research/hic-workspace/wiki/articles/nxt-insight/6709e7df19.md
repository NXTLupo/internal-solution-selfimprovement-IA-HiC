Post-Helene farms became living laboratories because no one knew how to restore lost soil

Hurricane Helene's 2024 flooding washed away or tainted soil on thousands of southern Appalachian farms; Will Runion's 736-acre Tennessee farm lost a third of its fields to debris and had holes the size of football fields gouged 12 feet deep, with sections buried under 8 feet of sand [articles/agr-the-fern/e33c09b049.md#c1]. Turns out, no one really knew how to solve that problem. This confirms that extreme-weather soil loss is a recovery-uncertain, uninsurable-in-fact peril distinct from covered structure damage.
