Medical-necessity denials hinge on documentation gaps automated clinical-evidence assembly directly addresses

Denials repeatedly trace not to the care itself but to missing or unsubmitted documentation: an insurer retroactively denied a Mayo Clinic CT because the provider failed to respond to a medical-information request [articles/reddit-deep-r-healthinsurance/acf9ed3d14.md#c0]. Commenters note denials happen 'sometimes because provider did not chart quick enough' [articles/reddit-deep-r-healthinsurance/9791428256.md#c5], and that the fix is the provider submitting the clinical reasoning via appeal [social-intelligence/reddit-deep/healthinsurance/1u3l63c.md#c4]. Automated clinical-documentation and evidence-assembly are the AI use cases aimed squarely at this gap.

Denial is very common for sometimes no reason, sometimes provider did not chart quick enough. [articles/reddit-deep-r-healthinsurance/9791428256.md#c5]
