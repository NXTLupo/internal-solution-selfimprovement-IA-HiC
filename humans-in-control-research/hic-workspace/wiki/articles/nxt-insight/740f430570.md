Perceived farm risk exceeds actual risk, forcing worst-case pricing that starves viable credit

ISF Advisors research shows perceived risk in agriculture routinely exceeds actual risk, particularly among formal financial institutions and offtakers who are now the largest lenders to smallholder farmers [articles/vertexaisearch-cloud-google-com/90018ada56.md#c5]. These actors lend to secure supply but operate on thin balance sheets unable to absorb a single climate shock, so they default to undifferentiated worst-case pricing [articles/vertexaisearch-cloud-google-com/90018ada56.md#c7]. Better insurance data lets lenders separate systemic from idiosyncratic risk and move away from worst-case scenario pricing, translating into more appropriate lending terms and expanded viable credit [articles/vertexaisearch-cloud-google-com/90018ada56.md#c7].
