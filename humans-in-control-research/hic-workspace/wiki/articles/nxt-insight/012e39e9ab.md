El Nino drought risk skews to the upside because a warmer planet compounds existing conflict and hunger

FAO's Jorge Alvar-Beltran states this El Nino is unlike previous cycles because the planet is much warmer, and with conflict and food insecurity widespread the phase hits hardest where coping capacity is limited. FAO analysis of 41 years of ASIS satellite imagery maps drought risk above 50 percent across the Sahel, Southern Africa, South and Southeast Asia, and Central America's Dry Corridor. More than 80 percent of drought impacts hit low- and middle-income countries.

This isn't like previous El Niños. The planet is much warmer today, and with conflict and food insecurity widespread, this new phase will hit hardest in places that are already vulnerable
