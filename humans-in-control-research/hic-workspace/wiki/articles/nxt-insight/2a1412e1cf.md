Patients self-deploy consumer AI to decode insurance because the system assumes no human can navigate it unaided

A double-insured member reports routinely using AI to explain coordination-of-benefits basics to their own providers, treating a general-purpose chatbot as a stopgap for a process providers themselves mishandle [social-intelligence/reddit-deep/healthinsurance/1t6jxte.md#c4]. This sits against a broader sentiment that ordinary people are forced to 'become an insurance specialist' to get a bill paid [social-intelligence/reddit-deep/healthinsurance/1q0i6su.md#c4]. The pattern signals grassroots, bottom-up AI adoption in healthcare administration that is occurring outside any health-system AI strategy. It is patient-driven, not provider-deployed, and reveals demand for AI navigation tools at the eligibility and billing layer.

Honestly, I use AI to help me explain basics like this to providers. It's usually pretty good.
