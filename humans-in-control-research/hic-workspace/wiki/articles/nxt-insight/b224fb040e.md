Farmers rate AI's usefulness low even as generative tools spread, exposing a value-perception gap

In Purdue's May Ag Economy Barometer of 400 US farmers, 59% said AI would not improve their operation at all, 37% said it would help a little, and only 4% believed it would help a lot [articles/agr-precision-farming-dealer/b1b98e0735.md#c1]. Only 14% said their operation was better off than a year prior, with 45.5% citing high input costs as the top constraint [articles/agr-precision-farming-dealer/b1b98e0735.md#c1]. Syngenta's Jeremy Groeteke notes growers move fast with LLMs like Gemini and ChatGPT but agentic AI hasn't hit, so the future is here but unevenly distributed. This perception gap is a core adoption barrier separating tech-forward from skeptical farmer personas.
