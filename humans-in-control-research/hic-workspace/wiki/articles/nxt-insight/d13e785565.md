Dark-pattern urgency is legal but erodes the trust Gen Z rewards

A consumer-protection scholar in The Conversation explains that most 'dark patterns', repeated pop-ups, guilt-inducing buttons, are annoying but lawful, only deception or coercion crosses the line. Consumers 'recognize ordinary annoyance for what it is' and take their business elsewhere. Set against Gen Z's demonstrated filtering of inauthentic content, this warns the skincare redesign against manipulative countdown/guilt tactics that technically convert but corrode the authenticity the same audience buys into.

A repeated pop-up can be obnoxious. A guilt-inducing button can be tacky. But consumers recognize ordinary annoyance for what it is. [articles/the-conversation-ai/8401eb19e8.md#c1]
