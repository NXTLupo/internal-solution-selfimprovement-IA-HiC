Hurricane Helene shows farms can lose the one asset no policy replaces: their soil

Hurricane Helene dropped up to 30 inches of rain on southern Appalachia, burying hay pastures under sand or silt up to 12 feet deep and causing an estimated $4.9 billion in agricultural damage in North Carolina and $1.3 billion in Tennessee [articles/agr-the-fern/e33c09b049.md#c2]. Farmers lost crops, machinery and buildings, but the harder loss is soil itself, with UT Extension specialists admitting they didn't know how to fix it. Rainfall from Helene was 10% heavier due to man-made climate change, and NSF research suggests 100-year storms will become three times more likely and 20% more severe over 50 years. Soil loss makes farms uninsurable-in-fact even when insured.

When you see 4 feet of sandy soils on top of your topsoil, you know that's going to be a challenge.
