Climate hazards have driven $3.8T in farm losses, pushing smallholder lending past the viability frontier

Over three decades, climate-related hazards caused an estimated US$3.8 trillion in agricultural losses, with more than half the world affected by drought and nearly 2 billion people facing rising flood risk. The UNDP deep-dive argues these shocks can push the cost of lending to smallholder farmers to unsustainable levels, driving business models built on serving farmers beyond the viability frontier. This directly threatens underwriting viability and farm solvency, the core of the 2026 objective.
