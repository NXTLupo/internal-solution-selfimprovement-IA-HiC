Automated claim denial at intake is the dominant operational AI footprint members actually experience

Members describe insurers using 'an automated process to automatically deny many claims when they are first submitted, esp for emergencies' that frequently reverse on appeal [articles/reddit-deep-r-healthinsurance/dad4815933.md#c4]. Others describe being told a doctor with no medical degree, sitting behind a computer, overrides physician orders [social-intelligence/reddit-deep/healthinsurance/1pn9f11.md#c6]. This is the one place automated decisioning is already operational and visible in the corpus - on the payer denial side, not the clinical-AI side the market outlook anticipates. The sentiment is uniformly adversarial, foreshadowing trust barriers any clinical-AI rollout will inherit.

Often insurance companies use an automated process to automatically deny many claims when they are first submitted, esp for emergencies.
