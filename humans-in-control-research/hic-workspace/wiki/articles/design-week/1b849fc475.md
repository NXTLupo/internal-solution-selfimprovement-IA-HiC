# The Outline: TypoCircle 50th, WWF coffee, and a laundry bag – 28/09/2026

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/the-outline-typocircle-50th-wwf-coffee-and-a-laundry-bag-28-09-2026/)

Welcome to your weekly digest of noteworthy stories, announcements, and creative happenings from across the design industry.
This week, BrewDog leans into its messy past with disarming honesty, Canva and Monotype make a major push for multilingual typography, a home phone for kids proves nostalgia can be genuinely innovative, and more.
Here’s what caught our attention.
Business News
TypoCircle Celebrates Its 50th Anniversary
The Typographic Circle, the volunteer-run London organisation that has brought together anyone with an interest in type and typography since its founding as The Type Directors Club in 1976, is marking its 50th anniversary this year.
The milestone is being celebrated with a special poster series and events throughout 2026. Over its five decades, TypoCircle has hosted talks from some of graphic design’s most significant figures, with each speaker invited to design the poster for their own event, building an extraordinary archive of work.
Canva and Monotype Expand Partnership with 1,000+ New Font Styles
Canva and Monotype have expanded their partnership to bring more than 1,000 new font styles to Canva’s global creator community, taking the total number of Monotype and foundry partner fonts on the platform past 3,000. The expansion significantly broadens typographic choice across languages that have historically had fewer professional-quality options in creative tools, with substantial additions in Arabic, Bengali, Hindi, Khmer, Lao, Thai, and Vietnamese.
The largest increases come in Hindi (99 families, 298 styles), Bengali (72 families, 237 styles), and Vietnamese (114 families, 249 styles). Highlighted typefaces include Basis Grotesque Arabic from Colophon Foundry, Noort Bengali from TypeTogether, and Malila from Cadson Demak. Since the partnership launched in 2023, Monotype fonts have been used in more than one billion Canva designs.
Antalis Acquires Assets and Trade of Ovenden Papers
Antalis has acquired the assets and trade of Ovenden Papers from its administrators, strengthening its presence in the UK paper merchanting market. The long-established family business entered administration due to cashflow pressures and will continue to operate under the Ovenden name, supported by Antalis in the UK and Ireland.
The acquisition includes customer relationships, employees, premises, and relevant operational assets. Nick Thompson, Regional Managing Director at Antalis UKI, said the immediate priority is to provide stability and continuity for customers, suppliers, and employees while a detailed review of the business is undertaken.
WWF Launches Earthbrew Coffee Brand, Appoints Blackdog for Launch
WWF has entered the coffee market with Earthbrew, a new coffee range with 100% of profits invested in protecting forests, wildlife, and natural environments. The launch product is a Colombian Supremo whole-bean coffee made from 100% Fairtrade-certified Arabica beans, grown and hand-picked in the high-altitude regions of Colombia’s Andes, with a ground version planned for October.
The beans are roasted and packed in the UK at a carbon-neutral certified roastery powered by 100% renewable electricity, and the packaging uses 100% recyclable materials. Specialist retail agency Blackdog has been appointed to lead the launch brief following a competitive pitch. Research estimates that climate change could erase more than 50% of suitable coffee-growing land by 2050.
FutureBrand helps Bentley reimagine luxury automotive retail with new global showroom concept
Brand strategy and design company FutureBrand has worked with Bentley Motors to create a new global showroom concept, unveiled at the marque’s London flagship dealership Jack Barclay. The design deliberately moves away from the conventional car dealership, replacing the transactional with a hospitality-driven journey through lounges, lifestyle moments and a standout Atelier commissioning space where customers explore leathers, veneers and materials around a central table with an adaptive digital interface.
A Living Theatre transforms the traditional car handover into a multisensory cinematic reveal. FutureBrand developed the concept as a scalable system that can flex across Bentley’s global network, from compact urban sites to large multi-storey dealerships.

Velvet Badger reveals two-year role behind Sir Kenny Dalglish-backed sports investment platform Keeps
London-based independent studio Velvet Badger has revealed a two-year strategic creative partnership behind Keeps, the regulated sports investment platform founded by Liverpool legend Sir Kenny Dalglish that aims to give retail investors access to sport as an asset class. The studio, whose previous clients include Gucci and Christian Louboutin, has been embedded in the business from its earliest stages, shaping Keeps across naming, positioning, product architecture, visual identity, UX and launch creative. Co-founder Chris Joyce described it as “a dream brief”, adding that the studio drew on ten years of experience in blockchain and technology to help build the platform, which is expected to launch in Q4 2026.
Design Bridge and Partners transforms Leone 1857’s heritage into the Happiness Factory in Turin
Design Bridge and Partners has led the creative vision and experiential identity for the Happiness Factory, a new visitor experience in Turin for Italian confectionery brand Leone 1857. The 2,000-square-metre space spans 14 experiential zones, from the Leone Wonder Ramp to the Garden of Delights, all built around the brand’s live working factory. Design Bridge’s relationship with Leone dates back to 2022, when it evolved the brand’s strategy and packaging around the concept of Sensory Escapism.
The Happiness Factory takes that idea off the pack and into a physical space, prioritising analogue interaction, kinetic mechanics and tactile materials over screens. Exhibition design was by Studio Paolo Monzeglio, with interactive and multimedia elements by Fo.LL.iA.
Design Bits
Yoto Unveils Its Biggest Redesign Yet, Built Around a Simple Idea
Kids’ audio platform Yoto has launched the 4th Generation of its Yoto Player and Yoto Mini, three years in the making. The headline feature is a new Green Button that gives children instant, cardless access to up to 20 daytime and 20 nighttime favourites, set by parents through the app. It’s the kind of deceptively simple idea that changes how the product is used day to day.
Both players are rebuilt from the inside out with a new processor, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 6.1, and 64GB storage (double the previous generation, enough for 1,000+ hours of offline audio). The Yoto Player 4th Gen gets a redesigned full-panel night light with seven colours and wireless charging, while the Yoto Mini 4th Gen is now IP67 dust and water resistant. Founded by Ben Drury and Filip Denker via Kickstarter in 2017, Yoto has now reached over six million children in 200+ countries and holds a King’s Award for Enterprise, a D&AD Pencil, and a TIME Best Inventions listing. The Yoto Mini 4th Gen also sits in the Henry Ford Museum of Innovation alongside the original iPhone.
Aetha Designs the UK’s First Home Phone Made for Kids
Poole-based product design consultancy Aetha has designed Yapa, a home phone built specifically for children aged 5 to 14. Connected to domestic Wi-Fi and operating through VoIP, it has no apps, no social media, and no internet access. A small display shows names and missed calls, while contacts must be pre-approved by a parent.
Founded by Tom Parsons, a former senior designer at Dyson, Aetha drew on the aesthetics of the early 2000s for the design, from the interchangeable covers of Nokia phones to the aluminium shells of the iPod Nano. Priced at around £100, Yapa arrives at a moment when growing numbers of parents are looking for ways to give children voice communication at home without handing them a smartphone.
Rare Ideas creates brand identity and packaging for healthier ice cream brand Monk’d
Pune-based studio Rare Ideas has designed the brand narrative, naming, visual identity and packaging for Monk’d, a new healthier ice cream brand built around the idea of everyday indulgence rather than restriction. The studio’s strategic work began with primary interviews, store walks, category research and consumer behaviour analysis, identifying an opportunity to move away from the guilt-free, subtraction-led language that dominates the healthier ice cream space.
The visual identity was designed to feel calmer than the category while still commanding shelf attention, with a natural, confident palette and typography that brings structure and personality to the system. For the packaging, Rare Ideas moved away from the literal ingredient imagery typical of ice cream tubs, instead building an illustration-led world where each flavour becomes its own small landscape of indulgence. The result is a cohesive range where the illustrations create desire and distinction, while the information architecture makes the healthier proposition easy to understand.
BrewDog Launches ‘Under New Ownership’ Campaign by LOVE
BrewDog has launched its first major marketing campaign since being acquired by Tilray Brands in a £33 million rescue deal earlier this year. Created by LOVE, the ‘Under New Ownership’ campaign takes the brand’s recent history head-on with sharp wordplay and self-deprecating honesty: ‘No More Cunning Stunts. Just Great Beer’ sits next to Punk IPA, ‘We’ve Flipped the Bird’ accompanies Wingman, and ‘A Little Less Allegation’ runs alongside Elvis Juice.
The multi-platform campaign spans large-format OOH, digital screens in high-traffic locations, and a personal letter from Tilray published across 70,000 copies of City AM. Since acquiring BrewDog, Tilray says it has invested nearly £50 million across operations, working capital, and people, relaunching 16 bars and releasing new beers.
