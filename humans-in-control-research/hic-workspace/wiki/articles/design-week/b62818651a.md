# Thirst brings sun-soaked optimism to Harbour Brewing Co.

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/thirst-brings-sun-soaked-optimism-to-harbour-brewing-co/)

Harbour’s range of beers has long carried a tidy, restrained aesthetic, but for a Cornish seaside brand, it rarely shouted fun or summer holidays. Design agency Thirst has shifted that narrative, bringing the brewery into a lighter, more vibrant, and heavily colour-saturated visual world.
Built around the core strategy of a “Sun-Soaked Playground”, the redesign shifts Harbour from being defined merely by location to capturing the feeling of post-outdoor leisure by the sea. A palette of sunshine yellow, clear-sky blue, and deep ocean navy is paired with dynamic wave graphics. The brand’s signature ‘H’ remains, now converted into a clean white masterbrand marque inspired by harbour structures and wayfinding, complemented by new illustrations created with illustrator Con McHugh.
The resulting brand system is designed to grow across cans, taps, glassware, and merchandise – helping the brewery build recognition both on retail shelves and in the on-trade.
“Harbour already had a brilliant spirit. We wanted to bottle that feeling,” says Sam Cutler, Creative Director at Thirst. “We kept coming back to those moments after the action – when the boards are down, the sun’s still out and everyone settles in for a beer. The identity needed to feel as relaxed and playful as that, while working incredibly hard as a brand. The hardest thing to create is something that feels effortless.”
The commercial rationale behind the makeover is already paying off: since the relaunch, Harbour reports sales up 45% year-on-year and running 18% ahead of budget.
“Thirst understood very quickly that this wasn’t just about making Harbour look different – it was about helping us grow,” adds Eddie Lofthouse, Founder of Harbour Brewing Co. “What they’ve created feels unmistakably ours, while giving us far more room to evolve.”
For Thirst – an independent studio focused strictly on the beverage sector with clients like Skyy, Heineken Studio, Tennent’s, and The Famous Grouse – the project highlights the power of hyper-specialisation. While deep sectoral focus is sometimes criticised in the design industry for potentially limiting creative crossover, cases like Harbour demonstrate its strength: category fluency paired with sharp commercial execution.
