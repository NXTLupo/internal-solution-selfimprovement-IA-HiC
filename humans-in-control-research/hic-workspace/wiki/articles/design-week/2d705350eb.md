# Design sector outpaces retail to become £136.7bn pillar of UK economy

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/design-sector-outpaces-retail-to-become-136-7bn-pillar-of-uk-economy/)

The UK design economy contributed £136.7bn in gross value added (GVA) in 2023, overtaking the retail sector in its economic footprint, according to new research from the Design Council.
Published today, the Design Economy 2026 report reveals that design activity now accounts for 5.5% of total UK GVA, or approximately £1 in every £20 generated in the UK. The sector expanded by 40% between 2019 and 2023, nearly double the 24% growth seen across the wider UK economy during the same period.
“These figures should change the way we think about design.” – Mat Hunter, Chief Executive of the Design Council
The industry’s economic contribution is now larger than the UK’s entire £114bn retail sector, and almost twice the size of the £70bn accommodation and food service industry.
Crucially, the data highlights a structural shift in how the corporate and public sectors utilise design capability. Two-thirds of the GVA created by the design economy – totalling £90.6bn – is generated by designers working outside specialist design agencies, in fields such as manufacturing, healthcare, engineering, and finance. Around 80% of designers now work in non-design industries.
Between 2020 and 2025, the employment of designers in these non-design sectors grew by 20%, compared with just 6% growth within traditional design industries. The findings indicate that organisations are increasingly embedding design capabilities in-house to drive strategic transformation and product development. Average productivity across the design economy reached £60,704 per employee in 2023, rising to £96,200 per worker in product and industrial design.
Mat Hunter, chief executive of the Design Council, said the findings mandate a fundamental change in how the profession is perceived.
“These figures should change the way we think about design. It is not a niche creative activity sitting at the edge of the economy; it is a £136.7 billion capability embedded in the way organisations effectively innovate, tackle complex problems and grow,” Hunter said. “The opportunity now is to make sure that capability is available everywhere – in businesses and public services, across every region and nation – and that we invest in the skills and diversity needed to sustain it for the future.”
Jane Geraghty, global CEO of Landor and WPP Brand & Design and a Design Council ambassador, said the research underlines the direct connection between commercial differentiation and national economic growth, as well as the need for future-ready design talent equipped with technological fluency and commercial judgment.
“£136.7 billion shows the scale of design’s contribution to the UK economy. In brand design, our role is to create valuable difference – turning business strategy into something distinctive that people can recognise, remember and choose,” Geraghty said. “More differentiated brands grow faster, so when UK businesses invest in creating that difference, design doesn’t just strengthen individual brands; it helps power the wider economy.”
Digital dominance and global trade
A booming digital sector
Digital design remains the primary engine of sector growth. Generating £81bn in GVA – a 50% increase since 2019 – digital design now accounts for 59% of total design economy GVA and employs 1.09 million professionals, representing nearly half of the entire design workforce.
Across other disciplines, product and industrial design generated £21.6bn (up 33% since 2019), architecture and the built environment contributed £18.4bn (up 30%), multidisciplinary design generated £6.6bn (up 23%), graphic design contributed £3.2bn (up 14%), advertising design reached £2.9bn (up 33%), craft generated £2.6bn (up 8%), and clothing design contributed £400m (up 45%).
Strong export performance
The UK’s global design footprint also expanded significantly. Total design exports reached £88.2bn in 2022, accounting for 10% of all UK exports. Design service exports grew to £71bn, representing 17% of total UK service exports – equivalent to around 77% of the value of UK financial services exports. Digital design alone drove £50.4bn of these service exports.
Geraint Edwards, chief growth officer at design consultancy tangerine, highlighted the resilient international demand for UK design expertise.
“We’re seeing strong international demand for UK expertise in transport and infrastructure projects, where British design’s real competitive advantage lies in combining creativity with technical excellence,” Edwards said.
Regional growth outpaces the capital
De-centering from London
While London and the South East continue to generate almost half of total design GVA (£66.7bn combined), regional employment growth is significantly outpacing the capital. Between 2020 and 2025, design employment in London grew by just 3%, whereas regions such as the East Midlands (+25%), the South West (+23%), North East (+22%), and Wales (+20%) experienced rapid workforce growth.
Regional GVA figures reinforce this momentum. Since 2019, design GVA increased by 88% in Wales, 67% in the North East, 32% in Scotland, 44% in the East of England, and 39% in the North West.
Emerging growth clusters
The report identifies distinct regional clusters, including digital design in Middlesbrough, product and industrial design in East Renfrewshire, craft in Stoke-on-Trent and Orkney, and architecture in the South West corridor.
Professor Mariana Mazzucato, founding director of the UCL Institute for Innovation and Public Purpose, emphasised that design acts as a catalyst for broader structural transformation.
“Design increasingly demonstrates that it is not merely another productive sector,” Mazzucato said. “It is a strategic capability that shapes how products are made, services are organised, technologies are governed, institutions operate and places develop in fields including healthcare, finance, advanced manufacturing, digital technologies, and the built environment. The report therefore provides evidence not simply of economic expansion, but of design’s role in steering economic transformation.”
Ian Murray MP, Minister of State in the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, added that design supports regional economic strategies.
“This is a story that extends beyond London,” Murray said. “Across the UK, design supports local economies, creates high-value jobs and helps places build distinctive strengths. As we deliver our Industrial Strategy and Creative Industries Sector Plan, the evidence here shows how design can support growth and opportunity in every region and nation.”
Education pipeline and diversity deficits
The skills pipeline under threat
Despite record economic figures, the report warns that future growth faces critical vulnerabilities. Secondary education entries for Design and Technology (D&T) GCSEs fell by 68% over the decade to 2024, threatening the long-term talent pipeline just as industry demand for advanced design skills accelerates.
Tony Ryan, chief executive of the Design & Technology Association, called for immediate action in schools.
“Rebuilding the design talent pipeline starts in our schools,” Ryan said. “Our Regional Membership Initiative connects education, industry and communities, inspiring more young people to see design, innovation and engineering as exciting career pathways.”
Persistent workforce imbalances
Workforce diversity also remains severely lagging. Women account for only 25% of the overall design workforce, dropping to just 9% in product and industrial design. Black designers represent 2% of the workforce compared to 5% of the wider UK working population, while disabled designers account for 16% compared to 18% nationally.
Alisha Morenike-Fisher, founder of Black Females in Architecture, underscored the need for systemic change.
“Talent exists everywhere, but opportunities do not,” Morenike-Fisher said. “I look forward to the day when Black women can look across London’s buildings, landscapes, and public spaces and see themselves reflected in the city; knowing that women who look like us have helped design, shape, and transform it.”
Policy recommendations for government
To address these structural challenges and maintain the UK’s competitive edge, the Design Council has outlined five policy asks for government:
- Regional Design Growth Hubs: Establish regional hubs led by Mayoral Strategic Authorities to connect local businesses and public services with design expertise.
- Industrial Strategy Recognition: Explicitly integrate design as a core innovation capability across national R&D, commercialisation programmes, and public procurement frameworks.
- National Skills Pipeline: Fund early careers engagement and establish the Design Council Learning Lab to train designers in AI, green design, and systems thinking.
- Targeted Export Support: Create tailored international trade programmes and practical IP support for design SMEs entering global markets.
- National Design for AI Adoption Challenge: Launch a national initiative demonstrating how human-centred design can drive ethical, sustainable, and effective AI deployment across public and private sectors.
Design’s not dead. But it’s under pressure.
