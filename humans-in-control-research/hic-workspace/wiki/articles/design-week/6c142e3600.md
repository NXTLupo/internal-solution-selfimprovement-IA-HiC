# Faber Layers Three Generations of Family Heritage into Rossella Muswell Hill

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/faber-layers-three-generations-of-family-heritage-into-rossella-muswell-hill/)

Completed in May 2026, the Muswell Hill venue marks the next chapter for third-generation owner Luca Meola. The family’s hospitality lineage originated in Italy during the 1960s before establishing its first UK restaurant in 1984. Following their site in Kentish Town, this second North London location refrains from replicating a single period, bringing together stylistic influences across the decades.
Faber creative director Tony Matters outlined the studio’s intention to avoid artificial nostalgia within the interior, saying, ‘We didn’t want to create something artificially nostalgic. The idea was to layer references from different generations, so the family’s history could be felt throughout the space.’
Stained oak, polished chrome, marble, leather and backlit onyx form the material palette, drawing on architectural details from the 1960s, 1970s and 1980s. Meola emphasised the importance of authentically reflecting the business’s heritage in the physical environment. He said, ‘You don’t want to walk into a restaurant with 60 years of history and have everything feel brand new. You want to feel that history from the moment you arrive.’
Space and lighting
The floor plan is anchored by a central illuminated bar, which separates the main dining room from Nonna’s Lounge, a dedicated bar-led space created to adapt across different times of day. Large windows supply natural illumination for lunch service, while evening dining transitions through dim-to-warm fixtures, candlelight, and deeper tonal palettes.
Suspended above Nonna’s Lounge are six bespoke Halo Light Panels created by Faber specifically for the project. Each one-metre-square fitting features a mirror-polished stainless-steel shell containing a circular opal-acrylic diffuser. Together, the fixtures form a geometric ceiling arrangement that shifts from bright daytime lighting to a soft orange illumination in the evening.
Personality and community
Intimate archival details are integrated across the interior scheme. A dedicated picture wall displays family photographs spanning generations, while a small booth positioned near the kitchen incorporates an old clock sourced from Meola’s grandfather’s home.
The resulting layout combines historic family references with contemporary functional requirements for the local area. Meola said, ‘Every community needs a community restaurant. That is what we have always tried to be, and I think that is why people have embraced us.’
