# CIWM and URGE Collective reveal circular design sprint outcomes tackling industrial waste

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/ciwm-and-urge-collective-reveal-circular-design-sprint-outcomes-tackling-industrial-waste/)

The nine-month Design Skills for Embedding Circularity pilot programme has come to a close, with participating designers presenting innovative concepts to address critical end-of-life material challenges. Funded by the Chartered Institution of Wastes Management (CIWM) and delivered alongside the Design Council, WRAP, the Circular Economy Institute (CEI), and URGE Collective, the initiative was established to provide professional designers with direct experience in waste management operations.
Since January, the cohort toured industrial waste sites across the UK under the guidance of circular economy expert Sophie Thomas. The site visits included Sherbourne Recycling’s Super MRF, SWEEEP’s electronic recycling plant, DS Smith’s recycled paper mill, Enfinium’s energy-from-waste facility, Biffa’s Edmonton MRF, Enovert’s Gloucestershire landfill sites, and SUEZ’s Renew Hub in Manchester. The designers also visited Decathlon’s London headquarters and Allermuir’s furniture remanufacturing hub to observe existing circular business operations first-hand.
Richard Hudson, technical manager at CIWM and project lead, said, “A circular economy depends on bridging the gap between front-end product design and end-of-life resource recovery. By putting professional designers directly onto facility floors alongside our sector’s waste and resource experts, this pilot has demonstrated the power of cross-sector collaboration. The technical insight gained through this sprint empowers designers to design out waste from the outset, ensuring materials remain in high-value use for as long as possible.”
Prototypes and digital tools tackle live waste briefs
Moving from recycling plant floors to design studios, the cohort applied technical insights directly to live commercial briefs set by Biffa and Decathlon during the six-week sprint phase. Biffa tasked designers with tackling the fire hazards caused by discarded single-use vapes and creating methods to disassemble small electronics to capture lithium batteries. Decathlon’s briefs focused on festival tent waste, tennis ball disposal, and reusable packaging for buy-back schemes.
The resulting proposals address systemic issues across manufacturing, material flow, and consumer behaviour. Jess Wright from Imperial Brands partnered with Biffa to conduct user research on altering consumer behaviour around vape disposal to reduce facility fire risks. Pippa Bridges at Designability focused on redesigning small electronic products to simplify battery removal and detection prior to processing.
Digital and structural solutions were also brought forward. Jonathan Irawan proposed Mobius, a data-driven digital platform designed to improve material flow communication between product designers and waste operators by tracking material lifecycles in real-time. Luke Pearson and Rory Mullins of Pearson Lloyd created concepts for decentralised waste management through responsive 3D-printed products. Addressing physical sorting limitations, Abby Brazier designed a toothbrush specifically structured for material recovery, preventing items from falling through the holes of industrial sorting trommels by integrating a wider base structure.
Amy Hooper, director of sustainability and innovation at Biffa, said, “The programme created a unique environment for collaboration, learning and fresh perspectives, and the quality of the ideas, creativity and insight throughout was exceptional. Most importantly, it demonstrated the value of bringing people together from across the value chain to embed circularity into products and systems from the very beginning.”
Industry response and upcoming London symposium
Industry feedback from corporate partners highlighted the immediate practical value of the cross-sector approach. Chris Allen, head of sustainability at Decathlon UK, commented, “The programme provides a clear, grounded model for circular innovation. URGE Collective, CIWM, and the Design Council built a process that exposed designers to the complete lifecycle, from industrial waste facilities to the retail reality at Decathlon, where they saw how circular models actually function for consumers.”
Cat Drew, chief design officer at the Design Council, said, “This programme offers a pioneering model for how designers learn. Providing immersive experiences that they will never forget, cohort learning that builds relationships and networks that will create future innovations, and industry partnerships that deepen the way design is embedded across the wider economy.”
Mat Hunter, chief executive at the Design Council, added, “The cohort of designers left with an irreversibly altered perspective on the need for a circular economy. They saw each other as an enduring support group as their practice developed, and they generated powerful ideas for tools and platforms that will move the whole design economy forward.”
Following interest from design professionals across the UK, Europe, and Australia, URGE Collective is currently seeking funding partners for future iterations of the programme. The public will be able to access details online in October, preceding an invite-only symposium held at the Design Museum in London on 12 November 2026, where complete findings and insights from the pilot will be formally presented.
