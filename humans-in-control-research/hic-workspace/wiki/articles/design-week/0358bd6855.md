# design/leader: Thomas Kurppa, executive creative director at Kurppa Hosk

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/design-leader-thomas-kurppa-executive-creative-director-at-kurppa-hosk/)

This week, we interview Thomas Kurppa, executive creative director at Kurppa Hosk. With over 20 years of experience in developing identities that build strong emotional bonds between people and brands, Thomas is one of Sweden’s most merited designers, and his work has made Kurppa Hosk one of the few Swedish agencies working outside of Scandinavia.
Thomas talks to us about synthesizers, the standardised shipping container, and stylistic conviction.
Design
What would your monograph be called?
A Life in Frequencies.
Electronic music and design have always existed side by side for me. Two ways of thinking through rhythm, repetition, tension, and structure. A Life in Frequencies is about that parallel journey and how one has quietly shaped the way I understand the other.
What recent design work made you a bit jealous?
The New Museum expansion by OMA’s Shohei Shigematsu and Rem Koolhaas in New York.
There’s something about the project’s confidence: how it feels both rational and slightly unruly, precise without becoming polite. I’m always drawn to work that seems to understand the rules well enough to know exactly where to break them. It also seems to provoke people, which is usually a good sign.
What’s an unusual place you get inspiration from?
Driving at night in the rain, with a hypnotic electronic wall of sound.
There’s something about movement, darkness and repetition. The road dissolves into reflections, the world reduced to light, rhythm and direction. It creates a strange state somewhere between focus and drift. That’s often where ideas appear, before I’ve had the chance to think them into submission.
Name something that is brilliantly designed, but overlooked.
The standardised shipping container.
One of the most consequential design objects of the 20th century. Packaging, storage, home, architecture, truck cargo, ship module – all in the same object. Even the codes, numbers, and warnings have a graphic quality that contemporary design often tries very hard to achieve.
What object in your studio best sums up your taste?
The Superlative SB01 synthesizer.
We developed the brand identity and became close to its founder, Ben Stamas, who shared a very similar view of design and aesthetics. For me, it connects two parallel histories: electronic music and design. It sounds like where I come from and looks remarkably close to how I think.
Leadership
What feedback felt brutal at the time, but turned out to be useful?
A professor once told me to stop listening to designers who were suspicious of style. Style isn’t a limitation; it’s what conviction looks like over time. Sometimes versatility is just uncertainty with a nice portfolio.
What’s an underappreciated skill that design leaders need?
Knowing the difference between conviction and ego.
What keeps you up at night?
That consensus is incredibly good at producing things nobody hates.
What trait is non-negotiable in new hires?
Curiosity without permission.
Complete this sentence: “I wish more clients…”
…asked for fewer answers and better questions.
