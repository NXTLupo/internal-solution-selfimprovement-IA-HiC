# Baby Teeth rebrands Royal Society of Arts with archival system and Dame Judi Dench film

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/baby-teeth-rebrands-royal-society-of-arts-with-archival-system-and-dame-judi-dench-film/)

The Royal Society of Arts (RSA) has unveiled a new visual identity designed by creative agency Baby Teeth, marking a major repositioning for the 272-year-old institution around its commitment to act as the home of creativity for the common good. This pivot arrives at a critical moment, asserting the societal case for creativity against a contemporary backdrop of stripped-back UK arts funding and creative education.
Rooted in principles of creative adaptation, the design system treats the RSA’s extensive archive as working material. Historical image treatments incorporate disruption patterns such as historic collisions – combining multiple eras into direct visual dialogue – alongside vertically sliced split portraits and offset crops.
These disruptive graphics are designed to interrupt continuity across physical applications, from the biannual journal to environmental touchpoints. Notable past Fellows, including Marie Curie, Vivienne Westwood, Bernardine Evaristo, David Attenborough, and Tim Berners-Lee, feature alongside historic initiatives like the Blue Plaque Scheme, the Fourth Plinth, and early lifeboat inventions.
Monogram, type, colour
The core graphic marks take direct inspiration from the RSA’s historic central London headquarters, RSA House. A new monogram draws from the six-lobed rosette motif within the building’s oculus façade, incorporating a dropped, angled letter ‘A’ designed to introduce movement and structural disruption. A complementary façade stamp distils the building into a secondary mark to support the main wordmark.
Typography plays a central role in the system across three primary typefaces. Goldschneider, a refined display serif, forms the wordmark and handles headline messaging. Steps Mono provides a functional counterpoint for environmental information, event details, and dates, while Helvetica Neue Light handles long-form editorial copy.
The colour scheme pairs institutional authority with energetic intervention. Royal Blue forms the civic foundation, Signal Red provides a sharp intervention colour to introduce urgency, and White creates openness and clarity across layouts.
The Case for Human Imagination
Launching alongside the visual identity, a short piece of video art titled The Case for Human Imagination uses archival footage that deconstructs and rebuilds to demonstrate creative thinking. Narrated by actor and RSA Fellow Dame Judi Dench, the film concludes with the organisation’s new rallying call: “Imagine what we can do”.
Charlotte Thomson, director of strategy and creative at the RSA, said, “The Royal Society of Arts is coming out of its shell to meet the moment. Its belief is timeless: imagination leads to impact. This storied institution inspires, excites, and brings together people from all walks of life. And it’s needed more than ever. Standing on its history of fantastic Fellows and their world-changing ideas, the RSA wears a new brand identity that truly fits: intriguing, inviting and contemporary, with a touch of playfulness. Baby Teeth immediately grasped the character and significance of the RSA, and why it feels exciting and urgent today.”
Lynsey Atkin, founder and partner at Baby Teeth, said, “When the Royal Society of Arts was founded, what people lacked were the means – the access, the knowledge, the technology – to make their visions for a better world a reality. Today, in a world abundant with means, the RSA’s urgent call is for vision: to imagine a better, fairer world for all. Spending time with RSA Fellow Dame Judi Dench, talking about the importance of human imagination to societal progress, was unsurprisingly both a thrill and an honour. We live in challenging times, so to demonstrate her words of conviction and hope through the rich archive of the RSA has proved fascinating and energising.”
The brand rollout encompasses all Fellowship communications, digital platforms, social channels, the biannual journal, and physical touchpoints at RSA House, alongside a new public events series structured around the RSA’s renewed mission.
