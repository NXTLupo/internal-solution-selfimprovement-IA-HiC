# The Outline: a green pop-up, Adobe’s Slack, and a newspaper – 07/09/2026

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/the-outline-a-green-pop-up-adobes-slack-and-a-newspaper-07-09-2026/)

Welcome back to another edition of The Outline, your weekly digest of spatial activations, software shake-ups, analogue print revivals, and standout sustainable craft. This week, we dive into Adobe’s massive push into team chatrooms, a completely monochromatic matcha destination in Berlin, and a loving homage to New York’s newsstand culture.
Announcements
Adobe launches native workspace app for Slack
Digital design titan Adobe has officially rolled out Adobe for Slack, bringing over 70 creative and productivity tools directly into Slack workspace chats for Business+ and Enterprise+ users. Powered by Slackbot, the integration allows team members to generate campaign assets, edit images in bulk, transform raw spreadsheets into PDF product catalogues, and pull Creative Cloud files straight into team conversations using natural-language prompts. By embedding tools from Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, and Firefly into everyday workplace chat, Adobe is making content creation an immediate, collaborative fluid process.
Royal College of Art establishes new School of Communication & Design
The Royal College of Art has announced the creation of a new School of Communication & Design, unifying its communication and design disciplines into a single academic faculty. Led by Dean Professor Kerry Curtis, the strategic merger streamlines the world-leading postgraduate institution into a three-school structure alongside the existing School of Architecture and School of Arts & Humanities.
The new school will deliver twelve postgraduate programmes—including MA Visual Communication, MA Design Products, and MDes Design Futures—aiming to foster deeper interdisciplinary collaboration as emerging technologies like AI redefine creative practice. RCA President and Vice-Chancellor Professor Christoph Lindner noted that aligning the fields equips students to lead industry-wide transformations while tackling complex societal challenges through critical enquiry and material intelligence.
Business News
Etihad Airways launches global Beyond Borders brand campaign
UAE national carrier Etihad Airways has unveiled Beyond Borders, an expansive global campaign highlighting human ambition through real-life, non-actor stories across five continents. Built around six core brand films, the initiative follows five individuals who crossed personal and geographical boundaries, including architectural-turn-wildlife photographer Suaad Al Suwaidi and Michelin-starred chef Arivu Ravikkumar. Filmed on location across Abu Dhabi, New York, Nairobi, Phuket, and Paris, the international effort was coordinated with post-production teams spanning Barcelona, Warsaw, Sydney, and Copenhagen.
The campaign leverages Etihad’s star-studded sponsorship portfolio to amplify its narrative, featuring Manchester City forward Erling Haaland revisiting the European cities that shaped his career, along with McLaren Formula 1 driver Oscar Piastri. Further stories draw on athletes from the Chennai Super Kings, Euroleague, and Badminton World Federation. Supported by heavy paid media investment, the campaign aims to ground Etihad’s international reach in authentic personal storytelling.
Cee Cee Creative applies monochrome green concept for Oatly Berlin pop-up
Berlin-based agency Cee Cee Creative has transformed a grey shipping container space on Berlin’s Ku’damm into a striking, all-green immersive retail environment for Oatly Oatdrink Matcha. Rather than relying on overt corporate logos, the spatial design concept uses a curated palette of green shades to communicate brand identity through interior architecture, local design finds, and collectible furniture.
Designed with a social-first digital strategy from its inception, the pop-up features curated photogenic perspectives and local Berlin ties—including green-tinted design objects and local cultural references—turning traditional retail into an interactive content studio and community gathering hub.
Design Bits
Forenext Design Studio creates mechanical 3D planner for SYN Universal Calendar
Los Angeles-based studio Forenext Design and founder Johnny Jiasheng Chen have introduced the SYN Universal Calendar, a Silver A’ Design Award-winning three-dimensional mechanical planner designed as an analogue alternative to digital scheduling apps. Drawing heavily on the philosophy of Lo-TEK design, the tactile desk object adapts to any month and year, offering a physical structure to track and record events without screen fatigue.
Constructed as a minimalist, freestanding sculpture, SYN optimizes information density through physical alignment and geometric intervals. The studio hopes the tactile interaction will encourage users to pause and engage more thoughtfully with their monthly routines and time management.
RCA graduates craft sustainable interventions for Hyundai Excellence Awards
The Royal College of Art has named the winners of its fourth annual Hyundai Motor Group Awards for Excellence in Sustainability and Creative Practice at its Battersea campus. Among the top honours, Ruben Greyson secured the Inspiration Award for Re-Jig, a digital timber framework processing felled urban trees into carbon-sequestering structures. Emma Loughnane captured the Innovation Award for This Green Unpleasant Land, combining fashion and performance architecture to challenge feudal land ownership.
Meanwhile, Hiền Hoàng received the Aesthetics & Craft Award for How to Touch the Elephant, an installation translating bioelectrical soil data from post-Agent Orange landscapes in Vietnam into dynamic artistic interventions. Judged by design leaders from Hyundai, Kia, and the RCA, the winning projects demonstrate how multidisciplinary craft can confront climate breakdown and environmental trauma.
Kirkby Unveils Major Brand Reset
Textile brand Kirkby Design has launched a comprehensive identity refresh, shortening its name to Kirkby to reflect its expanding portfolio of fabrics, rugs, and interior accessories. Under the new brand proposition “The New Cultural Fabric,” the repositioning reinforces the idea that textiles reflect modern culture, craft, and technology.
Designed in collaboration with Stephenson Edwards, the new identity is built around three core principles—Reinvention, Self-Expression, and Consideration—and introduces a bolder visual system. Designed by the internal creative team, it features an updated brand colour, a redesigned logo, and a distinct new symbol created to capture the brand’s progressive approach to contemporary interiors.
Desk Curiosities
Newspaper Club celebrates the NYC newsstand resurgence with a fresh print title
Print specialist Newspaper Club is launching a publication, The Printing Press, at Brooklyn’s Secret Riso Club on 17 September to champion the ongoing renaissance of analogue newspapers. The issue highlights US-based creative talent using newsprint as a medium for community building, featuring layout tips from New York Magazine Art Director Suzanna Hayward and stories from independent publishers.
To mark the release, Newspaper Club is taking over Manhattan’s Old News Stand on 34th Street and 9th Avenue for a print giveaway. Having printed over 40 million newspapers since 2009—including 3.5 million in 2025 alone—the company reports explosive revenue growth driven by creatives seeking tangible alternatives to digital noise.
