# Mother Design exercises restraint with new M&S pack system

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/mother-design-exercises-restraint-with-new-ms-pack-system/)

Mother Design has come up with a new packaging system for Marks & Spencer‘s thousands of Home and Lingerie products. The branding and design studio, which is part of Mother creative agency, stripped back the retailer’s sub-brands, range names, claims and competing hierarchies.
Monochrome, minimalist solutions are favoured by homeware brands such as The White Company and Zara Home. However, while such packaging is common in the homeware sector, Mother Design’s inspiration came from information design – and how typography, grids, hierarchy and structure have been used to make complex systems feel simple and intuitive – rather than other retailers.
Mother Design creative director Jo Tulej adds that: ‘The redesign was about responding to M&S’s own brand and customer needs. Home is increasingly viewed as an extension of our personal style, and you can see that not only in buying habits but also the way brands are turning up in this space, be that fashion brands like Zara and H&M, or smaller brands like Hay and Ferm Living.
‘Customers are expecting home brands to bring that desirability and style confidence into the experience. So the redesign of the packaging not only needed to help people buy, but also position M&S in a more confident style-orientated way.’
Mother Design’s aim was three-fold: to make the packaging feel as stylish as the M&S brand; to strengthen the role of the M&S core brand across these products; and to make ranges easier for customers to navigate and shop, particularly in an increasingly self-service retail environment where packaging has to work harder to support purchase decisions. (Recent sales figures for home and lingerie were not available from M&S.)
Typography and hierarchy
Mother Design describes the visual system as deliberately restrained. ‘We retained the brand’s MS London typeface, which was previously being used in inconsistent weights and hierarchies across the many ranges, and created a unified system across three weights,’ Tulej says. ‘We increased the tracking to ensure everything felt more refined and visually consistent.’
In terms of the new system, the team standardised on using caps for product titles and sentence case for benefits and body copy, to improve visual hierarchy and legibility at small sizes.
What’s new is MS London Geo, a numeral set crafted by Mother Design, as ‘the pre-existing one didn’t lend itself to being used at scale,’ Tulej adds. ‘It’s optimised for use at large scale, which felt more elevated, contemporary and geometric. Design-wise, it was heavily informed by MS London and classic British type design to ensure they worked well together.’ Monotype, which originally built MS London, developed the final typeface (MS London Geo).
Visual layout and material packaging
In Home, a hierarchy separates front- and back-of-pack information, while a consistent ‘benefits grid’ and those MS London Geo numerals help shoppers identify key product features, pack contents and sizing at a glance.
The monochrome palette built around M&S black and white is intended to have a sharper presence on shelf, supported by colour-coded bedding size stickers to aid navigation. Some products have moved from plastic bags into premium-style boxes, while duvets now come in reusable cotton bags. The tone of voice has also changed, with what the team calls tongue-in-cheek levity in Home.
While the Home imagery is tightly cropped, the Lingerie collection uses natural light and relaxed styling. Lingerie packaging features warm whites and charcoal tones, incorporating a colour-coded sizing system that brings all sizes into a single range, rather than separating larger cup sizes into a standalone sub-brand. The tone here shifts to a relaxed, guiding confidence.
Beyond the shelf
‘As it has only just started rolling out, it’s too early to know results beyond anecdotal positive feedback from M&S colleagues and customers,’ says Mother Design partner Kathryn Jubrail.
The Mother ‘family’ has been working with M&S since 2023, with the aim of driving M&S’s visual aesthetic, style credentials, and flagship fashion campaigns, like the retailer’s first-ever runway show in Ibiza for the summer 2026 collection.
This system will appear in other categories, but not Food, as Mother’s remit only covers Fashion, Home and Beauty, says Jubrail. ‘Our task was to establish the foundational design system across the two categories that have the highest packaging demands (Home and Lingerie), so that internal teams can roll it out across the other business units, such as Kids.’
