# Public Address rebrands Code.org as CodeAI to tackle AI literacy

Source: [design_week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/public-address-rebrands-code-org-as-codeai-to-tackle-ai-literacy/)

Founded in 2013, the organisation has reached more than 155 million students and three million teachers across 190 countries. The rebrand addresses a growing education gap, with CodeAI research revealing that 84% of US students use AI tools while only 16% of high school leaders report that all their students learn about it in school.
While its original name established its presence in school classrooms, the curriculum expanded over the decade to encompass computational thinking, data science, and responsible computing. The updated identity reflects a strategic stance that understanding the underlying science of AI is essential for students navigating digital environments.
Mac Russell, vice-president of brand and marketing strategy at CodeAI, said, ‘Public Address established a brand system that says a student who learns computer science, AI science and data science here walks away with knowledge that holds up as the technology underneath it keeps changing. They built that into the system itself – technical and human, dark and light, held in contrast instead of resolved.’
Russell added, ‘That tension is the actual condition students live in: a physical world and a digital one, at the same time, all the time. The identity does not smooth that over, and that is why it works. Every student has to be prepared for both.’
Rejecting AI clichés
In developing the visual system, Public Address deliberately avoided popular visual shortcuts associated with artificial intelligence, such as sparkles, mascots or anthropomorphised imagery. The studio rejected elements that present AI as sentient, steering clear of features like eyes on generative shapes to encourage critical thinking rather than emotional attachment.
Riley McClimond, senior designer at Public Address, said, ‘There are a few that we really actively avoided. No sparkles, for instance.’ McClimond added, ‘The most important one though, we avoided anthropomorphising AI. We didn’t want young people forming an emotional attachment to AI, we wanted to encourage critical thinking and healthy scepticism.’
Instead, the brand presents AI as code and algorithm. The original logo’s modular box structure was adapted to form the letters for AI, pairing generative tools with handmade forms, illustrations and photography of real students and teachers.
Flexible and accessible design
The identity is designed to communicate with diverse audiences, ranging from classroom pupils to policymakers and donors. Layouts alter depending on context, using a simpler, conservative palette for institutional communications and contrasting colours for student-facing materials.
Accessibility was a primary consideration in the typography and digital implementation. The identity uses Space Grotesk alongside freely available open-source fonts to enable educators to recreate branded materials using accessible tools. Layouts and colour palettes meet AA and AAA contrast standards across teaching tools and digital channels.
Chris Braden, co-founder and chief creative officer at Public Address, said, ‘This was about helping CodeAI move into its next chapter without losing the equity it had already built. The challenge was to make that shift feel clear and confident.’
Braden noted, ‘The result is a system that can flex across different audiences and contexts while still feeling optimistic and grounded in the future of education.’
Design’s not dead. But it’s under pressure.
