# The Web Has a New User: AI

Source: [brian_solis](https://briansolis.com/2026/08/35942/)

For decades, we built the web for people.
Now, AI is becoming a user too.
It searches, reads, compares, summarizes, recommends, and increasingly acts on our behalf. That means the web is no longer just a collection of destinations for humans. It is becoming an intelligent layer where machines help decide what gets discovered, trusted, chosen, and acted upon.
This is a much bigger shift than AI-generated content or the future of search.
The web itself is changing. And as content becomes infinite, attention scarcer, and discovery increasingly mediated by AI, the advantage will go to those who become the signal…for humans and AI agents.
The Intelligence RenAIssance
I recently had the opportunity to keynote WP Engine’s DE{CODE} event, where this year’s theme explored one of the most important shifts now reshaping business, marketing, commerce, publishing, and customer experience: the rise of the intelligent web. [FULL VIDEO AT THE END]
This is not just the next chapter of the internet. It is a fundamental rewiring of how people discover, evaluate, decide, buy, learn, and engage. And for the first time, we are no longer designing digital experiences only for humans. We are also designing for the AI agents that increasingly search, summarize, compare, recommend, and act on their behalf.
Websites are no longer static destinations. Content is no longer enough. Search is no longer just search. And AI is not simply a tool to produce more, faster. Used in the right way, AI becomes a catalyst for relevance, trust, personalization, and meaningful engagement. Used in the wrong way, it accelerates noise, slop, sameness, and cognitive surrender.
In my keynote, I explored how leaders, marketers, agencies, publishers, eCommerce brands, SMBs, and creatives can navigate this shift with intention.
This article expands on the ideas I shared in the keynote. My hope is that it gives you a deeper way to think about what is changing, why it matters, and what to do next. Because the future of the web is not just being built by AI. It is being built by us.
Welcome to the Intelligent Web
We are entering an industry renAIssance.
Research shows that one-third of all new published websites are generated by AI. According to Wired, researches from Imperial College of London, Stanford University, and the Internet Archive found that not only are 35% of all new websites AI-generated, online writing is “increasingly sanitized and artificially cheerful,” which appears to be making the internet fake-happy. And according to projections from ARK Invest, AI-generated text has officially outpaced human-written content, symbolizing a shift in how information is created and consumed across the internet.
This is an inflection point.
Now, the web is being reimagined for two audiences at once: human beings and the AI agents increasingly acting on our behalf. Search is becoming synthesis. Browsing is becoming delegation. Content is becoming context. Websites are becoming intelligent business platforms. And every organization now has to ask a new question:
Are we building for synthetic traffic and transactions, or are we building for engagement and trust?
That was the heart of my keynote at WP Engine’s DE{CODE}, where this year’s event explored how AI is reshaping the web for both humans and agents. WP Engine framed the moment as a move from static digital delivery systems to “intelligent, context-rich experiences” that AI systems will seek and cite.
The intelligent web will not reward everyone equally. It will reward those who become useful, credible, discoverable, readable, actionable, and trusted. It will reward those who stop treating AI as a content machine and start treating it as a catalyst for better experiences, deeper relevance, and more meaningful relationships.
That is why I opened the keynote with a simple truth: none of this came with an instruction manual. There is no official playbook for how to build a web that serves humans and machines. There is no universal standard for agent-ready commerce. There is no mature operating model for trust in an AI-mediated customer journey. And yet, here we are, building the future in real time.
The question is not whether the web will become intelligent.
It already is.
The question is whether we will be intelligent enough to shape it in a meaningful way from here.
Cognitive Darwinism Comes for the Web
I call this moment Cognitive Darwinism, which represents a human moment in how we choose to work with AI. Do we let AI do our thinking or do we expand our thinking with AI?
Cognitive Darwinism is the evolution of behavior, decision-making, creativity, and trust under the pressure of artificial intelligence. Some people will use AI to think better. Others will use it to avoid thinking altogether. Some companies will use AI to augment imagination. Others will use it to automate mediocrity at speed and scale.
AI can extend us. It can help us explore the unknown, challenge assumptions, synthesize complexity, and design possibilities we may not have seen otherwise. But when we simply ask AI to do the work for us, we risk collapsing into the known. We ask for answers instead of better questions. We accelerate output without deepening insight. We publish without learning. We create without meaning.
That is artificial intelligence. And because it offloaded thinking, it’s being studied as “cognitive surrender.” And surrender has consequences.
Researchers are already examining how AI use can encourage cognitive offloading and affect critical thinking. Gallup found that Gen Z’s sentiment toward AI has become more negative over the past year, with excitement and hope falling and anger rising.
48% of Gen Z workers say that the risks of AI in the workforce outweigh its benefits and a staggering 80% say that using it as a shortcut makes learning more difficult. Forty-four percent of Gen Z workers admit to sabotaging their employers’ AI deployments as a form of rebellion. They “hate” AI because of 1) fears of job displacement, 2) security holes, and 3) AI paradoxically adds more work (that’s the AI Tax!)
This does not mean AI is bad. It just means the way we use it matters, and we have a lot of work to do.
The intelligent web cannot be built on cognitive surrender nor on through a heavy AI tax. It has to be built on cognitive augmentation and human empowerment.
That means AI should not replace our thinking, it should provoke it. It should not flatten our creativity. It should expand it. It should not remove the human from the equation. It should make the human more capable, more curious, more discerning, and more useful.
The future of the web does not belong to those who can produce the most.
It belongs to those who can matter the most.
AI Slop Is the New Spam
Before we continue, we have to talk about slop because it perfectly captures what happens when speed and scale divorce themselves from quality and care.
Merriam-Webster named “slop” its 2025 Word of the Year, defining it as low-quality digital content produced usually in quantity by artificial intelligence.
AI slop is what happens when organizations confuse publishing content with serving people. It is what happens when marketers use AI to meet quotas instead of fulfill needs. It is what happens when agencies sell volume instead of value. It is what happens when creators stop creating and start outsourcing their voice to the statistical average of the internet to hit arbitrary OKRs.
The problem is not that AI can’t create good content. The problem is that too many people use AI to create content without purpose, empathy, originality, or accountability.
And when that happens, trust erodes (hello LinkedIn peers!)
In the keynote, I made the point that when we use AI to scale content without thinking about the person on the other side, we are not just producing more noise. We are creating more work for everyone else. We are increasing the burden required to find what is useful, true, relevant, and actionable. That burden is the AI tax.
Humans pay it when they have to wade through garbage. And the won’t for long.
Agents will pay it when they have to determine what is credible enough to summarize, recommend, or act upon.
Brands will pay it when they are no longer trusted.
As the web becomes more polluted with synthetic sameness, the value of signal increases. Originality becomes more valuable. Authority becomes more valuable. Empathy becomes more valuable. Taste becomes more valuable. Trust becomes more valuable.
Slop creates the opening for substance, and that window is only growing.
Content Is No Longer King, Context Is (It’s Always Been)
For years, we said content was king and for years, in every era of the web, I’ve said that context is king.
In the early web, that made sense. Content gave people reasons to visit. Content helped brands become visible. Content powered search. Content built audiences.
But in the intelligent web, content alone is not enough.
Context is king.
Context means relevance. It means usefulness. It means the right information, in the right format, at the right moment, for the right human or agent trying to achieve a specific outcome. It is not just what you publish. It is why it matters, who it serves, how it is understood, and what it helps someone do next.
People do not search because they want search results. They search because they want progress and outcomes. They want an answer. A recommendation. A decision. A purchase. A fix. A path. A moment of clarity.
Agents are no different. They will not browse like humans. They will parse, compare, evaluate, summarize, rank, recommend, and act. They will look for structured meaning. They will reward clarity. They will need evidence. They will need permission and governance. They will need signals of trust.
This is why the intelligent web is not merely a publishing challenge. It is an experience design challenge.
We are no longer just designing pages. We are designing outcomes.
