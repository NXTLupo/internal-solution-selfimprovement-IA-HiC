# MagSafe Wallets Leave Your Cards Exposed but This $24 One Closes

Source: [yanko_design](https://www.yankodesign.com/2026/08/26/magsafe-wallets-leave-your-cards-exposed-but-this-24-one-closes/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=magsafe-wallets-leave-your-cards-exposed-but-this-24-one-closes)

The TUCCH PU Leather Mag Wallet does something most MagSafe wallets don’t bother with: it folds closed. Rather than leaving your cards exposed in an open slot on the back of your iPhone, this one shuts like a traditional bi-fold and stays that way thanks to internal magnets. At $23.99, it holds six cards, blocks RFID signals, and includes a clear window for your ID.
The problem with most MagSafe wallets is that they’re really just an open pocket attached to your phone. Your cards sit loose, there’s nothing stopping them from sliding out when you dig into your bag, and pulling the wallet off doesn’t make it any more secure. If you’ve ever watched a card quietly escape an open MagSafe slot mid-commute, you already understand why a wallet that actually closes is a more useful object.
Designer: TUCCH
The folding design here means the same magnets doing the MagSafe work also keep the wallet shut when it’s off the phone. Pop it off your iPhone before going through airport security, tuck it into your jacket pocket, and your cards stay in place rather than fanning out freely. It’s a small shift in how the accessory behaves, but it makes the difference between something that feels like a wallet and something that just holds cards.
Six slots is more than most MagSafe wallets offer, and the layout is more thoughtful than a generic card stack. One slot is a dedicated clear window for your ID or driver’s license, something the category almost universally skips. Two of the remaining slots have cutout openings for the cards you grab most, so you’re not pulling out a chunk of plastic to find your transit card or gym membership every time.
RFID blocking is built into the PU leather shell, which is worth having without having to think about it. Contactless card skimming isn’t something most people worry about until it happens, and having that protection passive rather than optional means one less thing to consider. The wallet handles it quietly, in the same way that the best security features tend to stay out of your way entirely.
Magnetic strength is strong enough to hold through a firm shake of the phone, which is about what you’d expect from a well-built MagSafe wallet at this price point. Compatibility extends beyond TUCCH’s own cases as well. Any MagSafe-compatible case from another brand works just as well, which matters if you’re already committed to a case you like and don’t want to switch just to add a wallet.
The wallet stays flat even when all six card slots are filled, which isn’t guaranteed with wallet accessories in general. A lot of them start to bow under a full card load, adding enough thickness to make a slim phone feel bulky. At approximately 0.4 inches thick fully loaded, the TUCCH keeps things compact enough that adding it doesn’t noticeably change how the phone sits in your hand or your pocket.
Compatibility covers the iPhone 12 series through the current lineup, excluding Mini models and cases without MagSafe support. The $23.99 price puts it well below what most leather MagSafe wallets ask without trimming the features that make this one worth buying. A MagSafe wallet that closes, organizes, and works as a standalone is simply a more complete object than the category norm.
