# Episode 450: Tying Employee Recognition to Work Performance

Source: [exs_workology](https://workology.com/episode-450-tying-employee-recognition-to-work-performance/)

Welcome to the Workology Podcast, a podcast for the disruptive workplace leader join host Jessica Miller-Merrell founder of workology.com as she sits down and gets to the bottom of trends, tools, and case studies for the business leader HR and recruiting professional who is tired of the quo. Now here’s Jessica with this Episode of Workology.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: Welcome to the Workology Podcast, sponsored by Ace the HR Exam and Upskill HR. These are two courses that I offer for HR certification prep and recertification for HR leaders. You can learn more about these courses over at workology.com. This interview you’re listening to now was recorded during the WorldatWork Total Rewards Conference in San Antonio, Texas.
I met so many smart people during this conference, and I’m thrilled to be able to highlight their expertise on this podcast. Employee recognition is everywhere: Slack shout-outs, Employee of the Month programs, even automated kudos tools. But here’s the question HR leaders need to ask: Is your recognition actually driving performance, or is it just noise?
In this episode of the Workology Podcast, we’re digging into what it really means to tie employee recognition to work performance in a way that’s meaningful, measurable, and aligned with business goals. Because when recognition is done right, it’s not just about making people feel good. It’s about reinforcing the behaviors and outcomes that move your organization forward. So we’re going to talk about how to move beyond generic praise, how to connect recognition to specific contributions, and how HR leaders and people managers can build systems that reward impact, not just effort.
So let me get into it. But before we do, I want to hear from you. Please comment “podcast” on the pinned post over on our Instagram account; it’s @WorkologyBlog. You can ask questions, leave comments, and make suggestions for future guests. We want to hear from you.
Today’s guest is Dami Akinduro, Head of Global Benefits, Well-Being, and Recognition at Equinix, where she leads enterprise-wide strategies that connect employee experience with business performance across a global workforce. In her role, she is responsible for designing and scaling benefits, well-being, and recognition programs that support employee engagement, drive performance, and align with organizational priorities in a highly distributed, fast-paced environment. Dami brings deep expertise in total rewards and global HR strategy, with leadership experience spanning multiple regions and complex markets. Prior to joining Equinix, she held senior total rewards roles at Barry Callebaut and Nestlé, where she led compensation and benefit strategies across Africa and the Asia-Pacific region, supporting employees and operations in more than 60 countries.
Earlier in her career, she worked at KPMG advising organizations on compensation, benefits, and HR strategy. She holds an MBA from the University of Cumbria and a degree in biochemistry from the Federal University of Technology at Akure. At Total Rewards, Dami presented a session entitled Recognition that Drives Performance, Belonging, and Retention in the Future Workplace. And that’s why I wanted to sit down with her for this topic today. Dami, welcome to the Workology Podcast.
Dami Akinduro: Thank you for having me.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: Of course. Isn’t it so funny when you get to listen to the bio and it’s like, “Is this me?” Yeah.
Dami Akinduro: Mm, not bad.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: Not bad at all. So you’ve talked about the evolution of employee recognition from tenure-based awards to value-driven programs. Can you talk about what’s driving the shift and why it matters for performance today?
How Employee Recognition Drives Performance and Engagement
Dami Akinduro: Absolutely. So there are two things that come to mind readily. The first is that there are different work models right now. You know, you have hybrid workers, you have fully remote employees, and you have people who spend all their days in the office, right? So it’s quite important to have plans that actually cut across the different employee sets that you have. So now that we have people that are not always in the office, what you find is that some people do stuff, but they’re not seen by the larger employee groups. So it’s important that as things are shifting, and we’re having these different models in place, that we’re also adapting our plans as well.
The other thing is that recognition has actually moved beyond the traditional plans where people have been recognized based on tenure, right? Now we’re trying to implement something that embeds the right behaviors that drive performance. So these two things are actually driving that shift in what we see today compared to where we want to be.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: Well, thank you for all that. And I think everybody listening right now is like, “Yes, I’m experiencing this right now. I’ve got people who are working in different ways across different time zones, in the office, hybrid, all of the things.” A lot of organizations are still treating recognition, though, separate from performance management. What does it look like when those two are truly connected in a meaningful way?
Connecting Employee Recognition to Performance Management
Dami Akinduro: You see, that’s where the magic really happens, you know. And like I said, in the past we’ve had traditional plans where people are recognizing people based on tenure. But now we want something more. We want it to be more than that. We want to recognize people for adding value to the business. So when you connect both of them, you actually find that people are motivated to do more.
I’ve once been a beneficiary of an award—it was a KPMG 2014 Innovation Award at the time—and I won the award then for building a model for them. But what’s most important for me is the experience that stayed with me because subsequently, I was looking for multiple opportunities to be able to do something else. I had already achieved through recognition a certain level of quality that was ascribed to my deliverable. So I wanted to do more. And that’s what we’re talking about: that you need to link recognition and performance so that people can actually do more for the business and for themselves as well.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: I mean, that makes sense, right? You’re doing a great job, somebody gives you a shout-out, further development might happen, and then more opportunities, training, and development to grow at the company versus moving on to another organization. There is a growing role of artificial intelligence in recognition programs, and we’ve seen a few here already. How can AI help HR leaders better connect recognition to measurable performance outcomes?
Using AI to Personalize Employee Recognition Programs
Dami Akinduro: Yeah, I can already think about many different ways. You know, I’ll start with personalization. What I’ve found with recognition is that different people come from different backgrounds; they have different cultural orientations. So what makes sense for Employee A may not make sense at all for Employee B, or how they’re motivated may be very different. So you can actually deploy AI there to take note of what their preferences are so that when a line manager wants to recognize an employee, they already understand what those preferences are. And you can actually deploy AI in curating something for them that appeals to them.
If they are from cultural backgrounds that think, “No, you shouldn’t pay me for doing a good job,” that’s also okay, because then there’s a mix of the monetary recognition piece and the non-monetary. So AI, it’s smart, right? It’s advising you. I was also in a session today on recognition where somebody had asked a question. And the question was, you know, they have this plan, right, but people are actually not going into the platform to recognize people. AI can actually generate prompts for you and say, “Hey, you know, this person has registered that they have completed this in the platform, and then this is what they have done.” You know, it can generate those prompts for line managers. It can also help write the things or guide the things that people want to write, so they don’t think, “How do I recognize this person?” or “What do I start to put together?” So there are many ways we can actually deploy AI there to help us through and drive engagement.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: I love these ideas, and it starts with, I think, a lot of education and training in the moment.
Dami Akinduro: Yes.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: Because if you have an employee from another culture or another country, they might not want to be publicly praised.
Dami Akinduro: Yes.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: And so AI could just send out a gentle nudge like, “Hey manager, this is the best way to approach this person.” And I love that because as an HR person, we tend to get the bad side of the workplace. And so somebody comes in and they’re upset because their culture wasn’t respected, or they didn’t feel like they needed recognition this way. So perhaps that could help reduce some of our workload in HR, and then maybe prevent that one moment that might be the beginning of the end for that person—you know, that turning point.
Dami Akinduro: Mm-hmm. Absolutely. And another thing to think about is it can also help us with reporting and sharing data analytics—you know, who has been recognized in the past six months, what have they been recognized for? Are there things that should be brought to the attention of senior leadership teams, as an example, or where people are not actually using something or doing it? AI can also help you reform that—like highlight the gaps, what the strengths are, more like an audit, you know, and provide the data that you need to be able to continue to drive engagement in the plans that you have.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: I like that. So a lot of new employees get a lot of recognition,
