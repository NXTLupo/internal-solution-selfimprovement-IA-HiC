# Workology Podcast Episode 452: Careers in Benefits with IFEBP

Source: [workology_blog](https://workology.com/workology-podcast-episode-452-careers-in-benefits-with-ifebp/)

Welcome to the Workology Podcast, a podcast for the disruptive workplace leader. Join host Jessica Miller-Merrell, founder of Workology.com as she sits down and gets to the bottom of trends, tools, and case studies for the business leader, HR, and recruiting professional who is tired of the status quo. Now here’s Jessica with this episode of Workology.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: Welcome to the Workology Podcast, sponsored by Ace the HR Exam and Workology’s Manager Training Program. These are two courses and resources that we offer for certification prep and leadership and development. You can learn more about these courses over at Workology.com. If you’ve been feeling burned out in human resources or wondering what your next career move might look like, this episode truly is for you.
One area that’s often overlooked is employee benefits. It is specialized, a high-impact career path that blends strategy, compliance, analytics, communication, and business acumen. As benefits become more complex and employees and employers face rising healthcare costs, changing regulations, and growing employee expectations, organizations increasingly need dedicated benefits professionals, not just HR generalists wearing another hat. So today we’re exploring what a career in benefits really looks like, why the Certified Employee Benefits Specialist or CEBS designation has become one of the industry’s most respected credentials, and how specializing in benefits can open doors to new opportunities and long-term career growth.
Whether you’ve considered a career pivot or simply want to better understand this essential function, this conversation is for you. So let’s get into it.
But before I introduce our guest for today, I do want to hear from you. Please comment “podcast” on the pinned post over on our Instagram account—is @Workologyblog—or send me a DM. You can ask questions, leave comments, and make suggestions for future guests. I want to hear from you.
Today’s guest on the Workology podcast is Stacy Van Alstyne, CEBS Member Experience Director at the International Foundation of Employee Benefit Plans, or IFEBP. At IFEBP, Stacy helps connect thousands of benefits professionals with the education, research, and resources they need to navigate an increasingly complex benefits landscape. Having spent much of her career with IFEBP, Stacy is passionate about elevating the employee benefits profession and helping practitioners build rewarding, impactful careers. She also hosts conversations and creates educational content that helps benefits professionals stay informed on those emerging trends, compliance issues, and workforce challenges. As a Certified Employee Benefits Specialist (CEBS), Stacy is a strong advocate for lifelong learning and professional development. Throughout her career, she’s worked closely with benefits leaders across corporate, public sector, multi-employer, and consulting organizations, giving her a unique perspective on the evolving role of benefits professionals. Today we’ll talk about why benefits is becoming one of the most exciting career paths within HR, what it takes to succeed in the field, and how the CEBS designation can help professionals build expertise and stand out in a competitive marketplace. Stacy, welcome to the Workology Podcast.
Stacy Van Alstyne: Hey Jessica, thank you. Very excited to be here.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: I’m excited too. I want to start with the big picture. Why is now such an exciting time to build a career in employee benefits? And what is driving the demand for the benefits professional?
Why Employee Benefits Is a Growing HR Career Path
Stacy Van Alstyne: Yeah, it is a phenomenal time to get your toes into a benefits career. So recently the US Bureau of Labor Statistics released that over the next several years leading into 2028, there’s going to be a five percent job growth for benefit-oriented roles. And a lot of that is driven by a couple of factors sort of coming together. First, benefits today is so much different than it was decades ago. We’re seeing so many more laws, regulations, and best practices changing at a much more rapid rate. So that’s pulling in all kinds of experts needing to stay on top of that. Especially on the healthcare administration side, that’s growing really, really complex.
Layered on top of that is your own employees having different demands and trying to meet those demands and expectations. You know, and this is the underthought of all benefit plans—is that attraction and that retention issue. And so you’ve got this wide audience that you’re trying to attract and retain, and so you need a very complex offering. Always with benefit plans, there’s the “let’s make this attractive, but let’s also make it cost-effective and make sense for the organization.” Organizations are recognizing that benefits is a huge issue, both in attraction, retention, and culture. And on the flip side, it can also pose a risk if it’s not handled appropriately. So put all that together: you’ve got a strong demand for this job role, and it’s a really unique skill set too.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: I love that. And thank you for sharing. I have been having conversations with a lot of HR professionals who are maybe thinking about leaving the profession because of burnout, or maybe they’re just wanting to try something new. And it sounds like benefits, while still part of HR, is very much growing. And it’s an area that I think is relatively easy for an HR leader who’s more of an employee-bent, employee kind of generalist to be able to transition to.
Stacy Van Alstyne: Yeah, for sure. You know, benefits is really interesting because while it’s really nested often within HR and they work hand-in-hand, it is quite different than HR when you talk about that day-to-day job role. First and foremost, many—not all, but many—roles in benefits are not employee-facing. It’s behind-the-scenes work. It might be very, very detailed work, looking at claims, that sort of thing, or can go into a much more strategic level. But oftentimes that is not with day-to-day working with the employees. So that’s a big contrast right there. And it evolves so much around maintaining compliance. Again, the laws, the regulations, the reporting, all of that has to be done with the utmost care, so that can be a little bit of a contrast from HR where you’re working in general with a little bit more of the areas of gray. And when you put all of that detail, that strategy together with the outcomes—you’re meeting people when they’re at these like huge transition points in their lives. They might be having a healthcare scare, they might be having a baby, getting married, getting divorced, retiring. All of these major milestones are usually when the benefits come right at it. And it’s just a huge, huge reward to be there for people when they really need it most. So when you put all that stuff together, it’s a really great field to be in.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: It sounds like this is a great long-term career path. It’s growing, it’s changing. We can really impact people, as you’re saying, in these important life milestones. And I think increasingly employers are really starting to shine a spotlight more on benefit plans and how they can help or hurt the profitability of an organization.
Stacy Van Alstyne: Absolutely. Absolutely, yeah.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: One other thing that I have come to realize as I’ve kind of dove deeper into benefits within the HR space is that it’s much broader than people realize. And I wanted to ask if you could paint a picture of the different career paths available within benefits, maybe from entry-level roles to executive leadership.
Employee Benefits Career Paths: From Specialist to Strategic Leader
Stacy Van Alstyne: For sure. You know, in talking with a lot of the International Foundation’s members, I hear stories of how they fell into benefits. And it does start more at that benefit specialist level—again, looking at claims, doing some very detailed financial analysis, and then growing in that. And benefits has so many different tentacles of where you could go. We’ve got actuaries, attorneys, data analysis, all these different specialties. And they happen on both the plan sponsor side or the consultancy side. So completely different environments you could work in there. And when you start building that skill set—starting with maybe working with healthcare and jumping to the retirement space and getting that more well-rounded picture—that just positions you to work up that ladder and do more strategic work. There are so many different paths to come into benefits, and again, that hopping over from say a retirement to a healthcare or vice versa happens a lot.
And then a completely different way to get into it, which a lot of our members are in, which is very interesting: if you work in the public space—like you work for a city or a county or a state—or if you’re in a collectively bargained space, you might have a skill set where you might be a firefighter, you might be a policeman, you might be someone in the construction industry, and you get called upon to be a trustee for your healthcare plan or your retirement plan. And you get to flex a very different muscle than you do in your day job. And that’s just another really unique way to get into this industry, but still flex those analytical skills, those communication skills, that detail-orientedness that’s really important in this career.
Jessica Miller-Merrell: What an interesting kind of transition point in so many different places that’s really unexpected if you think about it from the traditional HR world.
Stacy Van Alstyne: Absolutely. Yeah, it’s hand-in-hand, but also so different.
