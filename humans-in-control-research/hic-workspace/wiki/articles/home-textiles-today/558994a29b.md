# Sleep accessories complete the sleep solution for mattress retailers

Source: [home_textiles_today](https://www.hometextilestoday.com/mattress-bedding-news/what-builds-bedding-ticket-sleep-accessories-complete-the-sleep-solution/)

Sheila Long O’Mara// Executive Editor, Furniture Today//August 24, 2026
High Point, N.C. — Mattress retailers want more traffic, need more traffic.
Spending on advertising, promotions and financing offers help get consumers through the door at a time when every door swing seems harder to come by and has become more critical than ever before.
But once that consumer finally walks into the store ready to spend thousands of dollars on a new sleep system, too many retailers are letting part of the sale walk right back out the door.
Sheets. Pillows. Comforters. Mattress protectors. The products consumers need to finish the bed.
That’s according to leading sleep accessories executives who see opportunity for retailers to capture additional dollars by incorporating the products into the overall selling proposition for mattress consumers.
Mattress protectors have become a relatively easy attachment for many retailers, thanks largely to their role in protecting mattresses and maintaining warranties. Beyond that, however, sleep accessories executives say retailers are leaving significant revenue on the table.
The rub: Consumers aren’t necessarily deciding they don’t need those products. Instead, they’re buying them somewhere else.
Kristin Sugar, senior vice president of wholesale sales for Cariloha, said consumers increasingly view the mattress retailer as a logical destination for the entire sleep environment. Years ago, shoppers had multiple destinations for quality bedding products: Bed Bath & Beyond stores, Linen & Things and range of other specialty stores. Today, she sees mattress stores as well-positioned to fill that role.
The hurdle is getting retailers to stop thinking about accessories simply as attachments and start presenting the mattress, adjustable base, protector, pillows and top-of-bed products as a complete sleep solution.
For retailers struggling with traffic, the distinction matters. The consumer is already in the store making capturing the additional sale – and a larger ticket – a bit easier.
Purecare CEO Sean Bergman said the math starts with the relatively simple mattress protector. On a $1,000 mattress purchase, adding a $100 protector increases the ticket by 10% without attracting another consumer or spending another dollar on advertising.
But that’s just the baseline, he said.
Consumers also need pillows, sheets and other products to complete the bed, and Bergman said many consumers remake their beds with new products shortly after purchasing a mattress.
“The potential is so massive compared to the slice our industry is receiving of that pie,” he said.
Chealen Martin, president of DreamFit, sees a particularly large opportunity in sheets.
He estimates that sheet attachment rates at many mattress retailers are less than 5%, while 67% of consumers buy new sheets around the time they invest in a new mattress.
“They’re just not buying from you,” Martin said, adding consumers are spending those dollars at mass merchants, warehouse clubs, direct-to-consumer brands, like Brooklinen and Boll & Branch, and other retailers.
For Martin, that gap illustrates the larger opportunity. Retailers don’t need another consumer to build revenue. They need to capture more of the spending from those they already have.
One obstacle may be the bedding industry’s own approach to accessories.
Years of free-sheet and free-pillow promotions have helped condition consumers, retail sales associates and retailers to think about sleep accessories as giveaways instead of profit opportunities.
Martin said retailers that are succeeding with the category are moving away from that approach and connecting accessories to the performance of the mattress. A consumer investing in a cooling mattress, for example, could undermine the performance of that mattress by covering it with an inexpensive, nonbreathable protector and sheets.
“The skin is touching these products,” Martin said, “and not the mattress, which ends up buried under all of the layers.”
Instead of searching for the least expensive product to fulfill a promotional promise, retailers can merchandise better accessories as part of the sleep solution.
Bergman said margins on soft goods can equal or exceed mattress margins, which hover close to 70% for some dealers. Even when retailers offer a discount for buying several products together, he said the incremental margin dollars can make the package worthwhile.
That becomes increasingly significant as retailers consider what they are paying to generate each store visit.
“The value of a door swing, that’s expensive,” Bergman said. “I see huge opportunity for retailers to capitalize on higher tickets by incorporating accessories into the selling process.”
Mattress manufacturers also appear to be recognizing the potential of the accessories business, particularly pillows.
Spring Air International, for example, has expanded its sleep lineup with pillows coordinated with several of its mattress brands, including its Sleep Copper program.
Malouf also brought its High Loft Talalay latex pillow back this summer, while Bedgear has built its broader sleep assortment around matching products, including mattresses and pillows, to individual sleepers.
Mlily USA is also expanding its sleep accessories offering with a range of pillows, new protectors and range of new sheets.
The growing attention from mattress brands further blurs the traditional line between the mattress and the products surrounding it.
It also gives retail sales associates another way to talk about the pillow as part of the mattress fitting rather than something to tack onto the sale at checkout.
Bergman said a pillow fitting should happen almost immediately when a consumer begins trying mattresses. Put the appropriate pillow under the shopper’s head, he said, and if the consumer likes it, let that pillow travel with the shopper from mattress to mattress.
“The pillow and the mattress need to work together,” he said.
That same approach can apply to sheets.
Sugar and Martin both recommend introducing sheets during the mattress fitting, when shoppers can feel and experience the product, instead of waiting until the mattress decision has been made and asking whether they’d like to add a set of sheets.
“Bring it up early and often,” Sugar said, adding that helping consumers create a “total sleep environment” results in not only a higher ticket, but also a happy consumer.
Martin said incorporating the sheet into the mattress presentation makes it feel like part of the solution instead of a separate sale.
Financing provides another opening. A consumer financing a $2,000, $3,000 or $5,000 sleep system may be able to add pillows, sheets or other products with a relatively small impact on the monthly payment.
Retailers also don’t necessarily need to stock a warehouse full of sheet colors and styles. Most vendors offer drop-ship capabilities that allow stores to display colors and fabrics while products are shipped directly to consumers.
Product and merchandising alone, however, won’t move the needle. Executives in the category pointed to RSA training and retail discipline as critical to increasing attachment.
Martin said retailers need buy-in from ownership, buyers, sales leadership and RSAs, backed by training that is accessible, memorable and delivered in bite-sized pieces.
Bergman advocates establishing key performance goals, identifying store-level accessory champions and creating compensation incentives that give sales associates a reason to focus on the category.
