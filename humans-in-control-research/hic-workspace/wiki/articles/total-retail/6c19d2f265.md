# Why Physical Retail Matters More Than Ever in the Age of Digital Fatigue

Source: [total_retail](https://www.mytotalretail.com/article/why-physical-retail-matters-more-than-ever-in-the-age-of-digital-fatigue/)

For years, retail centered on convenience through faster shipping, more online options, and frictionless transactions.
Today, consumers search for something more. They want opportunities to reconnect with the physical world. Younger consumers, in particular, embrace hobbies like needlepoint, quilting, and embroidery as a way to unplug from digital overload.
This growing appetite for offline experiences creates a significant opportunity for retailers: building communities focused on inspiration, education and expertise, while leveraging artificial intelligence and automation to operate more efficiently behind the scenes.
Use Technology to Create More Human Interaction
AI and automation are transforming retail, but their greatest value isn't replacing people — it's giving retailers more time to engage with customers.
Retailers should leverage technology to streamline processes like inventory management and demand forecasting, automate email marketing campaigns, implement loyalty programs, and simplify purchasing. This ultimately reduces the administrative burden that often consumes valuable staff time.
The result is more capacity for meaningful customer interactions. This could be as simple as a store associate helping a beginner sewer choose materials for a new project, or as complex as working with a seasoned quilter tackling an advanced pattern with different fabrics and stitching.
The retailers that see the greatest success don’t use technology to spend less time with customers, they use it to make their experiences more personal.
Deliver Experiences Worth Paying For
While ongoing economic uncertainty makes consumers more selective about their purchases, Gen Z and millennial shoppers are investing in experiences that feel meaningful, memorable and enriching.
For example, a sewing class or quilting workshop delivers more than a product because it provides education, creativity, and social connection.
This shift is also tied to growing digital fatigue. According to Michaels' 2026 Creativity Trend Report, searches for analog hobbies such as knitting, crocheting, embroidery, journaling, painting, and sewing increased 136 percent. The same report found sewing pattern searches rose 152 percent, while needlepoint searches increased 251 percent.
To further paint this picture, the Associated Press published a story showcasing younger consumers increasingly embracing traditional "grandma hobbies" to unplug from technology, reduce stress, and build meaningful connections with others. Psychologists interviewed for the story note that these activities support well-being while providing a sense of community and accomplishment.
At the same time, younger consumers also demonstrate a renewed interest in physical retail experiences. According to data from Circana, shoppers aged 18 to 24 made 62 percent of their general merchandise purchases in physical stores last year, compared to 52 percent among older consumers. Furthermore, 58 percent of Gen Z consumers enjoy taking time to discover new things while shopping, and 63 percent say they’ve purchased something because of the experience around it, according to a report from Snapchat and Portas.
This presents a significant opportunity for retailers. In the craft and quilting space, for example, consumers aren’t just purchasing fabric, thread or patterns. They’re investing in a hobby that enriches their lives, helps them develop new skills, and connects them with like-minded people.
The retailers that succeed are those that recognize they’re selling more than products. They’re providing experiences, education, and community.
Win Through Community, Not Scale
Perhaps one of the biggest advantages retailers can lean into is building authentic communities. Big-box stores may compete on price and selection, but they can't replicate the soul of a local quilt shop rooted in genuine relationships, shared passion, and community connection.
Classes, sewing groups, charity projects, creator meetups, and educational events that bring customers together around shared interests are how retailers can not only engage in their communities, but foster loyalty among their customers — transforming stores from transactional spaces into community hubs.
Creating More Time for Inspiration
As AI continues to reshape retail operations, those that stand out won't necessarily be the most automated. They’ll be the ones that use automation to create more opportunities for inspiration, education and community.
In a world dominated by screens, physical retail offers something consumers actively seek: a place to disconnect, create, and connect with others.
