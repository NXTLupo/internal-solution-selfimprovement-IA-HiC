# Tesco CEO: Brand activity should ‘surprise’ customers

Source: [marketing_week](https://www.marketingweek.com/tesco-brand-surprise-value/)

Tesco CEO: Brand activity should ‘surprise’ customers
In the run up to Christmas, Tesco is building on rising sales and its highest ever customer satisfaction score through a “sharpened value position”.
Tesco’s CEO has identified the element of “surprise” as critical for engaging customers, as the brand approaches a competitive golden quarter in a strong position.
The supermarket’s ‘Need anything from Tesco’ brand platform, launched in March, is an example of this, Ken Murphy told Marketing Week on a call with media today (8 October), and a key part of its brand investment.
“What we really wanted to do [when we launched the brand platform] is make ‘Every Little Helps’ come to life for customers and really mean something,” he said. “The brand platform of ‘Need anything from Tesco’ is really designed to surprise customers with little moments where they can rely on Tesco to meet those needs, whatever those needs are.”
This plays hand-in-hand with its community outreach work, such as its free fruit and veg scheme for schools, as Murphy said the team is “trying to bring the brand to life in every aspect of what Tesco does” and how it “influences life in the communities” it serves.
Value is another key area where he feels the brand needs to continue to deliver, particularly as he claims it is the “cheapest full-line grocer in the UK”, which Murphy feels is due to Tesco having “sharpened its value position”.
Murphy told Marketing Week that in the run up to Christmas, the brand is looking to stand out in the market by “increasing its buys on all the key Christmas lines”, because he feels “customers are really going to want to celebrate”. In line with this, value, innovation and quality remain top of mind for the golden quarter, as it looks to “deliver in a comprehensive way”.

“We will be gearing up to make sure we deliver it [Christmas] in a really friendly and helpful way, with a consistent, high quality satisfaction score in terms of our on-time delivery service, availability in shops, and the shopability of our shops,” he claimed.
Murphy believes Tesco is in a strong position to do this, with its interim results showing group like-for-like sales up 1% year on year, with adjusted operating profit up 6.3% at constant rates to £1.8bn.
The brand achieved its highest ever customer satisfaction score, with UK NPS at 33, up three points versus the year prior, which was put down to improvements in value, quality and range.
Market share is also strong at 27.8% as it continues to top the Worldpanel UK charts, having seen a two-year increase of 23bps and a four-year increase of 113bps.
Winning in food
Murphy also emphasised Tesco’s food-first strategy as its “core business”, saying its growth strategy “begins and ends in winning in food”.
UK food like-for-like sales increased 2.4% supported by innovation with over 800 new and improved products launched. Tesco Finest sales also increased 8.9% in the UK, and almost 13% overall, with sales expected to exceed £3bn this year.
Elsewhere, UK online market share remains strong at 36.7%, with UK online sales increasing 8.4%, and Whoosh delivery increasing to cover 75% of UK households. Clubcard also continues to become more personalised in line with Tesco’s AI partnership with Adobe, with Your Clubcard Prices now offered to around 2.5 million customers.
Despite its strong position, Murphy said there’s an opportunity to grow Tesco’s market share further by extending its “usefulness to the customer base through other missions”. This includes convenience, quick commerce and shopping categories, such as clothing and home. This year, the business repositioned its F&F clothing and home business, however like-for-like sales declined by 0.6% given a “subdued” backdrop.
Yet Murphy remains hopeful there is more market share to gain, despite talks of a Sainsbury’s Morrisons merger earlier this week.
“I was told six years ago that we had topped out and life was over for Tesco. In the last four years, we’ve grown share by 113 basis points, which I think most people have been surprised by. So, we like to surprise people. We have a great team in it that runs a great brand, and we’re very proud of it. So, we have an ambition to keep on growing,” Murphy said.
Overall, the business is on track to deliver its £500m Save to Invest target for the full year, with group adjusted operating profit expected to be between £3.15bn and £3.30bn, versus the projected £3bn to 3.3bn predicted in April 2026.
