# Why Three is returning to its youthful roots post-merger

Source: [marketing_week](https://www.marketingweek.com/three-youthful-roots-post-merger/)

Why Three is returning to its youthful roots post-merger
Following its merger with Vodafone, Three is focusing on a younger target audience as the combined business looks to “have the widest reach across the nation” with the two brands.
A distinct brand positioning is always important and becomes even more so following a merger. May 2025 saw mobile network companies Three and Vodafone merge, necessitating work to clarify the brand positioning to drive distinct futures for both brands.
Three has today (14 August) launched a new platform, developed with Wonderhood Studios, which is the outcome of the work it did to clarify and codify the brand’s positioning post-merger.
A merger of this type inevitably raises questions about the future of both brands, chief brand officer at VodafoneThree, Maria Koutsoudakis, tells Marketing Week.
“When people go into a merger, [the questions become] where is my brand? Where does it play? What’s the role of Three going forward?” says Koutsoudakis. “[We wanted] to really answer that question for Three employees, so they understand the future of the brand within VodafoneThree.”
We wanted to understand how we can differentiate these brands so that we have the widest reach across the nation.
Maria Koutsoudakis, VodafoneThree
Those questions coming internally were also crucially important for Three’s external image. The brand had already been in a “quiet period” prior to the merger, so it made sense to slow down and re-evaluate what the Three brand meant to its customers.
“[The marketing team] did a review of all the creative to see which campaigns were the strongest, so they could put in some best practice in terms of branding,” Koutsoudakis says.
It was also important to understand Three’s positioning alongside its sister brands, Vodafone, VOXI and Smarty, and make that distinction clear.
“We wanted to understand how we can differentiate these brands so that we have the widest reach across the nation and part of that journey, of course, was going back to Three, and understanding the strengths of that brand when that brand was at its best,” Koutsoudakis explains.
Finding an audience
In order to drive a differentiated future, Three looked to its past. For much of its history, the mobile network brand looked to be deliberately different from competitors in the category.
Campaigns including ‘Flamingo’ and ‘Dancing Pony’ made Three famous, and saw it grow its appeal with younger people. That’s something the brand had moved away from in recent years, with Koutsoudakis noting that the brand pre-merger had been seeking to “broaden” appeal.
However, with Three now being part of a portfolio of brands it makes sense for it to serve this younger audience, she says. The brand’s research suggested Three had established existing strength there.
Three ramps up ‘single-minded pursuit’ of brand strategy
“When we looked at the portfolio of brands that we had, ages 16 to 29, Three had a lot of strength with the younger audience,” she says. “It had a high level of awareness and a high level of consideration. So it really felt like a good place for us to create a differentiated audience to the more family-orientated audience versus Vodafone.”
The result of this was a new creative platform, “far more targeted towards the younger audience”, Koutsoudakis says.
‘Let Fun In’ was created in partnership with Wonderhood Studios and seeks to bring back the irreverent, energetic character that made Three famous.
The film, which leads the campaign, features a child ice skating before her costume switches to an inflatable chicken costume. The ad ends with the tagline ‘Let Fun In’. The ad looks to echo the humour and irreverence of Three’s past famous campaigns.
‘Reignite the full funnel’
The new platform is being launched off the back of a period of reduced marketing activity for Three. This means it must nurture its brand across the full funnel, says Koutsoudakis.
While it is not necessarily seeking “pure-play awareness”, the brand wants to ensure customers are aware of what the brand stands for.
“We’ve got to reignite the full funnel on this brand,” Koutsoudakis says. “It’s been through quite a journey over the last few years.”
The intention is to rebuild consideration and purchase intent based on what Three stands for.
While Three is seeking to particularly do this with a younger audience it has been historically strong among, its research also suggested the brand’s historic appeal was about more than its target demographic. The brand had previously stood for a particular attitude: being on the customer’s side, having fun and deliberately taking a different route from the rest of the category.
History proves to us that the second time, even the third time, something is even more successful when it comes to return.
Maria Koutsoudakis, VodafoneThree
“Three was always at its best when it was on the side of the customer. It was that irreverent brand, that sense of fun, that sense of energy, we zagged when everyone zigged,” Koutsoudakis says.
The new platform is intended to give the brand a longer-term identity rather than simply provide a new advertising campaign. At its centre is the idea that people can sometimes prevent themselves from having fun, with Three inviting them to let more of it into their lives.
“For us, it’s more an attitude,” Koutsoudakis says. “Our insight kind of led us to believe that sometimes people’s own ambitions are the handbrake to letting fun into their lives. There’s this idea of sometimes people hold themselves back.”
Looking longer term
Its ambition to fuel the whole funnel means the team is resisting the temptation to treat the launch as a short-term campaign. Instead, Let Fun In is intended to remain in market for as long as it continues to perform.
“As long as it keeps working,” Koutsoudakis says. “As long as we can keep evolving it, it stays fresh, it stays relevant, and it’s doing its job.”
She argues marketers can sometimes move on from campaigns too quickly, driven by business priorities and the pressure to launch something new.
“History proves to us that the second time, even the third time, something is even more successful when it comes to return. And you start to really leverage the equity you build on that asset and that message,” she says.
The approach to media is also drawing on the combined capabilities of VodafoneThree. Television and out-of-home remain important, while YouTube is becoming a greater focus, partly because the wider Vodafone business has already built up experience with the platform.
VodafoneThree unveils first joint campaign since merger
The shared media operation means learnings from Vodafone can be applied to Three relatively quickly, including around frequency, diminishing returns and the use of creator content.
“We’ve got a lot of experience on the Vodafone and VOXI side working with YouTube,” Koutsoudakis says. “We’ve got a very strong relationship with Google as a partner. So we’ve lent in with them, understanding how we can work together.”
The next stage will be to expand the platform beyond advertising and into the wider customer experience, including opportunities for employees to become part of the brand story. The idea is intended to work internally as well as externally, giving employees a clearer sense of Three’s role within the newly merged business.
Ultimately, the measure of whether the repositioning has worked will be whether Three can establish a distinct place in the minds of younger consumers without simply competing with Vodafone for the same audience.
There’s no reason why this wouldn’t be one of the top considered brands amongst that audience, if not the considered brand amongst that audience.
Maria Koutsoudakis, VodafoneThree
“In 12 months’ time, I hope what we start to see is a strong level of consideration growth amongst this audience,” Koutsoudakis says. “A lot of the work that we do is to minimise the overlap, and then we want to maximise the appeal with the audiences.”
More ambitiously, she believes the work could take Three back to the kind of brand salience it enjoyed at its peak.
“Emotionally, the way it seems to be resonating, and all the pre-testing, if that comes to fruition, there’s no reason why this wouldn’t be one of the top considered brands amongst that audience, if not the considered brand amongst that audience.”
