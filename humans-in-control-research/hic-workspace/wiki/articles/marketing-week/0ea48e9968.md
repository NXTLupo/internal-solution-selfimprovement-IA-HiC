# Many brands taking ‘self-defeating’ approach to sports sponsorship, research finds

Source: [marketing_week](https://www.marketingweek.com/sports-sponsorship-self-defeating/)

Many brands taking ‘self-defeating’ approach to sports sponsorship, research finds
Less than half of brands feel confident that sports sponsorship delivers business results, despite increasing investment in the channel.
Sports sponsorship is a huge market, forecast to be worth $181.38bn globally by 2034. Yet new research finds just four in 10 brands, agencies and sports rights holders are confident that sports sponsorship delivers business results.
Among brands specifically, less than half (45%) express confidence that sports sponsorship delivers business results. The data comes from a study from the IPA and the Sponsorship Effectiveness Forum (SEF) and is based on 143 brands, agencies and sports rights holders.
The results were presented at the IPA Effectiveness Conference yesterday (7 October). At the conference, SEF co-founder and chair Rory Natkiel highlighted that while the lack of confidence in sport sponsorships’ ability to deliver commercial results is “a real challenge”, the data also presents creates an “opportunity” to build confidence.
He pointed to a further 29% of respondents who said they are “somewhat” confident in sports sponsorship’s ability to deliver commercial results.
“We could shift those over to mostly confident and slip this finding entirely on its head,” Natkiel said.
Work together as one
One theme identified as to why stakeholders lack confidence in the effectiveness of sports sponsorship is that these are often not treated as true partnerships, Natkiel said.
“It’s rare that a brand shares meaningful commercial data with its rights holder,” he said, calling this “self-defeating”.
“It’s like briefing a media agency to run a campaign for you, not giving it access to your analytics, and then asking them to report back about what it’s done for the business,” he says.
“The more you’re working as one team, the more effective the sponsorship will be.”
We don’t operate in silos. We are part of the ‘winning in the UK’ mission, and this partnership is very much front and centre of that work.
Caitlin McRoy, Sage
Also speaking at the event was Claire Humphris, CMO at British Cycling, who spoke about the sport’s partnership with Lloyds Bank. This has seen the banking brand work hand-in-hand with British Cycling.
“There’s complete transparency around the investment, and we understand what’s working and what’s not, and where to focus our time in terms of optimisation,” Humphris said, adding that the teams work together with “one shared KPI”.
Avoiding silos
That theme of working together as a team, also apply to how brands treat sponsorship investment.
“The structure of a lot of brand teams is that you have a sponsorship team that’s maybe competing for budget with the marketing team, and so they can end up doing quite different things. And the sponsorship can end up sitting in a silo, it’s not integrated with what the rest of the brand is doing.” Natkiel said.
One brand that is putting it sponsorship work “front and centre” of what it is overall trying to do as a brand is accounting software firm Sage. It has begun a partnership with the English Football League (EFL).
The partnership allows Sage to have a presence in local football stadiums up and down the country, said its senior sponsorship manager Caitlin McRoy. This was in aid of Sage’s mission to be perceived as being for small businesses as well as for big firms.
“If you think about a football stadium, especially at those levels, who are the partners that you see around it? It’s the local conservatory makers. It is the local accountants. It is the people that we want to talk to,” she said.
The way Sage thinks about this partnership is in aid of a wider brand mission, she said.
“We don’t operate in silos,” she said. “We are part of the ‘winning in the UK’ mission, and this partnership is very much front and centre of that work.”
Effective sports sponsorship requires a different set of 4Ps
The SEF identified three pillars for effective sports sponsorship: setting clear commercial objectives, measuring commercial impact and integrating sponsorship within wider brand communication.
The research found those constantly practising these fundamentals are “almost six times more likely to be confident that their sponsorships deliver business results”.
Additionally, the data found appetite for further research to prove the effectiveness of sports sponsorship, with 43% of respondents in the research saying industry-wide measurement standards and a common currency would make the biggest positive difference to sponsorship effectiveness.
“Sports sponsorship is a very effective marketing channel. The more that we research, the more we’re proving that,” Natkiel said.
