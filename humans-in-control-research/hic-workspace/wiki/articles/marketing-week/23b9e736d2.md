# Primark claims to be ‘redefining value’ as it preps home delivery launch

Source: [marketing_week](https://www.marketingweek.com/primark-redefine-value-ecommerce/)

Primark claims to be ‘redefining value’ as it preps home delivery launch
Primark claims online will drive “profitable” growth, as the retailer hails the impact of increasing marketing investment and strengthening its digital capabilities.
Primark is set to enter the online retail and home delivery space, four years after launching its click-and-collect service.
The move online will be a “profitable channel for growth that complements” – rather than replaces – its in-store model, CEO of parent company Associated British Foods (ABF) George Weston told investors today (10 September) as he shared the firm’s fourth quarter results.
The ecommerce push is made possible by Primark having built “strong digital capabilities”, including digital marketing, he added. The business has signed a £90m deal to purchase a warehouse facility from Boohoo to support the expansion, while Weston claimed much of the digital infrastructure for home delivery is already in place thanks to the click-and-collect proposition.
The hot UK weather seems to have impacted sales, according to Weston. For the fourth quarter, Primark like-for-like sales fell 3%, although ABF expects sales overall to grow by around 2% ahead of full-year reporting later this year. The business also anticipates sales growth in its grocery business, which includes Twinings and Ovaltine, but expects profits to fall below expectations.
“We’ve sharpened prices [at Primark], we’ve improved our product offers, we’ve increased investment in marketing, strengthening our digital capabilities,” said Weston, moves he believes set the business up for online expansion.
“We’re seeing the benefits and there’s more to come.”
One standout from Primark noted by the CEO is the retailer’s ‘Iconic Value’ campaign, which he described as a “strategic investment” to introduce lower prices and connect deeper with customers. While noting its still early days for the campaign, which launched in July, the initial impact on volume sales has been “encouraging”.
“Primark is redefining value and not just through price, but also in our continuous improvements in quality, style and the store experience,” he said.
In terms of the returns Primark is getting from its increased investment in marketing, Weston believes the retailer has got the “marketing mix about right”.
“We are driving incremental sales. There’s a lot more beyond simply above the line advertising going on, but the brand metrics in particular have benefited from the advertising,” he explained.
Looking ahead
Weston also provided an update on Primark’s demerger from ABF, which was initially announced as a possibility last year. He expects the two businesses will separate by the end of 2027.
“This gives us time to build the systems and processes necessary to operate both businesses on a standalone business, and this is important,” he said. “It also gives us time to explain more about our businesses, particularly in food, to the market. We’re not going to rush that.”
In July, ABF completed its £75m acquisition of Hovis and is “well under way” with its integration into the business.
“We expect this transaction to deliver significant synergies in both production and distribution. This will give us the ability to create a sustainably profitable bakery business, supported by investment in marketing and innovation,” said Weston, noting the bakery brand is already back “on air” with TV advertising.
Last month, Hovis announced it was investing £4m behind its ‘Strength Baked In’ campaign between now and the end of the calendar year, with more investment planned in 2027. Aimed at strengthening the foundations of the brand, the multi-channel campaign is set to reach 79% of main household shoppers and represents the first time Hovis has appeared on TV in a major way for more than 15 years.
