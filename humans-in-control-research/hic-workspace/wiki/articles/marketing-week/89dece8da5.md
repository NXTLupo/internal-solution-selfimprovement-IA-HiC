# Inflation, in-game advertising, performance tracking: 5 interesting stats to start your week

Source: [marketing_week](https://www.marketingweek.com/inflation-in-game-advertising-performance-tracking-5-interesting-stats-to-start-your-week/)

Inflation, in-game advertising, performance tracking: 5 interesting stats to start your week
We arm you with all the stats you need to tackle the week ahead.
Shop price inflation reaches two year high
Shop price inflation increased to 1.5% year on year in August, against growth of 0.9% in July, above the 3-month average of 1.2%, according to new research from the British Retail Consortium.
Non-food inflation increased to 0.9% year on year in August, against growth of 0.2% in July, which is above the 3-month average of 0.6%. In non-food, electrical prices rose amid the ongoing AI boom, which is forcing up the price of things like memory chips and storage. Meanwhile, retailers seem to have kept clothing prices low to offer value for families getting ready for the new school year.
On the other hand, food inflation increased to 2.8% year on year in August, against growth of 2.2% in July. This is above the 3-month average of 2.5%. Factors such as higher energy, input and commodity costs have been found to filter through into food prices.
Ambient food (any food product that can be safely stored at normal room temperature without needing refrigeration or freezing) has been found to be particularly impacted by this. Price inflation increased to 2.5% year on year in August, against growth of 1.1% in July, which is above the 3-month average of 1.8%.
Whereas fresh food inflation decreased to 3.0% year on year in August, against growth of 3.1% in July, in line with the 3-month average of 3.0%.
Source: British Retail Consortium
Six in ten C-suite members feel a pressure to adopt AI, despite data misgivings
Over half of C-suite marketers and SVP/VPs (60% and 52%, respectively) say they feel under pressure to implement AI tools right away, despite knowing their underlying data isn’t ready.
According to the research from Validity, based on a survey of 500 B2B and B2C marketers, over three quarters (78%) of C-suite and a vast majority (92%) of SVP/VP respondents say they have acted on an AI recommendation they later suspected was wrong because of poor underlying data.
This is a growing problem as two thirds of organisations delegated more decisions to autonomous AI agents this past year, while only a fifth (21%) of marketers say their CRM data is “very well prepared” to support AI, and two thirds (62%) report losing revenue directly because of poor data quality.
Source: Validity
Go big in Q4 found to be poor advice for most categories
The fourth quarter was only found to be the most profitable window for five out of thirteen product categories, following an analysis of more than 168,000 Meta video ads published between July 2025–June 2026.
Several categories were found to have summer peaks, such as home and garden, with a ROAS of $5.33 per ad dollar, apparel and accessories with a ROAS of $4.77 per dollar, and software with a ROAS of $2.06 per dollar.
There is also a back-to-school peak in September among certain categories like sporting goods ($4.76 ROAS) and services ($2.88 ROAS). While other categories have new year/winter peak, with the baby and toddler having a ROAS of $5.83 per ad dollars spent, as well as arts and entertainment peaking at $5.67.
The categories most likely to have a Q4 peak are understandably categories which are most likely to be associated with gifting. These included: animals and pet supplies ($2.08 ROAS), health and beauty ($2.44 ROAS), food and beverages ($3.07 ROAS), as well as toys and games ($2.90 ROAS). Perhaps more surprisingly, the business and industrial category also has a peak at this time with $2.69 ROAS per ad dollar spent.
Source: Billo
In-game brand interactions linked to higher consideration among gamers
Around one third (33%) of all UK gamers say they are more likely to consider a brand in real life after an in-game brand interaction, rising to almost half (47%) among 18 to 24-year-olds, according to new research from Captify.
The age factor is reinforced by the fact that over half (55%) of 18 to 24-year-olds learn about brands through in-game presence, compared with over a quarter (27%) of gamers overall.
For example, The Sims 4 audience became 40% more likely to search for Coach following the brand’s collaboration with the game, while Fortnite players were four times more likely to search for Lamborghini than another heritage brand like Porsche.
Gaming is also evolving as part of consumers’ social calendars. Searches for “Game Night” were found to have increased 60% year-on-year, while gaming intent over-indexes 1.9x on weekend evenings, slightly ahead of the 1.8x index for movie and date-night intent. Nearly one in ten consumers searching for evening plans also display co-op and social gaming intent.
Source: Captify
Over three quarters of marketers admit to lacking a clear view of campaign performance
More than three quarters (82%) of marketers admit to lacking a clear view of what’s working with their campaigns, according to a new report from Bitly.
Alongside this, more than half (56%) of marketers say they often or always rely on gut instincts or past experiences to make marketing decisions, while around three quarters (73%) of marketers say they regularly realize a campaign is underperforming only after it’s too late.
As part of the research, it was found that 5 days is the average time it takes for marketers to take action after new marketing insights emerge.
An excess of tools to track performance frequently came up as a factor for these slower reaction times. The majority of those surveyed used an average of six tools to analyse performance, with more than a third using 7 or more.
Source: Bitly
