# Klarna CMO: Too many brands ‘drink the Kool-Aid’ on the idea of ‘fandom’

Source: [marketing_week](https://www.marketingweek.com/klarna-cmo-fandom-kool-aid/)

Klarna CMO: Too many brands ‘drink the Kool-Aid’ on the idea of ‘fandom’
Brands that talk about driving fandom and being part of pop culture are misguided, says Klarna’s CMO, who argues they should focus on being true to themselves.
Too many marketers “drink the Kool-Aid” when it comes to believing in the idea of brand “fans”, claims Klarna CMO David Sandstrom.
Earlier this week, it was announced Sandstrom would be departing Klarna after almost a decade leading the fintech brand. During his tenure, the brand has leant into a playbook that differs from many other finance brands, reaching an audience that skews younger and more female.
Klarna has worked with celebrities including Paris Hilton and Snoop Dogg in its campaigns, using cultural figures to drive conversation.
However, as he tells Marketing Week, Klarna doesn’t see itself as “part of pop culture” or as a brand with “fans”. Sandstrom believes marketers who talk about “fandom” or their brand being in pop culture are misguided.
“There are too many brands that believe they have fans or are part of pop culture, they’re just not,” he argues.
Klarna is essentially a utility brand, Sandstrom states, something people don’t necessarily jump to engage with. The brand meets that challenge by creating “big and bold” ideas people do want to talk about.
CMO cites a new big idea-led campaign from the brand coming after the summer he hopes will get people talking.
I would never say I think that Klarna has fans, we don’t.
David Sandstrom, Klarna
“I believe people are going to talk about that idea as part of pop culture, rather than talking about Klarna as part of pop culture,” he says.
Sandstrom firmly believes too many brands presume they can become part of culture, with the end goal of creating “fandom” for their brand.
“I would never say I think that Klarna has fans, we don’t,” he says. “We have very loyal consumers and customers because our products are fantastic.”
But at the end of the day, Klarna sees itself as “a utility brand that is fantastic for managing [consumers’] everyday spend”, he adds, rather than something in culture.
“Many [brands] would benefit from not drinking this Kool-Aid of having to force themselves into culture, but rather being what they are, being true to their brand,” says Sandstrom.
Marketing Week will be publishing a full interview with Klarna CMO David Sandstrom on the brand’s journey over the last decade in the coming weeks.
