# Unlocking Leadership Motivation: Beyond Positive Encouragement

Source: [tim_sackett](https://timsackett.com/2026/01/14/unlocking-leadership-motivation-beyond-positive-encouragement/)

I’ve been thinking recently about the concept of leadership motivation. Particularly around what we mean when we ask a leader of people to motivate said people.
What you find is that it’s not about what most people are capable of. Humans are capable of amazing things when they are properly “motivated”. The problem is that we tend to think of motivation in one aspect. Positive motivation. I’m going to get you all juiced up, and you’ll be amazing! Great locker room speeches for players. A visionary leader gets the sales team jazzed up to go sell more.
But when faced with unimaginable negative consequences, we also see humans reach amazing levels of performance we never thought possible! Mother’s lifting cars off their injured children. Entrepreneurs with only a few dollars left to their names got out and build million-dollar businesses.
So, leadership motivation is about getting someone to do something they would not normally be willing to do, given normal circumstances.
A portion of people will do it without any external motivation. This small part of the population is internally motivated, but realistically, probably externally motivated by something; you just don’t know why or what is motivating them.
The other portions of people are motivated by positive motivation. They need a cheerleader. Or negative motivation. They need a kick in the ass.
A problem with this is that most people don’t know, or aren’t willing to admit, the motivation that will yield the best results. I find that if you ask 100 people what kind of motivation they prefer, around 97% would claim they perform better in a positive motivation environment. And there’s a lot of research to back this up regarding long-term, sustained motivation.
What should leaders do?
Ideally, you identify each person’s motivation preference on your team and give them what they need. Sounds easy, right!?
Herein lies another issue: most leaders are wired to give one type of motivation! And almost always, that type is positive because almost all of us hate conflict.
This is why motivating employees is so difficult!
So, is there another route we should take? Should we just make sure everyone is accountable to a standard?
What do the best “motivational” leaders have in common?
I think it’s fairly simple but uncomfortable for most people to pull off, which is why many can’t.
- Set a standard.
- Hold everyone accountable to the standard. Everyone.
We hear this in sports all the time from certain coaches or programs: “The standard is the standard. In business, we tend to use different words and phrases, but we all have examples of leaders or organizations that were relentless in their approach.
Again, it’s extremely hard to pull off, mainly because the standard you set doesn’t include timeouts, vacations, or breaks. It’s always there, and it always has to be followed, and that can feel very restrictive for those in it and those holding everyone accountable to it.
Greatness isn’t for the majority. Heck, above average isn’t for the majority!
It’s so simple that it’s confusing to most people. Here’s what we are doing. And we will do it every time. Or we will find those who will do it every time. Well, ouch, that doesn’t feel good! You know what feels good: delivering what you say you will, every single time.
