The Brainfood
1. AI and the UK Labour Market: the Evidence So Far
Has AI reduced headcount in your organisation? Some counter intuitive findings from UK think tank, Centre for British Progress, with meta data analysis from ILO and Anthropic Economic Index amongst others. Turns out that the most AI exposed roles saw employment stability, rather than the expected job loss. Excellent piece of research, with the commendable takeaway that, for now, we just don’t know. Great read, beautifully presented. 
AI
2. I May Or May Not Have Vibe Coded My Own ATS 
The democratisation of software production might be one of the most profound long term consequences of AI. We’re seeing evidence of it already in our own community, with examples like this becoming more common as recruiters begin vibe coding their own toolkit for custom purpose. There are dangers in so doing though, as we’re going to learn the hard way (talking about this with Kiku next month on the 'dangers of shadow AI’ - register here), though some commentators like brainfooder Matt Staney has an interesting heterdox line of thinking that vibe code might in some ways be a safer way to operate, at least for some companies. H/T to brainfooder Stanislaw Wasowicz for the share in the online community - we need to continue to advance the conversation here.
AI
3. The Death of the Three-Legged Stool: Rebuilding HR Architecture for the Age of Agentic AI
We’re not short of articles describing the future of TA or HR. This one is pretty good as it traces the incumbent model back to David Ulrich’s Three-Legged Stool of Centers of Expertise (CoEs), HR Business Partners (HRBPs), and Shared Services. Service centre evolution into Employee Relations offers most promise, but the underlying reality remains unstated - the HR function is set for dramatic reduction in size based on this projection. Maybe that’s no bad thing, but maybe no good thing either. Lots to think about.

TALENT OPERATIONS
6. Workers Want Training but Employers Don’t Always Deliver. Can Policy Help?
Interesting data from Spain, which seems to link incidence of permanent hiring with commensurate increase in employer investment in skills development. Makes sense, because few things hurt an employer more than putting resources into a worker who then leaves after being up-skilled. Big implication for the increasingly impermanent workforce - gig workers, freelancers, contractors, fraction types - the loss of employer investment in your personal development is pretty much an iron law of the universe at this point. Good stuff as ever, from HiringLab
CULTURE
7. Chinese Tech Workers are Starting to Train their AI Doubles–and Pushing Back
With the news that Meta is making another round of mass lay offs, days after the internal announcement that it’s going to use employee keystrokes as training data for it’s AI models, the penny is starting to drop for the early AI enthusiasts who are building the machines that are going on to replace us all. Employers using employee metadata to train their AI Agent replacements is certainly going to be resisted, and I’m beginning to wonder whether then slow adoption of AI in enterprise may in fact in some part surreptitious sabotage by employees concerned that they will not be the ones sharing in the productivity gains delivered by AI. Resistance is happening in China, where “Colleague Skill” - a Github repo where you can distill your team mates metadata in an effort to make your colleagues more dispensable - went viral, whilst other developers have built ‘anti-distillation’ tools, a form of data poisoning which make sure any AI replacements trained on your data actually become incompetent AI workers. Read every link here - we’re at a fascinating moment in human history.

TALENT INTELLIGENCE
9. AI Job Scams are Booming – and I Was Fooled by One. Here is How to Avoid Them
Most talk on 2026 has been on candidate fraud - that is, fraudulent behaviour by job seekers - but there is an under reported phenomenon going the other direction: fraud targeted at job seekers. Interesting article, as the author is a journalist who found herself to be a victim, with the scam being as simple and low value as a redirect to a CV rewrite service. AI is making target acquisition and personalisation a lot cheaper, and I suspect we’re all going to have to up our infosec game in this era where default skepticism unfortunately has to become default.
