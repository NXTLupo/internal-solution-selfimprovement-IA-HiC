The Brainfood
1. AI Doesn’t Have ROI
Ed Zitron has been an inveterate critic of the AI economy which he characterises as both a speculative bubble and a circular fraud. His newsletters are usually behind a paywall but this edition is free because he wants to warn his readers not to pile into the forthcoming SpaceX, Anthropic and Open AI IPO’s, which together are likely to be valued at over $4 Trillion - quite a big number for loss making businesses. The AI economy has ramifications for us, especially as we’re under pressure to automate away payroll - we had a fantastic conversation on this last week, which you can watch on Youtube. Also: open source AI, downloaded to local, completely undercuts the business model of these three giants, and it seems that users are learning that lesson already…lots to think about folks, and we in People Ops have to be involved in this conversation on AI token economics.
AI
2. Geopolitics & the Talent Migration Report 2026
Hard truth: you cannot achieve political power in democracies today without being explicitly anti-immigrant. Yet political convenience does not translate into economic growth, the lack of which can be largely attributed to demographic decline and increasing dependency ratio of rapidly aging societies. Deel doing its best to educate, excellent report on the impact of anti-immigration policies on talent flow.
SOCIETY
3. How to Design an Employee Relocation Package (and What to Do When a Move Means a Pay Cut)
Here’s another dimension of migration - when a person who is already an employee wants to move to another country, how do you recalculate the comp? Pretty easy when they move from low to high cost locations; somewhat trickier when the move is high to low cost. Great post here from Ravio on how-to.
CULTURE
4. AI Job Search 
Forget AI Mass Apply, why we’re in the era of AI DIY. This is the open source Github repo of Mads Lorentzen, a Data Scientist and ML Engineer, who built an agent who looks for jobs, evaluates them against his criteria, drafts cover letters and sends applications, 24/7. Forked nearly 1000 times, and I suspect will be forked 1000’s more times, because why not have an agent constantly looking for jobs (deals, leads, partners, whatever) for you? Correlates with historical milestone measured by Cloudflare last week - bots overtaking humans on internet traffic for the first time. 
H/T to brainfooder Pedro Oliveira for the share
RECRUITMENT ADVERTISING
5. Being AI-native Matters More Than Experience
Years of experience are now the tiebreaker between two AI-native candidates, not the gate that decides who gets to the table.
Hiring for AI fluency is increasingly becoming a requirement for almost any role where the input / output is digital. Zapier have been the most prominent in formalising this as a hard rule, and building a framework to help decision makers make this assessment. More are going to follow, using their own flavour. Check out this one from Posthog, where AI fluency has now become pass / fail gate for all hires. H/T to brainfooder Mark Deubels for the share.
ASSESSMENT
6. Meta Workers Can Opt Out of Being Tracked at Work - But Only for Half an Hour at a Time
It’s fashionable to condemn Meta these days for some of their cultural decisions but I reckon they are just ahead of the curve. Employers will inevitably become more authoritarian as external competition and internal coherence require them to build organisational intelligence, which can only come from rolling out more bossware. Big decision ahead for those of us who still have options - do we accept surveillance in exchange for job security, or jump to the freedom but uncertainty of the open market?
WORKFORCE AUTOMATION
7. Swanson vs IBM
Another day, another US court case to keep an eye on. It’s age-ism again and I suspect that this previously under discussed discrimination will end up driving the majority of the legal cases going forward, as the chances of securing a job really do collapse from late 40’s onward, we’re going to see a lot of experienced, highly motivated (and somewhat annoyed) job candidates press their case. Brainfooder Martyn Redstone once again doing great work in bridging the distance between the case and how it applies for us in TA / HR.
D&I
8. Wisdom Report: LinkedIn Report 2026
150,000 people surveyed on how they use LinkedIn, with some very interesting insights. Two major takeaways for me was the ‘Trump effect’ in collapsing the conversation in D&I, and the how people’s perception of employers can be changed on what they see on LinkedIn. Company profiles remain one of the most under utilised EB assets a company has. Easy reading - must read for TA / EB folks. Download here. H/T to brainfooder Lauren Levine for allowing the share outside of reg wall. 
EMPLOYER BRANDING
9. Flying Solo: The Rise of China’s One-Person Companies
Worrying about AI impact on employment is a global conversation, and the idea that entrepreneurship might absorb some of the surplus labour created by AI disruption is a story both Sam Altman and the Communist Party of China seems to be happy to promote. The OPC - an official classification in Chinese labour law - registered 16 million net new in 2025 - a number which we can track to see how many are chancing their arm / hedging their bets / or just doing what they can in this AI enabled world. btw: I think this is a community building / event building opportunity for somebody.
FUTURE OF WORK
10. What 20,973 r/recruitinghell Posts Reveal about Hiring in 2026
We collected every post made to r/recruitinghell in the first half of 2026 and classified them by complaint. The top grievance is not rejection. It is how rejection gets delivered. And across five months, the mix barely moved.
Super interesting, especially on AI / Automation being part of the story but not as much as we might have been led to believe. It’s still mainly ‘the how’ of the rejection that matters most. Have a read.
