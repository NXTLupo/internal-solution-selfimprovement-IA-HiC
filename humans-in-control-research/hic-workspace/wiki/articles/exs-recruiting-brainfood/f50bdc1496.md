The Brainfood
1. Open AI & Anthropic launch Deployment Service Lines
There is perhaps no greater evidence that the bottleneck for AI-adoption at Enterprise level is real than the near simultaneous launch by both Open AI and Anthropic this week of AI deployment service lines. The P/E backed nature of both ventures is also consistent with the idea that this is additionally a grab for Enterprise AI market share, with now 1000’s of organisations in each portfolio now likely locked into either Open AI or Anthropic universe. Three things to take from this: a) some reprieve for beleagured functional heads that they are not going fast enough on unlocking value from AI, b) probably some very decent hiring demand from this emerging AI deployment / implementation / support sector and c) further evidence that individual productivity ≠ organisational productivity.

2. Workday’s Last Workday?
The ‘other’ story about AI + Capital was this rather bluntly titled essay on the future of Workday, and by extension, all legacy players in the HCM / RecTech space. It’s a brilliant essay that provoked the great and good of the industry analyst space to weigh in with support and rebuttals alike. For people who didn’t keep up with the narrative (me included) Brainfooder George LaRocque does us a solid by summarising and linking back to the arguments, before adding his own hugely valuable take. This is kind of what blogging should be isn’t it?
TALENT OPERATIONS
3. Amazon Just Stepped Into the Hiring Space
Brainfooder Glen Cathey will some succinct thoughts on Amazon Connect Talent which kicked off a great conversation thread which is as valuable as the original post. Something to read after No2 in this newsletter, as the entrant of one of the big tech beasts into the talent space asks some big questions of rectech sector, and supports the LaRocquian view that the next big disruptor to come to WorkTech won’t be a seed funded startup but a big beast in another category making a sideways move. PS: anyone have an early view of Amazon Connect Talent, let me know, love to get your view of it…
TALENT OPERATIONS
4. Hurtful, Honest, or Helpful
This is a brilliant essay which could do with an editor! The central point being made is ultimately a techno-optimist one, but one which has the necessary recognition that the path to the future is too narrow for too many of us. The key, emotionally nourishing insight, is that AI will remove the ‘the work that was never the work’ - the wrestling with software and tools which was so inadequate that it required humans to develop additional processes in order to get value from them. The question then becomes, what will we do with the time saved?
WORKFORCE AUTOMATION
5. Samsung Chip Workers Reject $340,000 One-Time Bonus
Some interesting class conflict happening in Korea, with Samsung’s massive unionised workforce rejecting a one-off deal from HQ and holding out for perpetual bonuses tied to profitability. Workers of the world who can follow suit are likely to do so, as companies in the AI economy reap record revenues and the workers demand their share. This will eventually turn into an EB story - what is your company’s policy when it comes to sharing future benefits of AI?
EMPLOYER BRAND
6. Are Some Companies Using the Interview Process as a Stealthy Shortcut to (unpaid) Innovation? 
Brainfooder Amanda-Jane Lamont with one of those stories which sits uncomfortably between urban myth and all-too-plausible reality - ‘employers’ using the interview process as a means to soak up competitive intelligence / know-how. I’ve heard too many anecdotes from personal contacts to believe that this does indeed happen, which is why AJ’s post is so valuable, particularly in comments which include useful links on individual workers rights on IP - have a read.
CANDIDATE EXPERIENCE
7. The “AI Job Apocalypse” Is a Complete Fantasy
More from Andreesen Horowtiz - who, to their credit as vocal techno-optimists - consistently make the case with well researched, and well argued pieces like this. As ever though, it is written from the narrow perspective of those who can capture the value of technology innovation, ignoring those who suffer consequences of not being able to do so. There is also a classic capitalist validation of human work ‘we found new and different productive endeavors to fill our time’ - now here’s a man whose not read his Charles Dickens. I went back to brainfooder Jan Tegze essay The Workers Who Lost to Steam Engines Didn’t Write the History Books for balance to read alongside.
SOCIETY
8. Four Ways Europe’s Big Immigration Experiment has Changed Spain
It’s kind of not an experiment really, as we know how the immigration powered Western economy goes; short term economic boost + long term increase of tax payer / consumer base, in exchange for exacerbated social tensions and consequent political swings to the right. Tough balancing act to play for current PM Pedro Sanchez, and there may be no easy answers other than constant patching, updating and moving forward as best we can. Good article from the FT, likely all of us will have some resonance to the analysis here.
SOCIETY
9. The HR One-Stop Shop Is Finally Within Reach 
This essay from Volker Jacobs describes the ‘information routing’ problem that human bureaucracy evolved to solve, agreeing in large part with Jack Dorsey vision that AI is going to be basically replace the function because it is obviously better at transporting information. Volker is less extreme in what this means though, and pays much greater attention to where to deploy the time saved. Some good ideas here for TA / HR on what the future AI-enabled Talent Operations function looks like. H/T to brainfooder Ivan Harrison for the share.
