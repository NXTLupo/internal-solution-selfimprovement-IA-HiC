Friends,
I’m writing this from the Sheraton Hotel in Tung Chung, on Lantau Island, the final stop on what has been an epic tour of East Asia. Blasting through a dozen cities in 30 days was probably too much 🤣 but the tempo kind of made sure you never took a day off. There are so many great memories but the ones which stand out for me are the simple interactions with local people who are invariably helpful and kind; the DiDi driver in Shanghai who urgently corrected my erroneous x10 overpay of the fare on Alipay; the salon team in Busan who used Google translate to explain the haircut they were going to give me (!), the server in a Tokyo ramen shop who sorted out our food when another mistake of mine nearly caused us to miss out on last orders and countless others along the way. As much as I’ll not forget the sights and sounds of these fantastic places, these human moments are the memories to treasure more.
On cultural observations, I will write this up in the Open Kitchen segment of TWIR whilst I’m in the air and have it ready to publish when I land in Heathrow Monday AM.

The Brainfood
1. Airbnb’s AI for HR Playbook
Is this AI in HR post we have all been waiting for? Mary Kate Stimmler attends a talk from Airbnb’s Iain Roberts and summarised the main points, which are: a) trade PDF’s for .md files, b) convert the company’s best presentations into a “skill”, c) use meeting records to measure distance between rhetoric-to-reality d) leaders must not delegate but build (in order to learn) and e) hire and train ‘embedded organisation architects’. Love to see this presentation, but whilst I scout around for it, this summary from Mary Kate, as well as the comments, are gold.
TALENT OPERATIONS
2. LinkedIn Sent Me A Job Alert…
Have you seen a job advert which is in fact a training session for an AI model? Interesting story from a TA Leader who encountered such a case, apparently from LinkedIn itself, where the job advert is a pay-per-hour work through of how they do their job. OP warns her readers not to respond to these adverts nor do the training but as brainfooder Mitch Sullivan comments, recruiters who are already leaving the industry will have nothing to lose in helping train a replacement for someone else’s job. It’s a decision I think we’re all going to face, so I’ve got another poll running on LinkedIn on it - have a vote on this folks.
WORKFORCE AUTOMATION
3. Optimizing Your Website for Generative AI Features on Google Search
GEO or ‘Generative Engine Optimisation’ has become the employer branding / recruiter marketing buzzword of 2026, so we got to take notice when Google itself comes out with how-to on GEO for your website. In the end, the recommendation is simply to do better SEO, an argument which brainfooder Alex Chukowski has been banging on about for some time. Still, I reckon converting your public documents to markdown probably makes sense.
EMPLOYER BRANDING
4. AI Brain Fry, Workslop and the Ironies of Automation
Some super interesting ideas in this exceptional essay on the impact of AI on human work: the best two? Automation is anti-human by design and in workflows cannot be fully automated, the worst bits of the job remain concentrated for us to still do, and AI workslop produced by one person induces ‘AI brain fry’ in the person who receives it, because asking someone to read your AI gen report / essay / post is a transference of cognitive effort, which we resent. Going to think about this one for a long time. Make sure you read it.
CULTURE
5. Zhou Won. Brussels is Still Drafting.
Super interesting case in employment law in China where not one but two courts ruled that firing a human worker to replace them with AI is illegal because the arrival of new technology does not void existing employment contracts. Brainfooder Glen Cathey - as ever - will the concise summary, and from which I discovered Julie Valérie Belião newsletter article which provides a great comparison on regulatory regimes in US, EU and China. The Hangzhou People’s Court original summary of the case here
WORKFORCE AUTOMATION
6. The AI Paradox: More Exposure, Less Confidence in Gen Z
Is Gen Z turning against AI? Given that we are seeing what seems like the collapse of the entry level job market, we perhaps should not be surprised if they are. Survey data from Gallup suggests AI usage plateauing in the cohort we expect to be leading it. I suspect more granular segmentation will reveal a bifurcation between Gen Z super users vs everyone else.
SOCIETY
7. Chippy Boss Installing Self-service Tills as ‘Abrupt’ Customers Query Prices
Policy by rage tweet has innumerable downstream impacts but one consistent outcome of living in Trump 2.0’s world is that running businesses become more expensive. The best thing any sensible entrepreneur will do is hire less people and automate more functions, a rule which applies even to the good old fashioned British chippy. I’ve said it before so let me say it again: Trump is terrible for recruitment.
ECONOMY
8. When Did Employees Become the Audience That Doesn’t Matter?
Steve Cadigan with a critique on the phenomenon of CEO’s publishing layoff news on X, often times revealing to the public even before the employees. It’s hard to disagree with Steve when he calls this as bad policy, though I think the more we digitise and AI-enable our processes, the less viable human-speed processes are going to be. He’s right when he says CEO’s seem to have stopped caring about any PR backlash - maybe evidence of the anti-DEI, DGAF cultural revolution exemplified by Trump / Musk is being adopted across US industry?
CULTURE
9.  How do Big Career Transitions Affect Mental Health?
The dotted line is when the definitive event happened, so this chart shows how mental health operates much more like a process with important trends both before and after the event. Job change has high impact for mental health, especially involuntarily losing your job. OP btw has a pretty awesome blog on workforce mental health.
SOCIETY
10. AI and Coder Employment: Compiling the Evidence
Competing narratives on whether AI is boosting s/w engineering employment or decimating it are each so sure of themselves, that I’ve resorted to speaking to my developer mates and asking them how they are finding things. The stories I hear from them aligns much more closely to the findings in this paper - s/w jobs are still being posted but rate of growth of those jobs are far weaker than we might’ve otherwise expected had ChatGPT not crashed into the mainstream in 2022.

The Podcasts
11. Why UK Economy Became an Impossible Job
My favourite commentator on UK economy, on why switching out PM’s isn’t going to change a thing; there is a fracture between the politics and the economy with the former having lost control over the latter. Some actual sovereignty would help, but as OP suggests, so would switching taxation focus from businesses to land ownership, whilst reducing the cost of energy being a priority concern.

12. Taiwan’s Wealth Paradox - High GDP, Low Wages w/ “Mr. T”
It was great to visit Taiwan for the first time last week, and whilst I wasn’t able to get a crew together to do a How To Hire whilst on the island, I did get the chance to have dinner with locals who live and work there. Why is pay so low, when GDP (per capita even) is so high? TSMC has an outsized - and maybe negative - influence according to this super interesting conversation on Xiangyu’s Strait Times podcast.
