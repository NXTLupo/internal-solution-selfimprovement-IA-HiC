The Brainfood
1. The Great Rennovation
6,000+ people surveyed by Notion on the state of AI transformation within their organisations. The introduction included a perfect analogy, which ended up as the title for this report - AI transformation is like a home renovation - it’s more expensive than the estimate, takes longer than the planned timeline and there’ll be 1 or 2 catastrophic mistakes along the way. But. In the end, it will be worth it. Reading this report certainly is - key points: most of us (88%) are laggards, C-suite and employees see very different businesses when it comes to AI.

The most interesting part of this research piece from McKinsey is how the structure of different country economies will mean variation on how AI will impact the workforce there. ASSESSMENT
9. The Labour Market is a Network, Not an Auction
Any one who cites Mark Granovetter has an evens chance of making it to this newsletter 🤣. Granovetters’ seminal 1973 paper on The Strength of Weak Ties had more impact on my professional life than I can measure, and helped cure me of any lingering resentment I had from previously holding a Just World fallacy. It’s weak social ties which are critical to information transfer, because they bridge to networks which you otherwise would not know. Great to see brainfooder Andrew Marritt apply Granovetter to the job search - and it could be applied to anything. Follow Andrew btw, he’s going to feature a lot more on here I suspect, so you might as well get it from source.
NETWORKING
10. Love’s Labor’s Found: The Beijing Scholar Speaking Up for Hired Hands
Day Labour markets feel like an echo of the past, but they are still around today and I wonder whether they may make an unwelcome comeback in the future if the pessimists are right about AI impact on job displacement. Interesting interview with a Tsinghua University Ph.D. student who worked as a day labourer in the oldest and ultimate form of the gig economy.

The Podcasts
11. Cambridge “Unleased Racism” by Hiring Jason Arday
Generalising a singular story as proof of the inherent behaviour of the entire group is a common form of propaganda to which we are collectively subjected. Novaria Media correctly leads with this angle on the story of former Cambridge University professor Jason Arday, who resigned this week following an avalanche of claims of plagiarism, academic fraud and copious amounts of lying on the CV. The angle for us in recruitment is deeper understanding as to how misaligned incentives always encourage the wrong behaviours - in this case, suspension of due diligence in the rush to secure social approval, and perhaps, employer branding back in the day.

12. How Your 401K Automatically Funds the AI Replacing Your Job
AI being a bubble economy is now a well known theory - maybe even approaching mainstream acceptance - but one of the financial mechanism which is helping inflate it has been under discussed. The 401K - mandated employer- sponsored retirement plan - pile into index tracking funds, which in term automatically buy equities based on %age share of market. Bigger percentage share, more funds pile in until…pop. Nice explainer video, not all AI generated is slop…
