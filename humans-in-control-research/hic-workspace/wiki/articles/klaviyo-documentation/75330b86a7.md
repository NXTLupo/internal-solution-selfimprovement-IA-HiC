# Dark mode email design best practices

Source: [Klaviyo documentation](https://klaviyo.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049181631-Dark-mode-email-design-best-practices)

Learn about dark mode, a setting users can turn on in their device or email app to darken their displays. This setting impacts how emails are displayed. Klaviyo does not have direct control over how each inbox applies dark mode.
Dark mode has become popular because it is believed to have a variety of benefits. Users may choose dark mode to:
According to Litmus, the number of people viewing emails in dark mode increased from 28% in 2021 to 34% in 2022.
Dark mode impacts your email branding by adjusting the HTML used to render it in the inbox. These adjustments can be as small as inverting colors in text-only emails (while leaving HTML emails unchanged), or as substantial as adjusting every color in your fully-designed email template.
If you don’t consider dark mode when designing your emails, it can result in hard-to-read text, colors that don’t align with your brand styles, and more.
Consider SWAK Cosmetics’ logo. On a white background, it looks great.
But when viewed on a dark mode device, it is impossible to read.
Note the changes in this email header between light and dark mode. The email background color, text color, and link bar color all were inverted.
The specifics of dark mode changes vary between email apps, devices, and operating systems. Litmus offers a resource on the email clients that offer dark mode and how they treat HTML emails.
In general, inboxes fall into 1 of 2 categories: those that make minimal or no changes, and those that invert email colors.
Some inboxes, like Apple Mail for desktop, leave the colors in fully designed HTML emails unchanged unless you include dark mode metatags. Text-only emails can still change in dark mode. When the email content stays the same, only the surrounding inbox changes color.
Other inboxes, like Outlook.com, partially invert your colors. This means some lighter colors are swapped out for darker hues, and some darker colors are swapped with lighter ones (e.g., dark text that was previously on a light background). The end result is an email that’s notably darker than the original.
Some inboxes apply full color inversion, changing both light and dark backgrounds. For example, Outlook 2021 for Windows can turn a dark background light.
There are several strategies for maintaining your branding while optimizing for light and dark mode. Choose the one that best aligns with your team’s capacity and branding needs.
If you use Klaviyo’s drag-and-drop editor to send your emails, this strategy is best. Develop your email templates while keeping in mind what changes they’ll undergo on dark mode devices:
If you have a developer team that can custom-code your HTML emails from scratch, you can provide CSS that detects whether a viewer is using dark or light mode and adjust the design accordingly. This can override certain dark mode defaults in some inboxes, so you have more control.
Every inbox, device, and OS is different, so you’ll need to consider a wide variety of email recipients when designing dark and light mode versions of an email.
You can use @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) and [data-ogsc] to target dark mode users specifically in your email’s code. Learn more about which email clients support these tags and what code to use in your own designs.
We don’t recommend using image-only emails for a variety of reasons:
Many brands use image-only emails because image files preserve the colors and fonts built into the image. However, image-only emails still rely on HTML for layout, spacing, and surrounding backgrounds. An inbox can change those surrounding colors in dark mode, so this approach does not guarantee that the entire email will match your original design.
Before sending, check that your logos, text, and links are readable in both modes. Inbox testing is available for paid Klaviyo accounts and includes 100 tests per user per month. Each inbox preview you generate counts as 1 test.
Learn more about using inbox testing. You can also send a test email to inboxes you can access and compare it in light and dark mode.
