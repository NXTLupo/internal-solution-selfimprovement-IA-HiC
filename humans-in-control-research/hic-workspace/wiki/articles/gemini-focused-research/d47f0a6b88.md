# AI Research (gemini): insurance consumer perception AI adoption 2026

Source: [gemini (focused research)](deepresearch://0bf1fdf880c829)

### 1. Key Findings Organized by Theme

#### Theme A: Shifting Consumer Sentiment and the Trust Calibration
*   **Recalibrated But Conditional Sentiment:** According to the *Insurity 2026 AI in Insurance Report*, there has been a stark shift in consumer openness to AI in property and casualty (P&C) insurance. In 2026, **39%** of consumers agree it is a good idea for their insurer to use AI to improve services—nearly double the **20%** who expressed support in 2025. 
*   **Declining Brand Resistance:** Avoidance behaviors are tapering. The percentage of consumers who say they are less likely to buy from an insurer that publicly utilizes AI fell from **44%** in 2025 to **36%** in 2026.
*   **The Security and Transparency Deficit:** Despite growing acceptance, a distinct trust deficit remains regarding data integrity. The *Smart Communications 2026 Customer Experience Benchmark* reports that overall customer trust in AI usage in insurance actually dropped from **46%** in 2025 to **40%** in 2026. Only **39%** of consumers feel confident that AI tools handle their information securely or use it ethically. Crucially, **85%** of respondents assert it is highly important that insurers explicitly disclose when AI is used in their communications.
*   **Polarized Sentiment in Customer Reviews:** Sentiment analysis of 145,000 U.S. insurance customer reviews shows that reviews mentioning AI averaged a sentiment score of **-0.15** (compared to **+0.68** for non-AI reviews). However, the overall sentiment toward AI in reviews improved by **38%** year-over-year, indicating a closing trust gap when proper human-in-the-loop and transparent measures are used.

#### Theme B: Support Task Comfort vs. Decision Autonomy (Drawing the Line)
*   **Routine Tasks Welcome:** Policyholders are highly receptive to AI handling transactional, informational, or assistance-based operations. According to Insurity's survey data:
    *   **46%** are comfortable with AI generating insurance quotes.
    *   **39%** are comfortable with AI tracking claims statuses.
    *   **38%** are comfortable using AI to update personal details.
*   **Rejection of Autonomous AI Decision-Making:** Consumers strongly push back when AI shifts from an advisory or assistance role into an autonomous decision-making agent. Only **22%** of policyholders feel comfortable allowing AI to file an insurance claim on their behalf, and a mere **16%** accept AI autonomously canceling or renewing their policies.
*   **The Catastrophe Response Exception:** Interestingly, severe weather and environmental crises represent a massive catalyst for AI acceptance. Insurity’s May 2026 update reveals that **51%** of consumers support insurers using AI to monitor severe weather and issue real-time risk alerts (up from **45%** in 2025). Additionally, **51%** of respondents would trust an AI-validated claim using satellite or meteorological data to expedite settlement (up from **38%** in 2025).

#### Theme C: The Rise of "Agentic Commerce" and Algorithmic Purchasing
*   **Omnipresent Generative AI Usage:** Generative AI has crossed into mainstream daily utility. Globally, **82%** of insurance consumers are actively utilizing generative AI tools, with **84%** of Americans using them at least occasionally and **27%** daily.
*   **Delegating the Purchasing Journey:** The Accenture *Talk to My AI Agent* global report (surveying 25,000+ consumers across 16 countries) highlights that **72%** of consumers expect AI to influence at least half of their insurance purchasing decisions within the next 12 months, surging to **86%** among Gen Z and Millennials. Furthermore, **74%** would trust a personal AI agent to assist with finding deals, comparing policies, or managing renewals, while **32%** are willing to let an AI agent select the final insurance product for them under preset parameters.
*   **AI Overtaking Traditional Comparison Engines:** A fundamental disruption is occurring in insurance distribution. For younger demographics, generative AI platforms have officially surpassed traditional price comparison websites. Specifically, **27%** of Millennials and **26%** of Gen Z utilize AI platforms for insurance research, compared to just **16%** and **11%** who use standard comparison websites. Furthermore, **37%** of consumers report that generative AI has prompted them to consider insurance brands they previously would not have evaluated.

#### Theme D: Regulatory Demands for Transparency and Bias Mitigation
*   **FCA Evaluation of Outcomes:** In the UK, the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is actively prioritizing consumer outcomes in AI adoption. During 2026, the FCA is executing a review to evaluate the risks and opportunities of AI in insurance underwriting, claims, and consumer services. Under the FCA's Consumer Duty framework, firms must closely monitor outcomes, with the regulator set to publish findings from its "AI Live Testing" sandbox initiative by the end of 2026.
*   **Mandated State Guardrails in the US:** In the U.S., regulatory compliance has become a primary driver of how insurers design customer-facing AI. As of Q1 2026, over **26 states** have adopted the NAIC Model Bulletin on the Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems by Insurers, which enforces strict guidelines around explainability and the prevention of unfair discrimination. Colorado's Regulation 10-1-1 (Quantitative Testing) remains the strictest framework, requiring active bias testing, while California’s SB 1120 explicitly prohibits health coverage denials based solely on AI without a physician's review.

### 2. Gaps Identified

While the 2026 data clearly defines general consumer attitudes toward P&C and auto insurance, several key blind spots remain in the available literature:

1.  **Life and Specialty Lines Specificity:** Most of the consumer perception surveys in 2026 (such as those by Insurity and Accenture) heavily bias toward personal lines like auto, homeowners, and health insurance. There is a lack of deep consumer sentiment data regarding highly complex, long-term products like whole life insurance, annuities, or specialized commercial policies.
2.  **Long-Term Impact of AI Failures (Hallucinations) on Loyalty:** While customer operations data suggests a Net Promoter Score (NPS) drop of up to 70 points when AI handovers fail, there is little empirical data demonstrating actual customer retention or churn rates over a 12-month period specifically following a publicly acknowledged AI underwriting or claim "hallucination" error.
3.  **Willingness to Pay for Human-in-the-Loop vs. Pure AI Premium Differences:** Although consumers heavily favor human oversight in high-stakes decisions, there is no quantitative market research illustrating whether policyholders are willing to pay a premium surcharge to guarantee human-only claims processing, or conversely, if they expect steep discounts for opting into fully autonomous, algorithmic policies.
