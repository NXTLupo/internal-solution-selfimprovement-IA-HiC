# AI Research (gemini): 2026 insurance consumer attitudes customer experience

Source: [gemini (focused research)](deepresearch://0690de286bc414)

### Executive Summary

As the insurance industry progresses through 2026, consumer attitudes and customer experience (CX) benchmarks indicate a profound market shift. The era of high rate increases is beginning to moderate, transitioning the landscape **from a pricing crisis to an experience challenge**. Consumers remain highly cost-sensitive, with comparison shopping hovering at historically high levels. 

The defining competitive frontier of 2026 lies in bridging the "integration gap" for advanced technologies. While policyholders are rapidly adopting artificial intelligence (AI) tools to understand and compare coverage, insurers are struggling to scale backend systems to deliver the seamless, cross-channel experiences that modern consumers expect.

---

### Key Findings by Theme

#### 1. Transitioning from Rate Shock to Experience-Led Shopping
* **Stabilizing Price vs. Experience Friction:** According to the *J.D. Power 2026 U.S. Auto Insurance Study*, overall customer satisfaction with auto insurers remains flat at **644** (on a 1,000-point scale). Although fewer consumers are experiencing carrier-initiated price increases, satisfaction is held back by digital friction.
* **Persistently High Comparison Shopping:** The *J.D. Power 2026 U.S. Insurance Shopping Study* found that while the overall share of consumers actively shopping for auto insurance declined from 57% to **53% year-over-year (YoY)**, it remains historically elevated.
* **Aggressive Search Volume:** Data from *TransUnion’s Q1 2026 Personal Lines Trends* shows that auto insurance shopping rose **10.6% YoY** and property insurance shopping grew **5.3% YoY**. According to the *LexisNexis® Insurance Demand Meter*, **47.1%** of auto policies-in-force were shopped at least once over a 12-month period as of Q4 2025 (up from 45.2% in Q4 2024). 
* **Generational Communications Divide:** *TransUnion's 2026 Trends and Outlook Report* reveals that Gen Z and Millennials favor strong brand identity and prefer email-driven digital outreach, whereas Gen X and Baby Boomers demand proactive human-to-human phone outreach regarding pre-renewal rate adjustments and discount offers.

#### 2. The Digital and AI Experience Paradox
* **Rapid Adoption of Consumer AI:** Approximately **one-third (33%)** of auto insurance shoppers now use public AI tools to compare coverage options and policies. Shoppers utilizing AI are significantly more likely to switch carriers. 
* **The "Transactional" Trust Gap:** According to the *Capgemini World Property and Casualty Insurance Report 2026*, **86% of policyholders** still view their relationship with their insurer as strictly "reactive and transactional," because digital front-ends are not evolving as fast as consumer expectations. Only **58% of consumers** state they fully understand their auto policy coverage.
* **Failing Digital Service Channels:** The *J.D. Power 2026 U.S. Insurance Digital Experience Study* notes that **47%** of new auto and home policies are now purchased digitally. However, overall customer satisfaction with insurance websites and mobile apps has declined YoY, with only **11%** of customers using the virtual assistants and chatbots designed to streamline support. 
* **The Scale Inefficiencies of AI:** The *NTT DATA Insurtech Global Outlook 2026* highlights that while **66% of insurance employees** utilize AI tools daily, only **22% of insurers** have successfully scaled AI models into full production. Capgemini also highlights an "architecture mismatch" where **81% of insurers** cite legacy IT infrastructure and **74%** cite poor data quality as roadblocks to deploying CX-enhancing AI.

#### 3. Claims Experience: Efficiency Offsetting Premium Pressures
* **Property Claims Satisfaction Rebound:** Driven by investments in digital channels and a relatively calm catastrophe year, overall property claims customer satisfaction rose by 20 points YoY to **702** on a 1,000-point scale, according to the *J.D. Power 2026 U.S. Property Claims Satisfaction Study*.
* **Faster Resolution Cycle Times:** Homeowners' property claims were resolved an average of **3.4 days faster** in 2026 compared to 2025. Despite this improvement, the average baseline timeline to move from first notice of loss (FNOL) to final payment remains high at **40.7 days**.
* **The "Pricing Shock" Threshold:** The claims process remains highly vulnerable to financial friction. The 19% of homeowners who filed claims while facing a combination of a carrier-initiated rate hike, out-of-pocket costs, and a deductible of $1,000 or more reported a satisfaction score of just **606** (nearly 100 points lower than the study average).
* **The Financial Value of CX Retention:** ProfitWell benchmarks in 2026 reveal that customer acquisition costs (CAC) for P&C insurers have risen **60%** since 2020, while retention costs have grown by only **12%**. Data compiled by Bain & Company shows that carriers that invest equally in customer retention and acquisition see **190% higher revenue growth** than those focused solely on customer acquisition.

#### 4. The Under-40 Disconnect and Evolving Lifestyle Trends
* **The Youth Disconnect in Life Insurance:** The *Capgemini World Life Insurance Report 2026* found that although **68%** of individuals under the age of 40 believe life insurance is essential, **32%** refuse to purchase it because they feel traditional products are not designed for their current life stage. 
* **Absence of Traditional Life Triggers:** Life insurance’s share of individual investment wallets has fallen **23% over the past 15 years**. This is primarily because younger buyers are delaying or skipping traditional triggers: **63%** of under-40 consumers have no immediate marriage plans, and **84%** do not plan on having children.
* **Demand for Flexible, Experience-Led Products:** Capgemini's Voice of the Customer Survey reveals that **45%** of consumers plan to increase discretionary spending on experiences (like travel) rather than acquiring physical assets, and **70%** do not plan to purchase a home. This signals a massive consumer demand for flexible, transient, and modular insurance products.
* **Prevention-First Health Shift:** The *NTT DATA Insurtech Global Outlook 2026* indicates that customer demand for prevention-first, hyper-personalized healthcare solutions is growing rapidly, with a projected hyper-personalization compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of **35%+**. In response, Capgemini notes that **43% of health insurers** cite the underuse of preventive services as a primary driver of medical cost inflation, encouraging a structural transition of health insurance brands from passive payers to active wellness partners.

### Unanswered Research Gaps

Despite the depth of recent data, several key strategic questions remain unanswered by the available evidence:
1. **The Financial Performance of AI-Adopting Customers:** While J.D. Power establishes that one-third of shoppers use AI tools to switch, there is no public data detailing the *lifetime value (LTV)* or long-term retention rates of these "AI-assisted" policyholders once they move to a new carrier.
2. **Independent Agent Integration Metrics:** While J.D. Power refers to the *Independent Agent Satisfaction Study*, specific quantitative scores showing how independent brokers and agents respond to carrier-provided AI tools (specifically regarding how they influence the end-consumer experience) are unspecified.
3. **Global Variation in Property Claims Timelines:** Available property claim metrics (e.g., the 40.7-day cycle time) are heavily skewed toward North American carriers; granular European and Asia-Pacific property claims handling timelines and consumer satisfaction comparisons remain unspecified.
