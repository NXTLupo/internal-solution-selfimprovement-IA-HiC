# AI Research (gemini): retail investor experience fintech platform shifts 2026

Source: [gemini (focused research)](deepresearch://1f3ccc0dc06a37)

### 1. Key Findings by Theme

#### Theme A: The Emergence of Prediction Markets as a Core Retail Trading Vessel
Fintech platforms are structurally shifting to integrate prediction (event) markets directly into the retail investing experience. Once considered a niche or gamified corner of the internet, these platforms are becoming heavily institutionalized and accessible without separate crypto wallets or specialized apps.
*   **Rapid Trading Volume Scaling:** The prediction market industry is projected to reach **$240 billion in trading volume in 2026** (on a trajectory toward $1 trillion by 2030). Monthly notional trading volume has scaled from under $100 million to over $13 billion over an 18-month period.
*   **Institutional Valuation & Integration:** Mainstream prediction platforms Polymarket and Kalshi are valued at **$8 billion and $11 billion**, respectively. Retail giant Robinhood is bypassing third-party venues (like Kalshi and ForecastEx) to route trades through its own effectively controlled, CFTC-regulated exchange, **Rothera** (built with Susquehanna International Group). 
*   **Retail Platform Volume and Pricing Shifts:** Robinhood reported more than 12 billion event contracts traded in 2025, with over 16 billion traded in the first half of 2026. To aggressively capture market share, Robinhood is pricing event contracts with a capped fee of **1 cent per contract**.
*   **State-Level Regulatory Friction:** The rise of these platforms has triggered rapid regulatory pushback. For instance, Tennessee’s Public Chapter 1109 (effective July 1, 2026) makes it a Class E felony to hold a prediction contract while attempting to influence the event's outcome. Illinois (via SB 3019) has introduced a **1.75% transaction tax** on prediction wagers for the FY2027 budget.

#### Theme B: The $25 Trillion Mass-Affluent Opportunity and Earlier Lifecycle Engagement
Wealth tech and brokerage platforms are pivoting their retail experiences downward. Instead of reserving advisory-led experiences for traditional HNW minimums ($250,000 to $1 million+), platforms are engaging younger, moderately wealthy users earlier in their financial lifecycle.
*   **Sizing the Mass-Affluent Market:** Total U.S. household financial assets reached **$102 trillion** at the end of 2025. Middle-market and mass-affluent households (holding between $100,000 and $2 million) represent **$25 trillion of this total across 46.9 million households** (up from $14 trillion in 2013).
*   **Age-Gated Customer Acquisition Pressure:** Platforms are racing to capture these investors early, as customer acquisition opportunities drop sharply by age: firms have a **44% chance of successfully onboarding prospects under age 30**, compared to only **24% for prospects in their 50s**.
*   **The Rise of Single-Provider Bundling:** Approximately **32% of affluent retail investors** report a preference for using the same provider for both their investing and banking needs. This rate climbs to **61% among "Collaborative Prospects"** (younger, early-stage investors), driving fintechs to build comprehensive financial "super-apps."

#### Theme C: Hyper-Personalization and the Structural Dominance of Active ETFs
Retail investor preferences are shifting away from rigid, pre-packaged mutual funds in favor of hyper-customized direct indexing and active exchange-traded fund (ETF) vehicles.
*   **Active Mutual Fund Outflows vs. Active ETF Inflows:** In Q1 2026, active ETFs collected **$135 billion in inflows**, while traditional active mutual funds suffered **$332 billion in outflows**. Active ETFs have surged to a **10% market share** of the total U.S. ETF market in 2026, up from just 1% in 2019.
*   **Launch Dominance:** Showing a massive platform supply shift, active ETFs accounted for **79% of all ETF launches** in early 2026. Additionally, **66% of surveyed ETF investors** state they prefer active management over passive over the next 12 months.
*   **Direct Customization Becomes Baseline:** According to MSCI's *Wealth Trends 2026* report, **98% of new High-Net-Worth (HNW) portfolios** now include some form of customization, up from only 60% of advisers expecting customization demand in 2025. 

#### Theme D: Transitioning AI from Advisor "Copilots" to Structural Infrastructure
Fintech and wealth management firms are moving past basic generative AI productivity "add-ons" (e.g., text generation) toward integrated backend architectures—coined by analysts as "Unified Client Brains"—to combat margin compression and advisor shortages.
*   **Widespread Investment Commitment:** **95% of wealth management professionals** plan to increase AI investments over the next three years, with 68% declaring it a prerequisite for future competitiveness.
*   **The Advisor Execution Gap:** Despite high funding, **44% of wealth managers** believe their segment's AI adoption lags the broader financial services industry, and only 27% feel they are leading.
*   **Measurable Productivity Gains:** BCG analysis shows that deep-tier integration of "agentic AI" is delivering **over 50% productivity gains in retail lending** and driving **over 30% fee income growth** in wealth management by making low-ticket mass-affluent services economically viable.

#### Theme E: Retail Resilience and "Dip Buying" Amidst Macro Pessimism
The retail investor experience in 2026 must support users navigating extreme macro-pessimism. Despite negative market outlooks, retail investors remain highly active participants.
*   **Sharp Bearish Shift:** According to Schwab's *Q2 2026 Retail Client Sentiment Report*, **58% of retail clients are bearish** on the U.S. stock market (up from 41% in Q1 2026), while bullish sentiment fell to **28%**. Top concerns include geopolitical conflict (25%), market volatility (24%), and the D.C. political landscape (9%).
*   **Persistent Market Volume:** Despite widespread bearishness, retail flows continue to account for roughly **20% (one-fifth) of total U.S. equity volume**. 
*   **The "Buy the Dip" Mentality:** Within active retail trading segments, **82% of clients** state they are likely to "buy the dip" if opportunity arises, and **39%** describe themselves as actively "risk-seeking".

### 3. Gaps Identified

While the 2026 data clearly details the shifts in retail platforms, several critical questions remain unanswered by the current evidence:

*   **Retail Profitability/Loss Rates in Prediction Markets:** While transaction volumes (e.g., Robinhood’s 16+ billion contracts) and platform valuations are heavily documented, there is a lack of transparent, empirical data on the actual win/loss ratios of retail investors utilizing prediction markets. It is unclear if these operate more like net-negative sports-betting apps or legitimate portfolio hedges.
*   **PDT Rule Changes Timeline:** Robinhood’s executive leadership noted that the SEC is actively revising the $25,000 Pattern Day Trader (PDT) rule in Q2 2026 to "unlock more active trading". However, the exact regulatory rollout timeline and the final threshold structure remain unspecified.
*   **Execution Costs of Custom Direct Indexing:** While customization has skyrocketed to 98% in new HNW portfolios, the data does not specify the asset floor at which direct indexing becomes cost-profitable for the mass-affluent ($100k-$2M) segment, given the clearing, custody, and technology administration overhead of managing highly fractionalized, customized index baskets.
