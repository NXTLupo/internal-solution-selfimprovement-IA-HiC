# AI Research (gemini): higher education fundraising advancement donations 2026

Source: [gemini (focused research)](deepresearch://1b1b114e58b952)

### 1. Key Findings

#### Theme A: Voluntary Support Trends & Financial Performance
* **Record Philanthropic Totals vs. Inflation:** Philanthropic gifts to U.S. colleges and universities reached an estimated **$78.8 billion** in Fiscal Year 2025 (ending June 30, 2025), representing a **4% year-over-year increase** according to the CASE Insights Voluntary Support of Education (VSE) Survey released in **April 2026**. While this total signals robust donor support, the 4% growth was just enough to keep pace with inflation, masking underlying system pressures.
* **Public vs. Private Growth Divergence:** Sector-wide growth was uneven. Public institutions outpaced private peers; **63.7%** of public colleges reported gains in actual funds received, and **57.6%** saw increases in new funds committed. In contrast, only **55%** of private colleges grew their funds received, and **53.1%** saw gains in new committed funds.
* **Allocation of Funds:** Major donors continued to restrict where their money went, with **81%** of all contributions designated for specific uses (e.g., student success programs, financial aid, and research). Of total funds received:
  * **60%** went to current operations (53.2% restricted, 6.2% unrestricted).
  * **27.8%** went to endowments.
  * **10.3%** went to capital expenditures.
* **Endowment Inflow Decline:** According to the NACUBO-Commonfund Study of Endowments (released Feb 2026), new gifts directly to college endowments **dropped 9.2% in FY 2025** to just under **$14 billion**. To cover operating deficits and federal funding pressures, colleges withdrew **$33.4 billion** from their endowments, an **11% year-over-year increase**.
* **2026 Mega-Gifts:** High-net-worth individual giving remains strong at elite campuses. Notable 2026 principal gifts include:
  * **University of Texas at Austin:** A **$750 million** commitment from Michael and Susan Dell for a research campus, medical center, student housing, and AI computing.
  * **University of California, Davis:** A **$120 million** gift from Sanford and Joan Weill to rename its veterinary school and build an animal teaching hospital.

#### Theme B: The "Dollars Up, Donors Down" Pipeline Vulnerability
* **The Shrinking Donor Base:** Although total alumni giving climbed **10.9%** in FY 2025, the absolute number of alumni donors shrank. This "dollars up, donors down" trend is a critical long-term vulnerability, as it leaves institutions highly dependent on a small, aging base of ultra-wealthy major donors.
* **Operational Metrics and Under-tracked Retention:** According to the *2026 Philanthropy Pulse Survey* of higher education institutions (published February 2026), advancement departments heavily prioritize immediate activity-based metrics over long-term pipeline health:
  * The most tracked metrics are contacts (**34%**), donor visits (**34%**), and proposal close rates (**32%**).
  * Conversely, donor retention is tracked by only **16%** of institutions.
* **The Retention Chasm:** While **63%** of institutions successfully increased their volume of new donors over the past year (up from 51% the year prior), only **47%** managed to retain 45% or more of those donors over a rolling three-year period.
* **Cost to Raise a Dollar (CTRD):** Higher education operations remain highly efficient. Nearly half (**47%**) of surveyed institutions report spending less than $0.25 to raise a dollar, with **9%** spending under $0.10. However, **one-third** of institutions do not measure or are unsure of their CTRD.

#### Theme C: Strategic Shifts – Giving Days and Unrestricted Funds
* **The Power of "Giving Days":** Giving Days have matured from experimental digital sprints to critical annual pillars. The CASE and Almabase *State of Giving Days* findings reveal that **24%** of higher ed institutions now generate between **11% and 25%** of their total annual fundraising from a single 24-hour Giving Day event. Nearly **40%** of institutions noted that these campaigns are their best tool for widening the donor pool and converting first-time givers. 
  * *Case Studies:* NC State's Day of Giving raised **$50 million+** in 2025, and the University of Oklahoma broke records with **$30 million**.
* **The Push for Unrestricted Revenue:** EAB's 2026 advancement predictions indicate that institutions are putting immense pressure on advancement teams to secure more unrestricted operating revenue. This is driven by a predicted **15% enrollment decline** in 2026 (the post-2008 "demographic cliff"), which prevents universities from balancing operating budgets through tuition hikes alone.
* **Alumni Engagement Portfolios:** To bolster engagement, development teams rely heavily on reunions and events (**50%**), alumni boards (**39%**), annual giving campaigns (**29%**), and giving days (**24%**).

#### Theme D: Policy and Legislative Disruption (The "One Big Beautiful Bill" Act)
* **Sweeping Tax Shifts for 2026:** Signed into law on July 4, 2025 (Public Law 119-21), the "One Big Beautiful Bill" (OBBB) Act reshapes donor tax incentives starting in the **2026 tax year**:
  * **The Non-Itemizer Benefit:** Standard deduction earners (who represent ~90% of U.S. households) can now claim an "above-the-line" charitable cash deduction of up to **$1,000 for single filers** and **$2,000 for married couples filing jointly**. Advancement leaders see this as a key tool for reversing the shrinking donor trend among younger, mid-tier alumni.
  * **The Itemizer Floor:** High-earning donors who itemize face a new **0.5% Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) floor**. Charitable deductions can only be claimed for contributions that exceed 0.5% of the donor's AGI (e.g., a donor with $200,000 AGI cannot deduct the first $1,000 of their giving). Financial consultants are advising donors to "bunch" donations or pivot to Donor-Advised Funds (DAFs).
  * **The Corporate Floor:** C corporations face a new **1% charitable contribution floor** of taxable income before allowing deductions, altering corporate sponsorship strategies.
* **The Financial Aid Squeeze:** The education title of the OBBB Act slashes federal higher education aid and programs by over **$220 billion** (restricting graduate student loans and tying program-level aid eligibility to graduate earnings outcomes). This massive funding cut places a much higher burden on university advancement offices to raise private philanthropy to cover student financial aid.

### 3. Gaps Identified

* **Realized Impact of OBBB Tax Changes:** While the OBBB Act went into effect on January 1, 2026, there is no retrospective national data showing how many non-itemizers utilized the new $1,000/$2,000 deduction, nor is there concrete evidence showing whether major donors actually reduced their giving or switched entirely to "bunching" and DAF vehicles to bypass the 0.5% AGI floor.
* **National FY 2026 VSE Survey Data:** The complete data for the current fiscal year (FY 2026, ending June 30, 2026) is **unspecified**. The deadline for institutions to report is October 2, 2026, meaning final aggregated metrics will not be compiled and published by CASE until early 2027.
* **AI and Predictive Tech Performance Metrics:** While **70%** of higher ed leaders noted technology adoption is a top priority in 2026, there are no industry-wide benchmarks illustrating the exact return on investment (ROI) of predictive modeling or artificial intelligence in donor cultivation compared to traditional outreach methods.
