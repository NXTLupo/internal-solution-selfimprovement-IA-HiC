# Robot Raggy taking on one of farming’s most persistent weed problems

Source: [agr_future_farming](https://www.futurefarming.com/tech-in-focus/field-robots/robot-raggy-taking-on-one-of-farmings-most-persistent-weed-problems/)

A small autonomous robot is preparing to head out into the fields of Dorset this summer – and farmers dealing with ragwort infestations are watching closely. Raggy, an electrically powered farm robot developed by UK robotics firm Robotriks, has arrived at Dorset Innovation Park near Wareham for its final round of testing before commencing field trials across farms and land in the county. For farmers and landowners, the arrival could hardly come soon enough.
Ragwort (Jacobaea vulgaris) is one of Britain’s most problematic agricultural weeds. It can contaminate grazing land and cause serious health problems if eaten by livestock. The plant contains alkaloids that cause irreversible liver damage. For arable and mixed farmers, it represents both a regulatory obligation and a significant drain on time and labour. Currently, control is usually targeted, with plants removed from areas where they pose a risk to livestock. The standard method – hand-pulling – is slow, costly, and physically demanding work that also carries risks to those doing it.
At the heart of the project is Robotriks’ Robotic Traction Unit (RTU), a platform the company has been developing and refining since its early days at the University of Plymouth. The RTU costs just £7,000 (€8,114) – almost a tenth of the cost of most comparable products on the market. Powered by batteries lasting up to 24 hours, it can be deployed for a wide range of tasks from crop monitoring to harvesting.
For the Raggy project, the RTU has been equipped with sophisticated detection and removal capabilities. Using machine vision and connected technology, it locates ragwort plants and mechanically removes them at the root. The system is powered by the Qualcomm Dragonwing platform – meaning the robot can make decisions in the field in real time.
The robot reduces the need for harmful chemicals, supports healthier soils and protects animals. What makes the platform particularly interesting for the wider farming community — including arable growers — is its modular design. The RTU is a fully electric, modular platform designed to perform a range of agricultural tasks in real farm conditions.
Robotriks has previously demonstrated attachments for precision spraying, autonomous planting, and crop monitoring. With cameras and custom-trained AI to recognise different types of weeds and crops, the system can target and directly spray weeds growing too close to main crops – a capability with clear potential for arable farming, where herbicide resistance and the cost of blanket chemical applications are growing concerns.
Raggy has been developed collaboratively by Robotriks, technology solutions company Telint, and network architecture specialists Neutral Networks, with backing from Dorset Council. Funding came from Qualcomm Incorporated through its Qualcomm for Good Initiative. Ben Timmons, Senior Director of Business Development at Qualcomm Technologies International, described Raggy as ‘a powerful demonstration of what’s possible with intelligent connected technologies’.
Dorset Council’s Leader, Cllr Nick Ireland, welcomed the trials, noting that council rangers currently spend considerable hours each year removing ragwort by hand from the county’s country parks and nature reserves.
The trials are taking place at Dorset Innovation Park, which is part of an emerging South West Global Autonomy Cluster. The cluster has recently been awarded up to £20 million (€23,2 million) of government funding to enhance the region’s reputation as one of the world’s best places to develop, test and deploy autonomous technologies.
