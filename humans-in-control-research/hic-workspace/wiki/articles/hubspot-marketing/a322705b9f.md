# How much does AEO cost? Pricing by agency, tools, and software

Source: [hubspot_marketing](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/how-much-does-aeo-cost)

How much does AEO cost? The short answer is roughly $30 a month for a monitoring tool you run yourself to over $15,000 a month for a full-service agency program that handles everything for you — with a wide middle in between.
What lands you at one end of that range or the other is how much of the work you’re actually paying for. AEO pricing goes up with scope. A tool that only monitors your visibility in AI answers sits at the low end, while strategy, content production, and off-site authority building push the number up, and buying all of it as a managed service sits at the top.
This guide breaks down AEO cost by approach, by budget tier, and by what a realistic pilot should run, so you can size the spend against what your team needs.

How much does AEO cost across agencies, tools, and software?
AEO cost spans a wide range, from monitoring tools that start in the low tens of dollars a month (such as HubSpot AEO at $50/mo) to full-service agency programs at thousands of dollars a month (such as RevenueZen’s $15,000 Total Market package). Agency services can cost even more for custom packages. Where you land depends on how much of the work you’re buying, from simply tracking your visibility in AI answers to producing optimized content and building off-site authority.
AEO is just one line inside a larger AI budget, so it helps to know what AI costs across model types and tools before you size the AEO portion.
Most teams weighing the cost of AEO are choosing among three approaches:
- An agency that runs the program for you
- A monitoring tool you subscribe to and operate yourself
- A software-led, in-house setup where your own team does the work with a tool such as HubSpot AEO
Here’s how the three AEO options compare on price, billing, and what you typically get.
The spread between the low and high ends comes down to scope rather than vendor markup, which the next section breaks down.
Why AEO Pricing Varies So Much
The gap between the low and high ends of those ranges comes down to how much of the work you’re buying and how large your content library is. Five factors drive most of the spread:
- Content scope. Optimizing a handful of existing pages costs far less than producing net-new, buyer-specific content at volume. The more pages and formats you create or rework, the higher the bill.
- Schema and entity work. Structured data and consistent entity signals across your site, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, and review profiles take technical time to implement and maintain.
- Off-site authority building. Earning mentions on the third-party sources answer engines trust — such as review sites, industry press, and Reddit — is labor-intensive and ongoing, which makes it the widest swing between lean and high-investment programs.
- Monitoring coverage. Tools are priced by how many prompts you track and how many answer engines they cover, so broader coverage moves you into a higher subscription tier.
- Reporting depth. A basic visibility dashboard costs less than competitor benchmarking, citation analysis, and prioritized recommendations.
These drivers explain the range. How you pay for them (by retainer, subscription, or a mix) is the next question.

AEO Pricing Models Explained
AEO help is sold in four main ways, and the label on the invoice tells you a lot about what you actually get. It’s easier to compare quotes once you understand the different pricing models.
- Monthly retainers are ongoing agency engagements that bundle content production, schema and technical fixes, off-site authority building, and citation tracking into a recurring fee. Need real-life examples? Rates run from about $249 a month for an entry plan covering audits and strategy from NetWebMedia to $15,000 a month for a full-service program from RevenueZen (which includes up to 15+ articles, LinkedIn posts, and a custom analytics dashboard). Link building, original research, and content repurposing are often add-ons rather than inclusions at lower tiers, and some agencies may ask for a three-month minimum.
- One-time audits plus implementation split the work into two. A diagnostic audit maps where your brand does and doesn’t appear across engines, flags schema and content gaps, and benchmarks competitors, priced anywhere from free to a few hundred dollars. The fixes are usually billed separately, either as a fixed-scope project or an ongoing retainer. Marketing company Digital Elevator offers a single sprint starting at $3,000, and its highest-priced engagement is over $24,000.
- Software subscriptions are self-serve monitoring tools that track brand visibility, share of voice, and citations across answer engines, metered by prompts, responses, credits, or seats. Standard self-serve tiers run from about $29 a month (Otterly.ai Lite) to roughly $489 a month (Otterly.ai Premium), with plans like Profound‘s Growth ($399) and Ahrefs Brand Radar ($199/mo per answer engine) in between. Most of these tools track and report rather than create or publish content.
- Hybrid models mix these approaches: An audit that rolls into a retainer, a retainer backed by a performance guarantee, or a software license paired with a human operator you can later swap for your own staff.
What You Get at Different AEO Budget Tiers
Those models map onto three rough spend tiers. What separates them is how much of the content, off-site, and reporting work from the sections above you’re paying someone else to do.
- Lean (roughly $30 to a few hundred dollars a month). A monitoring tool subscription on its own, in the low-tens-to-$50 range covered in the table above, occasionally paired with a one-time diagnostic audit. You get visibility tracking, share of voice, and recommendations, but your team executes every fix. 
Best for: Small teams, solo marketers, and pilots for testing whether AEO moves anything before committing budget
- Mid-range (roughly $1,500 to $5,000 a month). A smaller retainer or done-for-you plan that adds modest content production, schema work, and monthly reporting on top of monitoring. Nugentive’s Done-For-You plan at $1,497/mo, which handles schema and three to five pages of content a month, sits near the entry of this band. 
Best for: Teams with some in-house content capacity that want execution help without a full program
- High-investment (roughly $10,000 to $15,000+ a month). A full-service managed program: strategy, high-volume content, sustained off-site authority building, technical work, competitor benchmarking, and pipeline-level reporting. 
Best for: Larger teams treating AEO as a core channel, or those without the in-house resources to run it themselves
Whatever tier you choose, AEO spend works best alongside SEO rather than instead of it. That’s why it helps to know how much to budget for SEO, too.
AEO vs. SEO and When You Need Both
Before you shift budget toward AEO, understand what the two disciplines share and where they split, because their budgets are related but not interchangeable.
SEO and AEO run on the same foundation: clear, credible content on a technically sound site. They diverge on where that content surfaces and how you measure it. SEO works to rank your pages in search results and earn clicks, measured by rankings, click-through rate, and impressions. AEO works to get your brand cited inside AI-generated answers, measured by mentions, share of voice, and citations. SEO also writes for a person skimming links; AEO has to satisfy the engine building the answer before any person reads it.
AEO complements SEO by improving your brand’s visibility in AI answers rather than replacing traditional search optimization. The two reinforce each other: Pages with strong Google rankings tend to get picked up as citations in AI answers (though it’s not a guarantee). Cutting SEO to fund AEO can weaken the rankings that feed those citations.
You need both because buyers use both. AI referral traffic has roughly tripled from January 2025 to December 2025, according to Search Engine Land’s research, but still represents less than 2% of referral traffic in the study’s dataset. That overlap is also why some vendors sell ordinary SEO as AEO, which is one of the pricing red flags worth spotting before you sign anything.

AEO Pricing Red Flags to Avoid
The category is new enough that pricing and promises aren’t standardized, so a few patterns should make you slow down before you sign.
- Rebranded SEO. If a proposal is ordinary SEO with “AEO” stamped on it, and success is still defined only by keyword rankings and clicks, you’re paying a premium for work you may already have. The two disciplines share a foundation, but AEO adds the “zero-click” metrics of brand mentions and citations that SEO misses.
- Guaranteed citations. Similar to SEO, no AEO vendor can promise they’ll win you citations or mentions. Some vendors may offer money-back guarantees, which is fine, but don’t fall for a vendor who promises increased AI search visibility but doesn’t put their money where their mouth is.
- Vague scope. “Full AEO optimization” with no page counts, engine list, or named deliverables makes quotes impossible to compare and invoices impossible to check. Ask how many prompts get tracked, which engines, and how many pages get produced or reworke
