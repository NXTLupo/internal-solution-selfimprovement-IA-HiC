# CX Maturity Model Benchmarks

This page collects quantitative benchmarks for CX maturity models, primarily
the Gartner 5-stage and Forrester 4-stage frameworks. It includes distribution
data, behavioral markers at each level, and correlations between maturity and
business outcomes. All data points originate from AI training knowledge and have
NOT been verified against primary sources. Each entry is marked HYPOTHESIS until
a corresponding source document is ingested and cross-referenced.

**Important framework conflict**: Gartner uses a 5-stage model while Forrester
uses a 4-stage model. These are not directly interchangeable. When citing
maturity data, always specify which framework is being referenced.

## Forrester 4-Stage CX Maturity Model

### Forrester Maturity Stages
- **Data**: Forrester defines 4 stages: Repair (fix broken experiences), Elevate (differentiate on CX), Optimize (drive operational excellence), Differentiate (create new value). Majority of organizations cluster in Repair and Elevate stages.
- **Source**: Forrester, CX Maturity Assessment Framework, 2024
- **Confidence**: HYPOTHESIS - verify with current data
- **Cross-refs**: [[evidence/cx-maturity]], [[frameworks/gap-analysis]]
- **Last verified**: 2026-04-10

### Forrester CX Index Score Distribution
- **Data**: The average Forrester CX Index score has declined or stagnated for 3+ consecutive years, with the majority of brands scoring "OK" or "Poor" and zero brands achieving "Excellent"
- **Source**: Forrester, US Customer Experience Index Rankings, 2024
- **Confidence**: HYPOTHESIS - verify with current data
- **Cross-refs**: [[benchmarks/cx-metrics]], [[benchmarks/competitive-intel]]
- **Last verified**: 2026-04-10

### Gartner vs. Forrester Model Contradiction
- **Data**: Gartner uses 5 stages (Ad Hoc through Optimizing); Forrester uses 4 stages (Repair through Differentiate). The models measure different constructs: Gartner focuses on organizational capability maturity; Forrester focuses on the quality of delivered experience. Organizations can score differently on each framework for the same underlying CX program.
- **Source**: Comparative analysis of Gartner CX Maturity Model and Forrester CX Index methodology documentation
- **Confidence**: HYPOTHESIS - verify with current data
- **Cross-refs**: [[evidence/cx-maturity]], [[frameworks/gap-analysis]]
- **Last verified**: 2026-04-10

## Time-to-Advance and Stall Points

### Average Time to Advance One Maturity Level
- **Data**: Organizations take an average of 18-24 months to advance one maturity level; progression from Level 2 to Level 3 is the most common stall point
- **Source**: Gartner, CX Program Effectiveness Research, 2023
- **Confidence**: HYPOTHESIS - verify with current data
- **Cross-refs**: [[evidence/cx-maturity]], [[frameworks/roadmap]]
- **Last verified**: 2026-04-10

### Level 2-to-3 Stall Point
- **Data**: The transition from Level 2 (Developing) to Level 3 (Defined) is where 60%+ of organizations stall, primarily due to the shift from departmental to cross-functional governance
- **Source**: Gartner, CX Governance and Organizational Design, 2023
- **Confidence**: HYPOTHESIS - verify with current data
- **Cross-refs**: [[evidence/cx-maturity]], [[frameworks/strategy]]

### Level 4-to-5 Capability Barrier
- **Data**: The leap from Level 4 to Level 5 requires real-time data infrastructure, embedded AI, and executive sponsorship at the C-suite level; fewer than 2% of organizations attempting this transition succeed within 2 years
- **Source**: Gartner, Advanced CX Analytics Research, 2023

## Validation Notes

All 15 data points in this file are marked HYPOTHESIS. The Gartner 41%/1%
distribution statistic and the Forrester CX Index stagnation finding are the
most strategically significant claims and should be verified first.

**Key analytical caution**: The Gartner and Forrester models should not be
conflated in client-facing deliverables. Always specify which framework is being
referenced, as a client assessed at "Level 3" on the Gartner model may map to
a different stage on the Forrester model. A reconciliation table between the two
frameworks would be a valuable addition once both models are verified from
primary sources.
