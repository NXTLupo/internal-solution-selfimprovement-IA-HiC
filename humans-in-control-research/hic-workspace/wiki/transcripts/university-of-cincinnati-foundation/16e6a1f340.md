# UC Epilepsy Center delivers comprehensive, data-driven and patient-centered care

Source: [University of Cincinnati Foundation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G1Xlf-hOtVw)

Epilepsy is a disorder of the brain where the brain cells synchronize too much. It can produce clinical symptoms, everything from a brief stare into what people know as a grand mal or convulsive seizure. Even though the symptoms occur when the seizure occurs, people can have ongoing symptoms that can occur in between seizures like mood disorders, they could have medication side effects. Those are things that we are looking at here and trying not only to prevent seizures but how they do day-to-day, hour-to-hour, minute-to-minute. The Epilepsy Center at UC Gardner Neuroscience Institute has outpatient and inpatient programs. The outpatient program, we have nine different epilepsy neurologists specialists who see people with either early stage all the way up to advanced cases of epilepsy. We have a special inpatient unit called the Epilepsy Monitoring Unit where we bring people with epilepsy into the hospital and then often take medications away while they're hooked up on video and EEG to actually trigger seizures to help us understand what kind of seizures they are, where those seizures come from, and what kind of treatments we might be able to use to control the seizures better. The people who have the more difficult, complex cases really need the expertise that we have here at the Neuroscience Institute. We have expertise across all areas of epilepsy, surgery, medicines, clinical trials, and integrative medicine approaches to epilepsy. The Gardner Neuroscience Institute is the regional expert in epilepsy. We have a learning health system. The learning health system here in our epilepsy program has been going on for the past four to five years. It's a way of looking at the disease of interest and understanding your patient population with data and using that data to develop interventions and then to learn from that intervention. So it's a team approach where we have patient and family partners, we have quality improvement experts, providers, data people that are all involved in developing quality improvement methodology. But it's not just looking at the end result, it's how do you get to that end result? How can we develop innovative treatment strategies, management strategies to improve our patient outcomes? Each person is different, but we can learn from all of our patient population as a whole and develop treatments and approaches that result in excellent outcomes for all of our patients. I think without philanthropy, our program would be maybe half the size it is. We were able to kick off projects. We were able to retain faculty that otherwise we might not be able to retain. We're able to hire new people so that we can see more patients. We now see people at four different sites. Our group now can see people at any of those sites, so we have a bigger draw. In terms of the research, you have to have an idea to write a grant, but you also have to have preliminary data. You have to be able to show that this idea really makes sense, and we need a bigger study to do it. It's very hard to get funding for those initial pilot projects. That's where philanthropy is critically important. I definitely want to send a thank you to all of the donors that have been active in helping us expand our neuroscience care. We want to continue that growth and continue that effort to be the best neuroscience center in the world.
