# Hidden Gems of the University of Iowa Libraries

Source: [University of Iowa Alumni](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5dV_A6tJ6-w)

As the largest library system in Iowa, the University of Iowa Libraries are dedicated to fostering student success, high-impact research, and creative discovery. So the university libraries consist of seven libraries. There's the main library, as well as six branch libraries that specialize in different areas of study, like arts, business, engineering. Each location offers both physical collections, as well as access to an incredible, almost endless range of digital resources. And so students can interact with the library as a space anywhere they're at on campus. Special collections and archives is a really great resource here at the main library for researchers and the curious at large. The oldest thing in our collection is 4,000 years old. It's a cuneiform tablet. We have medieval manuscripts, scrapbooks from the 20th century, first edition, even artist books that were made just last year. We have over 4,000 miniature books. The smallest miniature book we have in our collection literally fits on my pinky. It's about a millimeter each side. We have a lot of theses and dissertations that capture the work of these students being done here at the university, including Catlett's thesis. We've got works from famous faculty that have been here, Kurt Vonnegut, Grant Wood, which people always love to get their hands on as well. So the John Martin Rare Book Room is a collection of about 6,500 items focused on the history of the health sciences. Our earliest works are from 1470. Very heavily illustrated. It's an amazing collection that's really a wonderful resource for folks on campus as well as in the community. We have a book from 1597, De Curtorum Curgia Per Incitonum. It was really all about rhinoplasty or recreating a nose. The first half is all text, but then the last half is all illustrations, and they are amazing illustrations demonstrating how to take a flap of skin from your bicep, attach it to one side of the nose, and you have an apparatus on to hold your arm there for as long as it takes for that blood flow to establish there. So it's a really amazing, fun book with some really great illustrations in it. The Cantor Rare Book Room is located in the music library, and it contains a little bit of everything. So we have books, we have scores, and we have recordings. One of people's favorite items to see is the manuscript of the Iowa Fight song. It's in the Meredith Wilson collection, which is actually quite small. It's just one box. He donated the autographed manuscript of the song that he wrote for us, and you can see we're in the manuscript. He wasn't sure what we should yell at one point. We go Go Hawks today, but back then he has rah rah question mark, so they were still kind of workshopping that. It's one of everybody's favorite things for us to show because it's such a Hawkeye tradition. These distinct collections all come together in the Main Library Gallery. The Main Library Gallery has a new exhibition each semester, and it's guest curated by faculty and staff from across campus, and each one is wildly different. The Fall 2025 exhibition is Paper Engineering in Art, Science, and Education. The John Martin Collection has some amazing anatomical flat books, and these are books that are trying to take a two-dimensional format and really give a sense of the three-dimensionality of the body. The Spring 2026 exhibition in the gallery will be called Orchestrating Community, the Public Service of Iowa Conductor James Dixon. It includes scores and papers and photographs and recordings, a little bit of everything, and we're talking about the role that orchestras play in our communities. So come and see it. It will be extraordinary. So we want people to see the libraries as a place of exploration, discovery, connection, where they can see that learning is really active and is creative. Whether someone's exploring an exhibit or learning a new research skill, we hope they feel inspired to ask questions, to make connections, and really feel that scholarship is a conversation that they can contribute to.
