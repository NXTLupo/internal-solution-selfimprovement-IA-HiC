CASE STUDY · NORTHEASTERN GIVING DAY
Giving Day delivered Northeastern's largest single-day result by embedding philanthropy inside an event
ANNUAL GIVING CAMPAIGN · BOSTON, MA · 330K+ ALUMNI BASE TIMEFRAME Single day (April 2026) INVESTMENT Events team production · Cvent
CHALLENGE
Annual giving faces a structural headwind: alumni have become more sophisticated and are less responsive to direct solicitation. The events team needed to drive participation at scale without making the event feel like a fundraising ask, and without adding solicitation headcount the budget does not carry.
APPROACH
Every event charged a registration fee that included a fractional gift component, an event fee gift, so philanthropy was embedded in the transaction rather than bolted on. The Giving Day itself was produced entirely by the events team as an educational and community experience, with fundraising woven into the architecture rather than announced from the stage.
OUTCOME
Giving Day produced the largest one-day result in Northeastern's history, with thousands of individual donors giving in a single day. The model bypassed direct solicitation friction entirely: the gift arrived as a natural part of participation, not as a separate decision the alumnus had to be talked into.
LESSON
Embedding philanthropy inside an experience removes the psychological friction of a direct ask. When the gift is part of the transaction, not a separate decision, participation scales without requiring a larger solicitation team.
