FULL INTERVIEW · 47 MIN · 3 SPEAKERS · DE-IDENTIFIED · 2024-03-12
"Switching cost one semester. It saved my career."
interviewer · alum · advisor
Interviewer00:42
Walk me back to the week you decided to switch.
Alum 0247-K00:48
It was week three of fall freshman year. I'd been in Business for exactly fourteen class meetings and I was absolutely certain I was in the wrong room. I went to office hours not to ask about course content but to ask about transferring.
Interviewer01:21
Who did you go to first?
Alum 0247-K01:24
A TA I'd had for the one CS elective I'd been allowed to take as a non-major. He pointed me at Dr. Mehta in Khoury and said "tell her your story and she'll know what to do." I walked across campus that afternoon.
Dr. Mehta (advisor · interviewed separately)02:08
I remember the meeting. The student knew what they wanted; my job was to map the credit clock. We mapped it in about 90 minutes. The hardest part was making them believe the switch wasn't a failure — they kept apologizing for changing their mind.
Alum 0247-K02:55
The thing I didn't know was that switching at week three was easy. If I'd waited until end of semester, it would have cost me a year. Nobody had told me that. The whole system rewards moving fast.
Interviewer03:31
What about co-op?
Alum 0247-K03:34
Fall junior, Hubspot. I almost didn't apply because the listing said "rising senior preferred" and I was a year behind from the switch. My advisor pushed me to apply anyway. They didn't care about the year — they cared about the work in my portfolio. By month two of co-op I was leading a small project. They wrote me a return offer at the end of the term.
Alum 0247-K05:42
The thing nobody tells you about being first-gen at a four-year is that your parents will be supportive but they won't have advice. So you have to find the people whose job it is to have advice. Northeastern's advantage is that those people are everywhere — TAs, advisors, alumni networks — but you have to walk up and ask. The cost of asking is way smaller than the cost of not asking.

Pattern 01 · 11/18 journeys
"The students who switched majors at or before week 6 of their first semester paid the lowest credit-clock penalty and reported the highest career satisfaction. Switching later is more expensive — both in time and in narrative."
Pattern 02 · 9/9 first-gen journeys
"Every first-gen alum cited a SPECIFIC named person — an advisor, a TA, a peer — who told them an option existed that they didn't know about. The information asymmetry, not the talent gap, is the dominant friction."
Pattern 03 · 14/18 co-op stories
"First co-op employer significantly over-determined the second and third co-ops via referral networks. The 'wrong first co-op' is recoverable but expensive — it shifts the network neighborhood for the rest of the degree."
