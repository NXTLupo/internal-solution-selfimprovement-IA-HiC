# Case Study Framework Specification

## Purpose

This page is the authoritative reference the AI reads before generating any case-study deliverable (single slide or a multi-slide collection). It codifies the canonical structure, evidence-density floor, content budgets, citation protocol, narrative discipline, and the anti-patterns that have produced visual and substantive defects in prior runs. Every generated case study must comply with these standards or be flagged for human review.

This page exists because its absence (`404` on `/hic-workspace/wiki/frameworks/case-study.md`, observed 2026-05-24 on the Northeastern run) directly caused the corpus-relevance skew (87% LOW relevance), the thick-evidence-all-drop silent fallback, and the visual export defects documented in `wiki/synthesis/export-failures.md`.

---

## Canonical Framework

### What a case study IS

A case study is a single named institution / company / program that solved a specific, measurable version of the problem the client is facing. It carries five non-negotiable elements:

1. **A named subject** — the specific organization, fully identified (not "a peer institution"). The subject's distinguishing characteristics are stated up front (size, sector, geography, market position).
2. **A specific challenge** — the version of the user's problem this subject faced, stated as a single sentence with named consequences.
3. **A specific approach** — the named mechanism, system, intervention, or program the subject deployed. NOT a category ("they used AI") — the specific implementation ("they deployed an AI-driven Virtual Prep Advisor that profiles students before formal advisor assignment").
4. **A quantified outcome** — at least one keyMetric with value, unit, and direction (e.g., "85% of first-year students completed co-op readiness assessments"). The outcome must be measurable and sourced.
5. **A portable lesson** — the structural feature of the subject's mechanism that ports to the user's situation, stated as a one-sentence implication.

### What a case study IS NOT

- A hypothetical scenario or narrative speculation about what an institution *might* do.
- A high-level industry trend statement with no named subject ("Universities are adopting AI for advising").
- A generic benchmark without the supporting story (a statistic with no mechanism explanation).
- A composite "average" institution. The whole point is the specific evidence of the specific subject.

### Three valid case-study shapes

| Shape | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct precedent** | The subject solved the same problem in the same industry | "Drexel's mandatory Co-op 101 prep portal..." for a co-op program client |
| **Adjacent precedent** | The subject solved a structurally identical problem in another industry (per the `analog-finder` skill) | "Toyota's just-in-time inventory" applied to a healthcare staffing client |
| **Negative precedent** | The subject tried the approach and it failed (essential for pre-mortem evidence) | "The 2018 Kansas City CRM consolidation collapse" cautioning against a similar move |

Decks should include at least one negative precedent per recommendation tier. Three positive precedents in a row signals a confirmatory bias problem, not robust evidence.

## Required Sections (10 Components)

Every case study generated by the system must include all 10 sections. Omitting a section requires an explicit justification in the output metadata.

### Section 1: Subject + Subject Meta

The named institution / company / program. The `subjectMeta` field is the one-line context-anchor: type, location, size, market position.

**Good**: "Northeastern University — Private Research University · Boston, MA · 36K students · Experiential Leader"
**Bad**: "A large university in the Northeast"

Length: subjectMeta MUST be ≤120 chars. Use `·` (U+00B7 MIDDLE DOT) as the separator, never `/`.

### Section 2: Action Title

A complete sentence stating the case study's central finding WITH data. Per `.claude/CLAUDE.md` Action Title rules:

- **Required**: a verb, a quantified outcome, the named subject
- **Length**: 60-120 chars
- **Examples (good)**: "Drexel's mandatory Co-op 101 prep portal increases first-round placement rates by 28%"
- **Examples (bad)**: "Drexel's Approach" (topic label, no data), "A leading university improved placement rates" (no named subject)

### Section 3: Timeframe + Investment

Concrete duration and resource investment. Both fields appear inline as metadata.

- **Timeframe**: stated as `N months/years (period)` e.g., "12 months (Academic Year 2024-2025)"
- **Investment**: stated with dollar amount AND team size e.g., "$850K curriculum overhaul · 8 faculty leaders"

If investment cannot be sourced precisely, state an order of magnitude with confidence flag (`~$1M, est.`). Never write "Significant investment" or "Substantial resources."

### Section 4: Challenge

A single paragraph (max 400 chars) stating the version of the user's problem this subject faced. Required elements:
- The specific problem being solved (not the symptom, the underlying issue)
- The downstream consequence the subject was trying to prevent
- The metric that quantified the problem before the intervention

### Section 5: Approach

A single paragraph (max 400 chars) stating the named mechanism, system, intervention, or program. Required elements:
- The specific intervention named (not "an AI solution" — "an AI-driven Virtual Prep Advisor")
- The mechanism by which it works (one phrase explaining the causal lever)
- Any explicit constraints or design choices the subject made

### Section 6: Outcome

A single paragraph (max 400 chars) stating the quantified result. Required elements:
- The keyMetric improvement (with value + unit + direction)
- The timeframe over which the improvement materialized
- At least one second-order effect (what changed downstream of the headline metric)

### Section 7: keyMetric + supportingMetrics

Structured numeric panel. Schema:

```yaml
keyMetric:
  value: "85%"            # the headline number with unit
  label: "first-year student co-op readiness completion rate"
supportingMetrics:
  - value: "-40%"
    label: "advisor onboarding time"
  - value: "92%"
    label: "student profile completion"
  - value: "4.2/5.0"
    label: "student satisfaction score"
  - value: "3.2x"
    label: "increase in early resume drafts"
```

- Exactly 4 supportingMetrics (no fewer, no more — the NU template renders a 2x2 grid that breaks below 4).
- Each value: ≤8 chars (renders in large display font; longer values overflow the grid cell).
- Each label: ≤45 chars (single-line).
- Every metric must trace to the `sources[]` array. No fabricated numbers.

### Section 8: pullQuote

A single verbatim quote that grounds the case study in a human voice (per `.claude/CLAUDE.md` quote-discipline rules). Schema:

```yaml
pullQuote:
  text: "How might technology support a student in their co-op journey..."   # ≤250 chars
  attribution: "Andrew Fegley"
  role: "Co-Founder, NXT Humans"
```

- `text` must be a verbatim excerpt from a real source. Generic / fabricated quotes are auto-rejected by [[learned_constraints]] (`persona-quote` constraint applies here too).
- `attribution` must be a named individual or named organization.
- `role` provides the credibility context.
- Quote MUST appear within the right-side dark panel (Key Outcome region). Quote attribution MUST NOT inherit `overflow: hidden` from the panel; see `wiki/synthesis/export-failures.md` 2026-05-24 entry for the bug this prevents.

### Section 9: lesson

A single sentence (max 200 chars) stating the portable structural insight. Required elements:
- The mechanism that ports to the user's situation (not the surface features)
- An explicit causal claim ("X causes Y because Z")

**Good**: "Standardizing first-year preparation through automated, scalable digital tools ensures all students achieve a baseline of professional readiness before meeting their advisors."
**Bad**: "This case shows that preparation matters." (no mechanism, no portability)

### Section 10: implication

A single sentence (max 200 chars) stating what the user (the client) must do given this evidence.

**Good**: "Northeastern must replace fragmented college-level prep methods with a centralized, AI-enhanced first-year readiness standard."
**Bad**: "We should consider similar approaches." (no specificity, no decision)

## Evidence Density Floor (mandatory)

Every case study must clear ALL of these gates before shipping:

| Gate | Floor | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct named sources in `sources[]` | ≥2 | Single-source case studies cannot survive cross-examination |
| At least 1 quantified metric per section (challenge, outcome) | ≥1 | Qualitative claims invite "compared to what?" rebuttals |
| Total numbers across all sections | ≥6 | Density signals research depth |
| Verbatim quote present | ≥1 | Generic paraphrase auto-fails the [[learned_constraints]] persona-quote rule (applies here too) |
| Subject + Subject Meta complete | ≥2 sentences | Anchor the reader in a real organization |

Case studies failing ANY gate are rejected by `slidePostProcessor` and re-run with explicit feedback.

## Section-Label Vocabulary (Visual Defect Prevention)

Per `wiki/synthesis/export-failures.md` 2026-05-24 entry, the case-study template has a narrow section-label column that breaks words mid-character when labels exceed ~8 characters. The allowed section labels:

| Section | Allowed labels (≤8 chars) | Forbidden (would break) |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge | `CHALLENGE`, `ISSUE`, `PROBLEM` | `THE CHALLENGE`, `OUR ISSUE` |
| Approach | `APPROACH`, `MOVE`, `METHOD` | `OUR APPROACH`, `HOW THEY DID IT` |
| Outcome | `OUTCOME`, `RESULT` | `KEY OUTCOMES`, `WHAT HAPPENED` |
| Lesson | `LESSON`, `INSIGHT` | `KEY LESSON`, `THE LESSON` |
| Implication | `SO WHAT`, `IMPACT` (use `IMPLICATION` only if column widens) | `IMPLICATION` (9 chars — risky) |

The `validate_slide_variety.py` Stop hook + `export-paint-sync` Step 5b together enforce this at runtime.

## Brand Application (NU vs NXT)

The `brand=nu` and `brand=nxt` annotations on `:::slide template=case-study brand=X` select different visual treatments. The structural rules above are identical; only typography + color tokens differ. See `public/nu-design-gallery/colors_and_type.css` (NU) and the NXT brand tokens (`.claude/CLAUDE.md` § "Brand Colors").

The header separator is **always `·` (U+00B7 MIDDLE DOT)** regardless of brand. Forward slash is wrong (NU gallery rule + observed export drift 2026-05-24).

## Anti-Patterns (auto-rejected by `slidePostProcessor.ts`)

1. **Unnamed subject** — "A leading research university..." is auto-rejected. Name the institution.
2. **Composite subject** — "Several peer institutions reported..." is auto-rejected. Pick one.
3. **Unsourced metric** — every value in keyMetric and supportingMetrics must trace to `sources[]`.
4. **Fabricated quote** — quotes with no `attribution.role` or whose text doesn't match a known sourceable record are auto-rejected. Verbatim only.
5. **Topic-label title** — "Case Study: Drexel" is a topic label, not an action title. The title must STATE A FINDING WITH DATA.
6. **Section labels >8 characters** — would break mid-word on export per the 2026-05-24 defect log.
7. **`KEY OUTCOMES` (plural)** — the panel renders ONE keyMetric + 4 supporting. Always singular: `KEY OUTCOME`.
8. **`Comparables` section** — explicitly removed per 2026-05-24 user feedback. Do not regenerate.
9. **Generic citation** — "research shows" / "studies indicate" / "data suggests" are auto-rejected by `validate_citation_resolution.py`.
10. **Editor-mode UI elements** rendered inside the slide content — `Delete` / `Edit` / `Save` buttons must carry `className="editor-only"` so the export-mode CSS hides them. See export-failures.md 2026-05-24.

## Quality Gates Active for Case Studies

These Stop hooks enforce the rules above at every generation:

- `validate_citations.py` — every `sources[]` entry must be a named source identifier (not "research")
- `validate_evidence.py` — `evidenceBasis` fields must contain specifics (numbers / quotes / names)
- `validate_titles.py` — action-title compliance
- `validate_source_diversity.py` — ≥50% external sources
- `validate_budgets.py` — all content budgets above
- `validate_quantification.py` — recommendations within case studies need the four-tuple
- `validate_citation_resolution.py` — no unresolved [CHUNK_XXX] or placeholder citations
- `validate_slide_variety.py` — when in a multi-case-study deck, force template variety
- `validate_framework_page_exists.py` — this page must exist (it does as of 2026-05-24)
- `pre_export_dom_audit.py` — checks the editor-only class chain before export
- `validate_no_silent_fallback.py` — catches degraded generation paths

## When to use Case Study

Use case studies when:
- The deliverable needs to demonstrate that a recommendation is **precedented**, not theoretical
- The user is building a business case where a peer's outcome is the closest evidence
- A `pre-mortem` skill output identified an analogue failure that should be surfaced

Do NOT use case studies when:
- The corpus has only one source for the named subject (single-source case studies fail evidence density)
- The subject's situation differs so substantially from the user's that the lesson doesn't port (forced precedents are worse than no precedent — flag and skip)
- The recommendation is purely additive housekeeping (no precedent needed — strategy-author's Coherent Actions phase handles these directly)

## Composition with Skills

- `recommendation-architect` (skill) — each recommendation that needs precedent triggers a case-study slide
- `analog-finder` (skill) — provides cross-industry case studies for adjacent-precedent shape
- `quantification-architect` (skill) — the keyMetric / supportingMetrics MUST inherit from the four-tuple
- `pre-mortem` (skill) — provides negative precedents (the "what failed" case studies)
- `slide-title-lab` (skill) — generates the action title with the 8 title formats
- `deck-narrative-composer` (skill) — sequences multiple case studies into a coherent argument
- `experience-prototype-composer` (skill) — different surface, but borrows the case-study evidence-density rules
- `verbalized-sampling` (skill) — used at the case-study selection step to avoid mode collapse (always picking the same 3 famous institutions)
