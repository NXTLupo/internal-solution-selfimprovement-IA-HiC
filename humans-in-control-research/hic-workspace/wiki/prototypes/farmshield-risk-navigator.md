Frame: convention-honoring (probability 0.4)
Title: FarmShield Risk Navigator
Thesis: A guided property-risk assessment that scores a farm's exposure across climate, equipment, and practices, then generates an underwriting-ready readiness report.
Why this frame: This is what a competent insurtech designer builds: a self-serve risk questionnaire feeding an AI scoring engine and a shareable report. Schema-congruent with agricultural insurance and clearly advances the underwriting-viability research goal.

PRIMARY PERSONA:
  Name: Dale Hendricks
  Role: Owner-operator of a 1,400-acre corn and soybean farm
  Context: Fourth-generation Iowa row-crop farmer preparing his 2026 property insurance renewal. Works with a regional independent agent, carries aging grain-handling equipment, and has weathered two flood-related losses in the past decade.
  Emotional state: Anxious about rising premiums and dismissive quotes; skeptical that a website can capture the reality of his operation but tired of feeling like a number to underwriters.
  Pain points:
    - Premium quotes arrive without explanation, making it impossible to know which improvements would lower his cost
    - Farm documentation lives in shoeboxes, spreadsheets, and his memory — assembling it for renewal takes days
    - Underwriters flag aging equipment and drainage practices he considered normal, surprising him at renewal
    - No visibility into how his climate exposure (flood, hail, derecho) actually maps to insurer risk categories
  JTBD: When my insurance renewal approaches, I want to understand and improve how an underwriter sees my farm's risk, so I can secure fair coverage at a defensible premium without days of paperwork.
SECONDARY ACTORS:
  - Independent Insurance Agent (Marcy): Receives Dale's readiness report, uses it to shop carriers and pre-fill applications, and coaches Dale on high-impact mitigations.
  - Underwriter (carrier-side): Consumes the standardized readiness report as a pre-qualified risk packet, reducing back-and-forth information requests.
  - Ag Lender / Farm Credit Officer: Uses the same report to validate collateral protection and risk posture for operating-loan renewals.
  - Farm Operations Manager (Dale's son, Cole): Helps complete equipment inventory and implements mitigation action items in the field.

DEMONSTRATED MOMENT: Dale completes a farm profile, watches his scattered details resolve into a single composite risk readiness score with category breakdowns, and downloads a lender/insurer-ready readiness report with a prioritized mitigation plan.

USE CASES (10) - the prototype MUST demonstrate at least 5 of these as live flows:
  1. Farm Profile & Asset Intake
     Trigger: Dale starts a new assessment 60 days before his renewal date and lands on a guided intake flow.
     Flow: He enters farm location and acreage → the tool auto-fetches parcel geometry and county from a map pin → he confirms crop mix and structures (grain bins, machine sheds, dwellings) → he adds equipment either by scanning a receipt photo or picking from a make/model catalog → progress bar shows completeness across five categories.
     AI role: Vision extraction reads uploaded equipment receipts and insurance declarations pages to auto-populate make, model, year, and replacement value; an assistant asks clarifying follow-ups only where data is missing.
     Outcome: A structured farm profile is assembled in under 20 minutes instead of days, with 80%+ of fields auto-filled from documents and geodata.
  2. Climate Exposure Scoring
     Trigger: Dale's confirmed parcel geometry triggers automatic peril modeling for his specific location.
     Flow: The tool overlays FEMA flood zones, historical hail/derecho frequency, drought indices, and soil drainage class onto his parcels → each peril gets a 0-100 exposure sub-score → Dale sees which fields drive the exposure → he can toggle 'what-if' mitigations like tile drainage or windbreaks.
     AI role: A climate peril model synthesizes multi-source geodata into per-peril exposure scores and plain-language explanations of why each field scores as it does.
     Outcome: Dale understands his climate risk at the field level and sees which mitigations move the needle most before he spends a dollar.
  3. Aging Equipment Risk Audit
     Trigger: Dale's equipment inventory is complete and he opens the Equipment Aging screen.
     Flow: Each asset gets a condition and failure-likelihood indicator based on age, hours, maintenance history → a timeline shows which assets are past expected service life → high-risk items (e.g., a 1998 grain dryer) are flagged with fire/breakdown exposure notes → Dale can log recent maintenance to improve scores.
     AI role: An equipment failure prediction model estimates remaining useful life and breakdown/fire probability per asset class, adjusting for logged maintenance.
     Outcome: Dale sees a ranked list of the equipment most likely to cause a claim, so he can prioritize replacement or maintenance ahead of renewal.
  4. Practices & Compliance Gap Check
     Trigger: Dale answers a set of guided practice questions (grain storage protocols, chemical handling, electrical inspections, fire suppression).
     Flow: Each answer is compared against carrier underwriting expectations and best-practice benchmarks → gaps are surfaced as specific findings → each finding links to a recommended remedy with estimated effort and premium impact → Dale marks items as planned or complete.
     AI role: A practice-gap detection model maps Dale's stated practices against a knowledge base of underwriting guidelines and flags deviations with severity ratings.
     Outcome: Dale gets a concrete, itemized list of the practice gaps an underwriter would penalize, translated into actions he can take.
  5. Composite Risk Readiness Score
     Trigger: All five categories reach sufficient completeness and the tool computes the headline score.
     Flow: Weighted category sub-scores (climate, equipment, practices, structures, financial-continuity) roll up into a single 0-100 readiness score → a radial dial animates to the value → each category shows contribution and trend arrows → Dale can expand any category to see its drivers.
     AI role: A composite scoring engine weights and calibrates sub-scores against an underwriting-legible rubric and generates a one-paragraph rationale for the overall grade.
     Outcome: Dale's scattered inputs resolve into one number an insurer will recognize, with total transparency into what built it.
  6. Mitigation Recommendation Plan
     Trigger: Dale taps 'Improve My Score' from the readiness summary.
     Flow: The tool ranks all open findings by score-impact-per-effort → each recommendation shows projected point gain, estimated cost, and typical premium influence → Dale selects items into a personal action plan → the plan re-scores in a 'projected readiness' preview.
     AI role: An optimization pass sequences recommendations by ROI and simulates the projected score if the selected actions are completed.
     Outcome: Dale leaves with a prioritized, cost-aware improvement roadmap and a preview of the score he can reach by renewal.
  7. Underwriting-Ready Report Export
     Trigger: Dale clicks 'Generate Report' to share with his agent and carriers.
     Flow: The tool assembles a branded PDF with the readiness score, category breakdowns, peril maps, equipment schedule, practice attestations, and mitigation plan → an auto-written executive narrative summarizes the operation → Dale reviews, redacts optional fields, and exports or shares a secure link.
     AI role: A narrative generator writes a professional, underwriter-toned executive summary and per-category commentary from the structured data.
     Outcome: Dale hands his agent a standardized, professional risk packet that pre-qualifies his application and reduces information requests.
  8. Renewal Timeline Tracker
     Trigger: Dale sets his renewal date during onboarding.
     Flow: A countdown timeline shows key milestones (assessment complete, mitigations started, report finalized, agent submitted) → reminders nudge Dale toward incomplete high-impact items → completed mitigations update his live score → the timeline shows whether he's on pace to hit his target score.
     AI role: A pacing model recommends which actions to complete each week to reach the target score by renewal, adjusting as items are checked off.
     Outcome: Dale stays on track across weeks without dropping the ball on the improvements that matter most before renewal.
  9. Agent Collaboration Handoff
     Trigger: Dale invites his agent Marcy to review the assessment.
     Flow: Marcy receives a scoped view of the profile and report → she can comment on specific findings, suggest additional attestations, and export carrier-specific views → Dale sees her notes inline → both track the shared action plan.
     AI role: The assistant translates carrier-specific requirements Marcy selects into additional profile prompts for Dale, keeping the packet aligned to real carrier appetites.
     Outcome: Dale and Marcy work the renewal from a single source of truth instead of trading emails and PDFs.
  10. Year-Over-Year Progress Comparison
     Trigger: Dale returns for his next renewal cycle with a prior assessment on file.
     Flow: The tool clones last year's profile → prompts only for what's changed → recomputes the score → shows a side-by-side of last year vs this year across every category → highlights which completed mitigations delivered score gains.
     AI role: A diff-and-attribution pass identifies which changes drove score movement and confirms which past recommendations paid off.
     Outcome: Dale sees measurable proof his improvements worked, reinforcing trust and making next year's renewal a 10-minute refresh.
