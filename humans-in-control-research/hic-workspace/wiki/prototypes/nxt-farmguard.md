## Concept
=== APPROVED PRODUCT BRIEF (the user picked this from a 5-brief set; build EXACTLY this) ===

Frame: convention-honoring (probability 0.4)
Title: NXT FarmGuard
Thesis: A guided, always-on risk planning workspace that turns a static farm risk document into a living, prioritized, continuously updated action plan.
Why this frame: This is what a top-tier ag-insurtech designer would build: a structured risk assessment wizard plus a maintained plan dashboard. It maps cleanly to how farmers and underwriters already think about risk.

PRIMARY PERSONA:
  Name: Dale Hutchins
  Role: Owner-operator of a 900-acre corn and soy operation
  Context: Third-generation farmer in central Iowa managing threats reactively; deals with weather volatility, equipment breakdowns, commodity price swings, and a single lender relationship. Has never formalized a written risk plan though his insurer keeps recommending one.
  Emotional state: Quietly anxious but skeptical of paperwork; wants control but distrusts anything that feels like a bureaucratic form that won't help when a real crisis hits.
  Pain points:
    - Risk lives in his head, not on paper, so nothing gets prioritized or acted on until it becomes an emergency
    - Existing risk 'plans' are static PDFs that go stale the moment weather, markets, or equipment change
    - No clear sense of which of dozens of worries actually matter most this season
    - When disaster strikes he improvises recovery instead of executing a rehearsed playbook, losing days and dollars
  JTBD: When the season quiets down, I want to turn my scattered worries into a ranked, actionable plan that stays current so my farm recovers fast when something goes wrong.
SECONDARY ACTORS:
  - Farm insurance underwriter: Consumes the exportable risk profile to price coverage and validate mitigation; nudges farmers toward completing plans
  - Spouse / farm co-manager (Rita): Shares the plan, gets assigned tasks, and receives event alerts when Dale is in the field
  - Ag extension advisor: Reviews prioritized risks, offers expert commentary, and validates mitigation strategies against regional best practices
  - Agricultural lender / loan officer: Views recovery readiness score as a signal of operational resilience during loan review
  - Seasonal farmhand: Receives specific mitigation tasks with deadlines during peak periods

DEMONSTRATED MOMENT: Dale completes a guided risk assessment during winter downtime, sees his top five prioritized risks rendered as a heat map, and accepts three AI-suggested mitigation actions each with an owner and a deadline he actually commits to.

USE CASES (10) - the prototype MUST demonstrate at least 5 of these as live flows:
  1. Guided farm risk self-assessment
     Trigger: Dale opens FarmGuard during winter downtime and starts a new assessment, or the app prompts him at season's end.
     Flow: He answers a conversational interview about operation type, acreage, crops, equipment age, financing, water, labor, and past losses → the wizard branches based on his answers → he can skip, revisit, or answer by voice → a progress ring fills as sections complete → on finish he reviews an auto-generated risk inventory before scoring.
     AI role: A natural-language interview agent adapts follow-up questions to Dale's answers, infers unstated risks (e.g., single-lender concentration) from operation shape, and pre-populates fields from prior seasons.
     Outcome: A complete, structured risk inventory of 20-40 candidate risks built in under 25 minutes without a blank-form staring contest.
  2. AI risk prioritization and scoring
     Trigger: Assessment completes, or Dale taps 'Re-score' after conditions change.
     Flow: FarmGuard scores each risk on likelihood and severity → renders a 5x5 heat map → surfaces the top five as a ranked list with plain-language rationale → Dale can override a score with a slider and see the ranking recompute live.
     AI role: A scoring model weights Dale's inputs against regional loss data and farm-shape benchmarks to compute likelihood x severity, and explains each score in one sentence.
     Outcome: Dozens of vague worries collapse into five ranked priorities Dale can actually reason about and defend to his lender.
  3. Mitigation strategy recommendations
     Trigger: Dale taps a prioritized risk to see what to do about it.
     Flow: For the selected risk, FarmGuard proposes 2-4 mitigation strategies with cost/effort/impact tags → Dale compares them side by side → he accepts, tweaks, or dismisses each → accepted strategies become draft plan items.
     AI role: A recommendation engine matches the risk profile to a library of proven mitigations, personalizes by budget and operation size, and ranks by projected impact-per-dollar.
     Outcome: Every top risk gets concrete, farm-specific options instead of generic advice, so Dale knows exactly what he could do.
  4. Actionable plan builder with tasks
     Trigger: Dale accepts one or more mitigation strategies.
     Flow: Each strategy expands into discrete tasks → FarmGuard suggests owners (Dale, Rita, farmhand), deadlines aligned to the seasonal calendar, and dependencies → Dale confirms or edits → tasks land on a tracker and calendar with reminders.
     AI role: AI decomposes strategies into ordered tasks, proposes realistic deadlines against the crop calendar and weather windows, and flags conflicts.
     Outcome: Abstract intentions become a scheduled, owned, trackable to-do list that actually gets executed.
  5. Seasonal plan review and refresh
     Trigger: Season changes, or FarmGuard detects the plan has drifted from reality.
     Flow: A refresh prompt summarizes what changed → Dale reviews stale items, completed tasks, and newly relevant risks → he approves a re-score → the plan updates and versions itself.
     AI role: Plan drift detection compares current farm state, weather, and market signals against the last assessment and flags exactly what needs revisiting.
     Outcome: The plan stays alive across seasons instead of going stale in a drawer.
  6. Weather and market event alerts
     Trigger: A monitored external event crosses a threshold (severe weather forecast, commodity price move, input cost spike).
     Flow: FarmGuard pushes an alert tied to a specific plan risk → shows which mitigation tasks to accelerate → Dale taps to reprioritize or activate a playbook → affected tasks resurface with new urgency.
     AI role: An event-monitoring agent maps incoming signals to Dale's specific risks and recommends which plan items to act on now.
     Outcome: External threats trigger the right pre-planned response instead of scrambling reaction.
  7. Recovery playbook activation
     Trigger: A disruptive event actually occurs (hail, flood, equipment failure, price collapse).
     Flow: Dale taps 'Activate recovery' for the affected risk → FarmGuard presents a step-by-step recovery checklist (document damage, contact insurer, salvage actions, financing options) → tracks completion and timestamps → generates a claim-ready record.
     AI role: AI assembles a personalized recovery playbook from the pre-built mitigation plan plus event-specific guidance, and drafts insurer-ready documentation.
     Outcome: Dale executes a rehearsed recovery in hours, not days, and has documentation ready for his claim.
  8. Underwriting-ready risk profile export
     Trigger: Dale's insurer or lender requests his risk posture, or renewal season arrives.
     Flow: Dale selects export → chooses recipient (underwriter, lender, advisor) → FarmGuard generates a formatted profile with risk scores, active mitigations, task completion rate, and recovery readiness score → shares via secure link or PDF.
     AI role: AI summarizes the plan into an underwriter-friendly narrative and computes a recovery readiness benchmark against comparable farms.
     Outcome: Dale's proactive planning becomes a credential that can lower premiums and strengthen loan standing.
  9. Recovery readiness benchmarking
     Trigger: Dale opens his farm risk profile summary.
     Flow: FarmGuard computes a readiness score → shows how he compares to similar operations → highlights the biggest gaps → suggests the two moves that would raise the score most.
     AI role: A benchmarking model positions Dale against anonymized peer cohorts and identifies highest-leverage improvements.
     Outcome: Dale sees resilience as a measurable, improvable number and knows his fastest path to raise it.
  10. Voice-driven field capture
     Trigger: Dale spots a new risk while in the field (a failing tile drain, a fence gap).
     Flow: He taps the mic and speaks a quick note → FarmGuard transcribes, classifies it against his risk taxonomy, and drafts a candidate risk → later he reviews and confirms it into the assessment.
     AI role: Speech-to-text plus classification converts a spoken observation into a structured, scorable risk entry.
     Outcome: Risks get captured the moment they're noticed instead of forgotten by the time Dale is back at a desk.
