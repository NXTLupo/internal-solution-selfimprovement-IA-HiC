HUMANS IN CONTROL

A research workbench that runs the full arc of a consulting engagement: gather evidence, synthesize it, and turn it into board-ready deliverables and working experience prototypes. The machine does the labor. You stay in control at every checkpoint.

01What Humans in Control does

Humans in Control (HiC) compresses a multi-week consulting engagement into a single guided session. You give it a goal and a few source documents. It assembles a research corpus from your uploads plus a curated external evidence base, synthesizes that corpus into named themes, and then lets you generate any consulting artifact on demand by typing a hashtag in the Analyst Desk chat. The same corpus that powers a strategy deck also powers a clickable, on-brand experience prototype.

Every output is grounded in real, cited evidence. Every output carries the NXT Humans or Northeastern University design system. Nothing ships without passing a defense-in-depth quality layer that strips AI slop, resolves citations to named sources, and enforces the consulting frameworks a senior partner expects.

The one-sentence version

Research goes in, a cited corpus is built and synthesized, and any deliverable, from a strategy deck to a live prototype, comes out on demand, on brand, and grounded in evidence, while the system learns from your edits.

02Why it matters: end-to-end consulting automation

Traditional consulting work is a relay of specialists: researchers gather, analysts synthesize, strategists frame, designers prototype, and editors enforce quality. HiC runs that whole relay as one continuous pipeline, with you as the single decision-maker.

Research to deliverable in one session
No handoffs. The corpus the research stage builds is the same corpus every deliverable cites. Themes extracted once are reused across the deck, the personas, the blueprint, and the prototype.
Evidence you can defend
Every claim resolves to a named source ("Higher Ed Dive", "Reddit r/cscareerquestions", "OpenAlex: Smith 2024"). At least 60% of cited sources are external, not your own uploads.
On brand by construction
Colors, type, logo, spacing, and a 294-archetype component library are pulled live from the Figma design system at generation time. Output is NXT or Northeastern branded without manual styling.
Research becomes a working product
The same insight that fills a slide can be rendered as a clickable, multi-screen experience prototype with real AI behavior inside it, not a static mockup.
It gets better with use
Your edits at checkpoints are logged. After repeated corrections the system promotes a constraint and injects it into every future generation. The workbench learns your standards.
Humans in control
Checkpoints let you edit, approve, or reject at each stage. The AI proposes; you decide. Nothing is autopilot.

03Operator quickstart

  1. State a goal. On the launch screen, write a research goal in the goal box (for example, "Identify unmet co-op program needs for Northeastern undergraduates over the next 24 months, focused on employer-side friction and student-side decision points"). Use Suggest from goal or Enhance to sharpen it.
  2. Add evidence (optional). Upload client documents (PDF, DOCX, TXT) and paste any source URLs. These become the uploaded-document layer of the corpus.
  3. Pick sources. Leave the run in fresh mode to query the external evidence base, or use Curate to select a knowledge-library (KL) subset.
  4. Run Research Analysis. HiC builds the corpus, ingests and chunks sources, and extracts themes. Review, edit, approve, or reject at each checkpoint.
  5. Open the Analyst Desk and generate. Type a hashtag deliverable command (see section 05). For example #slides, #journey, #personas, or #proto a co-op match dashboard #NU.
  6. Export. Push any deck to PPTX or PDF; prototypes run live in the browser and can be revised in chat.

Two front doors

Research Analysis opens the Analyst Desk, the corpus-grounded chat where hashtag deliverables and experience prototypes are generated. Design Desk is a lighter designer chat for freeform brand documents (briefs, explainers, reports). UX Heuristic Analysis runs a tri-model audit of an interface brief.

04The end-to-end pipeline

This is the spine of the product. Data flows top to bottom; the corpus and its themes are reused by every downstream generator.

Goal & intake
A structured research goal plus optional client uploads and source URLs define the engagement. Engagement memory checks for prior runs on similar goals and loads what was learned.
Data collection
The research fabric fans out across 200+ external sources (academic, news, government, social, video) through eight connector types, plus your uploaded documents. A connector-health ledger reroutes around failing sources.
Ingestion & chunking
Each source is parsed to plain text, content-hash deduplicated, and split into retrievable chunks with stable IDs. Chunks are written into a manifest so deliverables can ground on specific passages rather than bulk text.
Synthesis & theme extraction
A stateful multi-turn analysis pass clusters the corpus into named themes with supporting evidence and verbatim quotes. You edit, approve, or reject themes at a checkpoint. Themes become the shared backbone for every deliverable.
Analyst Desk deliverable generation
You type a hashtag. The generator retrieves the relevant manifest chunks, loads the matching consulting framework and brand standards, generates the artifact, then runs it through the post-processor quality layer.
Experience prototypes
#proto runs a five-stage flow that turns the concept into a clickable, on-brand, multi-screen prototype composed from the design-system component library, with real AI calls wired inside it.
Export & persistence
Decks export to PPTX and PDF; prototypes run live and persist for revision. New artifacts and ingested articles are written back to the knowledge base.
Self-improvement loop
Your checkpoint edits are logged. Recurring corrections become learned constraints injected into future prompts. The skill library is mined offline and improved. The next session starts smarter.

Data collection

The research fabric aggregates evidence from two categories. Uploaded client documents (a handful of PDFs you provide) supply organizational context. The external evidence base (roughly 200 articles from academic, news, government, and social sources) is the primary evidence. The system deliberately weights external sources heavily: a deliverable dominated by your own uploads is treated as a failure. Connectors cover REST, RSS, CKAN, Socrata, arXiv, OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Reddit, YouTube transcription, and more. A connector-health ledger tracks which sources are responding and routes around the rest.

Synthesis & theme extraction

Synthesis is where raw chunks become an argument. The analysis pass groups the corpus into themes, each anchored to named sources and verbatim quotes, and surfaces convergence where multiple independent sources agree. Themes are the only auto-synthesized layer; every other deliverable grounds on the manifest chunks rather than re-injecting raw text, which is what keeps output from going hollow. You review themes at a checkpoint and steer the synthesis before any deliverable is built on top of it.

The Analyst Desk

The Analyst Desk is the corpus-grounded chat at the center of the workbench. Plain questions get answered as corpus Q&A with citations. A hashtag command triggers a full deliverable build. Before generation, the system assembles a grounding context: the relevant manifest chunks, the consulting framework for that deliverable type, the brand standards, learned constraints, and engagement memory. The model generates against that context, and the output passes through the post-processor before you see it.

The self-improvement engine

HiC is designed to compound. Four mechanisms feed the loop:

  • Edit learning. Edits you make at checkpoints are logged. After several similar corrections, a constraint is promoted and stored in the learned-constraints ledger, then injected into every subsequent generation prompt.
  • Engagement memory. Prior runs on similar goals are matched and loaded so the system builds on earlier insight instead of repeating analysis.
  • Manifest-chunk retrieval. Deliverables ground on specific cited chunks, and on-demand outputs are persisted back into the manifest so they are retrievable next time.
  • Offline skill optimization. A training pipeline mines past sessions, proposes edits to the agent skill library, gates them through an independent validator, and promotes the approved ones. Skills get better between sessions, not worse.

05The hashtag deliverable system

06How to generate each deliverable

Decks and analysis artifacts

  1. Complete a Research Analysis run so a corpus and themes exist.
  2. In the Analyst Desk, type the hashtag plus any direction, for example #journey the first-year student co-op search or #competitive #NU.
  3. The deliverable renders as preview slides. Review the action titles, citations, and evidence.
  4. Edit or refine in chat. Add #dense if the slide should carry more detail.
  5. Export to PPTX or PDF when satisfied.

Documents (brief, explainer, report)

  1. Open Design Desk.
  2. Type #brief, #explainer, or #report with your topic and a brand tag.
  3. The designer chat emits an on-brand HTML document you can iterate on.

Corpus questions

Type a plain question (no hashtag) in the Analyst Desk to query the corpus. Answers cite named sources. Fire two or three angle variants of a broad question to reach different parts of the evidence base.

07Experience prototypes (#proto)

This is what separates HiC from a slide generator: the same research becomes a working, clickable product. Type #proto a co-op match dashboard #NU and the system runs a five-stage pipeline:

Concept decomposer
Turns your one-line concept into a structured experience brief: persona, jobs-to-be-done, the demonstrated moment, success criteria.
Interaction architect
Designs the screens and transitions, and identifies where real AI inference is the value.
Visual designer
Applies the brand and selects the relevant patterns from the design-system component manifest. The build is catalog-aware: it composes from real components and does not invent UI shapes.
Prototype coder
Writes the full multi-screen React prototype. AI features call the proxy live; structured AI responses parse defensively and degrade gracefully; every loading state animates a catalog pattern (skeleton, token stream, progress, spinner).
Critic & refiner
Audits the prototype against a depth, accessibility, and brand checklist, then fixes blocking issues before it ships.

The result is a runnable prototype with a screen switcher, real seed data, and live AI behavior. It persists and can be revised by chatting (for example, "make the match cards show a skill-gap bar"). Prototypes are brand-correct: the real Northeastern N monogram or NXT Humans wordmark, brand colors, type, and the responsive breakpoint grid are pulled from the design system at generation time.

Grounded, not guessed

The prototype is composed from a library of 294 named design-system component archetypes per brand, spanning AI-chat, navigation, data display, charts, forms, feedback, overlays, AI-transparency, commerce, compliance, accessibility, and interaction states. The model reads each component's exact structure and token bindings from the manifest and builds to match.

08The design system & component manifest

Generation is grounded in a living design system maintained in Figma, mirrored to the workbench at runtime.

  • Brand tokens. Colors, fonts, spacing, and radius are served live from the Figma token sheet. Generators never hardcode brand values; they read the current tokens at build time.
  • Brand logos. The official Northeastern N monogram and the NXT Humans wordmark are sourced from Figma and rendered in every masthead. A logo is never invented.
  • Component manifest. A 294-archetype component library per brand is chunked into a manifest the prototype pipeline reads at generation time, so prototypes compose from real, on-brand components with exact token bindings.
  • Responsive breakpoint grids. A mobile-first grid system (xs 360, sm 480, md 768, lg 1024, xl 1280, 2xl 1440, with columns, margins, and gutters per breakpoint) is part of the design system and is injected into prototype generation, so output is responsive from mobile to widescreen.

09Export

  • PPTX. Decks export to PowerPoint with the slide templates rendered at full fidelity.
  • PDF. Decks export to PDF for distribution.
  • Live prototypes. Prototypes run in the browser at their own URL and persist for revision; no export step is needed to share or demo them.
  • Presentations. #presentation output is handed to a scroll-driven presentation renderer.

11Quality & guardrails

Every artifact passes a defense-in-depth quality layer that the prompt alone cannot bypass:

  • Citation protocol. Chunk references resolve to named sources. Generic references ("research", "studies", "data") are rejected.
  • Evidence hierarchy. Lead with external evidence, corroborate with more external sources, then contextualize with client documents. At least 60% of cited sources are external.
  • Quantitative-claim rule. Numbers appear only inside a cited quote, never as a bare assertion in a title or tile.
  • Banned phrases. 350+ slop phrases and AI-writing tells are corrected automatically. Meta-narration, process references, and knowledge-cutoff disclaimers are stripped.
  • Action titles. Every slide title is a complete-sentence conclusion a partner can read on its own.
  • Consulting frameworks. Minto pyramid, Rumelt's kernel, and SCQA are enforced where the deliverable calls for them.
  • Format hygiene. No em-dashes, no markdown in content fields, no fabricated authors or statistics, no QA annotations in user-facing text.

Beyond the runtime layer, an independent subagent fleet audits deliverables (citation diversity, evidence quality, schema drift), and quality-gate hooks block a generation that violates a standing rule.

12Model routing & resilience

Model selection is centralized so call sites never hardcode a model. The canonical rule: plain (non-hashtag) Analyst Desk chat runs on Gemini 3.5 Flash, and every hashtag deliverable — including the #proto build pipeline's generation stages — runs on Claude Opus 4.8. A resilience layer adds a content-hash embedding cache, a per-provider health tracker, per-corpus provider commitment, and a cross-provider fallback (Opus, then Gemini) so a single provider outage does not kill a generation; the build path never falls back to GPT-5.5.

13Operations & troubleshooting

  • A change is not visible. The app is served as a static build. After a code change the container is rebuilt and the browser hard-refreshed. If a result looks stale, a rebuild was likely missed.
  • An external source is slow. Large uploads are parsed synchronously; very large PDFs can take up to a minute. The connector-health ledger routes around failing external sources automatically.
  • A deliverable looks thin. Confirm a Research Analysis run completed and themes were approved. Deliverables ground on the corpus; an empty corpus yields a thin artifact.
  • Branding looks off. Confirm the brand tag (#NXT / #NU) and that the design-system token feed is reachable; brand values are pulled live, not hardcoded.
