NXT HUMANS PERSONA

Co-op AI Strategy, Northeastern University

Four stakeholders whose unmet needs define where AI must act first

Declining placement rates, fragmented digital touchpoints, and a competitive labor market converge on four distinct humans whose goals, frictions, and expectations must shape any AI roadmap for co-op.

MA
Maya Andrade
The Anxious First-Placer
Undergraduate, Year 2 First Co-op Cycle EVIDENCED
Job To Be Done
When preparing for my first co-op, I want to know exactly where my profile falls short, so I can fix gaps before employers see me.
Goals
Life Goal Become a professional who earns her place through demonstrated competence, not luck or connections.
Experience Goal Feel genuinely prepared and not blindsided when she walks into a recruiter conversation.
Functional Goal Secure a first co-op placement in her target field before the application window closes.
Behaviors
Iterates on her resume repeatedly but lacks a benchmark to know when it is strong enough to submit.
Navigates multiple disconnected university portals to find job listings, advising slots, and skill resources.
Seeks informal guidance from older students because she cannot get timely access to a co-op advisor.
Applies broadly rather than strategically because she has no signal about which roles match her actual readiness level.
Pains
No real-time feedback on whether her professional profile is competitive relative to peers who landed placements.
The student digital ecosystem is fragmented, forcing her to context-switch across tools that do not share data.
Placement rates have dropped in competitive markets, raising the stakes of every application decision she makes.
Needs
An AI-driven preparedness assessment that tells her specifically what to fix, not just that she needs to improve.
A unified digital experience that surfaces relevant job matches, skill gaps, and advisor availability in one place.
Peer connection pathways to students who completed similar co-ops, so she can calibrate expectations with real experience.

DK
Daniel Kwon
The Strategic Returner
Undergraduate, Year 3-4 Second or Third Co-op EVIDENCED
Job To Be Done
When targeting a high-stakes second placement, I want AI to surface roles that match my evolving profile, so I can convert co-op into a full-time offer.
Goals
Life Goal Build a career trajectory that is intentional and differentiated, not just a sequence of jobs he stumbled into.
Experience Goal Feel like the university's systems are working for him, not creating friction he has to work around.
Functional Goal Land a placement at a target employer that aligns with his post-graduation career plan.
Behaviors
Researches employer reputations and return-offer rates independently because the university provides no structured signal on employer quality.
Tracks his own skill development across co-ops but has no institutional tool to surface that growth to employers.
Uses AI tools outside the university ecosystem to draft application materials, creating a parallel workflow the institution cannot see or support.
Actively networks with alumni and prior co-op supervisors to compensate for limited employer relationship data from the university.
Pains
Commercially available AI tools are disconnected and fragmented, so he stitches together his own stack rather than using university resources.
His learner profile is not rich enough to power personalized employer matching, leaving him to rely on keyword searches.
Competitive job markets have reduced placement rates, raising the cost of a misaligned application strategy.
Needs
A dynamic learner profile that accumulates evidence of his skills across co-ops and surfaces it to matched employers automatically.
AI-powered employer matching that goes beyond job title to surface roles where his specific competency trajectory is valued.
Transparent data on employer return-offer rates and co-op quality so he can make informed targeting decisions.

RP
Rachel Patel
The Overloaded Co-op Advisor
University Staff Co-op Coordinator EVIDENCED
Job To Be Done
When managing a high student caseload, I want AI to handle routine readiness assessments, so I can focus advising time on students who need human judgment.
Goals
Life Goal Be the advisor who genuinely changed the trajectory of students who needed real guidance, not just process management.
Experience Goal Feel like her expertise is being used for complex human problems, not consumed by repetitive administrative tasks.
Functional Goal Increase the placement rate across her student cohort while maintaining quality of employer relationships.
Behaviors
Manually reviews student resumes and profiles before advising sessions, a task she recognizes could be pre-processed by AI.
Tracks employer satisfaction and placement outcomes in spreadsheets because institutional data systems do not surface this automatically.
Spends significant advising time answering the same preparedness questions from students who could be served by a self-service AI tool.
Relies on institutional memory and personal relationships with employers rather than data-driven employer matching tools.
Pains
Placement rates have dropped considerably, creating institutional pressure she absorbs directly without better tools to diagnose root causes.
Student data is siloed across disconnected systems, preventing her from seeing a complete picture of any student's readiness before a meeting.
High caseloads mean she cannot provide the depth of personalized guidance that would meaningfully move placement outcomes.
Needs
An AI prep advisor that handles routine profile assessment and readiness scoring before students reach her, so sessions focus on strategy.
A unified dashboard that surfaces student progress, employer satisfaction signals, and placement risk indicators in one view.
Employer relationship intelligence that helps her prioritize outreach to partners most likely to place her current cohort.

TL
Thomas Liang
The Employer Relationship Manager
University Staff Employer Partnerships PARTIALLY EVIDENCED
Job To Be Done
When an employer posts a co-op role, I want to surface the most qualified matched candidates instantly, so I can protect Northeastern's employer relationships.
Goals
Life Goal Make Northeastern the university that employers call first because the quality and fit of its co-op candidates is consistently superior.
Experience Goal Feel confident presenting candidates to employers, knowing the match is grounded in data rather than intuition.
Functional Goal Reduce the time from employer posting to qualified candidate shortlist, improving employer satisfaction scores.
Behaviors
Manually curates candidate shortlists for key employer partners because automated matching tools do not account for relationship history or employer culture fit.
Tracks employer satisfaction informally through relationship calls rather than structured feedback loops that could inform AI training data.
Advocates internally for employer-facing features but finds that product decisions prioritize student-facing tools, leaving employer experience underserved.
Monitors placement rate trends as a proxy for employer satisfaction, knowing that a drop in placements signals a deteriorating employer relationship before formal feedback arrives.
Pains
Placement rate declines create reputational risk with employer partners that he must manage without real-time diagnostic data.
Employer satisfaction data is not systematically captured, making it impossible to identify which employer relationships are at risk before they deteriorate.
Student learner profiles are not rich enough to power the kind of precise employer matching that would differentiate Northeastern from competing universities.
Needs
An AI-powered employer matching engine that uses rich learner profiles and ML clustering to surface best-fit candidates for each role automatically.
Structured employer satisfaction feedback loops that feed into placement quality metrics and inform future matching logic.
Proactive relationship intelligence that flags employer partners showing declining engagement before placement rates reflect the problem.

Research Context

Five research themes anchor every persona to institutional evidence

Interviews, market research, and technology research surfaced five converging themes that define the current state of co-op and the opportunity space for AI.

Foundational Themes

Theme 1
Connect Student Journey

The student digital ecosystem is fragmented and causing frustration across every touchpoint from discovery to placement.

Theme 2
Build Learner Profiles

Core to personalization is a rich, dynamic learner profile that accumulates evidence of skills and experience over time.

Theme 3
Improve Co-op Placement

Placement rates have dropped in competitive job climates and markets, creating measurable institutional risk.

Theme 4
Personalize at Scale

Personalized experiences succeed when using dynamic cohorting and ML clustering models across the student population.

Theme 5
Build Student Connections

Students feel more prepared when connecting with others who have completed similar co-ops before them.

Key Institutional Signals

Placement rate decline is the primary institutional KPI driving the AI roadmap. Stakeholders named it explicitly as the metric that must move.
Fragmented tooling is the root cause of student frustration. Dozens of commercially available AI tools exist but are disconnected from each other and from institutional data.
Agentic AI is identified as the next wave of the AI economy. The roadmap positions Northeastern to lead by deploying agents across the student journey.
Multimodal and immersive AI is flagged as superior for education, learning, and AI reasoning, pointing toward richer interaction models than text-only interfaces.

Priority AI Application: Prep Advisor

Option 1, Drive Student Success
AI-Driven Preparedness Assessment
The roadmap identifies a Prep Advisor as a priority application: an AI-driven tool that assesses students' professional profile and co-op preparedness to recommend experiential learning and resources to optimize their resume and readiness. This directly addresses Maya's core pain and Rachel's capacity constraint simultaneously.

Shared Infrastructure Opportunities

Unified Learner Profile
The data layer all four personas need
Maya needs it to understand her gaps. Daniel needs it to surface his growth. Rachel needs it to prepare for advising. Thomas needs it to match candidates to employers. A rich, dynamic learner profile is the shared infrastructure that unlocks every downstream AI capability.
Connected Student Journey
Eliminating the fragmentation tax
Every persona pays a fragmentation tax today. A unified digital experience that connects job listings, skill resources, advisor availability, and employer data into one coherent journey removes the single largest source of friction across all four stakeholder groups.
Peer Connection Engine
Structured peer knowledge transfer
Students feel more prepared when connecting with others who have done it before. An AI-powered peer matching system that connects first-placers like Maya with returners like Daniel creates a knowledge transfer loop that scales without advisor bandwidth.

North Star Framing

AI that serves all four personas converges on one outcome: placement rates that reflect Northeastern's actual competitive advantage

The roadmap's stated objective is to distinguish Northeastern as the leader in experiential learning. That distinction is only credible when the placement metric moves. Every AI investment that does not trace back to a persona's unmet need and a measurable placement outcome is a distraction from that goal.

Sequencing Logic

Build the learner profile infrastructure first. Without rich, dynamic student data, no downstream AI capability (matching, prep advising, peer connection) can function at the quality level the roadmap envisions. This is the prerequisite that unlocks everything else.
Deploy the AI Prep Advisor as the first student-facing product. It addresses Maya's highest-urgency need, reduces Rachel's caseload burden immediately, and generates the behavioral data that improves matching quality for Daniel and Thomas over time.
Activate employer-facing matching intelligence. Once learner profiles are rich enough to power ML clustering, Thomas can surface precise candidate shortlists to employer partners, directly addressing the placement rate decline that motivated the entire roadmap.
Layer in peer connection and multimodal experiences. Structured peer matching and immersive AI interactions address the preparation and belonging needs that placement mechanics alone cannot solve, completing the north star vision.
