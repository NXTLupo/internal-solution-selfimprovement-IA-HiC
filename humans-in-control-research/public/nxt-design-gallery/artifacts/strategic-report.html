Executive summary

SCR · 248 words

Situation

Northeastern operates the largest co-op program in any R1 institution — 9,400 placements per year against a 3,300-employer partner base — while running a $1.6B capital campaign at 78% of target. The two engines run in adjacent rooms.

Complication

The cross-organizational signal — placement outcome → donor narrative → renewed gift — runs through 15 disconnected systems with an 11-day lag. Drexel, at half the placement volume, runs the same loop in 5 days. Young-alumni giving fell 12% even as overall fundraising grew 3%, surfacing a long-term donor-pipeline risk the aggregate metrics hide.

Resolution

Three coherent moves, sequenced over 12 months: (1) ship explainable triage for officer and coordinator queues at day 5; (2) compress the placement-to-donor handoff from 11 days to under 72 hours; (3) formalize the peer-mentor scaffolding three Boston dorms already invented. Funded from sunset of two satellite CRMs — no net-new SaaS.

§ 01

Diagnosis

Rumelt's kernel · part 1 Name the central challenge. Be specific about the root cause and quantify the impact.

The advancement function and the experiential-learning function share a constituent — every alumnus is a former co-op student — but their data spines do not share a backbone. The result is friction at the points where the two flywheels could compound.

Across 24 interviews and 32 internal documents, the same friction surfaces in three different costumes. Major gift officers spend roughly forty minutes preparing for a single coffee, cross-referencing Raiser's Edge, iWave, and student-affinity notes pulled from desk stickies and Slack DMs.1 Co-op coordinators carry 180:1 advising loads and discover application dropouts after the cycle closes.2 First-years route their most-urgent questions to upperclassmen at 11 p.m. on Reddit, three trust steps removed from the system they are nominally enrolled in.3

Each office has built a private workaround. None of them connect. The cost is not invisibility — the cost is the eleven-day lag between a placement event and the donor-side cultivation that should follow it. Drexel, at half the placement volume, completes the same loop in five days. Cincinnati flags inactive students at day 5 and routes them to a triage queue; Northeastern fires an untargeted template email at day 7 and waits.

The aggregate number is hiding the cliff under it. Young alumni giving fell twelve points while the total grew three. We are funding next decade's pipeline by accident. D. Chen · VP, Advancement Operations · advisory board read-out, 2026-01-22

Young-alumni giving change against +3% on the aggregate line. The cohort masks itself.

The diagnosis is not that the systems are broken. The systems run. The diagnosis is that three load-bearing patterns — officer prep, coordinator triage, first-year peer trust — operate as informal habits in different offices and do not connect to each other. Each is evidence of capacity. None of them is a system. The opportunity is to codify what already works, not to invent something new.

§ 02

Guiding policy

Rumelt's kernel · part 2 A trade-off, not a goal. State what we choose, and what we de-prioritize to do it.

We will instrument the connective tissue between experiential learning and advancement, sequenced by the fastest path to recovered officer and coordinator time — choosing the connective tissue over net-new platforms, and triage over recommender accuracy as the v1 surface for AI.

What we choose

Connective tissue between the two existing flywheels. Explainable triage as the first AI surface. Codification of three informal patterns already producing engagement lift in three Boston dorms.

What we choose against

Net-new SaaS platforms. Recommender accuracy as the v1 win. A second donor portal. A new mobile app build before mobile-web parity. Anything that requires General Counsel to invent a new privacy posture.

How we'll know it's working

Officer prep time drops from 40 min to under 10 by FY27 Q2. Placement-to-donor lag compresses from 11 days to under 72 hours by FY27 Q4. First-year application volume rises from 4 to 12+ per student in cycle 1.

The guiding policy resists two predictable pressures. First, the temptation to procure a new AI platform when the rate-limiter is trust, not capability — explainability ships before any new model deploys. Second, the temptation to treat young-alumni giving as a marketing problem when the cohort signal points to a stewardship problem the operations data could already solve.

If you can't tell me what dies, the answer is no. D. Chen · VP, Advancement Operations · sponsor framing for FY27 planning

Every initiative in the next section names the platform it retires or the manual workflow it replaces. The budget posture is re-allocation, not expansion. Two satellite CRMs and one legacy donor-events platform absorb the cost of the FY27 program.4

§ 03

Strategic pillars

Three MECE themes Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive. Every recommendation in §04 traces to one of these.

Three pillars organize the work. Each has a named owner, a leading and lagging indicator, and a sunset target. Pillars are deliberately MECE — no initiative belongs to more than one.

Connective tissue · placement ⇌ advancement

The data path between an outcome event and a stewardship action. Owns the 11→3-day compression and the cross-record identity layer.

Triage as the v1 AI surface

Who needs the human now? Silence-detection for students, prospect-readiness for officers — both explainable, both routed to a queue, not auto-actioned.

Codify the peer-mentor pattern

Pay the mentors, instrument the touchpoints, set a one-cycle outcome target, scale or retire on evidence. Lowest-cost lift in the portfolio.

Privacy & FERPA discipline

Aggregated signals only between the two flywheels. General Counsel sign-off on the data model in 30 days; written guardrails replace gray-zone defaults.

The remainder of this report (§04–§12) maps each pillar to specific recommendations, success metrics, an investment case, an implementation roadmap, and a risk register. Each section is 300–600 words and self-contained — partners can pull any single section into a stakeholder conversation without losing context.
