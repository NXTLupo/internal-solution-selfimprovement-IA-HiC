SECTION 01

CREATIVE BRIEF

This design system governs every visual output produced by the Humans in Control research platform. It exists to ensure that AI-generated consulting deliverables are indistinguishable from work produced by a top-tier design consultancy — and instantly distinguishable from the generic, soulless output that characterizes most AI-generated content.

THE PROBLEM WE'RE SOLVING

AI-generated visual output has a recognizable aesthetic: Inter or Roboto typefaces, purple-on-white gradients, rounded card layouts, evenly distributed pastel palettes, decorative gradient blobs, and cookie-cutter component patterns. This aesthetic signals "a computer made this" as clearly as clip art signaled "a non-designer made this" in the 2000s. In consulting, where trust is the product, that signal is fatal. Clients pay for expertise, judgment, and craft. If the deliverable looks automated, the expertise feels automated.

THE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Anti-slop business flat minimalism. This is not a decorative style — it's a discipline. Every element earns its place through function: communicating hierarchy, directing attention, establishing credibility, or making data legible. Nothing exists for decoration. The style is defined as much by what it refuses as by what it employs.

FLAT

No gradients. No drop shadows. No 3D effects. No glow. No skeuomorphism. Solid fills only. Depth is created through layering, overlap, and contrast — never through simulated light sources.

GEOMETRIC

Built from rectangles, circles, triangles, and straight lines. No organic curves, hand-drawn aesthetics, blob shapes, or wavy borders. Every shape has mathematical precision. Corners are sharp (≤4px radius only where functionally required).

VECTOR MINIMAL

The fewest elements possible to communicate the idea. Crisp at any size. No raster textures, no photographic elements mixed with illustrations, no noise/grain overlays. Negative space is a deliberate design element.

THE TONE

Authoritative without being corporate. Precise without being sterile. Bold without being aggressive. The visual tone sits at the intersection of Swiss International Style (grid discipline, typographic hierarchy, functional color) and editorial brutalism (oversized type, stark contrast, content-as-decoration). Think: if Massimo Vignelli designed consulting deliverables for a company that builds AI systems.

THE AUDIENCE

CX directors, VP-level stakeholders, C-suite executives, and product leaders at enterprise organizations. These people have seen thousands of presentations and reports. They have sophisticated visual taste and zero patience for generic design. They associate visual quality with thinking quality — if the deliverable looks lazy, they assume the analysis is lazy.

THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RULE

IF IT LOOKS LIKE AI MADE IT, IT'S WRONG.

Every design decision should be tested against this question: "Would a viewer suspect this was generated by an AI tool?" If the answer is yes — or even maybe — the design has failed. Redesign until the answer is a confident no.

TYPE
SECTION 03

TYPOGRAPHY

Typography is the primary design tool. Not color, not illustration, not layout — type. The system pairs a hyper-condensed display face with a clean humanist workhorse. The contrast between them creates the entire visual personality.

TYPE PAIRING

DISPLAY / HEADLINE

BEBAS NEUE

Fallback stack: Oswald → Impact → sans-serif

Ultra-condensed. Uppercase only. Used for page titles, section heads, stat callouts, sidebar branding, and any text intended to be read at a glance rather than studied. The narrowness is the feature — it allows oversized type to fit where it shouldn't, creating the brand's signature tension between enormity and constraint.

NEVER use Bebas Neue in sentence case. NEVER use it for body text. NEVER use it below 18px. It is a display face — treat it as such.

BODY / READING

Archivo

Fallback stack: Source Sans Pro → Helvetica Neue → sans-serif

Grotesque sans-serif with a wide range of weights (300–700). Optimized for screen readability at body sizes. Used for paragraphs, bullets, table content, captions, and any text intended for sustained reading. Carries the analytical voice of the brand — precise, neutral, trustworthy.

NEVER use Archivo in all-caps for headlines. That role belongs to Bebas Neue. Archivo in all-caps reads as shouting, not as branding.

MONOSPACE

JetBrains Mono — Used for code, CSS variables, technical specifications, file paths, and data labels inside charts. Fallback: Fira Code → SF Mono → monospace.

THE OVERSIZED HEADLINE TREATMENT

The signature NXT Humans typographic device: headlines set so large they crop at the edges of the container. The text is intentionally too big for its box. Characters may be clipped at the right edge, bottom edge, or both. This creates visual tension — the feeling that the content is bigger than the frame containing it. It signals confidence and scale.

Implementation rules:
  • • Container must have overflow: hidden
  • • Headline uses line-height: 0.85–0.92 (tighter than normal)
  • • At minimum 50% of the headline text must be fully visible and legible
  • • Cropping happens at right and/or bottom edges only — never left or top
  • • Use for page titles and section dividers only — never for body-level content
  • • The reader should be able to infer the full text from context even if characters are clipped

Reference: Slides 1, 5, 6, 8, 10, 15, 17 of The Ordinary Personal Shopper Analysis deck

SECTION 04

LAYOUT PATTERNS

Seven codified layout patterns. Every page, slide, and section in a deliverable maps to one of these. They can be combined (a page might have a Section Divider followed by a Split Panel followed by a Stat Row), but each individual section uses exactly one pattern.

PATTERN 1 — THE SPLIT PANEL

The workhorse. Used for any content that pairs analysis with evidence. Left panel (~55%) contains the text: headline, body, bullets, recommendations. Right panel (~45%) contains the evidence: screenshots, charts, data tables, diagrams, or illustrations. A 1px vertical rule in --nxt-rule separates the panels.

KEY FINDINGS

PATTERN 3 — THE RECOMMENDATION CALLOUT

A solid chartreuse block that interrupts the page. Used for the single most important takeaway on any given page. Maximum one per page — if you need two, one of them isn't important enough. Bold headline text, brief supporting sentence. Black text on chartreuse background.

REDESIGN ONBOARDING TO REDUCE TIME-TO-FIRST-VALUE FROM 14 DAYS TO 3 DAYS

Implement progressive disclosure with contextual tooltips. Estimated effort: 2 sprints. Expected impact: 15–20% improvement in 30-day retention. (Source: Forrester TEI, 2025)

PATTERN 4 — THE EVIDENCE BLOCK

Light cyan tint background with a 4px cyan left border. Contains cited data, verbatim quotes, or specific metric callouts. Source attribution is required — always in --text-xs at the bottom-right. This block exists to make evidence a first-class visual element, not a buried footnote.

41% of organizations remain at CX Maturity Level 1 (Initial/Ad Hoc), with only 1% reaching Level 5 (Optimized). The median enterprise has been stuck at Level 2 for three consecutive years, suggesting that incremental improvement approaches are insufficient for meaningful maturity advancement.

Source: Gartner CX Maturity Assessment, 2025

PATTERN 5 — THE STAT ROW

A horizontal row of 2–4 oversized statistics. Each stat has three elements: the number (Bebas Neue, --text-5xl, chartreuse), the label (Archivo, --text-base, secondary gray), and the source (Archivo, --text-xs, muted gray). Used to lead a section with high-impact data before the analysis explains it.

ICONS
SECTION 05

ILLUSTRATION &
ICONOGRAPHY

Every diagram, chart annotation, icon, and visual metaphor follows the flat geometric vector minimal discipline. No exceptions. No "just this once" gradients. No "small" drop shadows. The system is rigorous because rigor is what prevents the slow drift toward AI slop.

ILLUSTRATION RULES

DO — ALWAYS

  • Solid flat fills only — rectangles, circles, triangles, lines
  • ONE accent color per illustration (chartreuse OR cyan, never both)
  • Maximum 3 colors total: black + one gray + one accent
  • Crisp vector edges at any scale
  • Negative space as a deliberate compositional element
  • Geometric arrow heads: small, filled, triangular
  • Straight connecting lines with right-angle turns only
  • 1–2px consistent stroke weight across an entire illustration
  • Labels in Archivo at --text-sm, placed above or right of element
  • Flow direction: left-to-right or top-to-bottom exclusively

DON'T — NEVER

  • Gradients, gradient meshes, or gradient fills of any kind
  • Drop shadows, box shadows, inner shadows, or glow effects
  • 3D effects, isometric views, or perspective transforms
  • Rounded blob shapes, organic curves, or wavy lines
  • Hand-drawn, sketchy, or whiteboard aesthetics
  • Duotone photo treatments or photo-illustration hybrids
  • Icons inside colored circles (the Notion/generic-SaaS trope)
  • Neon glow, lens flare, or light-source simulation
  • Decorative elements that don't communicate information
  • Diagonal flow, curved arrows, or bezier connector lines
  • Raster textures, noise overlays, or grain effects
  • Emoji or emoji-style illustration in professional deliverables

DATA VISUALIZATION

Charts are arguments, not decorations. Every chart must make a specific claim. The title states the conclusion. The visual proves it. The source establishes credibility. If a chart doesn't change someone's mind or confirm a suspicion, it shouldn't exist.

THE PIE CHART BAN

Pie charts are banned from all NXT Humans output. McKinsey bans them. Tufte discourages them. The reason: humans compare lengths accurately but compare angles poorly. A bar chart communicates the same part-to-whole relationship with greater precision. There are zero exceptions to this rule.
