Fair enough. If I had anything critical or sensitive, I probably wouldn't be putting it on such a platform either. But while this is a lab resource for me, I also want to make sure that I can make recommendations for people that wish to harden their personal systems. But your point is well made. I would use a corollary piece of advice: use the right tool for the right job. Is CachyOS a platform that you should spend excessive amounts of time hardening? That depends. If this is your only system, then the answer is most definitely "yes". But if you want security as your primary focus, then you probably should consider using a different platform.   
  
OpenBSD is probably the best platform for security and privacy. Some might quibble and would consider Qubes. For me, I have too much invested in Linux to move to OpenBSD. Nevertheless, I still want to make my daily driver the most secure platform that It can be. So, I've spent time layering all sorts of non-standard (i.e., not installed with the base) options. But it feels like I may be at the end of what I should invest - unless I want to consider more fundamental changes - like OpenBSD or Qubes.
