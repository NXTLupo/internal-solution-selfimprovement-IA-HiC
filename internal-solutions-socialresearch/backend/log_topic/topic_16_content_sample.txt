A simple, non-invasive test that could revolutionize cancer diagnosis  The article was a bit confusing to me. Many screening tests are not fully diagnostic, and may still require some definitive test for confirmation, ie a 'gold standard'. For example, a PSA test (looking for prostate cancer) that is high needs to be confirmed with a prostate biopsy. A low-dose screening CT of the lungs (looking for lung cancer in a smoker) will need a needle biopsy of any detected lesions for confirmation, etc, etc, etc. So the 'screening' test does not replace a "painful" biopsy, it finds patients at high (or higher) risk of harboring a cancer and is followed by a definitive test.  So a 'liquid biopsy' may not replace an old fashioned tissue biopsy (fine needle, core needle, or scalpel) for ultimate diagnosis - that would need to be proven.
