Content 1 (length: 1249 chars):
For the warmer seasons: Basic shorts in blue, khaki, and brown or grey (b/c black is too hot). I try to have 6-8 pairs of shorts. That way we can get through a week of school without having to wash and then have at least 1 extra to send to school for an emergency, with potentially a few extra pairs in case I forgot to do laundry or something happens and you just run through everything. If you see a mostly clean pair one day, I'm happy to "air and wear" after a day, but for most of elementary school this isn't an option at all.  Then for shirts I do about 8-10 [short sleeved] b/c they tend to gather stains and rips faster. I try to make sure I choose colors that will go with any of the shorts or at least half of the shorts. I also will generally buy at least 1 white t-shirt (b/c they always end up doing an event at some point and ask kids to wear a white t-shirt). And I buy 2 school shirts. One for spirit day, one for pictures or fieldtrips. I do keep a stain stick by the front door so when kids come in from school I can stain-stick them immediately and toss shirts into the laundry. [Also I make my kids change out of school clothes after getting home so they don't get dinner on them or get them messy during after-school playtime.]

For winter, I grab about 6 long-sleeve t-shirts to put under regular t-shirts to keep kids warmer. And for even our warmer climate this is all we use most of the winter to keep warm. I have girls so my go-to was leggings and sweat pants b/c leggings are cheaper than jeans. I'd put the leggings on & they could cover with sweat pants. For boys I assume you could still choose sweat pants but also use jeans. In my warmer climate I only buy about 4 pairs of leggings w/ 2 pairs of sweat pants but in colder climates I used 6-8 pairs of leggings and 6 sweats. At the time the hanes matching tops & bottoms - I would actually buy several sets for school and play.
