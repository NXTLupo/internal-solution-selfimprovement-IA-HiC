I’m an ICU RN. The hardest thing for me is the ‘death by 1000 paper cuts’ burnout that results from the loss of ancillary staff. They cut housekeeping, so now we pull our own trash and linens every shift, and sometimes have to clean a room to accept an admission. We rarely have transport available, so I’m pushing a critical patient down to CT by myself while also managing lines, watching the monitor, etc.  They stopped having logistics folks at night, so if I need a specific supply I’m calling all over or walking to other floors to find it.

Nurse here, 23 years.  I say my burn out is from having 16 hours of work but only 11.5 hours to do it.    I’m currently long term/skilled care. I hear it from management, the patients, the families, other departments.  Anytime anyone says “not my job,” somehow becomes mine.  I have 25 residents to give medications to.  Not just once, but at bedtime, some again at midnight, a few at 4am, then a bunch with fingersticks at 6am.  It’s like sleep isn’t important anymore.  I have to help the one CNA i get for those 25 residents make sure they get their 3-4 showers, q2 hr bed checks, and 4-5 get-ups done.  I answer the phones.  I answer call lights.  I glove up when they had tacos or chili for dinner.  I deal with complaints.  I assess and report.  I walk all over the building looking for linens and trash bags.  I search laundry for hoyer lift pads.  I make sure dialysis people get breakfast.  I deal with COPD smokers complaining they can’t breathe.  I educate and encourage folks to use their cpaps.  I have to make sure no one is wearing gloves in the hallway, heaven forbid!!  I’m the one getting screamed at by family that visits 2x/year bc grandma “wasn’t like this til she came here.”  It’s my job to check and record the temp of every single mini fridge that’s in my assignment.  I have to check those mini fridges for outdated food.  Everything that goes on falls on me.  I get kicked in the face and get, “what could you have done better?” I’m supposed to know everyone’s diagnosis, all their meds, allergies, and code status by heart it seems.  A doc enters a questionable order, I’m the one being investigated if I don’t fix it.  Someone says their dinner was cold, it’s my fault somehow, not the fault of the resident that let it sit there for 30 minutes while they were on the phone, or whatever.  I went to nursing school, not ass-kissing school, not jack-of-all-trades school.  I didn’t sign up to deal with every problem everyone has.  I can’t empathize with people refusing dialysis screaming to go to ER bc they can’t breathe.  There’s no pity for the alert and oriented continent person that chooses to urinate in bed, refuses to be changed, and ends up with skin breakdown.  I’m expected to give and give physically and emotionally forever.  It has only gotten worse as the years pass.  My residents are younger on average than ever.  They are needier and more demanding on average than in the past.  No one wants to be held accountable for their own well-being…so let’s make it my fault.  I’m tired of saying I’m sorry when I’m really not.  My fix would be to bring back the ancillary staff!!! More CNAs, more EVS, more social services, more maintenance staff, more dietary.  Take some of this load off my back before it breaks!
